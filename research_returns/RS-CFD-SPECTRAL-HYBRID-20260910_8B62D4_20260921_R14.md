Two consequences are decisive.

First, `median(FFT)-median(hybrid)` is not the same statistic as `median(FFT-hybrid)`. The 16^3 `random32` case actually flips sign: the published arm-median summary is -0.203 ms while the repeat-paired median is +0.105 ms. Small sub-millisecond signs from the frozen five-repeat matrix are therefore summary-sensitive and must not be promoted into a stable speedup/slowdown claim.

Second, the 32^3 `random256` all-fallback control has paired deltas spanning **-11.547 to +14.941 ms** despite zero sparse calls. Its paired median (+1.475 ms) is larger than the 32^3 `random32` paired median (+0.328 ms). Hence the latter's small positive aggregate margin remains non-identifiable as a sparse-compute benefit from these frozen data.

A same-grid, same-repeat-index descriptive contrast `delta(random32)-delta(random256)` is `[+2.323,+7.613,+11.875,-15.773,-1.380]` ms, median `+2.323` ms, mean `+0.932` ms, three of five positive. This is deliberately **not** interpreted causally: the initial-condition cases are distinct and were not randomized as counterfactual pairs.

The Taylor-Green cases survive this audit better as revalidation candidates. 32^3 Taylor-Green has five of five positive paired deltas with median +24.783 ms, materially larger than the all-fallback control median; 16^3 Taylor-Green is also five of five positive with median +5.056 ms, although the frozen matrix contains no same-grid 16^3 zero-sparse control. With only five repeats and author-generated frozen evidence, neither is upgraded to an independent speedup certificate.
