## 5. Independent breaker theorem: `q_b=5` by character intersection

This is the first place where the present audit materially changes the proof route.

For an odd prime `q` with `q` not dividing `6`, the two parity-branch phase images associated with the scalar law are

`I_0(q)={-3x^2/2 : x in F_q}`,
`I_1(q)={-3x^2/2-1/2 : x in F_q}`.

Define the N2 readout predicate

`Break(q) iff I_0(q) union I_1(q)=F_q`.

No breaker object is postulated. `Break(q)` is simply a predicate on finite-field images of the already reconstructed scalar law.

Let

`Q_0={x^2:x in F_q}`

including zero. Multiplication by the nonzero scalar `-3/2` is a bijection. Since

`(-1/2)/(-3/2)=1/3`,

the covering condition is equivalent to

`Q_0 union (Q_0+delta)=F_q`, `delta=1/3`.

Both sets have `(q+1)/2` elements. Hence they cover the field iff their intersection has exactly one element.

Let `chi` be the quadratic character and let

`N_delta=|Q_0 intersect (Q_0+delta)|`.

For nonzero `delta`, an exact character calculation gives

`N_delta=(q+1+chi(delta)+chi(-delta))/4`.

For completeness, split off the two possible endpoints `y=0,delta`. On the remaining `q-2` points use the indicator
`(1+chi(y))(1+chi(y-delta))/4`. The required sums on the punctured domain are

`sum chi(y) = -chi(delta)`,
`sum chi(y-delta) = -chi(-delta)`,
`sum chi(y(y-delta)) = -1`.

Restoring the endpoint contributions gives the displayed formula.

Now solve `N_delta=1`.

### Case A: `q=3 mod 4`

Then `chi(-1)=-1`, so `chi(delta)+chi(-delta)=0` and

`N_delta=(q+1)/4`.

Equality to `1` forces `q=3`, which is excluded by `q` not dividing `6`.

### Case B: `q=1 mod 4`, `delta` square

Then both characters are `+1` and

`N_delta=(q+3)/4>1`

for every prime `q>=5`.

### Case C: `q=1 mod 4`, `delta` nonsquare

Then both characters are `-1` and

`N_delta=(q-1)/4`.

Equality to `1` forces `q=5`.

Finally, modulo `5`, `delta=1/3=2` is nonsquare and direct evaluation gives

`I_0(5)={0,1,4}`,
`I_1(5)={1,2,3}`,

## 8. Independent mixed-parity grade-gap extremal theorem

The physical word “longitudinal” is unnecessary for the native-instance arithmetic. Work only with the finite integer grade window

`W_k={0,1,...,k-1}`

for odd `k>=5`.

Choose distinct `u,v,w` such that `u` and `v` have the same parity while `w` has the opposite parity. Define

`D=|w-u||w-v|`.

The two factors are positive odd integers. Let `d_1>=d_2` be the unordered two distances.

Because the endpoints `0` and `k-1` have the same parity when `k` is odd, the largest distance between opposite-parity indices is at most `k-2`. There are now only two possibilities.

1. If `d_1<=k-4`, then
   `D<=d_1^2<=(k-4)^2<(k-2)(k-4)`.

2. If `d_1=k-2`, then the two indices realizing that distance must be one endpoint and the point one step from the opposite endpoint. For the same `w`, the second distinct index having the required parity is at distance at most `k-4`. Thus
   `D<=(k-2)(k-4)`.

The bound is attained, for example, by taking `w=1` and the two same-parity points `k-3,k-1`; their distances are `k-4,k-2`. Reflection gives the symmetric realization.

Hence

`max D=(k-2)(k-4)`

and every maximizing distance multiset is exactly

`{k-4,k-2}`.

`{5,7}`.
