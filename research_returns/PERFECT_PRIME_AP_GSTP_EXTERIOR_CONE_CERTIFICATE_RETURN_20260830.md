\[

\[

\left\{
a\,v_{I_0}

a\ge\sum_{I\ne I_0}|b_I|
\right\}.
\]

It is closed, convex, pointed, and solid. Since

\[
\mu_{I_0}=\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_j
>
\mu_*:=\max_{I\ne I_0}\prod_{i\in I}\lambda_i,

every nonzero vector of \(K_j\) is carried strictly into its interior:

\[
\sum_{I\ne I_0}|\mu_Ib_I|
\le
\mu_*\sum_{I\ne I_0}|b_I|
<
\mu_{I_0}a.
\]

For \(j=m\), take the positive ray generated by \(v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_m\).

Therefore, for the present finite-dimensional problem,

\[
\boxed{

T_m\text{ has positive simple spectrum}.

This equivalence is important: GSTP is not merely a clever way to prove simplicity of the known eigenvalue \(1\); it asks for the much stronger global spectral statement.
