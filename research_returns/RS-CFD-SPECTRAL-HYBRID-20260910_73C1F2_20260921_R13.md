## Exact pathwise break-even identity

Consider one trajectory with routing decisions `j=1,...,N`. Let:

- `d_j` be the dense FFT cost that would be paid at decision `j`;
- `s_j` be the sparse cost when decision `j` is certified sparse-eligible;
- `g_j >= 0` be exact guard/routing overhead;
- `I_j` be 1 when sparse is used and 0 when dense fallback is used;
- `DeltaSetup` be hybrid-only setup minus dense-only setup outside the per-decision terms.

If a fallback executes the same dense work as its dense counterfactual and common costs cancel, then the finite pathwise identity is

`DenseTotal - HybridTotal = sum_{I_j=1}(d_j-s_j) - sum_j g_j - DeltaSetup`.

Therefore **fallback calls contribute no positive sparse saving by themselves**. All guard/setup overhead must be financed by savings on decisions that actually execute sparse arithmetic.

For the homogeneous special case `d_j=D`, `s_j=S`, `g_j=G`, with `k` fallbacks among `N` decisions and fixed overhead `A=DeltaSetup`, the hybrid wins exactly iff

`N G + A < (N-k)(D-S)`, provided `D>S`.

The largest admissible integer fallback count is

`k_max = ceil(N - (N G + A)/(D-S)) - 1`, clamped to `[-1,N]`.

Only if one additionally adopts an expectation/IID model may this be rewritten as

`G + A/N < (1-p)(D-S)`.

That probability form is a cost model, not a stochastic theorem about Navier–Stokes trajectories.

The standalone checker exhaustively compared direct costs with the derived inequality for **31,752 exact rational finite cases**, including the strict-integer `k_max` boundary, and passed two heterogeneous pathwise identity controls.

The last row is the decisive negative control. `random256` made **zero sparse calls** and all 49 evaluations used FFT fallback, yet its aggregate median hybrid time was 1.475 ms lower than the dense median. Sparse-arithmetic attribution for that 1.475 ms is therefore **exactly zero**. The observed difference must come from setup/control-path/timing variation or another non-sparse implementation difference.

This yields a new performance-attribution gate:

1. `sparse_calls = 0` => no aggregate total-time delta may be called a sparse-compute speedup.
2. `sparse_calls > 0` but `D-H` near the all-fallback/control scale => aggregate medians alone do not identify sparse causal benefit; paired decomposition is required.
3. A positive frozen-trace margin `D-H` is only an **additional-overhead budget for that exact trace**: if a stricter safety guard adds more than this amount, the recorded total-time win disappears. It is not a portable speedup guarantee.

Consequently, the 16³ random32 result is already losing at the total-cost level, while the 32³ random32 +0.911 ms difference is smaller than the +1.475 ms all-fallback control delta and cannot be cleanly attributed to sparse arithmetic from these aggregates. Taylor–Green remains a bounded positive-total observation despite 46/49 fallbacks, but causal decomposition of its 5.029/24.258 ms margins still requires matched per-decision costs. The structural shear/edge cases have much larger recorded margins, so they remain the strongest candidates for provisioned-host revalidation; this statement concerns only the frozen finite trajectories.
