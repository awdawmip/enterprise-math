## Three-arm same-trajectory design

For each deterministic repeat of the primary 32^3 Taylor-Green case, run three numerically equivalent arms from identical state/Source input:

- `A`: unmodified dense FFT baseline.
- `B`: guarded hybrid with automatic sparse/fallback routing.
- `C`: guarded **forced-fallback** control: sparse execution disabled, but the same guard/wrapper/fallback machinery retained.

The decisive per-repeat contrasts are:

- `A - B`: end-to-end benefit of the automatic hybrid over the original dense path.
- `C - B`: attribution contrast. Because `B` and `C` share the guarded path and the same physical trajectory, positive values isolate the benefit of allowing sparse execution more directly than a different-initial-condition all-fallback case.
- `A - C`: negative-control diagnostic for guard/wrapper/order effects when sparse arithmetic is never executed.

Correctness is a hard gate: A/B/C must preserve Nyquist treatment, pressure/diffusion, Source ordering and RK4 staging; B and C must match A within the frozen numerical tolerances; route logs must prove B executes at least one sparse nonlinear call while C executes zero sparse calls. Shadow dense evaluation of each sparse-eligible call remains outside the production timing path and is used for numerical counterfactual checking.

## Exact order-balance and sign-test gate

Use complete cycles of all six three-arm orders:

`ABC, BCA, CAB, ACB, CBA, BAC`.

Across one complete cycle each arm appears exactly twice in each ordinal position. This removes deterministic first/second/third-position imbalance without assuming Gaussian timing noise. Report the confirmatory contrasts by order stratum; a sign reversal between order strata blocks promotion and requires diagnosis.

Predeclare two directional confirmatory hypotheses: `A-B > 0` and `C-B > 0`. Control familywise alpha 0.05 with Bonferroni alpha `0.025` per gate. For a non-tied contrast, use the exact one-sided sign tail `P[Bin(n, 1/2) >= k]`; ties are excluded from usable `n` and must be reported.

Exact admissible thresholds for designs made of full six-order cycles are:

| usable repeats n | full order cycles | positives required k | exact one-sided p | power if P(positive)=0.8 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 6 | 1 | 6 | 0.015625 | 0.262144 |
| 12 | 2 | 10 | 0.019287109375 | 0.558346 |
| 18 | 3 | 14 | 0.01544189453125 | 0.716354 |
| 24 | 4 | 18 | 0.011327922344207764 | 0.811071 |
| 30 | 5 | 21 | 0.02138697262853384 | 0.938913 |

Therefore `n=12` is the compact minimum that allows two sign failures while retaining both complete order balance and the per-gate alpha 0.025 criterion. `n=24` is the preferred powered design: it uses four complete order cycles, requires at least 18/24 positive contrasts on **each** confirmatory gate, and has about 81.1% power if the true probability of a positive contrast is 0.8. It requires 72 timed trajectory runs for the three arms before any separate shadow checks.

The forced-fallback arm is also a negative-control gate. If `A-C` itself produces a one-sided positive sign-test result at alpha 0.05, the harness shows an unexplained all-fallback advantage and sparse-speed attribution is blocked until diagnosed. Failure to reject that negative-control test is only a diagnostic; it is not an equivalence proof.
