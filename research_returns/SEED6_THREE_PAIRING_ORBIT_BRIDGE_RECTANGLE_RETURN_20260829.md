## 1. Executive result

Let

\[
A=\{a,b,c,d\}
\]

be four distinct multiplicative atoms. For the Seed-6 specialization,

\[
(a,b,c,d)=(2,3,p,q),\qquad p,q>3,\ p\neq q.
\]

There are exactly three unordered perfect matchings:

\[
M_0=\{\{a,b\},\{c,d\}\},
\]

\[
M_1=\{\{a,c\},\{b,d\}\},
\]

\[
M_2=\{\{a,d\},\{b,c\}\}.
\]

Under multiplication of each pair these become

\[
P_0=\{ab,cd\},\quad
P_1=\{ac,bd\},\quad
P_2=\{ad,bc\},
\]

and every state has the same total product

\[
(ab)(cd)=(ac)(bd)=(ad)(bc)=abcd.
\]

For Seed-6 this is

\[
\{6,pq\},\quad
\{2p,3q\},\quad
\{2q,3p\},
\]

with common product \(6pq\).

The common-product identity is not the main structure. It is a consequence of commutativity. The exact local object is instead:

1. the three perfect matchings of \(K_4\);
2. the natural action
   \[
   S_4\longrightarrow S_3
   \]
   on these three matchings;
3. the six two-atom supports, which form the Johnson graph
   \[
   J(4,2),
   \]
   i.e. the octahedral graph;
4. the complement involution on two-subsets, whose three antipodal pairs are exactly \(M_0,M_1,M_2\);
5. for each matching \(M_i\), a canonical bridge rectangle
   \[
   K_4\setminus M_i\cong C_4\cong K_{2,2},
   \]
   whose two alternating perfect matchings are the other two pairing states.

Thus the minimal state quotient is a three-state switch triangle \(K_3\), but the richer and more faithful local carrier is the six-vertex octahedral support cell \(J(4,2)\) together with its atom-support presentation and complement involution.

The exact action is:

\[
\ker(S_4\to S_3)=V_4,
\qquad
\operatorname{im}(S_4\to S_3)=S_3.
\]

The stabilizer of one pairing state has order \(8\) and is

\[
S_2\wr S_2\cong D_8.
\]

Every transposition of two pairing states has exactly two lifts that are single transpositions of atoms. Those two lifts differ by a nontrivial \(V_4\) kernel element.

The six-support incidence graph has

\[
|\operatorname{Aut}J(4,2)|=48,
\]

while atom permutations induce only \(24\) of these automorphisms. The additional central involution is support complementation

\[
S\mapsto A\setminus S.
\]

Therefore a bare unlabeled octahedron forgets part of the “generated by four atoms” semantics. A valid `PAIRING_CELL_V1` should retain the atom-support map or equivalent incidence typing rather than store only a six-node abstract graph.

For distinct prime atoms there is also an exact multiplicative incidence signature beyond the total product:

- within one pairing state, the two semiprime values have gcd \(1\);
- between any two distinct pairing states, the \(2\times2\) cross-gcd matrix contains the four underlying atoms exactly once;
- equivalently, among the six two-atom products, `gcd > 1` is exactly the \(J(4,2)\) adjacency relation.

This is genuine structural information, but it is generic four-atom support incidence rather than a Seed-6-specific theorem.

An exact regression over all \(\binom{25}{2}=300\) unordered pairs \((p,q)\) from the first 25 primes \(>3\) finds the same abstract pairing cell in every case. Numeric ordering adds two presentation strata—85 cases with \(2q<3p\), 215 with \(3p<2q\)—but this magnitude label is not intrinsic pairing geometry.

Accordingly:

\[
\boxed{\text{Seed-6 gives a marked realization of a generic four-atom pairing cell.}}
\]

No new unlabeled Seed-6-specific local combinatorial invariant was found in this task. The positive residue is to test whether nontrivial structure appears only after global gluing, degeneration, or seed transfer.

## 2. A — Exactly three unordered perfect matchings

A perfect matching of four labeled atoms partitions the set into two disjoint two-element subsets.

Fix atom \(a\). It must be paired with exactly one of \(b,c,d\).

- If \(a\) pairs with \(b\), the remaining pair is \(\{c,d\}\).
- If \(a\) pairs with \(c\), the remaining pair is \(\{b,d\}\).
- If \(a\) pairs with \(d\), the remaining pair is \(\{b,c\}\).

Hence there are exactly

\[
3=(4-1)!!
\]

unordered perfect matchings, namely \(M_0,M_1,M_2\) above.

There is no fourth unordered state.

For Seed-6:

\[
M_0=\{\{2,3\},\{p,q\}\},
\]

\[
M_1=\{\{2,p\},\{3,q\}\},
\]

\[
M_2=\{\{2,q\},\{3,p\}\}.
\]

Multiplication maps these to the three numerical presentations in the taskbook, but the matching exists before any numerical ordering or distance is imposed.

## 3. B — Minimal re-pairing moves

### 3.1 Atom transposition as the smallest local move

A minimal labeled move exchanges two atoms.

Relative to

\[
M_0=ab|cd,
\]

there are six atom transpositions.

The two within-pair swaps

\[
(a\,b),\qquad(c\,d)
\]

fix \(M_0\).

The four cross-pair swaps move to another state:

\[
(a\,c),(b\,d):\quad M_0\leftrightarrow M_2,
\]

\[
(a\,d),(b\,c):\quad M_0\leftrightarrow M_1.
\]

Thus every outgoing state edge from \(M_0\) has exactly two single-atom-transposition lifts.

The same statement holds cyclically for the other states.

### 3.2 Quotient switch

After forgetting the atom-level lift and retaining only the three matching states, the minimal nontrivial move is simply one transposition in \(S_3\):

\[
\tau_{01}=(M_0\,M_1),\quad
\tau_{12}=(M_1\,M_2),\quad
\tau_{20}=(M_2\,M_0).
\]

Each quotient switch fixes the third matching.

The state transition graph is therefore

\[
K_3.
\]

This graph is combinatorial only. No edge length is introduced.

### 3.3 Lift ambiguity

The two atom-transposition lifts of the same quotient switch are distinct operations on \(A\), but they induce the same permutation of \(\{M_0,M_1,M_2\}\).

Their ratio lies in the \(V_4\) kernel found below.

Therefore:

\[
\text{state switch} \neq \text{unique atom-level operation}.
\]

This distinction becomes important if future global gluing retains operator history.

## 4. C — Exact \(S_4\) action

### 4.1 Action

Every atom permutation \(\sigma\in S_4\) sends a perfect matching to a perfect matching. Therefore there is a homomorphism

\[
\Phi:S_4\to\operatorname{Sym}\{M_0,M_1,M_2\}\cong S_3.
\]

Exact enumeration of all \(24\) atom permutations gives all \(6\) permutations of the three matching states:

\[
|\operatorname{im}\Phi|=6.
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{im}\Phi=S_3.
\]

### 4.2 Kernel

The kernel has four elements:

\[
V_4=
\{
e,\,
(a\,b)(c\,d),\,
(a\,c)(b\,d),\,
(a\,d)(b\,c)
\}.
\]

Each of these fixes all three perfect matchings as unordered partitions.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{S_4/V_4\cong S_3.}
\]

This is a standard group-theoretic object, not a novelty claim.

### 4.3 Orbit and stabilizer

The action is transitive, so the orbit of every state contains all three states.

By orbit-stabilizer,

\[
|\operatorname{Stab}_{S_4}(M_i)|=\frac{24}{3}=8.
\]

For

\[
M_0=\{\{a,b\},\{c,d\}\},
\]

the stabilizer may swap within either pair and may exchange the two pairs. Thus

\[
\operatorname{Stab}_{S_4}(M_0)
\cong
(S_2\times S_2)\rtimes S_2
=
S_2\wr S_2
\cong D_8.
\]

The quotient of this stabilizer by \(V_4\) is the order-2 subgroup of \(S_3\) fixing \(M_0\).

### 4.4 Parity

Every element of the kernel \(V_4\) is even. Therefore the sign homomorphism on \(S_4\) factors through the state action:

\[
\operatorname{sgn}_{S_4}
=
\operatorname{sgn}_{S_3}\circ\Phi.
\]

So an atom-level move that induces an odd state permutation is odd, and one that induces an even state permutation is even.

However, an individual static matching state has no intrinsic parity. The action is transitive, and there is no canonical “even state” or “odd state”.

## 5. D — Bridge rectangle theorem

### 5.1 Rectangle associated to a carrier matching

Take

\[
M_0=ab|cd.
\]

Delete its two edges \(\{a,b\}\) and \(\{c,d\}\) from \(K_4\).

The remaining four edges are

\[
ac,\ ad,\ bc,\ bd.
\]

They form

\[
K_4\setminus M_0
\cong K_{2,2}
\cong C_4.
\]

Arrange them as

\[
R_{M_0}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
ac & ad\\
bc & bd
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its two diagonals are exactly the two other perfect matchings:

\[
\{ac,bd\}=M_1,
\]

\[
\{ad,bc\}=M_2.
\]

Thus a carrier state \(M_0\) canonically determines the switch between the other two states.

Cyclically:

\[
M_0\longleftrightarrow \{M_1,M_2\},
\]

\[
M_1\longleftrightarrow \{M_0,M_2\},
\]

\[
M_2\longleftrightarrow \{M_0,M_1\}.
\]

This is the exact `state ↔ opposite switch edge` correspondence.

### 5.2 Rank-one identity

The rectangle satisfies

\[
(ac)(bd)=(ad)(bc)=abcd.
\]

Equivalently the multiplicative \(2\times2\) determinant vanishes:

\[
(ac)(bd)-(ad)(bc)=0.
\]

This identity carries no new invariant by itself. It is the ordinary rank-one/commutativity identity

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
a\\ b
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
c&d
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The useful data are which four supports form the \(C_4\), which antipodal pair was omitted, and which two alternating matchings the rectangle connects.

### 5.3 One rectangle or two switches?

At the unordered state level, an un-oriented rectangle corresponds to one unordered switch edge:

\[
\{M_1,M_2\}.
\]

At the atom-operation level, that same quotient switch has two single-transposition lifts.

For \(R_{M_0}\):

- swapping rows \(a\leftrightarrow b\) exchanges the two diagonal states;
- swapping columns \(c\leftrightarrow d\) also exchanges the two diagonal states;
- doing both is the kernel operation \((a\,b)(c\,d)\), which fixes all three matching states.

Therefore the rectangle does not define two different quotient edges. It defines one quotient switch with two natural single-swap lifts.

### 5.4 Does rectangle orientation add intrinsic information?

Not in the unordered cell.

Row order, column order, and block order are presentation choices unless explicitly retained as extra decorations.

For example, exchanging the two carrier pairs by

\[
(a\,c)(b\,d)
\]

lies in \(V_4\) and fixes all three unordered matching states while changing the matrix presentation.

Therefore rectangle orientation is not an invariant of the basic pairing cell.

If a future task explicitly retains orientation or passage history, that is an additional structure and must be declared separately.

### 5.5 Triangle versus richer cell

At the quotient-of-states level, the minimal cell is exactly the triangle graph

\[
K_3.
\]

But the triangle forgets:

- the six individual two-atom supports;
- which support intersections share an atom;
- the atom-level lift ambiguity;
- the complement/antipodal relation;
- the bridge rectangle \(C_4\) itself.

Hence for local structural work the more faithful cell is the six-vertex support graph described next.

## 6. E — Exact invariants beyond total product

## 6.1 Valuation-support model

Associate to each two-atom product its support:

\[
ab\leftrightarrow\{a,b\},
\quad
ac\leftrightarrow\{a,c\},
\quad \ldots
\]

The six supports are exactly

\[
\binom{A}{2}.
\]

Each has Hamming/valuation support weight \(2\).

Define adjacency by one-atom overlap:

\[
S\sim T
\iff
|S\cap T|=1.
\]

This is the Johnson graph

\[
J(4,2).
\]

It has:

\[
V=6,\qquad
\deg=4,\qquad
E=12.
\]

Combinatorially it is the octahedral graph and also the line graph \(L(K_4)\).

### 6.2 Complement involution and matching states

Define

\[
\kappa(S)=A\setminus S.
\]

Because \(|A|=4\) and \(|S|=2\), \(\kappa\) is a fixed-point-free involution on the six support vertices.

Its three orbits are:

\[
\{\{a,b\},\{c,d\}\},
\]

\[
\{\{a,c\},\{b,d\}\},
\]

\[
\{\{a,d\},\{b,c\}\}.
\]

These are precisely the three pairing states.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{pairing states}=\text{antipodal pairs of }J(4,2).}
\]

The quotient by \(\kappa\) has three vertices and is naturally the \(K_3\) state graph.

### 6.3 GCD incidence for distinct primes

Now specialize to four distinct primes \(a,b,c,d\).

For support products \(x_S=\prod_{s\in S}s\),

\[
\gcd(x_S,x_T)>1
\iff
S\cap T\neq\varnothing.
\]

For distinct two-subsets, this is equivalent to

\[
|S\cap T|=1.
\]

Thus the positive-gcd incidence graph on the six semiprime products is exactly \(J(4,2)\).

Inside one matching state, the two supports are complements and disjoint, so

\[
\gcd(x_S,x_{\kappa(S)})=1.
\]

### 6.4 Cross-state gcd matrix

Take

\[
P_0=\{ab,cd\},
\quad
P_1=\{ac,bd\}.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\gcd(ab,ac) & \gcd(ab,bd)\\
\gcd(cd,ac) & \gcd(cd,bd)
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
c&d
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Hence the multiset of the four cross-gcds is

\[
\{a,b,c,d\}
\]

with each atom occurring exactly once.

The same property holds between any two distinct pairing states, up to a permutation of positions.

This is information beyond the common total product: it reconstructs the support-incidence pattern of the four-atom cell.

In this task it is used only as a structural invariant/certificate. No endpoint-recovery or factorization interpretation is made.

### 6.5 Bare octahedron overgenerates symmetry

Exact enumeration of all graph automorphisms gives

\[
|\operatorname{Aut}J(4,2)|=48.
\]

The subgroup induced by atom permutations has order

\[
24.
\]

The complement involution \(\kappa\) is a graph automorphism but is not induced by any single atom permutation.

Indeed:

\[
\operatorname{Aut}J(4,2)\cong S_4\times C_2,
\]

where the extra \(C_2\) is complement duality.

This is an important modeling guard.

If `PAIRING_CELL_V1` stores only an abstract octahedral graph, it permits symmetries that do not preserve the intended four-atom origin.

Therefore the cell should retain at least one of:

- the atom-support map \(S\subset A\);
- equivalent atom-incidence labels;
- or an explicitly typed \(S_4\) action distinguished from the full graph automorphism group.

### 6.6 Operator-decorated local holonomy

The bare state graph is \(K_3\). Decorate each edge with the transposition in \(S_3\) that exchanges its endpoints and fixes the third state:

\[
\tau_{01}=(01),
\qquad
\tau_{12}=(12),
\qquad
\tau_{20}=(20).
\]

Using right-to-left composition, the closed three-edge walk

\[
0\to1\to2\to0
\]

has operator product

\[
\tau_{20}\tau_{12}\tau_{01}
=
\tau_{12}.
\]

This fixes the returned state \(0\) but exchanges the other two states. Its square is identity.

Thus:

\[
\text{closed state walk} \not\Rightarrow \text{identity operator}.
\]

This is standard \(S_3\) groupoid behavior, not a novelty claim, but it is a precise warning for the global-gluing task: a state-only path forgets operator holonomy.

At the atom level, the corresponding lift remains nonunique up to \(V_4\).
