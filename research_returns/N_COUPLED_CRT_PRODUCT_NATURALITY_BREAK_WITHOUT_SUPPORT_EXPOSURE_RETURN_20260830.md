## 4. Theorem A — adaptive-history normal form

For every realized `G_hist-U` trace of arbitrary finite length `T`, including traces whose operations and stopping time depend on canonical representatives, quotient/remainder, carry and all prior history, there exist integer-unimodular matrices `U,V` such that

`B_T = U B_0 V`.

### Proof

Each allowed elementary row operation is multiplication on the left by an integer elementary matrix of determinant `+/-1`; each allowed elementary column operation is multiplication on the right by one. Products of integer-unimodular matrices remain integer-unimodular. The control semantics determine which factors appear and how many appear, but do not change this algebraic fact. Therefore

`U = U_{T-1} ... U_0 in GL_m(Z)`

and

`V = V_0 ... V_{T-1} in GL_n(Z)`,

with `B_T=UB_0V`. QED.

The theorem is deliberately transcript-agnostic: the control layer may fail CRT product naturality as violently as the frozen grammar permits.

## 5. Theorem B — all determinantal/Fitting ideals are invariant

For every integral matrix `B` and every integer-unimodular `U,V`,

`I_k(UBV) = I_k(B)`

for every `k`.

### Proof

By Cauchy-Binet, every `k x k` minor of `UBV` is an integer linear combination of `k x k` minors of `B`, because all minors of `U,V` are integers. Hence

`I_k(UBV) subseteq I_k(B)`.

Since `U^{-1},V^{-1}` are again integer matrices, apply the same argument to

`B = U^{-1}(UBV)V^{-1}`

to obtain the reverse inclusion. Therefore the ideals are equal. QED.

Consequences:

- every determinantal divisor is exactly invariant;
- the Smith/Fitting support visible through full-carrier minors is exactly invariant;
- no transcript-dependent sequence of allowed basis changes can create a new prime support event.

This is stronger than merely preserving determinant in the square full-rank case.
