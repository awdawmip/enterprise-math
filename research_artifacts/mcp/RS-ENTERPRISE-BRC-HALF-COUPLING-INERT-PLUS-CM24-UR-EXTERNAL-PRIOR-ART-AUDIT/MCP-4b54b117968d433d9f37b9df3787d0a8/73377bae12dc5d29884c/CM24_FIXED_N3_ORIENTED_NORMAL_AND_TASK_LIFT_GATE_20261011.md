## Principal-unit reduction and norm have different effects

If a diagonal period has valuation1, its class after dividing by p in \(K_{\rm fld}^{\times}/U_1\) retains its first nonzero unit residue. For odd p, \(-1\notin U_1\). Principal-unit reduction therefore does **not** by itself erase sign or all first-normal information. It discards higher principal-unit digits of an already chosen multiplicative representative.

The separately displayed operation
\[
a_i\longmapsto a_i^{p+1}=a_i a_i^p=N_{k/\mathbf F_p}(a_i)
\]
is the finite-field norm. Its kernel on \(k^\times\) has order p+1, and \(N(-a_i)=N(a_i)\). Even if \(a_i\in\mathbf F_p^\times\), the result is \(a_i^2\). This observation restricts the norm readout only.

The entire marked root set and H coordinate retain more information than the norm alone. For example, knowing the algebraic conjugate \(a_i^p\) allows algebraic rearrangement of \(N(a_i)\). That does not prove the rearranged algebraic factor is the Task's divided hypergeometric value. In particular, the source equality \(\bar\Phi(e_i,e_i)/p=N(a_i)\) is neither \(\Phi(e_i,e_i)/p=a_i\) nor \(G_p=c\,a_i\).
