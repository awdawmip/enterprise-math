## 3. Exact terminal presentation and observer types

This section gives the finite-module specialization of a support-bearing
terminal interface. It does not assert that every carrier permitted by the
Objective is a module or that every presentation allowed by the frozen
effective contract is a matrix presentation. Other terminal algebraic
types need their own interpretation and observer-preservation certificate;
the effective pipeline below remains available once such a type is supplied.

Fix an input integer `N ≥ 2`, and set `R_N = Z/NZ`. A finite explicit module
presentation is

`C = (d,e,A)`, with `d,e ≥ 0` finite and `A ∈ R_N^(d×e)`;

it denotes `M_N(C) = coker(A : R_N^e → R_N^d)`. Matrix entries have their
ordinary finite residue encoding. Additional named generators, ports,
relations or provenance, when required by the declared observer, must be
included as typed data; the unadorned cokernel does not preserve them by fiat.

For the distinct rational primes `ell | N`, define

`Supp_N(C) = {ell | N : M_N(C) ⊗_(R_N) F_ell ≠ 0}`

`             = {ell | N : rank_(F_ell)(A mod ell) < d}`.                 (1)

The equality follows from right exactness of tensor product: the fiber is
the cokernel of the reduced matrix, whose dimension is `d-rank(A mod ell)`.
This also covers `d=0` and `e=0` with their usual zero-dimensional meanings.
For a square matrix, this set is exactly the residue characteristics where
the matrix loses invertibility. It records support, not all valuation,
multiplicity or higher-order information. If those are required observers,
they must be retained separately. The primes in (1) are mathematical indices
in a proof, not secret inputs or calls to factor `N` in the construction.

Let `t` be a terminal object/state in the declared research scope. Only when
Source supplies its algebraic interpretation `M_N(t)` can one assert the
typed relation

`Pres_N(t,C) :⇔ M_N(t) ≅ M_N(C)`                                      (2)

with any required distinguished structure included in the isomorphism.
Equation (2) implies equality of the support sets by functoriality of
tensor product. If the original terminal object has no such supplied
interpretation, (2) is an unfilled type contract, not a theorem about it.

Fix the exact permitted observer/future-context family `H` for the claim.
An observer `o_h` has declared domain, codomain, interpretation and, if
needed, a declared error notion. This unit proves exact identities only.
Write `o_h(t)` for the observation after the specified context. No new
unbounded horizon or "all possible observations" is silently added to H.
A representation `q(t)=z` is observer-safe on this scope precisely when
there are target observers `o_hat_h` such that

`o_h(t) = o_hat_h(q(t))` for every reachable `t` and every `h ∈ H`.        (3)

If multiple observations concern the same state, (3) is a statement about
the tuple taken from that **same** state. Replacing them by independent
samples with the same marginals is not justified. Isomorphism (2) alone
proves (3) only for observers known to be invariant under the stated
isomorphism; it does not preserve generator labels, future mutations,
nonlinear relations or provenance automatically.

## 4. Commuting-square and composition lemma

**Lemma 1 (scoped representation and future composition).** Let `S` be the
reachable typed source states and `Z` a proposed representation carrier.
Let `q:S→Z`. For every allowed deterministic future operation `f:S→S`
(restricted to its declared domain), suppose a target operation `f_hat`
satisfies

`q ∘ f = f_hat ∘ q`,                                                  (4)

and source/target observers satisfy `o = o_hat ∘ q` on their declared
domains. Suppose these domains are compatible under the proposed
representation and under the compositions used. Then for every defined
finite allowed composition `w=f_k∘...∘f_1`,

`q ∘ w = w_hat ∘ q`, and `o ∘ w = o_hat ∘ w_hat ∘ q`.                 (5)

If `q` is not injective, a deterministic `f_hat` on `q(S)` exists exactly
when `q(s)=q(t)` implies equal definedness and, where defined,
`q(f(s))=q(f(t))`; a partial observer `o_hat` preserving both value and definedness exists
on `q(S)` exactly when its source domain `D_o ⊆ S` is q-saturated
(`q(s)=q(t)` implies `s∈D_o ⇔ t∈D_o`) and `o` is constant on each
q-fiber inside `D_o`. Mere value factorization of `o|D_o` through `q|D_o`
without saturation would not prove partial-map descent on all of S.
These existence statements are set-theoretic; effectiveness is separate.

*Proof.* The operation identity in (5) follows by induction on `k`:
substitute (4) for the final operation and the induction identity for the
prefix. Compose with `o_hat` to obtain the observation identity. If a
descended operation exists, equal represented states have equal images
and definedness because they are one target input. Conversely the stated
fiber condition defines `f_hat(q(s))=q(f(s))` unambiguously on `q(S)`;
for observers, saturation makes `domain(o_hat)=q(D_o)` have exactly
the required inverse-image domain, and fiber constancy makes its values
well-defined. Both conditions are necessary for partial-map descent.
Defining a function on quotient fibers does not supply a program computing it. This proves every assertion. ∎

Partial domains matter: a representation that forgets whether a later
operation is permitted is not a valid bridge merely because values agree
when both happen to be defined. Nor does validity for every finite word
establish preservation of an undeclared infinite-time limit observation.

For a declared Markov kernel on a sufficiently complete joint state, the
corresponding one-step condition is

`q_* K_f(s,·) = K_hat_f(q(s),·)`.                                    (6)

With measurable q and kernels on their declared measurable spaces, (6)
and the scoped observer identity preserve the joint represented finite
history law by iterated conditioning/integration. Each induction step
uses the kernel conditional on the **whole retained state**, so persistent
random state, aliases and correlations must be retained or certified safe.
Marginal equality for isolated calls does not supply (6). This is a
conditional kernel extension of the same square; it does not grant a
kernel's admission, measurability or effective sampler. We do not infer a
new infinite-horizon theorem from finite-horizon agreement here.

**Application to presentations.** If a terminal map `g(t)=C` satisfies
`Pres_N(t,g(t))`, support preservation in (1) is automatic. To continue
using `C` after that terminal boundary, the operations and other observers
must additionally satisfy (3)-(6) for the stated scope. Thus an algebraic
support certificate and an observer/future-operation certificate have
different, explicitly bounded jobs.

## 5. When the bridge is computed inside the universal simulator

**Lemma 2 (effective terminal pipeline).** Suppose a public, oracle-free
uniform effective runtime `P(N,p)` returns a finite code `z`, with the
probabilistic or effective-branching semantics required by the frozen
contract. Its public data are exactly the allowed inputs, not hidden
factors, correlated advice or secret implementation. Suppose a public
partial computable function `g(N,p,z)` is defined on every reachable
terminal code and returns a well-formed finite presentation C. Finally,
suppose the actual terminal interpretation `t` and this code satisfy

`Pres_N(t,g(N,p,z))`, together with (3) for the observers claimed.       (7)

Then the single public program `Q = run P; compute g; return C` belongs
to the frozen terminal-presentation semantics. In the probabilistic
case, assuming P terminates almost surely, Q does also: g takes finitely
many steps on each reached terminal input, with no uniform bound needed.
The existing RRBF7 theorem applies to Q's exact C law. The joint tuple of
any fixed finite set of public computable readouts, each defined and
terminating on every reachable terminal input, can be computed on that
same output/execution, preserving its joint law. An additional partial
readout not satisfying this promise needs its own termination/subprobability
scope; it cannot silently inherit probability-one termination. Equation (7) then
transfers the scoped support/readout assertion to the source object.

In the nondeterministic case, a public computable total-on-terminal map
g sends the existing c.e. terminal-code support to a c.e. presentation
support; dovetail its computations if needed. A bijection of terminal
codes or decidability of the terminal support is not required.

*Proof.* Composition of the public effective interpreters for P and g
is a public effective interpreter for Q. Its random input and branch
choices are those declared by the combined program; g does not add an
oracle. Almost-sure termination follows pointwise on P's probability-one
termination event. Apply the accepted emulator theorem to this Q, rather
than reprove it. Deterministic pushforward by the combined readout tuple
preserves the already equal law. The support claim in the branching case
is the effective image of a c.e. enumeration. Equations (1), (3) and (7)
provide the typed semantic transfer. ∎

Two important subcases prevent artificial obstacles:

* If P already returns C and that output type is the declared semantic
  presentation, g is the identity. There is no new representation problem.
* If the Task's complete intended terminal observation is a public
  effective scalar/word function `r(N,p,z)` defined and terminating on
  all reachable terminal inputs, it can be executed by the same simulator. Its law/support is reproducible without a separate
  native-state reconstruction, provided that is indeed the whole scoped
  observation and no stronger presentation assertion is claimed.

The second subcase alone does **not** prove that an arbitrary serialization
of z is a presentation of `M_N(t)`. Nor does preserving one scalar license
erasing a joint relation relevant to a permitted future context. It may
nevertheless discharge the Task's observation-level bridge if Source
expressly selects that scope. Requiring a stronger reconstruction when
Source requires only this effective readout would add an unjustified target.

## 6. A relational terminal contract: exactly what is and is not automatic

A statement `for every reachable z there exists a finite C satisfying R`
is not itself a construction of a uniform public function g. This is a
distinction between a specified mathematical relation and an effective
realizer, not a claim that the intended relation is uncomputable.

**Lemma 3 (sufficient effective selection certificate).** Let
`R(N,p,z,C)` be a computably enumerable relation on finite codes, and suppose
every reachable terminal `(N,p,z)` has at least one witness C. Then a
public partial computable selector g can return such a C on all reachable
terminal inputs: enumerate R, or dovetail its witness recognizers, until
the first witness for that exact input appears. It terminates there by
the nonempty-witness assumption. If every such witness satisfies (7) for
the declared observations, this selector supplies Lemma 2's bridge.

*Proof.* The enumeration procedure is uniform and public. Every output
satisfies R, and the first matching witness appears after finitely many
enumeration steps for each promised input. The stated semantic condition
on every witness makes that choice safe. No running-time bound follows. ∎

If R admits multiple different presentations, this procedure fixes a new
public choice rule. It preserves the observers certified for **all**
witnesses; it does not automatically preserve an original probability law
over literal matrices or presentation labels. If that law is part of the
claim, one must preserve the original selector/randomness and full joint
state, or separately prove the appropriate law identity. A mere module
isomorphism is insufficient for that stronger assertion.

If c.e. recognition or witness safety has not been supplied, Lemma 3 does
not apply. That failure to apply proves neither that no selector exists
nor that an admitted escape exists. Conversely, an effective observer may
already bypass selection entirely under the second subcase of Lemma 2.
These alternatives have to be checked against the actual terminal contract.
