## The admitted theorem

Let s ≥ 5 be an odd integer, q = 2s − 3 a prime, r = (s − 1)/2, and let all fractions and inverse operations below be interpreted in F_q. For a in F_q× define

λ(a) = −s a − (2a)⁻¹,     J_s = {−r, −r+1, …, r} as literal residues.

Then Im λ ⊆ J_s if and only if (s,q) = (5,7).

These exact quantifiers matter. The proof is not a theorem for arbitrary odd prime powers, arbitrary subsets of the same size, all near-extremal offsets, or arbitrary field extensions.

## Static dependency check

1. Parameter range, inversions and literal interval.
Since s is odd, q ≡ 3 mod 4 and q ≥ 7. In F_q, s=3/2 and r=1/4. The integers in the centered interval have largest pairwise difference 2r=s−1=(q+1)/2<q, so their residue map is injective. Therefore |J_s|=s=(q+3)/2. The denominators 2,3,5 and their products in the proof are units for every allowed q. In particular q=7 remains in range.

2. Fibres with fixed-point multiplicity.
For nonzero a,b,
λ(a)−λ(b)=(a−b)(−s+(2ab)⁻¹).
Every fibre is exactly an orbit of the involution a ↦ (3a)⁻¹. Its fixed points satisfy a²=1/3. With ε=η_q(3), their number is 1+ε. Thus, for U=Im λ, |U|=(q+ε)/2. Distinct fixed points have distinct images: a fibre containing a fixed point is that singleton orbit. The remaining image values have precisely two preimages. This is the multiplicity needed for image moments, not merely a cardinality bound.

3. Symmetry and zero.
λ(−a)=−λ(a), so U is symmetric. The zero equation is a²=−1/3. Since η_q(−1)=−1 and η_q(1/3)=ε, zero belongs to U exactly when ε=−1. This determines the missing set under a containment assumption.

4. Domain moments, including the q=7 endpoint.
For a nonzero Laurent exponent e among ±2,±4, q−1 does not divide e because q−1≥6. Consequently its sum over F_q× vanishes. Expanding λ² and λ⁴ leaves constants s and 3s²/2 respectively; the sum of 1 over F_q× is q−1=−1 in F_q. Hence
D₂=Σ_a λ(a)²=−s,
D₄=Σ_a λ(a)⁴=−3s²/2.
At a fixed argument, λ(a)²=2s and λ(a)⁴=4s². Restoring the singleton correction gives
2M₂=D₂+(1+ε)2s,
2M₄=D₄+(1+ε)4s²,
so M₂=s(1+2ε)/2 and M₄=s²(5/4+2ε).
The multiplicity correction has a plus sign because doubling the image counts fixed values twice while the domain counted them once.

5. Exact literal-interval moments.
The finite polynomial sums on {−r,…,r} are
J₂=r(r+1)(2r+1)/3,
J₄=r(r+1)(2r+1)(3r²+3r−1)/15.
Reducing r=1/4 in the allowed fields yields J₂=5/32 and J₄=−1/512. These use the full literal interval; an arbitrary symmetric set would not share these moments.

6. ε=+1 branch.
Under U⊆J_s, |U|=s−1 and 0∉U. Because both sets are symmetric, the one omitted value is exactly 0. Thus J₂=M₂=9/4. 7. ε=−1 branch.
Containment gives |U|=s−2 and 0∈U. Its two omitted values must be a nonzero pair {t,−t}. Therefore
5/32−2t²=−3/4, so t²=29/64.
The fourth moment is M₄=−27/16. Substitution yields

equivalently −845/2048=−3456/2048.
