## 2. Exact birational maps and their rational exceptions

Put `a=P+Q`, `b=P-Q`, `R=PQ`, and retain the parent's quartic coordinates

\[
T=U+V,\qquad Y=2TD,
\qquad Y^2=(a^2-T^2)(T^2-b^2).
\]

On the nonboundary domain the forward map is

\[
\xi=R\frac{a+T}{a-T},\qquad
\eta=\frac{aRY}{(a-T)^2}.
\]

Indeed

\[
\xi-P^2=\frac{aP(T-b)}{a-T},\qquad
\xi-Q^2=\frac{aQ(T+b)}{a-T},
\]

so their product with `xi` is exactly `eta^2` by the quartic equation. Conversely

\[
T=a\frac{\xi-R}{\xi+R},\qquad
Y=\frac{4aR\eta}{(\xi+R)^2},

\[
D=\frac{Y}{2T},\quad
U=\frac12\left(T+\frac{P^2-Q^2}{T}\right),\quad
V=\frac12\left(T-\frac{P^2-Q^2}{T}\right).
\]

Direct substitution proves both compositions are the identity on these domains. No square-root branch choice is hidden in either direction.

At `xi=R` the cubic value is `-P^2 Q^2(P-Q)^2`, and at `xi=-R` it is `-P^2 Q^2(P+Q)^2`. Both are strictly negative, so neither denominator exception is a rational point of `E`. The four points

## 3. Uniform torsion and exact descent information

A split-cubic rational two-torsion point `(e_i,0)` is divisible by two precisely when both other root differences `e_i-e_j` are rational squares. This is the classical two-division criterion, stated explicitly in Bekker–Zarhin, Theorem 2.1; zero counts as a square.

The roots `0` and `Q^2` each have a negative difference, so their points cannot be halved over `Q`. The point at `P^2` can be halved only if `P^2-Q^2=4AB` is a square. Coprimality then makes both `A` and `B` squares, contradicting the accepted impossibility of positive `u^4+v^4=C^2`. Hence there is no rational point of order four.

Mazur's rational torsion theorem therefore leaves exactly

\[
E(\mathbb Q)_{\rm tors}\simeq
(\mathbb Z/2)^2\quad\hbox{or}\quad
\mathbb Z/2\times\mathbb Z/6.
\]

This is resolved for any input core by a finite exact criterion, with no claim that the second alternative never occurs. Put `S=P^2+Q^2`, `K=P^2Q^2`, and

\[
\psi_3(X)=3X^4-4SX^3+6KX^2-K^2.
\]

The second alternative occurs if and only if there is an integer divisor `z` of `K^2`, of either sign, for which `psi_3(z)=0` and `z(z-P^2)(z-Q^2)` is a nonzero integer square. To verify the division polynomial without importing a formula, put `f(X)=X(X-P^2)(X-Q^2)`. The identity

\[
(3X^2-2SX+K)^2-4f(X)(3X-S)=-\psi_3(X)
\]

and the doubling law show that `psi_3(xi)=0` is equivalent to `xi(2H)=xi(H)` for a point with nonzero ordinate. Then `2H=-H`: the other possibility `2H=H` would force `H=O`. Necessity of an integer divisor follows from generalized Nagell–Lutz integrality and the rational-root divisibility rule. Conversely the square test supplies such a point of exact order three. This is a decision algorithm as a function of the retained core, not a numerical torsion guess.

For rational points away from `E[2]`, the standard Kummer map retains the three square classes

\[
\delta(\xi,\eta)=[\xi,\xi-P^2,\xi-Q^2]
\quad\text{in }(\mathbb Q^*/\mathbb Q^{*2})^3.
\]

Their product is a square. By the cited two-division theorem, the three classes are all trivial exactly when the point belongs to `2E(Q)`; the two-torsion boundary remains in its explicit table. Each class has a signed squarefree representative supported on primes dividing `2PQ(P^2-Q^2)`: outside this set the three distinct integral roots imply at most one factor has positive valuation, while negative valuations are shared by all three, so the square product forces every such valuation to be even. For representatives `d_1,d_2,d_3` of square product, the exact covering equations are `d_1 z_1^2-d_2 z_2^2=P^2` and `d_1 z_1^2-d_3 z_3^2=Q^2`, with nonzero `z_i`; the original point is recovered by `xi=d_1 z_1^2`, `eta=s z_1 z_2 z_3`, `s^2=d_1d_2d_3`. This supplies finite-support exact descent information; it does not compute all Selmer groups, ranks, or generators and asserts no local-global converse. Square-class projection does not replace the retained point for P11 reconstruction.
