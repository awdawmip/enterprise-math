## 3. Exact inverse-Bézout theorem

Define the order-`m` Bézout matrix `B_c=Bez(p_m,q_c)` by the convention

\[
\frac{p_m(y)q_c(x)-p_m(x)q_c(y)}{x-y}
=\mathbf v(x)^TB_c\mathbf v(y),
\qquad
\mathbf v(x)=(1,x,\ldots,x^n)^T.
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Theorem 3.1

For every `m>=2` and every `c>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal G_c=\kappa_m B_c^{-1},
\qquad
\kappa_m=(-1)^m m^n(n!)^2.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof

At a root `X_{c,i}` of `q_c`, evaluating (3.1) at two roots and taking the diagonal limit gives

\[
\mathcal V_c B_c\mathcal V_c^T
=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(p_m(X_{c,i})q_c'(X_{c,i})\bigr).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Because the roots `X_{c,i}` are distinct, `\mathcal V_c` is invertible; hence

\[
B_c^{-1}
=\mathcal V_c^T
\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac1{p_m(X_{c,i})q_c'(X_{c,i})}\right)
\mathcal V_c.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Now

\[
q_c'(X_{c,i})=(-1)^i i!(n-i)!,
\qquad
w_i=\frac{n!}{q_c'(X_{c,i})},
\]

while

\[
p_m(X_{c,i})=(-1)^m\prod_{j=0}^{n}(i+1+c+mj).
\]

Therefore

\[
w_iE_i(c)
=\frac{(-1)^m m^n(n!)^2}
{p_m(X_{c,i})q_c'(X_{c,i})}
=\frac{\kappa_m}{p_m(X_{c,i})q_c'(X_{c,i})}.
\]

Substitution into (3.4) proves (3.2). ∎

## 4. One Euclidean step lowers the Bézoutian dimension exactly

Set

\[
r_c(x)=q_c(x)-p_m(x).
\]

Because both `p_m,q_c` are monic of degree `m`, `r_c` has degree `n`. Its leading coefficient is

\[
\boxed{

\tag{4.1}
\]

Perform the first Euclidean division

\[
p_m(x)=s_c(x)r_c(x)+t_c(x),
\qquad
\deg t_c\le n-1.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The quotient is linear and its leading coefficient is `1/a_c`.

Since `q_c=p_m+r_c`, bilinearity gives

\[
B_c=Bez(p_m,r_c).
\]

Write the coefficient vector of `r_c` as `(\rho_c,a_c)`, separating powers `0,...,n-1` from `x^n`. Using (4.2),

\[
Bez(p_m,r_c)
=-\frac1{a_c}r_c r_c^T-\widehat{Bez}(r_c,t_c),
\tag{4.3}
\]

where `\widehat{Bez}(r_c,t_c)` is the order-`m` embedding of the order-`n` Bézout matrix with last row and column zero. Consequently

\[
B_c=
\begin{pmatrix}
-B_n(r_c,t_c)-a_c^{-1}\rho_c\rho_c^T & -\rho_c\\
-\rho_c^T & -a_c
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Taking the Schur complement of the last coordinate cancels the rank-one term exactly:

\[
\boxed{
B_c/(-a_c)=-B_n(r_c,t_c).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

This is a forced Euclidean reduction, not an adaptive pivot argument.
