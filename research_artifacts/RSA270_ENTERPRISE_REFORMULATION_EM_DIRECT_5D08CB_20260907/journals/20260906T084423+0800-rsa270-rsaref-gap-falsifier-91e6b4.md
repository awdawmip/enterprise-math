## Event

This stage tested the strongest concrete consequence of the provisional hypothesis that the original RSA Challenge decimal-list primes were generated by the exact contemporary RSAREF mechanism `uniform random candidate in [3*2^(b-2),2^b) -> make odd -> increment by 2 until probable prime -> accept only gcd(p-1,3)=1`.

The final-family constraints `binary prefix 11` and `p mod 3 = 2` are not sufficient to identify the generation path. Under the exact RSAREF next-prime mechanism, a final prime p is selected with probability approximately proportional to its predecessor-prime gap g=p-PrevPrime(p), because every odd starting point between the preceding prime and p maps forward to p. Thus the normalized observable `x=g/log(p)` should exhibit length bias. In a Cramer-style baseline, uniformly sampled primes give x approximately Exp(1), mean 1, while random-start/next-prime sampling gives the length-biased distribution, mean near 2.

All 36 publicly known factors of the 18 solved original decimal-list Challenge moduli used in the audit were independently checked as prime and satisfy the known family constraints used for matching. Their observed normalized predecessor-gap statistics were:

- n = 36;
- mean `g/log(p) = 1.179832500365499`;
- median `= 0.7256601093359593`.

A matched control was generated separately for each observed factor bit length, uniformly rejection-sampling primes constrained to the same bit length, the same forced high-bit interval `[3*2^(b-2),2^b)`, and `p == 2 mod 3` (equivalently odd candidate `5 mod 6`). Fifty controls per observed factor were produced, 1,800 controls total.

Matched-control statistics:

- pooled uniform-prime control mean `= 1.001662619362056`;
- pooled median `= 0.7168994180814908`.

A 500,000-replicate stratified resampling experiment drew one control from each of the 36 matched strata. Under uniform-prime selection:

- simulated mean of the dataset mean `= 1.001779114016486`;
- SD `= 0.1587362107967648`;
- 95% interval approximately `[0.7158832642, 1.3367380622]`;
- upper-tail probability for a mean at least as large as the observed 1.17983 was about `0.132734`.

The same matched controls were then reweighted within each stratum proportionally to `g/log(p)`, which is equivalent (up to the nearly constant log p within a stratum) to predecessor-gap length weighting expected under the exact uniform-start/next-prime mechanism. Under this proxy:

- simulated mean `= 1.894011197728514`;
- SD `= 0.2146172723781398`;
- 95% interval approximately `[1.4947401044, 2.3336983498]`;
- only `5.4e-5` of 500,000 replicates had a mean no larger than the observed Challenge mean.

Interpretation: the solved original-list Challenge factors look compatible with the matched uniform-prime population but do not show the strong predecessor-gap length bias expected from the exact RSAREF random-start/+2-next-prime path. This materially weakens the shortcut `RSA DSP challenge generator == RSAREF GeneratePrime/R_GenerateBytes`, despite the exact contemporary RSAREF source reproducing the visible prefix-11 and e=3 residue constraints.

This is not a proof that RSA DSP did not reuse any RSAREF components. The falsifier does not exclude a different initial-candidate distribution, pre-sieving or batching, pair-level rejection, another probable-prime search policy, or another DSP implementation that separately imposed the same high-bit and mod-3 constraints. It does exclude treating the exact RSAREF next-prime path as the default working model without new provenance evidence.

BRC interpretation: the terminal factor constraints are a coarse observer that collapses distinct generation histories. The predecessor-gap statistic is a future-operation/path-sensitive observable that separates the hypothesized next-prime path from uniform-prime selection. This is a concrete provenance-loss witness and supports retaining generation-path identity rather than inferring it from final factor features.
