# Eventual ordering for two fixed suffixes, including coincident limiting slopes

Fix a cut q≥2. Compare two legal schedules which follow A until the cut and then use two fixed suffixes from the same c=q+1. Set n=k-1; the input interval to the level-q suffix is [x_n,y_n] with x_n=P_n(0), y_n=P_n(1), P_n=g_{q+1}∘⋯∘g_n. The canonical maps are increasing, map [0,1] into itself, and have max g_r'≤1/(100r²). Thus the intervals are nested with positive width tending to zero and have a common limiting coordinate x_*.

The limit is biased toward the upper end on the scale of each interval. Indeed, the interval of all later possible inputs to P_n is contained in g_{n+1}([0,1]), whence x_*=P_n(t_n) for t_n in g_{n+1}([0,1]). The C62 coefficients give g_r(u)→56/65 uniformly: in g_r(u)=(1+a_r+b_ru)^(-1), a_r→9/56 and b_r→0. Thus t_n≥3/4 for sufficiently large n. By the C63 distortion estimate, inf P_n'/sup P_n'≥∏_{r=q+1}^n inf g'_r/sup g'_r>5/6, including all chain-rule evaluations. Consequently (x_*-x_n)/(y_n-x_n) ≥ t_n·inf P_n'/sup P_n' ≥ 5/8. In particular x_n+y_n−2x_*≤−(y_n−x_n)/4.

For suffix i, let S_i(u)=(a_i u+b_i)/(c_i u+d_i) be its final state-2 probability as function of the input u. This linear-fractional form follows by composing all conditional blocks and the exact terminal reweighting formula. Choose signs so c_i u+d_i>0 on [0,1]. Its determinant is nonzero, because each internal update is injective and all terminal likelihoods are positive. The final TV difference equals |S_i(y_n)−S_i(x_n)| by the terminal monotonicity relation proved in C63. Hence for i,j the exact ratio is
 R_n = C h(x_n)h(y_n),  C=|a_i d_i−b_i c_i|/|a_j d_j−b_j c_j|,  h(u)=(c_j u+d_j)/(c_i u+d_i)>0.
If C h(x_*)²≠1, its limit fixes the comparison for all large n. If h is constant, the ratio is constant and any tie is permanent. In the remaining case C h(x_*)²=1 and h is nonconstant. Since h is positive linear-fractional with no poles, H=log h has bounded second derivative on [0,1] and H'(x_*)≠0. Writing w_n=y_n−x_n, Taylor's theorem gives
 log R_n=H'(x_*)[x_n+y_n−2x_*]+O(w_n²).
The bracket is ≤−w_n/4. Therefore the eventual sign of log R_n is the negative of the sign of H'(x_*). This proves eventual ordering of each pair, with no distinct-limits assumption.

For each fixed positive rank J choose q₀ with binom(2q₀−1,q₀)≥J actual suffixes from canonical inventory c=q₀+1. Use a common finite M≥1 bounding the largest max|S'_i|/min|S'_A| among J chosen suffixes. These J schedules are all within M times the minimum for k≥q₀+2. The first-divergence estimate then puts every competing schedule of rank at most J (including boundary ties) among canonical prefixes with a suffix below q=max(q₀,63,ceil(41M)). This is a finite collection of fixed suffixes. The pairwise argument above gives eventual stability of its entire ordering and therefore of the leading J suffixes, with permanent equalities recorded as ties. For the finite initial k below q+2 the conclusion concerns only the tail of the sequence. The proof does not compute a general effective stabilization threshold for the slope-tie decision.
