## Output and population checks

The harness constructs the complete expected array with `std::sort` and compares
every element of the diagnostic and nullptr outputs with that array. This
checks both order and multiplicity, rather than only a checksum or monotonicity.

For the new candidates, `check_selection` reconstructs the selected model for
every node and replays membership on the actual corresponding input occurrences.
It checks the processed prefix, hits, residual occurrences, exact child input,
counter budget, original-sample mass threshold and output accounting. It uses
explicit interval comparisons for its membership oracle. An early rejected
scan is checked as a prefix and is not treated as a completed population count.

The output-only ablation is compared with the frozen phase-two combined
selector for model geometry, learning work, scan outcome, child sizes, attempt
decisions and recursive paths. The new-selector variants with separate and
fused output are likewise compared. These checks isolate the output change
from a change in selected representations.

Successful fused nodes record exactly n direct output-value writes and zero
legacy prefix/merge writes. Legacy `emitted_values` retains its semantic meaning
as the number of histogram-sourced occurrences, so it is not counted a second
time as a fused-output write.

## Mathematical source audit

The completed `selection_kernels.hpp`, inherited `make_grid`, `finish_layout`,
`select_compact_layout`, `count_sampled` and old backward merge agree with the
statements in THEORY.md. No substantive mismatch was found in this audit.

Retained counting intervals are ordered, disjoint and clipped to their coarse
cells; the compact model's origin equals its interval lower endpoint. Every
input value in a retained interval must be counted, so actual residuals lie
outside every retained interval. This supports the bucket-level fused emitter:
copy each preceding sorted residual block, then emit the bucket's counts.
The exactly n writes are element-level output work at one accepted counting
node. Recursive child sorting, histogram updates and fallback work remain
separate; no machine-store or cache-transfer count is inferred.

The old strict-greater-than merge comparison gives the exact identity

    W = e + #{counted occurrences greater than min(residuals)},

for nonempty residuals. The resulting saved-write expression W-e is therefore
consistent with the source. The actual fused implementation uses one
`lower_bound` per retained bucket, supporting the stated output-stage bound
`O(L + n + B log(e+1))`. The fixed grid-slot scan is absorbed by the modeled-node
and fixed-grid bounds.

The sample vector remains complete while gap windows shrink. Consequently the
mass denominator remains the original S. Positive-gap selection preserves
whole equal-value blocks; gap-size ties select the lower index and side-mass
ties retain the lower-valued side. Only gaps preserving at least ceil(S/2)
observations are eligible. There are at most three cuts and one actual scan of
the first selected model.

For the twenty-cluster sufficient family, `a=2^62` and `a +/- 2^60` stay within
the `uint64_t` domain. The initial shift is 51 or 52, placing all core clusters
in one oversized bin. After tail removal, shift 15 aligns every core-cluster
start to a coarse-cell boundary. At least two sampled endpoints per raw-width-8
cluster imply padding at most 10, left clipping and width at most 18, hence at
most 360 total slots. The strict sample-majority hypothesis and separately
assumed actual tail-population bound are both necessary to the stated argument.

### Failure patterns and interpretation limits

The largest gains are concentrated in separated-tail conditions. The density
and gap changes account for a larger aggregate improvement over the previous
combined selector than the output-only change in this measured set. The
combined result cannot be attributed solely to the exactly n-write lemma.

The remaining losses have distinct observed paths:

- On `selection_fresh_popular_wide`, the new selector accepts a density model
  on all eight banks, with mean root residual 18,743.75 out of 100,000 and no
  actual-half rejection. Nevertheless its combined time is 862.616
  microseconds versus 749.460 for offset radix. Better sample or population
  coverage does not itself establish a time advantage.
- On `selection_fresh_rare_wide`, both the previous and new selectors reach
  radix without allocating a counting table on every bank. The combined
  candidate takes 806.245 microseconds versus 738.552 for direct offset radix.
  This is a selection-then-fallback path; the measurements alone do not
  partition its time among individual selection operations.
- On `selection_fresh_interior_contamination`, both selectors have 48 root
  counter cells, 700 residuals and total recursive input 100,700. Fusing output
  reduces accepted-node output writes from 149,592.25 to 100,000 on average,
  but combined time remains 399.230 microseconds versus 342.276 for the
  strongest old control. Here lower write work and successful exact coverage
  coexist with a performance loss to another representation.
- On `selection_fresh_128clusters_12pct_tail`, the new gap model succeeds on
  every bank and leaves mean root residual 30,429.75 out of 250,000. Its
  combined time improves substantially over the previous combined selector,
  but 971.157 microseconds is still slower than frequency sorting at 862.283.
  The observed residual exceeds the nominal 30,000 tails, so this empirical
  family does not inherit the separate twenty-cluster theorem's exact-tail
  coverage conclusion.

The deterministic mechanism conditions also separate structural repair from
speed. In the density witness, new combined time is 610.834 microseconds versus
593.346 for the previous combined selector. In the 300-sampled-tail gap witness,
the new combined candidate improves over the previous combined selector from
1,096.238 to 287.551 microseconds, while frequency sorting remains faster at
260.641. The hidden-majority witness retains the actual-population rejection
requirement; a sample selection certificate does not certify true population
coverage.

The report should preserve all these rows. Seven within-process rounds and
descriptive quantiles do not establish statistical significance, a confidence
interval, or deployment prevalence. Exact element-write identities are not
machine-instruction counts. Current-host ratios do not authorize comparisons
with historical absolute timings. The finite selector enumeration's 1,629
half-gate misses support investigation of a certified fixed-grid selection
repair, but are neither measured sorter failures nor an estimate of their
frequency in arbitrary data.

The README review found one presentation scope correction: the constructive
family needed an explicit n>=1024 assumption and an oversized initial-grid
claim conditional on a sampled tail. With no sampled tail, its small original
grid is accepted directly with zero cuts. The parent applied these corrections
and the reviewer confirmed the updated text. Frozen THEORY.md already contains
these qualifications; no theorem or kernel change was required. The finite
enumeration is also described as a separately executed exact calculation,
without suggesting external review.

This author-side audit is complete for the frozen phase-three implementation
and the recorded run. It does not certify originality, external peer review,
formal proof, or a new general sorting-complexity result.
