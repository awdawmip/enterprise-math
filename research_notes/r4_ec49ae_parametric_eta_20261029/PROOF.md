## 1. Exact causal model, questions, and scope

Let `w,z in {0,1,2}` and `u in {0,1}`. History `w` is uniformly distributed. The two source laws are

```
t_eta(w,u) = 1/2 + (2u-1)(1-w) eta
P_eta(w,u,z) = t_eta(w,u) * (1/6 if w=z else 1/12)
Q_eta(w,u,z) = t_eta(w,u) / 9
```

For `0 <= eta <= 1/100`, these are 18 strictly positive leaves per source, each source normalized to 1. The `(w,u)` marginal is equal under P and Q. The raw likelihood ratio is `P/Q=3/2` for `w=z` and `3/4` for `w!=z`.

At `w` the observer chooses `STOP`, `A0,A1` (cost 1), or `B0,B1` (cost 2), according to behavioral probabilities `x_wc`; `w` is then erased. After observing `(c,u)`, it may purchase the `S` sensor (second cost 1) with fresh conditional probability `s_cu`; if purchased it observes `z`. Acceptance uses only the actually retained state and fresh local Bernoulli coins `a_cuz` or `a0_cu`. No retained strategy-mixture seed is available. The source-equal expected costs are fixed:

`C1=(sum_wc x_wc + sum_(w,c in B) x_wc)/3=43/48`, `C2=(sum_wcu x_wc t_eta(w,u) s_cu)/3=7/8`.

Because the source marginal of `(w,u)` agrees, the same cost formulas hold for P and Q. Set `alpha=P(accept)`, `beta=Q(accept)`, `delta=alpha-beta`. All strategy variables may be arbitrary *real* probabilities in `[0,1]`.

**Theorem AE1 (parametric one-prefix sufficiency).** For every real `eta` in `[0,1/100]`, the entire *raw*, unconvexified, source-labelled two-dimensional observation region at the specified costs equals the region generated by the SINGLE first-stage prefix:

```
w=0: A0 with probability 1
w=1: A1 with probability 1
w=2: (9/16) A0 + (1/16) B0 + (3/8) STOP.
```

In this prefix every paid path buys S, and only the locally observed terminal acceptance probabilities and STOP acceptance probability vary, with fresh independent coins. Therefore the complete fixed-budget `(alpha,beta)` region is a compact convex polygon (a two-dimensional zonotope) for every such `eta`; for rational `eta`, each rational feasible pair admits a rational fresh-coin policy.

**Theorem AE2 (explicit region and perturbation invariants).** Let `D=59/576`. For all `eta` in the same interval, `-D <= delta <= D`, and for `0<=delta<=D` the complete region is

`{ (alpha, beta=alpha-delta) : L_eta(delta) <= alpha <= 1-3 delta }`.

Here `L_eta(delta)` is computable *exactly* from the universal prefix: let `p_j(eta),q_j(eta)` be its 18 positive sensed-observation masses and `d_j=p_j-q_j`. Sort the eight `d_j>0` atoms by their rational-function slopes `p_j/d_j`; accept successively from the smallest slope until the cumulative discrimination reaches `delta`, accepting a fractional final atom. The resulting sum of accepted `p_j` is `L_eta(delta)`. For negative `delta` complement the acceptance rule:

`{-3delta <= alpha <= 1-L_eta(-delta), beta=alpha-delta}`.

For `delta=1/12` the exact fibers at three parameter points are:

| eta | minimum alpha | maximum alpha | beta=alpha-1/12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 113/368 | 3/4 | exact |
| 1/200 | 1229/4016 | 3/4 | exact |
| 1/100 | 5411/17744 | 3/4 | exact |

This is a genuine parameter-domain extension: the lower envelope changes continuously as eta varies, even though the global discrimination capacity and the optimal first prefix stay constant.

## 2. Reduction from all real causal strategies to 288 prefix endpoints

Put `T=sum_wc x_wc`, `b=sum_(w,c in B)x_wc` and `m=sum_wcu x_wc t_eta(w,u)(1-s_cu)`. Because `sum_u t_eta(w,u)=1`, the cost constraints imply

```
T+b=43/16,
T-m=21/8,
b+m=1/16,
0<=b<=1/16.
```

The history-STOP masses `h_w=1-sum_c x_wc` sum to `5/16+b` and the B masses `b_w` sum to `b`. The inequality `h_w+b_w<=5/16+2b<=7/16<1` means the independent allocation of STOP mass and B mass to particular histories is always row-feasible.

At fixed `b`, decompose `h` and `b_w` into simplex vertices (three choices each), and A0/A1 and B0/B1 labels into pure endpoints (`2^3 * 2` choices). Hence **every real feasible prefix** lies in the convex hull of `3*3*2^3*2=144` prefixes at fixed `b`; interpolation of `b` between 0 and `1/16` gives exactly **288 rational endpoint prefixes**. This is a convexity argument for an *upper bound* on possible observations: it does not introduce an initial shared seed in the actual behavioral protocol.

For a prefix `x`, let the *hypothetical always-S refined experiment* have 24 sensed atom masses `(p_j^x,q_j^x)` and one `STOP` atom with common source mass `h_x/3`. Its support function in direction `(1,-r)` is

`H_x(r)=sum_j [p_j^x-r q_j^x]_+ + STOP_x [1-r]_+`, where `[v]_+=max(0,v)`.

For an actual strategy with selective S skips, maximizing acceptance on each retained `(c,u)` is bounded above by the refined experiment's maximum, because `[sum_z v_z]_+ <= sum_z[v_z]_+`. Thus for all real `r>=0`,

`H_actual(r) <= H_x(r) <= max_{288 endpoints v} H_v(r)`.

The last inequality uses convexity of the positive-part map in `x`. The universal candidate X is itself a lawful `b=1/16` endpoint, with `m=0`, and has costs `43/48,7/8` at every eta. Consequently, it suffices to prove `H_v(r) <= H_X(r)` for all 288 endpoints, all `r>=0`, and all eta in the asserted interval.

## 3. Reduce the continuum of support directions to 19 moving ratios

Every observable atom has P/Q in `[3/4,3/2]` by nonnegative aggregation of the raw leaves, including the STOP atom with ratio 1. Therefore `H_v(r)=H_X(r)=1-r` for `r<=3/4`, and both vanish for `r>=3/2`.

For fixed eta, `H_X(r)` is piecewise affine on `[3/4,3/2]` with possible kinks at the `p_j^X(eta)/q_j^X(eta)` of its 18 nonzero sensed atoms and the STOP ratio `r=1`. On any interval between its kinks, `H_X` is affine and `H_v` is convex in `r`; if the endpoint inequalities hold, convexity gives the full interval inequality. We therefore check **19 moving ratio directions** for every eta: the 18 sensed ratios plus 1. The raw likelihood-ratio endpoints `3/4,3/2` are exact equalities requiring no enumeration.

For a chosen moving direction `r=p_J^X/q_J^X`, multiply the desired inequality by the strictly positive affine `q_J^X(eta)` and write the cross-atom expressions

`f_(v,k,J)(eta) = p_k^v(eta) q_J^X(eta) - q_k^v(eta) p_J^X(eta)`.

Each `p,q` is affine in eta, hence every `f` is a rational quadratic polynomial. Include the STOP expression `(q_J^X-p_J^X) STOP_v`. For the separate direction `r=1`, take `p_J^X=q_J^X=1` to obtain the same representation. We must prove

`G_(v,J)(eta) = sum_k[f_(X,k,J)]_+ + STOP_X[q_J^X-p_J^X]_+ - sum_k[f_(v,k,J)]_+ - STOP_v[q_J^X-p_J^X]_+ >= 0`.

These are *exactly* the original support inequalities multiplied by positive denominators; no fixed-r numerical approximation or grid interpolation occurs.
