## Theorem: exact online risk update

For any irrational theta and history m, suppose at some horizon the new cut t_(m+L)
splits a current circular gap G in a unique original parent cell I of P_m.
Let g be the length of G, a,b the two new lengths (a+b=g), and B the
longest *other* current gap in that same parent cell (B=0 if none).
Then the exact update is

  R(m,L+1)-R(m,L)
    = max(B,g)-max(B,a,b)
    = max(0,min(a,b,g-B)).

Proof. All parent-cell maxima outside I remain unchanged. Inside I the
largest refined gap changes from max(B,g) to max(B,a,b); subtracting the
two parent maxima in the displayed defining sum gives the formula.

## Offline-sorted / online-updated exact algorithm

For any declared finite N=m+LMAX, represent each cut by an affine pair
  t_j = A_j + B_j*theta,
with (A_0,B_0)=(0,0), and for j>=1
  (A_j,B_j)=(floor(j*theta)+1,-j).
Use the strict rational enclosure to certify floor(j*theta) uniformly
and sort all N cuts once. Check *each adjacent pair* has identical
strict order at both rational endpoints, hence throughout the interval.

Maintain a Fenwick tree containing the active cut ranks. At each
new cut, predecessor and successor are recovered in O(log N). The
unique containing gap stores its immutable parent-cell identifier,
which its two children inherit. Each parent has two lazy-deletion
max-heaps, ordered by exact gap length at the *two rational
endpoints*. Require the same symbolic affine expression to attain
both maxima; this makes the selected maximum valid throughout the
enclosure, since the difference of any two affine lengths is affine.
The difference of old and new maxima updates the symbolic risk.
Check all new positive gaps and nonnegative risk increments at both
endpoints. All comparisons are exact integers.

A stable comparison-based sort, Fenwick predecessor/successor queries,
and two max-heaps yield O(N log N) arithmetic comparisons and
O(N) space, for fixed m and horizon N-m, replacing the predecessor
certificate's repeated full per-horizon partition reconstruction.
This is a bound for the mathematical algorithm with balanced
ordering and heap primitives; bit complexity additionally depends on
the integer sizes, including the precision denominator Q. With the
fixed 24-digit Q used here, coefficient and key sizes grow with N.
