## 1. Remaining loophole addressed here

The previous note showed that after conditioning on the previous factor residue modulo `l^(e-1)`, the new branch contains only one base-l digit and can be represented in constant dimension. The only plausible escape from global character growth was therefore:

> keep the known previous additive residue fiber explicitly and push only that local section through the BRC product, never forming a global Dirichlet-character expansion.

This note shows that a **bounded multiplication-safe residue quotient cannot do this exactly**.

The statement is scoped to exact linear/additive residue-state propagation under the product operation. It is not a lower bound against arbitrary nonlinear or analytic algorithms.

## 2. Residue carrier

Fix

`n=l^(e-1)`

and let

`G=Z/nZ`

be the additive exponent-residue group.

At one stage of a Laurent/product computation, represent an exponent profile by an element of the group algebra

`R[G]`.

Multiplying Laurent factors adds exponent residues. Multiplication by a monomial `u^t` acts as translation

`T_t:r -> r+t`.

The BRC product contains exponent shifts `+1` and `-1` in its denominator factors and `+2,-2` in numerator factors. Since `+1` alone generates G, the natural future-operation family generated by the product contains all translations of G at the residue level.

The target-adaptive decoder wants to preserve whether mass lies in one known residue fiber `b in G` (and subsequently its l refinements at the next level).

## 3. Exact quotient theorem

Consider an equivalence relation `~` on G used as a compressed residue state. Require:

1. **translation safety:** `r~s -> r+t ~ s+t` for every future translation `t in G`;
2. **target observability:** the singleton `{b}` is measurable in the quotient, i.e. b is not equivalent to any `r!=b`.

**Theorem.** The only such equivalence relation is equality. Therefore every exact translation-safe residue quotient preserving one singleton target has n classes.

### Proof

Translation-safe equivalence relations on a group are exactly coset congruences of a subgroup H: the class of 0 is H and every class is a coset `r+H`.

If H contains a nonzero h, then b and `b+h` belong to the same class, contradicting target observability. Hence H={0}; all classes are singletons. QED.

Equivalently, without invoking the subgroup classification: if `r!=s` were merged, translate both by `b-r`. Then r lands at b while s lands at `b+(s-r)!=b`. Target observability distinguishes them, contradicting operation safety.

## 4. Predictive-quotient formulation

In the language of the existing Enterprise `T6_OPERATION_SAFE_QUOTIENT` tool:

`preserved observation = coefficient/fiber at b`,

`future operation family = translations generated by product exponent shifts`.

Every residue has a future that sends it to b, so distinct residues have distinct future signatures. The coarsest predictive partition is the discrete partition.

Thus

`MIN_EXACT_PRODUCT_SAFE_RESIDUE_STATE_CARDINALITY = n = l^(e-1)`.

For the pure ternary route at the Coppersmith handoff e=141, this is

`3^140`

residue states if one insists on an exact multiplication-safe additive-state propagation of the target fiber.

## 5. Linear-state version

The regular translation representation of `G` on `R[G]` has dimension n. If a linear state must represent arbitrary residue profiles reachable under translations and exactly recover the coefficient at b after arbitrary future translations, the translated coordinate functionals

`delta_(b-t)`, `t in G`

are n linearly independent outputs. Therefore any such exact linear predictive state also has dimension at least n.

This is the additive analogue of the previous multiplicative/Galois orbit-rank bound.

For l=3 their scales agree up to a constant factor:

- additive product-safe bound: `3^(e-1)`;
- primitive-odd Galois orbit bound: `2*3^(e-2)`.

The same exponential conductor growth is therefore visible in two dual bases.

## 6. Why conditioning on b does not save multiplication-safe state

Conditioning selects which coordinate is ultimately queried, but a sequential product algorithm does not know which current residues can be discarded unless no future factor can move them into b.

Because `+/-1` shifts generate all of G, **every current residue can reach b under some admissible future translation**. Therefore merging or deleting it is not safe under the declared operation family.

This is exactly the distinction between:

`LOCAL OUTPUT INFORMATION = one base-l digit`

and

`PRODUCT PROPAGATION STATE = all residues that may later flow into the requested digit`.

BRC's provenance language makes the distinction explicit: a narrow final branch does not imply a narrow safe intermediate quotient.

## 7. Scope boundary / possible escape

The theorem closes bounded state for the straightforward operation-safe product recurrence, but does not rule out:

- exploiting cancellations or special identities of the **specific complete BRC product**, rather than supporting arbitrary future translations;
- divide-and-conquer algorithms that never expose residue states explicitly;
- nonlinear arithmetic that computes the final trace without maintaining a predictive quotient;
- classical factoring/residue-class algorithms (already classified separately).

So the only remaining opening is now extremely narrow:

`SPECIAL GLOBAL IDENTITY OF THE COMPLETE PRODUCT`
` -> direct target local trace`

that bypasses both

- the full multiplicative character layer, and
- the full additive residue-state propagation.

No such identity is currently known.

## 8. APR-CL cross-check (finite negative result)

A contemporaneous cross-check tested whether standard APR-CL/Jacobi-sum cyclotomic congruences already provide the required level-9 split. The exact `p=3,k=2` Jacobi-sum check was implemented for auxiliary primes

`q in {19,37,73,109,127,163}`

and evaluated on small H2-consistent semiprimes from both RSA-270 level-9 branches `{8,8}` and `{2,5}` modulo 9.

The tested global-root-existence condition failed uniformly on both composite branches. It behaved as a compositeness witness, not a stable branch discriminator. This is finite negative evidence only, not a theorem about all Jacobi-sum constructions.

Classical APR-CL remains relevant prior art: passing its full cyclotomic conditions constrains every prime divisor of n to a short family of powers of n modulo an auxiliary s, followed by direct divisor checks. The tested standard condition does not supply the missing RSA-270 branch bit.
