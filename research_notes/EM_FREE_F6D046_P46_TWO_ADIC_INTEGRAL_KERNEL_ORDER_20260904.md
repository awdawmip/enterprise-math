## 1. The Frobenius--Verschiebung order

Let

\[
R=\mathbf Z[\alpha,\bar\alpha]
=\mathcal O_{F^+}[\alpha],
\qquad
\alpha\bar\alpha=49,
\qquad
x=\alpha+\bar\alpha.
\]

Since \(\mathcal O_{F^+}=\mathbf Z[x]\) and \(\alpha\) satisfies

\[
T^2-xT+49=0,
\]

the relative discriminant of the order \(R/\mathcal O_{F^+}\) has norm

\[
N_{F^+/\mathbf Q}(x^2-196)=4704.
\]

The maximal relative discriminant has norm

\[
N_{F^+/\mathbf Q}(\mathfrak d_{F/F^+})=24.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{[\mathcal O_F:R]=\sqrt{4704/24}=14.}
\]

This agrees with the explicit integral basis: \([\mathcal O_F:\mathbf Z[\alpha]]=686\), whereas \([R:\mathbf Z[\alpha]]=49\).

## 2. Localization at two

At the two-adic root \(x\equiv5\pmod8\), the relative discriminant is the unit square class \(5\), so \(R\) is already maximal in the unramified factor

\[
F_{\mathfrak q_u}=\mathbf Q_2(\sqrt5).
\]

At the root \(x\equiv6\pmod8\), the polynomial order has discriminant exponent five, while the maximal ramified field \(\mathbf Q_2(\sqrt{-2})\) has exponent three. Thus all of the local index two occurs in the ramified factor:

\[
\boxed{

=R_{\mathfrak q_r}\times\mathcal O_{\mathfrak q_u},
\qquad
[\mathcal O_{\mathfrak q_r}:R_{\mathfrak q_r}]=2,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal O_{\mathfrak q_u}=\mathcal O_{\mathbf Q_2(\sqrt5)},

\mathcal O_{\mathfrak q_u}/2\simeq\mathbf F_4.

\]

## 3. The Prym kernel is an integral unramified residue module

The order \(R\) is generated by Frobenius squared and Verschiebung squared and is stable under Rosati conjugation. Hence it acts integrally on the specialized Prym and preserves the polarization kernel.

On

\[
K_\lambda=\ker\lambda_P,
\]

In the ramified local factor, the residue of \(\alpha\) has polynomial dividing \((T+1)^2\); it cannot support the irreducible two-dimensional module. In the unramified factor its residue satisfies \(T^2+T+1\). Therefore the support is forced:

\[
\boxed{
K_\lambda\simeq\mathcal O_{\mathfrak q_u}/2\mathcal O_{\mathfrak q_u}
\simeq\mathbf F_4
}
\]

as a module over the maximal unramified local order. This upgrades the R80--R83 spectral coincidence to an integral action theorem.

## 4. Principalization lowers the local order

A degree-two principal quotient chooses an \(\mathbf F_2\)-line

\[
\ell\subset K_\lambda\simeq\mathbf F_4.
\]

The stabilizer of such a line inside the residue algebra \(\mathbf F_4\) is exactly \(\mathbf F_2\): if \(a\ell\subseteq\ell\), then the residue scalar \(a\) belongs to the base field. Consequently the largest unramified local order preserving a chosen line is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal O(\ell)=\mathbf Z_2+2\mathcal O_{\mathfrak q_u}.
}
\]

It has index

\[
\boxed{[\mathcal O_{\mathfrak q_u}:\mathcal O(\ell)]=2.}
\]

Thus principalization has a precise conductor cost: it turns the maximal unramified CM action into the conductor-two suborder stabilizing the selected isotropic line.

## 6. Consequence and boundary

The two-primary integral mechanism is now explicit. The original nonprincipal Prym polarization is compatible with the maximal unramified local CM order; every compatible degree-two principalization necessarily loses that maximal order at conductor two.
