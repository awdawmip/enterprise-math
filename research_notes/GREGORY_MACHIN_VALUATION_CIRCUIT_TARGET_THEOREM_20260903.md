## 1. General circuit theorem

Fix distinct reciprocal denominators

\[
S=\{D_1,\ldots,D_s\},\qquad D_j\ge2.
\]

Let \(V\) be their free split-prime valuation matrix. Its \(j\)-th column is

\[
v_j=(n_p(D_j))_p,
\]

and let

\[
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_s)
\in(\mathbf Z/8\mathbf Z)^s
\]

be the torsion row.

Assume the free columns form a **rational circuit**:

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbf Q}V=s-1,
\]

and every proper subcollection is independent. Then the integer kernel is generated by one primitive vector

\[
c^{(0)}\in\mathbf Z^s,
\qquad
\gcd(c_1^{(0)},\ldots,c_s^{(0)})=1,
\]

with every coordinate nonzero.

### Theorem 1.1 — valuation-circuit target theorem

Put

\[
e:=\varepsilon\cdot c^{(0)}\pmod8.
\]

There exists a signed reciprocal-turn word supported on exactly \(S\) whose endpoint is the diagonal torsion generator

\[
\tau=[1+i]
\]

iff

\[
\boxed{e\text{ is odd}.}
\]

When this holds, all endpoint coefficient vectors on this circuit are

\[
\boxed{
c=t c^{(0)},
\qquad
te\equiv1\pmod8.
}
\]

Since every odd residue is its own inverse modulo eight, this is equivalently

\[
\boxed{t\equiv e\pmod8.}
\]

#### Proof

The free endpoint equations are exactly

\[
Vc=0.
\]

Because \(\ker_{\mathbf Z}V=\mathbf Zc^{(0)}\), every free-coordinate cancellation is \(c=tc^{(0)}\). The diagonal endpoint additionally requires

\[
\varepsilon\cdot c\equiv1\pmod8,
\]

hence \(te\equiv1\pmod8\). This congruence is solvable iff \(e\) is a unit in \(\mathbf Z/8\mathbf Z\), i.e. iff \(e\) is odd. ∎

Freeze:

\[
\boxed{
\texttt{FREE_VALUATION_CIRCUIT}
+\texttt{ODD_TORSION_PAIRING}
\iff
\texttt{DIAGONAL_ENDPOINT_CIRCUIT}.
}
\]

This theorem is valid for every support size. The triple torsion test in the previous bounded census is its \(s=3\) case.

### Boundary

## 2. Why large coefficients are structurally forced

For a rational circuit, the primitive coefficient ratios are fixed by the valuation matrix before any analytic completion cost is considered. In particular, the large coefficients in efficient classical formulas are not arbitrary numerical fits: once the denominator set and Gaussian-prime orientation choices are fixed, the free-coordinate cancellations determine the primitive kernel up to sign.

The mod-eight torsion row then decides whether the primitive circuit itself, or an odd multiple of it, lands on the diagonal target.

Thus the architecture separates three questions:

1. denominator support selects a valuation matrix;
2. the free matrix forces coefficient ratios;
3. torsion selects the admissible scaling class modulo eight.

Only after all three native algebraic steps does completion evaluate arctangent convergence.

## 3. Størmer's four-term low-Lehmer identity as a rank-three circuit

Consider the classical four-term formula

\[
\frac\pi4
=44\arctan\frac1{57}
+7\arctan\frac1{239}
-12\arctan\frac1{682}
+24\arctan\frac1{12943}.
\]

Its Lehmer measure is approximately

\[
\mu\approx1.5860413586,
\]

substantially below Gauss's three-term value.

Use split-prime coordinates \((5,13,61)\). Exact Gaussian signatures are:

\[
\Sigma(U_{57})=(7;\ 3,-1,0),
\]

because

\[
57^2+1=2\cdot5^3\cdot13;
\]

\[
\Sigma(U_{239})=(3;\ 0,-4,0),
\]

because

\[
239^2+1=2\cdot13^4;
\]

\[
\Sigma(U_{682})=(0;\ 3,0,-2),
\]

because

\[
682^2+1=5^3\cdot61^2;
\]

and

\[
\Sigma(U_{12943})=(1;\ -4,3,-1),
\]

because

\[
12943^2+1=2\cdot5^4\cdot13^3\cdot61.
\]

The free valuation matrix is

\[
V=
\begin{pmatrix}
3&0&3&-4\\
-1&-4&0&3\\
0&0&-2&-1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

It has rank three and primitive integer kernel

\[
\boxed{(44,7,-12,24).}
\]

The coefficients can be read by elimination:

- the \(61\)-row gives \(-2c_{682}-c_{12943}=0\);
- the \(5\)-row then gives \(3c_{57}+11c_{682}=0\);
- the \(13\)-row gives \(-c_{57}-4c_{239}+3c_{12943}=0\).

The torsion pairing is

\[
44(7)+7(3)-12(0)+24(1)=353\equiv1\pmod8.
\]

Therefore the primitive circuit itself hits the diagonal:

\[
\boxed{
U_{57}^{44}U_{239}^{7}U_{682}^{-12}U_{12943}^{24}=\tau.
}
\]

The classical arctangent formula is the principal winding completion of this exact integer certificate.
