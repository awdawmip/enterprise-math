## 1. Dimensions and observation map

The target has M binary coordinates, and H is a q-by-M selector of q **distinct, labelled original coordinates**. Therefore q ≤ M, Y = HS remains a q-vector of signs, and every Y_a² = 1. The route errors and reports also have q coordinates. The diagonal matrices D_r act in that observed coordinate ordering; they are not M-by-M matrices when only q coordinates are visible.

This selector restriction has two separate uses. It justifies cancellation of the target sign in same-coordinate raw calibration, and makes M−q the number of unobserved original coordinates. The linear Hankel factorization itself can hold for more general H, but that does not extend these two interpretations to arbitrary mixtures or repeated channels. Full visibility is explicitly stated in the ordering H = I_M; a permutation of all coordinates would require the corresponding relabelling. No dimension mismatch was found in either file.

## 2. Independence and the raw/centered distinction

The supplement assumes mutual independence of the four whole-process sigma-algebras generated by S and the three errors. The equivalent formulation using independent error processes whose joint process is independent of S is valid. In particular, it implies independence of E^(r)_(a,t+l), E^(s)_(b,t), and the target pair for r ≠ s at every lag l. Fixed-time independence alone would not imply that statement. Dependence across times or coordinates *within* one route does not interfere with this factorization.

Stationarity supplies time-independent means. Writing v = E[Y_t], the independent factors give

`μ_r = D_r v`,

`R_l^(rs) = D_r E[Y_(t+l)Y_t^T] D_s`
`          = D_r(Γ_l + v v^T)D_s`.

Subtracting the report-mean outer product yields

`G_l^(rs) = R_l^(rs) − μ_r μ_s^T = D_r Γ_l D_s`.

The right-hand factor need not be written with a distinct transpose because D_s is real diagonal. Row time t+l and column time t are used consistently. Neither a lagged target covariance nor a cross-route matrix is silently assumed symmetric. There is no same-route whitening claim.

The raw scalar g_(rs,a) is the diagonal entry of **R_0**, not of G_0. The supplement's noiseless mean-1/2 example is correct: raw same-coordinate product is 1, while centered variance is 1−(1/2)² = 3/4. The newly supplied definition of G resolves the actual notation gap without rewriting the old source or claiming that it already contained this definition.

## 3. Calibration and exact inversion

At one coordinate and time, Y_a² = 1 cancels the target exactly. Independence of the two route errors then gives

`g_(rs,a) = η_(r,a) η_(s,a)`.

Each η is in (0,1], so all denominators in the three-view ratios are positive. For example,

`g_(12,a)g_(13,a)/g_(23,a) = η_(1,a)^2`.

The positive-bias assumption fixes the sign of its square root. The cyclic formulas similarly recover the other two gains, including noiseless gain 1. No division by a target mean occurs, so zero target means cause no failure. Zero gains and unfixed sign conventions are outside the stated hypotheses, rather than silently handled by an inverse or positive-root choice.

Thus D_r is invertible and

`v = D_r^(-1) μ_r`,
`Γ_l = D_r^(-1) G_l^(rs) D_s^(-1)`

recover the target quantities at selected coordinates. The centering uses report means already present in the population inputs; it is not circularly using the unknown target mean. Raw g values must remain the calibration inputs. Equality among reconstructions from different routes is necessary under the model, but it cannot certify whole-process independence or correct time alignment. None of these algebraic conclusions supplies finite-sample or ergodic estimation guarantees.

## 4. Additional model input, full inversion, and partial Hankel bound

The preceding calibration needs no affine target dynamics. The supplement correctly makes `C_l = A^l Σ`, Σ = C_0, an **additional inherited relation** from the specified binary copy/refresh update model. It does not infer this relation from agreement of routes.

For that model, the centered conditional mean relative to the full past is A(S_t−m). Iterating the tower property gives C_l = A^lΣ. With strict refresh probabilities and strict sign probabilities, every sign vector can be reached in one step by simultaneous independent refresh, with positive probability. The stationary distribution therefore has full support on the binary cube. A nonzero linear functional cannot be constant there, so its variance is positive and Σ is positive definite. This rechecks the particular C33 implication used here; it is not a whole-C33 audit or a new bridge added to the supplement.

When all coordinates are visible, C_1 = AΣ consequently gives A = C_1Σ^(-1). Invertibility of A is unnecessary. It would be incorrect to import the A-invertibility requirement of C33's different, unknown single-route noise procedure; the supplement does not do so.

With partial coordinates, calibration instead supplies Γ_l = HA^lΣH^T. In the proposed factorization the block at row i and column j is

`(HA^i)(A^jΣH^T) = HA^(i+j)ΣH^T = Γ_(i+j)`.

This establishes the entire u q-by-w q block Hankel factorization with inner dimension M. It does not commute Σ with A, assume reversibility, or require A to be invertible. Every certified exact finite-block rank ρ is therefore at most M. Because H selects q distinct original coordinates, M−q ≥ max(0,ρ−q), exactly the stated hidden-coordinate lower bound.

This is a necessary dimension bound inside the inherited family. It is not an upper bound from a finite rank plateau, an existence proof for a legal ρ-coordinate binary realization, recovery of an arbitrary hidden graph, or a way to count target coordinates from uncalibrated instrument modes. These restrictions agree between the prose and JSON interface.
