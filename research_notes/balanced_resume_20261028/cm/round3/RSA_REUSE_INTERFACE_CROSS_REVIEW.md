## Input reduction and exact orders

The object is an odd integer n ≥ 3, a nonempty finite list of units x_i,
and positive public integers Δ_i with x_i^Δ_i = 1 modulo n. Positivity,
unit membership and the annihilation equations are necessary checks. These
inputs are supplied evidence; their generation is not provided by the note.

The group H generated by the x_i is finite abelian. Its exponent divides
L = lcm_i Δ_i. Write L = 2^s u with u odd. In the primary decomposition of H,
the odd component has exponent dividing u, so exponentiation by u kills it.
On the 2-primary component, u is invertible modulo its exponent and thus
acts bijectively. The image of H under this homomorphism is exactly H₂,
and the images y_i = x_i^u generate it. This argument tolerates redundant
factors in every Δ_i; it needs no prime factorization of u or L.

Each y_i is killed by 2^s. The smallest t_i ≥ 0 for which
y_i^(2^t_i) = 1 determines its exact order: the order divides 2^s, and
no smaller power of two works. The identity case t_i = 0 must be removed
before nontrivial digit lifting, as the manuscript does. A wholly trivial
list, including s = 0, immediately yields the trivial certificate.

The known order 2^t_i now has its complete prime factorization. Thus the
antecedent's factored-order hypothesis is actually met, rather than silently
replaced by a factored annihilator. A maximal-order supplied g is usable as
the first base in every pairwise call with prime parameter 2. If the pair
is cyclic, its order is the lcm of the two generating orders and hence
equals ord(g); g generates the pair. Successful digit recovery therefore
gives every y_i as a power of this same g. No factoring oracle, hidden CRT
coordinate, or separate discrete-log oracle has entered the reduction.

Computing the lcm does not lose polynomial input-size control:
log L ≤ Σ_i log Δ_i. Exponentiation by u and at most s squarings per
element are polynomial in the supplied transcript size. This supports the
claimed conditional cost; it gives no cost bound for obtaining collisions.

## Cyclicity, projection injectivity, and divisor typing

For proof only, write n as a product of distinct odd prime powers. Each
coordinate unit group is cyclic. If every coordinate projection of a
2-subgroup K is injective, one projection embeds K in a cyclic group.
For nontrivial K its sole involution must have order two in every
coordinate, hence must be the global −1. Conversely, if a generator g
has order 2^t and half-power −1 globally, each coordinate order divides
2^t and fails to divide 2^(t−1). Each coordinate order is therefore
exactly 2^t, proving injectivity. The trivial case is separate and valid.

For two coordinates this is exactly the graph of an isomorphism between
the two projection images. With more coordinates, it is simultaneous
isomorphism to each image, not independence of the CRT coordinates.
The proof is correct for arbitrary odd n; squarefreeness is not used.

The counterexample K = ⟨4⟩ modulo 15 correctly distinguishes cyclicity
from this stronger condition: its mod-3 image is trivial. Thus the
additional global involution check is a real interface requirement, not
something implied by the abstract generator-or-divisor output.

For an exact order-two residue z, each odd prime-power coordinate is +1
or −1: that prime cannot divide both z−1 and z+1. If z is neither global
+1 nor global −1, both signs occur, and gcd(z−1,n) collects precisely
the entire prime-power blocks with sign +1. Hence it lies strictly between
1 and n. It can be composite: the manuscript's z = 19 modulo 45 has
z²−1 = 360 and gcd(18,45) = 9. This is a proper divisor certificate,
not necessarily a prime-factor certificate.

If n is one odd prime power, there is only one CRT coordinate; the
projection condition is automatic, and this square-root mechanism cannot
split n. The example modulo 9 has 2^6 = 1 and 2^3 = −1. A positive
membership certificate consequently cannot establish primality, the RSA
semiprime hypothesis, or hardness of other factoring mechanisms.

## Public root witness and certificate scope

The extra root field is not promised by an arbitrary black-box invocation
of Corollary 4.3. The note appropriately reconstructs its availability in
the dyadic specialization. After verifying g^(2^(t−1)) = −1, suppose the
current residual r = y_i g^(−e) satisfies r^(2^(t−k)) = 1. Its next test
w = r^(2^(t−k−1)) squares to 1. A value outside {+1,−1} immediately
supplies the root and proper divisor just proved. Otherwise choose b in
{0,1} with w = (−1)^b and replace r by r g^(−b 2^k). Raising that
replacement to 2^(t−k−1) gives w(−1)^(−b) = 1. Thus the next invariant
holds. After t digits the residual is 1, giving the public exponent.
This verifies the inherited internal invariant and explains the stronger
output format without assuming that every implementation emits it.

The public success certificate can be checked with modular arithmetic:
the selected g is one of the y_i; all membership equations hold; the
claimed positive depth has half-power −1; or every y_i is 1 in the
zero-depth case. These equations prove that this supplied H₂ has
injective projections. Consequently its own group words cannot produce
a mixed-sign square root. They say nothing about enlarging the supplied
generator set, generating collisions, or other uses of discarded odd-order
information. This matches the observer and future-operation boundary
