## 3. The actual Banach space has an integral partial-fraction basis

Let E={1,-1,1/2,-1/2}. Because p>=13, all nonzero differences between these four points are O-units. The algebra A0 is also O[r,(r-a)^(-1):a in E]. Ordinary monic division and partial fractions therefore give the unique O-basis

    B={r^j:j>=0} union {(r-a)^(-j):a in E,j>=1}.            (1)

Existence over O, not just K, follows because the denominators involved in separating distinct poles are products of their unit differences. Uniqueness holds already over the rational function field K(r). Thus a finite rational element belongs to p^n A0 precisely when every coefficient in (1) belongs to p^n O.

Taking p-adic completion identifies completion_p(A0) with the c0-family of O coefficients on this countable basis: modulo each p^n, only finitely many coefficients may be nonzero. After inverting p, Rhat is the K-Banach space of coefficients tending to zero with the maximum norm. This is a partial-fraction description of the stated completion, not an exchange of completion with a different localization.

Consequently a linear functional specified on (1) extends continuously to Rhat exactly when its basis weights have uniformly bounded p-adic absolute value. Sufficiency is the convergent sum of coefficient times bounded weight. Necessity follows by evaluating the functional on the norm-one basis vectors. This criterion uses no infinite-dimensional Hahn--Banach assertion.

## 4. Bounded row/scalar comparison and six generators after separation

Write the scalar operator as

    Kcal(v)=(Pv')'-2qv.

The original row elimination is a bounded comparison on the strong completion:

    c(b1,b2)=b1-2(Pb2)'-A b2,
    b-(c(b),0)=L(2P b2,0),
    c(L(h1,h2))=-2 Kcal(h2),
    L(-2Pv'+Av,v)=(-2 Kcal(v),0).                           (2)

All multipliers are integral polynomials, differentiation preserves the integral algebra and its p-adic ideals, and 2 is a unit. Hence these identities extend continuously to the completion. They give inverse continuous maps between Sbar and

    Tbar=Rhat/closure(Kcal(Rhat)).                          (3)

We now work in (3), retaining the indicated row representatives via (2).

Let V6 be the K-span of 1,r,(r-a)^(-1) for a in E. The inherited rational normal form states that every element of A0[1/p] lies in Kcal(A0[1/p])+V6. This rational subspace is dense in Rhat. Therefore the image of V6 is dense in Tbar. But Tbar is Banach, being a quotient by a closed subspace, and every finite-dimensional K-subspace of a Banach space is closed. The image of V6 is consequently all of Tbar. This proves dim Tbar<=6 and the surjectivity of the weak-to-separated comparison, without pretending that the old full boundary map is bounded.

## 6. The first infinity coordinate is also integral and continuous

Use the original good-infinity coordinate z=1/r and the normalized horizontal matrix H with H(0)=I. The inherited fixed Frobenius identity is

    H(z)=G(z) H(z^p) G(0)^(-1),
    G=M^(-T) in p^(-1)Mat2(Z_p[[z]]),
    G(0)^(-1)=diag(p/beta,beta).                           (5)

The coefficients of this normalized H are rational, so the displayed coefficient-Frobenius convention is exactly the one in the original source. We do not replace a general semilinear equation by a linear one.

Let h^(1)=H e1. Equation(5) gives

    h^(1)(z)=(p/beta)G(z) h^(1)(z^p).                     (6)

The matrix (p/beta)G is integral and h^(1)(0)=e1. For every coefficient of positive index n, the right side uses coefficients of h^(1) at indices at most floor(n/p)<n. Induction proves

    h^(1) in O[[z]]^2.                                   (7)

This is a uniform integral bound for the first column. The earlier logarithmic estimate for the whole H did not by itself imply (7); the actual normalized Frobenius eigenvalue p/beta is what pays it.

The first infinity functional on rational scalar inputs is

    Q1(b)=Res_z(-z^(-1)b(1/z) H_11(z) dz).

It sends r^j to -[z^j]H_11 and every finite-pole principal part to zero. By (7) all its basis weights are integral, so it extends continuously to Rhat with norm at most one and kills the strong image by the same density argument as above.

Its value at 1 is -1, while all finite residues vanish there. Together with the four independent residues this gives five independent continuous functionals on Tbar. In particular the classes of 1 and the four simple poles survive and are independent. The remaining representative r is killed by these five functionals, because the original normal-form coordinates have Q1(r)=0. Consequently the kernel of V6->Tbar is contained in Kr.

## 7. The remaining alternative is exactly one scalar boundedness problem

Put h_j=[z^j]H_12(z). On the partial-fraction basis,

    Q2(r^j)=-h_j,
    Q2((r-a)^(-j))=0,  j>=1.                             (8)

Therefore Q2 extends continuously to Rhat if and only if the sequence h_j has a uniform lower p-adic valuation bound. When it does, Q2 annihilates the strong image by density, and Q2(r)=-1/8 adds the sixth independent functional. Then dim Tbar=6 and the weak-to-separated comparison is an isomorphism.

There is an explicit rational recurrence for exactly this question. Let a_j be the coefficient of r in the rational normal form of r^j. The original normal-form formula identifies a_j=8h_j. With a_0=0, a_1=1 and a_-1=a_-2=0, the exact scalar identity

    Kcal(r^j)=-4(j+1)(j+2)r^(j+2)
                +(5j(j+1)+2)r^j-j(j-1)r^(j-2)

gives, for every integer j>=0,

    a_(j+2)=((5j(j+1)+2)a_j-j(j-1)a_(j-2))
                                      /(4(j+1)(j+2)).    (9)

This is a rational characteristic-zero recurrence before taking p-adic valuations. Its denominators are nonzero integers; no division by zero in F_p is being performed. All even a_j vanish. The first odd values are 1,1/2,5/16,25/112 at j=1,3,5,7. These are exact consequences of (9), not numerical evidence for boundedness.

If the sequence is unbounded in p-adic absolute value, choose indices j_n with |a_(j_n)| tending to infinity. Let b_j=[z^j]H_11, which lies in O by (7). Rational normal form gives a polynomial v_j with

    r^j-b_j-a_j r=Kcal(v_j).                              (10)

There are no finite principal parts in this identity. The rational vectors

    w_n=(r^(j_n)-b_(j_n))/a_(j_n)

have norm at most 1/|a_(j_n)|, hence tend to zero. Equation(10) says w_n-r belongs to Kcal(Rhat). Thus r belongs to its closure. Since the five other classes survive, dim Tbar=5 and the comparison kernel is exactly Kr.

This proves the complete equivalence:

    (a_j bounded p-adically)  <=> dim Tbar=6 <=> [r] survives separation;
    (a_j unbounded)           <=> dim Tbar=5 <=> [r] dies in separation.

No alternative is selected here. A finite list of coefficients cannot supply the uniform bound or an unbounded subsequence. Nor does logarithmic growth from the old paper decide between them. The sequence and ring are fixed; a future proof must determine this exact all-j question rather than changing the prime, coordinate, lattice, or Frobenius normalization.
