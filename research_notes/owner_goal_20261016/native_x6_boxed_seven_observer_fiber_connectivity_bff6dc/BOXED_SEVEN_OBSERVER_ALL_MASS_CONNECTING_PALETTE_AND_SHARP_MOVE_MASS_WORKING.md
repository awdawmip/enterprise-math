## 2. Sites, readouts, moves and the precise conclusion

Use the original restricted chart c+Z^6. Its six M_i readouts sum over
coordinate-i fibers, retaining the joint association of the other five
coordinates. The seventh N_v sums over cosets of Z v, v=(1,1,1,1,1,1).
No native dimension is removed, and time is separately typed and unused.

Consider occupancies supported in the complete six-coordinate box

    B={0,1,2,3} x {0,1,2}^5,                             (2)

where the exponent tuples denote the sites c+the tuple. Occupancy is zero
outside B. These are the original raw observer coordinates, not final
exported Cell addresses. All seven original readouts are still used,
including their zero bins; they are not coarsened at the box boundary.

A fiber means all finite nonnegative INTEGER configurations on B having
the same seven readouts. Their total mass is automatically the same by
summing any coordinate-fiber readout. No prescribed upper bound on that
mass is imposed in the theorem.

A move is a nonzero INTEGER signed array on B with all seven readouts
zero. Its positive and negative masses are equal. Define its move mass

    mu(h)=sum h_+ = sum h_- = (1/2) sum |h|.

For any palette of moves, an edge adds or subtracts ONE palette member
when the resulting occupancy is nonnegative. A palette is universally
connecting on B if every fiber is connected by finite such paths. This
is an auxiliary algebraic graph on configurations, not a physical motion
or sensing law. For each fixed fiber the vertex set is finite because
B is finite and the total occupancy mass is fixed; the union over all
fibers and masses need not be finite.

Put x=X_1 and Y=(X_2,...,X_6), U=X_2...X_6, and define

    g0=G,  g1=xG,  g01=(1+x)G=g0+g1.                    (3)

All three are supported in B. The new theorem is:

* Every move on B is uniquely h=a g0+b g1 with a,b in Z, and

      mu(a,b)=32(|a|+|b|)+31|a-b|.                      (4)

* Every such move is an integer sum of signed members of
  {g0,g1,g01} WITHOUT any coordinatewise cancellation. This triple
  therefore connects EVERY nonnegative integer fiber on B.
* A palette, finite or infinite, is universally connecting on B if and
  only if it contains each of g0,g1,g01 up to sign. Thus this is the
  unique inclusion-minimal connecting palette up to orientations.
  The least possible maximum move mass is64; its three required

## 3. The full boxed integer kernel has exactly two parameters

Let P be the polynomial of a nonzero move supported in B. It has no
negative exponents, degree at most3 in x, and degree at most2 in every
other variable. By(1), P=GQ for a finite INTEGER Laurent polynomial Q.

For any variable X_i, regard this equality as an equality of Laurent
polynomials over the integral domain Z[the other X_j^(+-1)]. In that
one-variable Laurent domain the least exponents add under multiplication:
the product of the two nonzero trailing coefficients is nonzero. The
greatest exponents add for the same reason. For G the least exponent in
each variable is0 and the greatest is2. Both trailing and leading
coefficients are nonzero polynomials in the other variables; setting
X_i=0 leaves the nonzero product of the other five axis factors.

Consequently the least exponent of Q in each variable is nonnegative.
Its greatest exponent is at most0 in X_2,...,X_6 and at most1 in x.
Every exponent in its support is therefore (0,0,...,0) or (1,0,...,0).
Thus

    Q=a+bx, a,b in Z.                                   (5)

There is no rational coefficient enlargement: integrality of Q is part
of the original exact integer kernel(1). The integer pair is unique
because the Laurent ring is a domain and 1,x are independent monomials.
Conversely any G(a+bx) is supported in B and has the seven zero readouts.
This proves that the entire boxed comparison lattice is Zg0+Zg1, not
merely that the three proposed moves belong to it. The zero move is the
same formula with a=b=0.

## 4. Actual site coefficients and exact move mass

Let

    A(Y)=product_(j=2)^6 (X_j-1).

It has32 unit coefficients. Its support is the five-cube {0,1}^5;
U A has the shifted five-cube {1,2}^5. The two supports meet only at
e=(1,1,1,1,1). At e the coefficient of A is+1 and that of U A is-1,
since there are five factors. Apart from e each cube has31 sites and
their supports are disjoint. These are exact finite supports and signs,
not probabilities or generated sample data.

The actual factorization is

    G=A(Y)[U x^2-(U+1)x+1].

Therefore the four x-level coefficient arrays of P=G(a+bx) are

    x^0: a A,
    x^1: (b-a) A-a U A,
    x^2: -b A+(a-b) U A,
    x^3: b U A.                                        (6)

At a lower-only Y site with A coefficient epsilon, the nonzero possible
four-level coefficients are epsilon times (a,b-a,-b,0).
At an upper-only site with U A coefficient eta, they are eta times
(0,-a,a-b,b). At the one common site e they are exactly

    (a,b,-a,-b).                                       (7)

Thus EVERY actual coefficient is one of +/-a, +/-b or +/-(b-a), or is
identically zero. Taking absolute values in the two31-site groups and
the one common site gives

    sum |P|=62(|a|+|b|+|a-b|)+2(|a|+|b|)
           =64(|a|+|b|)+62|a-b|.                        (8)

The total coefficient sum is0 by G(1,...,1)=0. Its positive and negative
masses are consequently half this L1 norm. Equation(4) follows.
In particular mu(g0)=mu(g1)=63 and mu(g01)=64.

This calculation retains the overlap site e. Dropping it or treating
the two five-cubes as disjoint would give an incorrect mass formula and
would not support the lower bound below.

## 5. Integer sign-conformal decomposition of every comparison

Call a decomposition sign-conformal when, at every actual site of B,
all summands have the sign of their total or are zero. In particular no
positive/negative cancellation occurs at a site. In view of(6)-(7), it
suffices to respect the signs of the THREE linear forms a,b,b-a.

The lines a=0, b=0 and a=b divide the integer parameter plane into the
following six closed cones. Each listed decomposition has nonnegative
integer multiplicities:

    a>=b>=0:   (a-b) g0 + b g01;
    b>=a>=0:   (b-a) g1 + a g01;
    a>=0>=b:   a g0 + (-b)(-g1);
    b>=0>=a:   b g1 + (-a)(-g0);
    0>=a>=b:   (-a)(-g01)+(a-b)(-g1);
    0>=b>=a:   (-b)(-g01)+(b-a)(-g0).                   (9)

Their sums are G(a+bx) by (3). To check signs rather than only vector
sums, in the first cone the forms have signs (+,+,-): g0 has forms
(1,0,-1) and g01 has (1,1,0), so both summands obey those signs. In the
second cone the signs are (+,+,+), and g1 has forms(0,1,1) while g01
has(1,1,0). In the third cone the signs are (+,-,-), obeyed by g0 and
-g1. In the fourth they are (-,+,+), obeyed by g1 and -g0. The last two
cones are the respective negatives of the first two with the correct
ordered inequality, and the listed generators obey all three form signs.
On a boundary a form may vanish; the corresponding displayed summands
also have zero value for that form unless their multiplicity is zero.
Thus no cancellation is introduced on the walls either.

Since each actual coefficient is a fixed sign times one of these forms,
the decompositions are sign-conformal on the original sites, including
the overlap coefficients(7). This supplies an INTEGER decomposition of
every boxed move. It is not a real cone decomposition whose rational
denominators or coefficient cancellations would later need repair.

For clarity, the last-cone sign check can also be read directly: when
0>=a>=b, -g01 has forms(-1,-1,0), -g1 has(0,-1,-1), matching (-,-,-).
When 0>=b>=a, -g01 and -g0 have forms(-1,-1,0) and(-1,0,1), matching
(-,-,+). This covers all parameter signs without symmetry assumptions
on the original observer data.

## 6. These signed sums give actual nonnegative paths

Let f,g be any two nonnegative integer configurations on B with all
seven readouts equal, and put h=g-f. Section3 gives its unique integer
pair a,b. Section5 writes h as a FINITE list delta_1+...+delta_L of
signed members of {g0,g1,g01}, with sign conformity at every site.
There is no upper-mass restriction on f or g in this construction.

Consider the path

    f_j=f+sum_(i=1)^j delta_i,   j=0,...,L.              (10)

If h(s)>=0 at a site s, its partial sums range from0 to h(s), so
f_j(s)>=f(s)>=0. If h(s)<=0, its partial sums range from h(s) to0, so
f_j(s)>=f(s)+h(s)=g(s)>=0. All entries remain integers. Every summand
is supported in B and lies in the actual seven-observer kernel, so
every f_j stays in B, preserves all seven readouts and preserves the
total occupancy mass. The final state is g.

This proves universal connection by the triple(3), not just connection
in the signed lattice or existence of a polynomial representation.
It also proves that any larger palette containing that triple up to
sign is connecting. No simultaneous illegal large move is hidden inside
a step: each delta_i itself is a permitted one-move edge, and the
coordinate inequalities prove legality before proceeding to the next.

## 7. All three members are necessary, and64 is sharp

First classify the possible small-mass BOXED moves using(4). If ab<=0,
including either zero, then |a-b|=|a|+|b|, and

    mu(a,b)=63(|a|+|b|).

If ab>0, let v=max(|a|,|b|), u=min(|a|,|b|)>=1. Then

    mu(a,b)=63v+u.                                     (11)

It follows over the integers that the ONLY nonzero moves of mass at
most63 are +/-g0 and +/-g1. The ONLY additional moves at mass64 are
+/-g01. These conclusions are for the entire boxed kernel by Section3,
not an assumption that a proposed palette already has this form.

Any move legal from a nonnegative occupancy of total mass M must have
move mass at most M: its negative part is dominated by that occupancy.
Thus moves of greater mass cannot help at the witness fibers below,
even if the palette is infinite or generates the signed lattice.

To fix the actual x-level counts, A and U A each have16 positive and16
negative coefficients. The overlap cancels in A+U A, leaving62 unit
coefficients with31 of each sign. Hence BOTH positive and negative
parts of g0 have x-level occupancies

    (16,31,16,0),

and both sign parts of g1 have

    (0,16,31,16).                                      (12)

The factorizations in(3) also give

    g01=(x^2-1)(U x-1)A
         = A-x U A-x^2 A+x^3 U A.

The four x-levels here are distinct; each contributes16 to each sign.
Thus BOTH sign configurations of g01 have x-level occupancies

    (16,16,16,16).                                     (13)

All these configurations are finite N0 occupancies on B. Each sign
pair has the same seven readouts, because its signed difference is
the corresponding actual kernel move.

Take the g0 sign pair at total mass63. If a connecting palette omitted
g0 up to sign, the only candidate moves it could apply at that mass
would be +/-g1, by(11). Both orientations require16 sites of negative
mass at x=3, while either g0 sign state has none there. They are illegal.
Moves of mass>63 are illegal as well. The first state is isolated despite
having its distinct g0 partner in the same fiber. Thus every universally
connecting palette must contain g0 up to sign. The g1 sign pair gives
the same necessity for g1: a g1 sign state has no mass at x=0, whereas
both orientations of g0 require16 negative units there.

Now take the g01 sign pair at total mass64. If the palette omitted g01
up to sign, its only moves that could possibly be legal would be
+/-g0 and +/-g1. Every orientation of g0 requires31 negative units on
the middle x-level1, and every orientation of g1 requires31 on level2.
Each state in(13) has only16 TOTAL units on either level. Therefore
none is legal. These two distinct states are isolated under every such
palette, and a move of mass>64 cannot start from them. This forces g01
up to sign in every universally connecting palette.

Together with Section6, necessity proves the if-and-only-if palette
criterion in Section2. It also proves uniqueness of the inclusion-minimal
palette, not just an upper bound for one construction. Since the required
g01 has mass64 while the triple attains that maximum,64 is the exact
minimum possible maximum move mass.

This is also a concrete FIRST noncritical obstruction to using all
critical-mass moves on this box: all those boxed moves are +/-g0,+/-g1,
yet their mass64 fiber fails to connect. At masses below63 there are no
nontrivial fibers; at mass63 any nonzero comparison has mass63 and is
one of those two moves, so the full critical set directly connects any
two distinct states in a fiber. None of this assumes or proves a
connection theorem for the entire unbounded chart at higher masses.
