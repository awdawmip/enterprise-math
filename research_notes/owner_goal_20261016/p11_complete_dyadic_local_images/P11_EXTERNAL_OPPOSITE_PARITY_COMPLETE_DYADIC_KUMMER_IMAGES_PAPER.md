## 3. The exactly-once-even case m=1

Here Q²=4v² and P²≡v²≡1 modulo8. For k=0, x and x−Q² are units, so the square equation forces v2(x−P²) even. If x were3 or7 modulo8, the latter valuation would be1, impossible. Thus a is1 or5, and b has even valuation, so b belongs to U.

At k=1 write x=2u. Dividing y² by4 gives the odd unit

    u(u−2v²)(2u−P²).

Modulo8 this is u(u−2)(2u−1). Since u²≡1, it equals (1−2u)(2u−1)=−(2u−1)²≡7. It cannot be a square. Hence no ordinary point has k=1.

At k=2 write x=4u. The factor b=x−P² is the unit3 modulo8. The total valuation on the right of the equation is4+v2(u−v²), so v2(u−v²) must be even. It is positive, hence at least2; therefore u≡v²≡1 modulo4. The class a is thus1 or5. The class b is still a unit, as required. This deduction is only necessary containment; no existence claim for every u has been inserted.

If k>2, the third factor has valuation2 and b is an odd unit. Thus the total valuation k+2 must be even; k is even and at least4. Moreover

    x−Q² = −Q²*(1−x/Q²),       b≡−1 (mod8).

The square equation says that a times (1−x/Q²) is square. At k=4 the correction is5 modulo8, forcing a=5. At even k≥6 it is1 modulo8, forcing a=1. In every allowed case a is1 or5 and b is a unit. The preceding negative, zero and positive branches therefore prove the asserted containment for every ordinary point at m=1.

## 4. The at-least-twice-even case m≥2

For k=0, (x−Q²)/x is1 modulo8 and square. Thus b is square. The classes x≡3,7 modulo8 would make v2(b)=1 and are impossible. Hence a is1 or5, satisfying a in<2,5>, and b=1.

For k=1, h=(x−Q²)/x is square because v2(Q²/x)=2m−1≥3. The equation forces b square. Since b=x−P² is a unit, its residue must be1 modulo8, so x≡2 modulo8 and u=x/2≡1 modulo4. Therefore a is2 or10 and b=1.

For k=2, b is3 modulo8. The same h is5 modulo8 when m=2 and1 modulo8 when m≥3. In either event b*h is7 or3 modulo8, respectively, not square. Since the equation is y²=x²*b*h, this branch is impossible.

It remains to treat k≥3. Now b≡−1 modulo8, so b has class−1. The equation requires h=(x−Q²)/x to have class−1 as well. Put l=2m−k. The following cases exhaust every integer l and force the normalized odd unit u of x to be1 modulo4:

* l≥3: h is1 modulo8, impossible; l=2: h is5 modulo8, also impossible.
* l=1: h=1−2v²/u. To have residue7 modulo8 requires u≡1 modulo4.
* l=0: h=(u−v²)/u. Its valuation must be even. Since u,v are odd and h is nonzero, that valuation is positive and at least2, so u≡v²≡1 modulo4.
* l=−1: h has valuation−1, impossible for class−1.
* l=−j with j≥2: h has valuation−j, so j must be even. Rewrite h=−Q²/x*(1−x/Q²). At j=2 the correction has class5, so h of class−1 forces a=5. At even j≥4 the correction is square, forcing a=1. Both conclusions imply u≡1 modulo4. Odd j have already been excluded by valuation parity.

Thus a has arbitrary allowed valuation parity but unit1 modulo4, equivalently a belongs to<2,5>. Throughout this k≥3 branch b has class−1. Combined with k<0, k=0,1 and the excluded k=2, all ordinary values lie in the claimed subgroup.
