## 1. Exact gap, types, and statement

Let P>Q>0 be coprime odd integers, put D=P²−Q², and assume v2(D)=4. Consider the classical elliptic curve

E: y²=x(x−P²)(x−Q²)

over Q2, with the fixed root order (0,P²,Q²). The split Kummer map has ordinary values

δ(x,y)=(x,x−P²,x−Q²)

in squareclasses, with product one. At a zero factor use the already declared product-of-root-differences convention, and δ(O)=(1,1,1). Write its first two coordinates (a,b); the third is ab.

## 2. Four symbolic local points

Write D=16d with d odd. For any odd 2-adic unit u set

x_u=P²+4u,
h_u=1+4d/u.

Then

x_u−P²=4u,
x_u−Q²=4(u+4d)=4u h_u,

so the curve equation is exactly

x_u(x_u−P²)(x_u−Q²)=16u² x_u h_u.

Since P is odd and d,u are odd,

x_u≡5 mod8,   h_u≡5 mod8.

Their product is an odd unit congruent to 1 modulo 8, hence a square in Q2. Choose w_u with w_u²=x_u h_u and put y_u=4u w_u. This gives a Q2 point on E. This uses the precise unit-square criterion at 2; it does not apply a false simple-root Hensel derivative condition to 2w_u.

For the four positive rational choices u=1,3,5,7, all three factors are nonzero: x_u>0, 4u>0, and 4(u+4d)>0 because d>0. Thus these are ordinary local points and no exceptional zero evaluation is being smuggled into the construction. Their Kummer values are

δ(P_u)=(5,u,5u).

Indeed x_u has unit squareclass 5; x_u−P² has class u; and h_u has class 5, giving third class 5u. A local square root is asserted by an exact residue proof, not numerically approximated.

## 3. Why these points prove the entire image

The Kummer map is a homomorphism with kernel 2E(Q2). The curve has all its two-torsion over Q2, and the exact split-cubic local-index theorem gives

|E(Q2)/2E(Q2)|=8.

This is Stoll's Lemma 4.4 specialized to genus one and three rational roots; it does not require a minimal Weierstrass model or good reduction.

Let A_u denote the image (5,u,5u) just constructed. The image subgroup contains A_1 and all products

A_1 A_u=(1,u,u),   u=1,3,5,7.

The latter are four distinct vertical classes. Multiplying each by A_1 gives the other four classes, whose first coordinate is the nonsquare unit 5. Hence the image contains the displayed eight-element subgroup. Its exact order is already eight, so equality follows.

This is a subgroup-generation and exact-index proof, not an inference that a chosen sample exhausts all points. It also covers negative valuations, points near roots, the exceptional two-torsion points, and infinity: those are in the same Kummer image, whose total order leaves no room for an additional class. No separate unproved containment argument for those cases is needed.

The four unit classes are all distinct by the standard Q2 criterion that two odd units represent the same squareclass exactly when they agree modulo 8. In particular the proof does not conflate unramified classes with all local squareclasses, or identify the nonvertical first-coordinate class 5 with a global Selmer element without checking the other places.

## 4. Exact replacement of the missing dyadic matrix rows

For a pair of rational squareclasses (a,b), let a_odd=2^(−v2(a))a. Membership in this local image is equivalent to the three linear squareclass conditions

v2(a)≡0 mod2,
a_odd≡1 mod4,
v2(b)≡0 mod2.

The residue statement is a condition on the odd unit squareclass, so it is well-defined for rational representatives with negative valuations and invariant under multiplication by a square. For a with even valuation, odd residue 1 modulo 4 means precisely the unit classes 1 or 5 modulo 8. The second coordinate has arbitrary odd-unit class once its valuation is even. The third coordinate is fixed by the product-square condition.

Thus the existing finite S-unit Selmer matrix for coprime odd P,Q can be extended to this dyadic branch by keeping its real and every odd-prime condition unchanged and replacing its old dyadic condition “first coordinate square in Q2” by exactly these three rows.
