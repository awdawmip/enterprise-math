## 3. Frontier equivalence and coarseness

It includes x only when there is a qualifying return witness; the known membership x in V is retained separately. Intermediate positions are distinct and outside V, while a terminal return to x is permitted. These conditions exclude an earlier future self-contact.

For necessity, take a witness word to v in F_H(V,x). The V observer first stops at its last step. Equal responses force the W observer to survive each earlier position and stop at the same last step. That last stop cannot arise from a future-prefix repeat, since the witness has no such repeat; it must be v in W. Its intermediates are then outside W, giving the same frontier witness for W. Applying the argument in both directions gives equal frontiers.

For sufficiency, suppose the two responses disagree and choose the earlier stopping step j, treating SURVIVE as no finite stop in this word. A future-prefix-repeat stop is determined by the common word itself. If neither observer stopped earlier, both would stop at j, so such a stop cannot be the earliest discrepancy. The earlier stop must instead reach an old Cell v along an old-free, self-repeat-free prefix. Hence v lies in that frontier. Equality of frontiers puts v in the other old set, which forces that observer to stop by j as well. This contradicts the discrepancy. The argument covers arbitrary admitted words, not only geodesic probes.

Thus frontier equality is exactly response equivalence. Different attainable frontiers have a distinguishing admitted word, so this is the coarsest semantic quotient.
