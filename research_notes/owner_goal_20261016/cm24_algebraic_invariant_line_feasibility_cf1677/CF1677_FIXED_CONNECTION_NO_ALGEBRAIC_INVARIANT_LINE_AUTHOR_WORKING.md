## 3. The rational argument survives arbitrary constant extension

First let C/K be any field extension. Suppose W=C(r)g is a stable line.
Clear denominators and remove the greatest common divisor so that

 g in C[r]^(1x2), gcd(g1,g2)=1,       g'+gD=lambda g.

At every finite point of an algebraic closure of C, g has a nonzero
component. Away from P=0 this makes lambda regular. At each of the four
simple roots a=1,-1,1/2,-1/2, lambda has at most a simple pole. With

 N_a=Res_a(D),       mu=Res_a(lambda),

taking residues gives mu g(a)=g(a)N_a. The literal identity

 [[A,1],[B,-A]]^2=4(S^2-1)I

gives N_a^2=0 since S(a)=1,-1,-1,1 respectively. Hence mu^2g(a)=0,
so mu=0. There are no finite poles even after algebraic closure; thus
lambda belongs to C[r].

For infinity use exactly z=1/r and J=diag(z,z^(-1)). The good connection
is

 D_inf=-J^(-1)J'-z^(-2)J^(-1)D(1/z)J,
 Q_inf=(z^2-1)(4-z^2),
 D_inf=(2Q_inf)^(-1)[
  [-z(4-z-2z^2), -1],
  [-z(16-12z^2-5z^3), z(4-z-2z^2)] ].

The literal rational matrix is regular at z=0 because Q_inf(0)=-4.
This is the same good frame and matrix as old13905 Section5, reused
without a change of lift or variance. Put g_good=g(1/z)J. The row chain
rule gives

 g_good'+g_good D_inf=-z^(-2)lambda(1/z)g_good.

Write the nonzero finite Laurent row as z^s v(z), v in C[[z]]^2 with a
unit component v_i. Dividing that component gives

 -z^(-2)lambda(1/z)=s/z+(v_i'+(vD_inf)_i)/v_i.

The right side has at most a simple pole. A nonzero polynomial lambda
would give pole order deg(lambda)+2, so lambda=0. Thus a rational stable
line over C would furnish a nonzero horizontal POLYNOMIAL row g over C.

This last possibility descends to K by finite linear algebra; it does not
require C to carry an extension of the p-adic weak topology. Let V be the
finite-dimensional K-vector space spanned by the finitely many coefficients
of g. Choose a K-linear functional ell:V->K nonzero on one nonzero
coefficient. In the cleared equation

                         2P g'+g[[A,1],[B,-A]]=0,

all coefficients lie in V, since the displayed matrix and 2P have
coefficients in K. Apply ell coefficientwise. This gives a nonzero
horizontal polynomial row g_tilde in K[r]^2. It belongs to the original
embedded R^2 and contradicts old9610 Section4. Consequently there is no
rational stable line over C(r) for ANY constant extension C/K.

This is a reuse of the actual rational pole bridge plus a new explicit
constant-extension descent check; it is not an unsupported inference
from irreducibility over K to irreducibility over every extension.

## 4. Formal local reduction with row variance and nilpotent residue

Let C now be algebraically closed of characteristic zero, t a local
coordinate, and

                         D_t=N/t+A(t),
                         A(t) in M_2(C[[t]]), N^2=0.

There is H in GL_2(C[[t]]), H(0)=I, satisfying

                  H'=D_t H-HN/t.                         (1)

Indeed, writing H=sum H_n t^n, H_0=I and A=sum A_i t^i, the coefficient
recurrence for n>=1 is

          (nI-ad_N)H_n=sum_(i+j=n-1) A_i H_j,             (2)
          ad_N(X)=NX-XN.

Since N^2=0, ad_N^3=0, and the inverse on matrices is exactly

       (nI-ad_N)^(-1)=I/n+ad_N/n^2+ad_N^2/n^3.

Thus every coefficient is defined in C, without fractional t powers or a
nonresonance assumption on p-adic valuations. This is FORMAL characteristic
zero reduction; no analytic convergence or effective precision assertion
is needed. Its determinant has constant term1.

For a row h put k=hH. Equation(1) gives the exact covariance

         h'+hD_t=lambda h   iff   k'+kN/t=lambda k.

The same identity preserves a line without fixing a generator or lambda.

## 5. An algebraic local stable line cannot carry ramification

Let C((t^(1/e))) be any Puiseux Laurent field. Consider a line stable for
the row connection d/dt+N/t, N^2=0. Multiplying the differential operator
by t does not alter stability.

If N=0, normalize a generator to (1,u) unless the first component is
identically zero, in which case the line is the constant second axis.
For (1,u), stability under t d/dt says (0,t u')=lambda(1,u), hence
lambda=0 and t u'=0. Characteristic zero forces every nonconstant Puiseux
coefficient of u to vanish. Therefore this is a constant C-line.

If N is nonzero, a constant change of two-dimensional basis over C puts
N into [[0,1],[0,0]]. Its row kernel is the second axis. Any other line
has a generator (1,u). Now

           (t d/dt+right N)(1,u)=(0,t u'+1).

Stability would force lambda=0 and t u'=-1. This is impossible in a
Puiseux Laurent field: the t^0 coefficient of t u' is zero, because the
only exponent capable of producing t^0 is exponent0 and its derivative
vanishes. Thus the ONLY possible stable line is the constant row kernel
of N. This is a statement about lines, not merely horizontal vectors.

Apply the formal H of Section4. Its entries and inverse have integer
power-series exponents. Consequently any algebraic stable line for D_t,
after choosing its Puiseux branch and multiplying by H, becomes one of
the just-classified constant lines. Its original projective coordinate
therefore belongs to C((t)), with no fractional exponents. A quotient of
two power series may have a pole, which is harmless: only absence of
ramification, not analytic regularity of a particular coordinate, is used.

For the actual matrix, at the four finite roots of P take t=r-a and
N=N_a; the remaining part is in M_2(C[[t]]). At every other finite point
D is regular, so use N=0. At infinity first apply the RATIONAL frame
J=diag(z,z^(-1)) of Section3. The actual D_inf is regular, so use N=0
there too. A rational frame cannot introduce fractional powers into a
projective coordinate. Thus EVERY place of P^1_C has the same conclusion.

## 6. From local unramified coordinates to a rational line

Suppose a stable line is defined over a finite algebraic extension of
C(r), with C algebraically closed. If its first coordinate is identically
zero, the line is already rational and excluded by Section3. Otherwise
write it as the span of (1,u), with u algebraic over C(r), and put
E=C(r,u). The unique extension of d/dr to E makes the same line stable:
its first component determines lambda in E and its second is the same
Riccati equation. Thus no auxiliary overfield is needed for the line.

At every place of E above t=0, the characteristic-zero algebraic Puiseux
theorem embeds the completed field into a Puiseux field over C((t)).
The embedding commutes with the uniquely extended derivation. All its
branches therefore satisfy Section5. Each branch of u lies in C((t)).
The completion of C(r,u) at that place is generated, after completion,
by C((t)) and this branch of u. It is exactly C((t)). In particular
every ramification index is1. This applies also to infinity after the
specified rational good frame. No singular point is omitted.

Let X be the smooth connected projective curve with function field E.
The finite separable map X->P^1_C has degree d_E=[E:C(r)] and no
ramification. The classical Riemann-Hurwitz formula gives

                         2g(X)-2=-2d_E.

Since g(X)>=0 and d_E>=1, necessarily d_E=1. Hence u lies in C(r).
Section3 excludes that rational stable line. This proves the proposed
statement when the constant field is algebraically closed.

For arbitrary C/K, embed C into an algebraic closure Cbar and embed a
finite algebraic L/C(r) into an algebraic closure of Cbar(r), extending
the inclusion of C(r). Such an embedding exists because the equations
are algebraic and separable. A stable line maps to a stable line in a
finite algebraic extension of Cbar(r): its projective coordinate remains
algebraic and derivations commute by uniqueness. The algebraically closed
case just proved gives the contradiction. Finally, an invariant line over
any algebraic extension is already defined by its one algebraic projective
coordinate, so the finite case suffices.
