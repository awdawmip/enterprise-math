## 3. Put an assumed rational stable line in a precise polynomial form

Suppose W is a one-dimensional F-subspace stable under nabla. Choose a
nonzero row in W, clear its polynomial denominators, and divide the two
polynomial components by their greatest common divisor. This gives

                  g=(g1,g2) in K[r]^(1x2), gcd(g1,g2)=1,
                  W=F g.

The gcd convention includes a zero component: the other component can
then be made a nonzero constant. Bezout gives u1g1+u2g2=1 with ui in K[r].
Thus at every finite closed point at least one component is a local unit.
This is a choice of generator WITHIN the assumed rational line, not a
change of the specified connection matrix or a claimed global weak gauge.

Stability gives a unique scalar lambda in F with

                       g'+gD=lambda g.                       (1)

A general rational function is not asserted to belong to R. Only this

## 4. Nilpotent residues force lambda to be a polynomial

Away from P=0, D is regular and one component of g is a local unit.
The corresponding component of (1) shows that lambda has no pole there.
This reasoning holds at an arbitrary irreducible closed point of K[r],
so it does not assume every polynomial splits in K.

The four zeros a=1,-1,1/2,-1/2 of P are simple. D has at most a simple
pole at each; let

                 N_a=Res_a(D)= [[A(a),1],[B(a),-A(a)]]/(2P'(a)).

For the numerator M(r)=[[A,1],[B,-A]], direct hand multiplication gives

                 M(r)^2=(A^2+B) I=4(S^2-1) I.

The four S(a) are1,-1,-1,1 in the displayed order. Therefore N_a^2=0.
This is also the old normalized residue fact, here checked from literal D.

Since a component of g is a unit at a, (1) first shows that lambda has at
most a simple pole there. Put mu=Res_a(lambda). Taking residues yields

                         mu g(a)=g(a) N_a.

Multiplying once more by N_a gives mu^2 g(a)=0. The row g(a) is nonzero
and K is a field, so mu=0. The possible simple pole is removable.

All finite poles of lambda have now been excluded. Hence
