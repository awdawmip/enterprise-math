## 1. Integral arrangements modulo q are classical

The finite-field method for hyperplane arrangements is classical, with foundational finite-field counting work including Crapo--Rota and Athanasiadis.

Most directly relevant to the present package:

- Hidehiko Kamiya, Akimichi Takemura, Hiroaki Terao, **Periodicity of hyperplane arrangements with integral coefficients modulo positive integers**, Journal of Algebraic Combinatorics 27 (2008), 317--330, DOI `10.1007/s10801-007-0091-2`.
- Hidehiko Kamiya, Akimichi Takemura, Hiroaki Terao, **Periodicity of non-central integral arrangements modulo positive integers**, Annals of Combinatorics 15 (2011), 449--464, DOI `10.1007/s00026-011-0105-6`.

These works prove quasi-polynomial complement counts modulo arbitrary positive integers and periodicity of reduced intersection data. Therefore the A-package MUST NOT claim invention of characteristic quasi-polynomials, finite-field complement counting, or arithmetic periodicity of integral arrangements.

## 2. Arithmetic Tutte / G-Tutte framework is broader than our counting formalism

Relevant reference:

- Ye Liu, Tan Nhat Tran, Masahiko Yoshinaga, **G-Tutte Polynomials and Abelian Lie Group Arrangements**, International Mathematics Research Notices 2021(1), 150--188, DOI `10.1093/imrn/rnz092`.

The G-Tutte formalism explicitly subsumes characteristic quasi-polynomials and point counting for arrangements over finite abelian groups. Hence any statement of the form “count arrangement complements over Z/qZ” belongs to an established broad framework.

The retained A contribution is only the explicit simple-line determinant-depth specialization:

`triple determinant valuation -> exact concurrence persistence depth`,

`finite-field scalar extension preserves the mod-p intersection defect`

`p-adic precision refines and eventually removes nonstructural modular concurrence`.

This is currently classified as an explicit synthesis/specialization, not independently established novelty.
