## 1. The remaining selection question

The four FCC slice charts form the vertices of a tetrahedron.  Every overlap is an unordered edge `{i,j}`.  The root-torsor theorem shows that a slice-to-slice transport contributes one chirality bit:

- bit zero if it carries `J_i` to `J_j`;
- bit one if it carries `J_i` to `-J_j`.

The algebraic classification leaves two fully `S4`-symmetric gauge classes: flat zero and uniform all-face twist.  This note shows that they arise from the only two fully tetrahedrally equivariant involutive transports that exchange the endpoints of an overlap edge.

Let an edge transport assignment be a map

\[
T:\binom{V}{2}\longrightarrow S_4
\]

such that:

1. `T_{ij}` exchanges `i` and `j`;
2. `T_{ij}` is an involution;
3. `T` is equivariant under relabeling:
   \[
   T_{\sigma\{i,j\}}=\sigma T_{ij}\sigma^{-1}.
   \]

### Classification theorem

There are exactly two such assignments.

If `{k,l}` is the complementary edge, they are

\[
\boxed{M_{ij}=(ij)}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{R_{ij}=(ij)(kl).}
\]

The first is an odd permutation and represents an improper/reflection-type transport.  The second is an even double transposition and represents a proper half-turn of the tetrahedral carrier.

## 2. Proof of the two-candidate theorem

By equivariance it is enough to classify the transport on the base edge `{A,B}`.  The setwise stabilizer of this edge is generated by

\[
(AB),\qquad(CD).
\]

Equivariance requires the base transport to commute with the stabilizer.  Because it exchanges `A` and `B`, its restriction to that pair is `(AB)`.  On the complementary pair `{C,D}`, commuting with `(CD)` and being an involution leaves exactly two possibilities:

- act trivially on `{C,D}`;
- exchange `C` and `D`.

Therefore the only base transports are

\[
(AB),\qquad(AB)(CD).
\]

Conjugating by `S4` gives the two displayed assignments on every edge.  Both assignments are manifestly equivariant, completing the classification.
