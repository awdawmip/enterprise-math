## 1. q-adic boundary measure of one nonzero alias fiber

Fix a coarse nonzero phase theta=k/N in (0,1). At depth m of q-refinement put Q=q^m and descendants a=0,...,Q-1. Normalize inverse spectral mass by the parent mass. The exact cylinder probability is

p_(Q,a)=sin^2(pi theta)/[Q^2 sin^2(pi(a+theta)/Q)].

For fixed a>=0,

p_(Q,a) -> sin^2(pi theta)/[pi^2(a+theta)^2].

For a=Q-1-b with fixed b>=0,

p_(Q,Q-1-b) -> sin^2(pi theta)/[pi^2(b+1-theta)^2].

For 0<=j<=Q/2, sin(pi(j+theta)/Q)>=2(j+theta)/Q up to the symmetric endpoint choice. Hence after retaining R endpoint aliases on each side, the middle probability has a uniform O(1/R) bound. Letting Q->infinity and then R->infinity proves that all boundary mass is supported on the embedded integer aliases ell in Z, with

p_ell(theta)=sin^2(pi theta)/[pi^2(ell+theta)^2].

This boundary law is independent of the refinement radix q. Different q only changes the filtration/digit revelation of the same boundary measure.

Normalization gives the bilateral csc-square identity without differentiating a finite-resolution observable:

sum_(ell in Z) p_ell(theta)=1,

hence

sum_(ell in Z)1/(ell+theta)^2=pi^2 csc^2(pi theta).

## 2. Dyadic Basel as a boundary normalization

For theta=1/2,

p_ell=4/[pi^2(2ell+1)^2].

Probability normalization gives

sum_(ell in Z)4/[pi^2(2ell+1)^2]=1,

so the positive odd reciprocal-square sum is pi^2/8. The even/odd decomposition then gives zeta(2)=pi^2/6.

Thus Basel is the normalization of the boundary probability generated by a single finite antiperiodic inverse-spectral mass.

## 3. Observer-factorization uniqueness of s=2

Suppose a coarse observer retains only the total inverse mass of a fiber and discards alias labels. The boundary arithmetic observable for exponent s is

O_s=sum x_ell |ell+theta|^(2-s).

Under one genuine refinement, a parent alias splits into one child retaining the old centered alias and other children with strictly larger absolute alias magnitude. Since total x mass is conserved, invariance of O_s under total-only collapse requires the label weight |ell+theta|^(2-s) to be equal on distinct child magnitudes. In the power family this occurs iff s=2.

Therefore

s=2 is the unique zeta/Hurwitz exponent whose boundary readout factors through the total-only alias-mass quotient.

This is the rough-observer version of Basel criticality. It does not use an N derivative or microscopic interpolation.

## 4. Finite dyadic zeta approximants with monotone direction

For M=2^m>=4 define the centered odd-alias estimator

zeta_hat_M(s)=pi^2/[(1-2^(-s))M^2] * sum_(1<=n<M/2, n odd) n^(2-s)/sin^2(pi n/M), s>1.

It converges to zeta(s). Under M->2M, each parent branch splits into a same-label child and a farther-label child.

Hence:

- 1<s<2: zeta_hat_M(s) increases strictly to zeta(s);
- s=2: zeta_hat_M(2)=pi^2/6 for every M;
- s>2: zeta_hat_M(s) decreases strictly to zeta(s).

At M=4 this yields the elementary one-step bound

zeta(s) > pi^2/[8(1-2^(-s))] for 1<s<2,

zeta(2)=pi^2/6,

zeta(s) < pi^2/[8(1-2^(-s))] for s>2.

The direction proof is purely finite branch splitting plus monotonicity of r^(2-s).

## 5. Renyi moment spectrum of the alias probability tree

For the dyadic antiperiodic tree let p_(m,a) be the normalized finite branch probabilities. Define

M_alpha^(m)=sum_a p_(m,a)^alpha.

Probability refinement gives:

- 0<alpha<1: M_alpha^(m) increases;
- alpha=1: M_1^(m)=1 exactly;
- alpha>1: M_alpha^(m) decreases.

The boundary moment converges iff alpha>1/2 and equals

M_alpha = 2(4^alpha-1) zeta(2alpha)/pi^(2alpha).

Thus

zeta(2alpha)=pi^(2alpha) M_alpha/[2(4^alpha-1)].

At alpha=1, probability normalization is exactly Basel. At alpha=1/2 the moment hits zeta(1) and diverges, so the earlier half-order criticality is reinterpreted as the Renyi-moment convergence threshold of the positive alias probability distribution, not as a mandatory native fractional carrier.

## 6. General phase: Renyi spectrum equals symmetric Hurwitz tower

For arbitrary theta in (0,1),

sum_(ell in Z) p_ell(theta)^alpha
=
[sin^2(pi theta)/pi^2]^alpha
[ zeta(2alpha,theta)+zeta(2alpha,1-theta) ].

Therefore the unlabeled exact-weight histogram of one alias fiber contains the whole symmetric Hurwitz tower through its Renyi moments.

## 7. BRC observer lattice

The finite/refinement semantics now separates three carriers:

1. total inverse mass only: enough exactly for s=2;
2. unlabeled exact branch-weight histogram: enough for symmetric Hurwitz values through Renyi moments;
3. labeled/oriented alias histogram: enough for character-weighted Dirichlet L-values.

For a real Dirichlet character chi with chi(-1)=(-1)^a, the boundary label observer

Phi_(p,chi)(n)=chi(n) sgn(n)^a |n|^(2-p)

on the common positive inverse-square alias mass mu_N(n)=L^2/[4pi^2N^2 n^2] gives

L(p,chi)=2pi^2 N^2/L^2 * sum_(n!=0) mu_N(n) Phi_(p,chi)(n), p>1.

Hence the previous `A^-1/2` parity-mismatch carrier should be reclassified as a compensation scheme after alias provenance was collapsed and holonomy differentiation was used to recover arithmetic information. If integer alias provenance is retained, a single A^-1 positive mass carrier suffices for all p>1; signed/complex character readout remains separately typed.
