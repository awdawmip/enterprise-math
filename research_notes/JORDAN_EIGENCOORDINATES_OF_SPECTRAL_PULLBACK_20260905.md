## 1. Primitive denominator pullback operator

Recall the formal primitive-channel operator

\[
\mathcal A_n[d]
=\sum_{\substack{g\mid n\\(d,n/g)=1}}[dg].
\]

The operators commute and satisfy

\[
\mathcal A_m\mathcal A_n=\mathcal A_{mn}.
\]

They are the denominator-level image of the finite phase-multiplication semigroup `R_n`.

## 2. Jordan totient eigenlaw

For integer `k>=1`, the Jordan totient is

\[
J_k(d)=d^k\prod_{p\mid d}(1-p^{-k}).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{g\mid n\\(d,n/g)=1}}J_k(dg)
=n^kJ_k(d).
}
\tag{JEC-1}

Proof is multiplicative in `n`.  For one prime `p`:

### p absent from d

\[
\mathcal A_p[d]=[d]+[pd],
\]

and

\[
J_k(pd)=(p^k-1)J_k(d).
\]

Hence

\[
J_k(d)+J_k(pd)=p^kJ_k(d).
\]

### p already present in d

\[
\mathcal A_p[d]=[pd],
\]

and

\[
J_k(pd)=p^kJ_k(d).
\]

Therefore `J_k` is a common left eigenfunctional for every `A_n`, with eigencharacter

\[
\boxed{\chi_k(n)=n^k.}
\tag{JEC-2}

## 3. Basic arithmetic observables fit the same hierarchy

At `k=1`,

\[
J_1=\varphi,
\]

so the previous primitive-mode multiplicity law is exactly

\[
\sum_{e\in\mathcal F_n(d)}\varphi(e)=n\varphi(d).
\]

Thus Euler totient is the first Jordan eigenmode of the pullback semigroup.

The endpoint von-Mangoldt readout is different: derived `Lambda(d)=log|Psi_d(0)|` is invariant rather than `n^k`-scaled.  It belongs to a distinct mass channel and should not be identified with the Jordan multiplicity hierarchy.

## 4. Primitive reciprocal moments in the Jordan eigenbasis

For fixed `s>=1`, the primitive reciprocal moment has already been shown to be

\[
\boxed{
Z_s^{\rm prim}(d)
=\sum_{r=1}^{s}a_{s,r}J_{2r}(d).
}
\tag{JEC-3}

Apply the pullback family and use (JEC-1):

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{e\in\mathcal F_n(d)}Z_s^{\rm prim}(e)
=\sum_{r=1}^{s}a_{s,r}n^{2r}J_{2r}(d).
}
\tag{JEC-4}

Hence reciprocal moments are a triangular mixture of the even Jordan pullback eigenmodes.

Examples:

\[
Z_1^{\rm prim}=\frac16J_2
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{e\in\mathcal F_n(d)}Z_1^{\rm prim}(e)
=n^2Z_1^{\rm prim}(d).
}
\tag{JEC-5}

For

\[
Z_2^{\rm prim}=\frac{2J_4+5J_2}{180},
\]

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{e\in\mathcal F_n(d)}Z_2^{\rm prim}(e)
=
\frac{2n^4J_4(d)+5n^2J_2(d)}{180}.
}
\tag{JEC-6}

## 5. Resolvent covariance is the same statement before diagonalization

Define the primitive endpoint resolvent

\[
\mathscr R_d(u)
:=-\frac{\Psi_d'(u)}{\Psi_d(u)}
=\sum_{s\ge1}Z_s^{\rm prim}(d)u^{s-1}
\]

near `u=0`.

Differentiate the exact polynomial pullback identity

\[
\operatorname{Monic}(\Psi_d(R_n(u)))
=\prod_{e\in\mathcal F_n(d)}\Psi_e(u).
\]

The constant monic sign disappears under logarithmic differentiation, giving

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{e\in\mathcal F_n(d)}\mathscr R_e(u)
=
R_n'(u)\,\mathscr R_d(R_n(u)).
}
\tag{JEC-7}

Coefficient extraction from (JEC-7) produces a triangular transformation among the ordinary reciprocal moments.  Passing from the `Z_s` basis to the Jordan basis diagonalizes that transformation, yielding (JEC-1)--(JEC-4).

For example, since

\[
R_n(u)=n^2u-\frac{n^2(n^2-1)}{12}u^2+O(u^3),
\]

the first two coefficients of (JEC-7) are

\[
\sum_e Z_1^{\rm prim}(e)=n^2Z_1^{\rm prim}(d),
\]

\[
\sum_e Z_2^{\rm prim}(e)
=n^4Z_2^{\rm prim}(d)
-\frac{n^2(n^2-1)}6Z_1^{\rm prim}(d),
\]

which is exactly equivalent to the diagonal Jordan formulas after substituting (JEC-3).

## 6. Generalized Jordan interpolation

The prime-local proof extends formally to the generalized Jordan function

\[
J_z(d)=d^z\prod_{p\mid d}(1-p^{-z})
\]

whenever a coefficient field/branch for `d^z` is chosen.  Then

\[
\sum_{e\in\mathcal F_n(d)}J_z(e)=n^zJ_z(d).
\]

For the native finite algebra, the integer exponents `k>=1` are sufficient and require no analytic branch choice.  The complex-`z` extension is therefore a later analytic compatibility layer, not primitive state.
