## Theorem
For this δ=1/4 model, O4(LR) contains a nonempty relatively open three-dimensional subset of **each of the 72 facets** of H. It also contains a nonempty four-dimensional open subset of H.

### General analytic rank lemma
Fix k distinct LR observation keys. Permit independent behavioral randomization over the available actions at each selected key; keep all other keys deterministic. Since a finite acyclic stopping trajectory meets each key at most once, the four-dimensional behavioral map W is separately affine in each selected key's action simplex, hence multiaffine on the product of these simplices.

If all deterministic corner profiles have their W in an exposed dimension-d face F, then the whole simplex-product maps into F by product-weight convex combinations. At one corner, let the difference vectors to the alternative action at each key together have rank d, with exactly d independent local simplex coordinates. These are the Jacobian columns. A nonzero d×d minor at the corner is a nonzero polynomial; it remains nonzero at nearby interior parameters. The inverse function/submersion theorem within aff(F) yields a relatively open d-dimensional subset of F in the actual behavioral image. This applies to all real action probabilities, including interior points arbitrarily near boundary actions. No convexity of O4(LR) is assumed.
