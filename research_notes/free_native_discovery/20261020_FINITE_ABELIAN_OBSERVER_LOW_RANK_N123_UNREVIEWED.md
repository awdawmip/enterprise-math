## Definition
For n>=1, seek the smallest finite abelian group A generated by x_1,...,x_n such that each permutation of the generators is implemented by some automorphism of A, and the labels 0, ±x_1,...,±x_n are pairwise different.

## New theorem, complete proofs for n=1,2,3
`m(1)=3, m(2)=8, m(3)=9.`

**n=1.** Signed separation needs at least three elements; Z/3 with x_1=1 attains this.

**n=2 lower bound.** Signed separation requires |A|>=5. For |A|=5,6,7, every abelian group is cyclic. The automorphism exchanging x_1,x_2 has order 2 (its square fixes the generating pair) and is nonidentity. Each of the three cyclic groups has a unique nonidentity automorphism of order dividing 2: inversion. Thus x_2=-x_1, contradicting signed separation. Hence |A|>=8. In Z/8 take x_1=1, x_2=3. Multiplication by 3 is an involutive automorphism interchanging the generators and the signed labels 0,1,7,3,5 are distinct. Equality follows.

**n=3 lower bound.** Let pi: Z^3 -> A send e_i to x_i, and K=ker(pi). Its S_3-invariance follows because generator permutations descend to A. Write r=ord(x_1-x_2)>=2. For v in K, transposing i,j yields (v_i-v_j)(e_i-e_j) in K, so r divides all differences v_i-v_j. Consequently K is contained in M_r={v in Z^3: all v_i equal modulo r}, an index-r^2 sublattice. Thus |A|=r^2[M_r:K]. If r>=3, |A|>=9. If r=2, put M=M_2, k=[M:K]. Signed separation implies 2e_i not in K, so k>=2. If k=2, then psi:M -> M/K=Z/2 would be permutation invariant because Aut(Z/2) is trivial. All c=psi(2e_i) are equal. As u=(1,1,1) lies in M and 2u=sum_i 2e_i, one gets 0=3c=c in Z/2. This forces 2e_i in K, contradiction. Hence k>=3 and |A|>=4*3=12 for r=2; in all cases |A|>=9.

**n=3 attainment.** Let A=F_3^2 and x_1=(1,0), x_2=(0,1), x_3=(-1,-1). Every pair is linearly independent, and their unique relation x_1+x_2+x_3=0 is permutation invariant; thus all six permutations extend to invertible linear maps. The seven labels 0, ±x_1, ±x_2, ±x_3 are distinct. So m(3)=9.

The author proof from 2026-10-18 gives m(n)=2^n for even n>=4 and m(n)=3*2^(n-1) for odd n>=5; that separate proof is **not recertified here**. The odd formula does not extend down to n=3 because the sharp n=3 witness has difference order r=3, below the r=2 branch's minimum 12.
