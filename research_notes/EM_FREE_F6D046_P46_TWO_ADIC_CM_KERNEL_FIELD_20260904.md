## 1. Splitting of two in the real quadratic subfield

The maximal real subfield is

\[
F^+=\mathbf Q(x),
\qquad x^2+5x-98=0,
\qquad\operatorname{disc}(F^+)=417\equiv1\pmod8.
\]

Hence two splits in \(F^+\). The two roots modulo eight are

\[
\boxed{x\equiv5,6\pmod8,}
\]

and the derivative \(2x+5\) is odd at both roots, so each lifts uniquely to \(\mathbf Q_2\).

The relative quadratic extension \(F/F^+\) is generated by a root of

\[
T^2-xT+49,
\]

whose discriminant is

\[
\Delta=x^2-196=-5x-98.
\]

At the root \(x\equiv5\pmod8\),

\[
\Delta\equiv5\pmod8,
\]

so the corresponding local factor is the unramified quadratic extension

\[
\mathbf Q_2(\sqrt5).
\]

The norm of the two relative discriminants is

\[
N_{F^+/\mathbf Q}(\Delta)=4704=2^5\cdot3\cdot7^2.
\]

Its square class is \(6\). Dividing by the square class \(5\) of the unramified factor leaves square class \(30=-2\cdot(-15)\), and \(-15\equiv1\pmod8\) is a two-adic square. Thus the second local factor is

\[
\mathbf Q_2(\sqrt{-2}),
\]

with discriminant exponent three. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
F\otimes\mathbf Q_2
\simeq
\mathbf Q_2(\sqrt{-2})\times\mathbf Q_2(\sqrt5).
}
\]

The first factor is ramified with residue field \(\mathbf F_2\); the second is unramified with residue field \(\mathbf F_4\).

The Prym polarization kernel is a two-dimensional symplectic \(\mathbf F_2\)-space,

\[

\[

\[
\boxed{

Thus \(K_\lambda\) is canonically one-dimensional over its Frobenius spectral field \(\mathbf F_4\).

## 3. Three principalizations as F4 nonzero elements

Because \(\mathbf F_2^\times=\{1\}\), the three projective lines in the two-dimensional \(\mathbf F_2\)-space are exactly its three nonzero vectors. Under the spectral-field identification,

\[
\boxed{\mathbf P(K_\lambda)=K_\lambda\setminus\{0\}=\mathbf F_4^\times.}

## 4. What is and is not proved

This bridge is canonical at the level of isomorphism type and Frobenius module. It does not yet prove that the full maximal order \(\mathcal O_F\otimes\mathbf Z_2\) acts integrally on the specialized Prym lattice or that \(K_\lambda\) is literally the reduction of a rank-one module over the unramified local factor. That stronger statement requires the actual integral endomorphism order and Rosati-stable lattice.

Nevertheless, it isolates the only plausible local support: any maximal-order-stable realization of the two-dimensional irreducible kernel must occur through the unramified factor with residue \(\mathbf F_4\), not through the ramified \(\mathbf F_2\) factor.
