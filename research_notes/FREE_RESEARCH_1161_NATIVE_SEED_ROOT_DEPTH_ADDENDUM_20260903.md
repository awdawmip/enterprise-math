## First true exact-lift barrier

The first geometric-mean update is

\[
\boxed{b_1=\sqrt{a_0b_0}=2^{-1/4}.}
\]

Let `F_line` be the field generated over `Q` by all established integer-trace sector lengths using finitely many rational field operations. Every finite expression in `F_line` belongs to some field

\[
E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{N_1},\ldots,\sqrt{N_k}),
\]

with positive integers `N_i` arising from integer Pythagorean norms.

After squarefree reduction, `E/Q` is a finite multiquadratic extension. Its Galois group is an elementary abelian 2-group, hence abelian, so every intermediate field is Galois over `Q`.

If `2^{1/4}` belonged to `E`, then

\[
\mathbb Q(2^{1/4})\subseteq E
\]

would be an intermediate field and therefore Galois over `Q`. But `Q(2^{1/4})` is real and does not contain the nonreal conjugates `± i 2^{1/4}` of `x^4-2`, hence it is not Galois.

Contradiction. Therefore

\[
\boxed{2^{1/4}\notin F_{line}},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{b_1=2^{-1/4}\notin F_{line}}.
\]

This is stronger than the rational-carrier obstruction: **even arbitrary finite rational combinations of all current integer-trace native lengths cannot reproduce the first new geometric mean.**
