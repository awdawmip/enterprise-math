## 2. The rational-turn group has an exact prime-orientation decomposition

Recall the native rational-turn group

\[
\mathcal T_{\mathbf Q}=\mathbf Q(i)^\times/\mathbf Q_{>0}^\times.
\]

Positive rational scale is erased, orientation is not.

For every rational prime

\[
p\equiv1\pmod4,
\]
choose once and for all one Gaussian prime \(\pi_p\) above \(p\), so

\[
p=\pi_p\bar\pi_p.
\]

Let

\[
\tau=[1+i].
\]

### Theorem 2.1 — prime-orientation decomposition

There is a noncanonical-coordinate but exact group isomorphism

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_{\mathbf Q}
\cong
C_8\oplus
\bigoplus_{p\equiv1(4)}\mathbf Z.
}
\]

The \(C_8\) factor is generated by \(\tau\); the free \(\mathbf Z\)-coordinate for a split prime \(p\) is generated by \([\pi_p]\).

#### Proof

Every nonzero Gaussian rational has a unique factorization with integer valuations

\[
z=u(1+i)^a
\prod_{p\equiv1(4)}\pi_p^{\alpha_p}\bar\pi_p^{\beta_p}
\prod_{q\equiv3(4)}q^{\gamma_q},
\]

with unit \(u=i^j\), \(j\in\mathbf Z/4\mathbf Z\), and finite support.

In the positive-rational quotient:

1. every inert rational prime \(q\equiv3\pmod4\) vanishes because \(q>0\);
2. for every split prime,
   \[
   [\bar\pi_p]=[\pi_p]^{-1}
   \]
   because \(\pi_p\bar\pi_p=p>0\);
3. units are generated by \(\tau\), since
   \[
   \tau^2=[(1+i)^2]=[2i]=[i].
   \]
   Hence \(\tau^4=[-1]\), \(\tau^8=1\).

The order of \(\tau\) is exactly eight. If \((1+i)^a\) were a positive rational, then \(a\) would be even, say \(a=2m\), and

\[
(1+i)^{2m}=2^m i^m
\]

would be positive only when \(m\equiv0\pmod4\), i.e. \(a\equiv0\pmod8\).

Thus every class has a representation

\[
\tau^\varepsilon
\prod_{p\equiv1(4)}[\pi_p]^{n_p},
\qquad
\varepsilon\in\mathbf Z/8\mathbf Z,
\quad
n_p\in\mathbf Z,
\]

with finite support.

For uniqueness, suppose this product is a positive rational. Comparing the two Gaussian valuations above each split prime forces every \(n_p=0\). The remaining \(\tau^\varepsilon\) is positive rational only for \(\varepsilon=0\pmod8\). ∎

### 2.2 Signature

Define the exact finite-support signature

\[
\boxed{
\Sigma(z)=
\left(
\varepsilon(z)\bmod8,
(n_p(z))_{p\equiv1(4)}
\right),
}
\]

where

\[
n_p(z)=v_{\pi_p}(z)-v_{\bar\pi_p}(z),
\]

and if the unit in a Gaussian factorization is \(i^j\),

\[
\varepsilon(z)=v_{1+i}(z)+2j\pmod8.
\]

This is independent of the chosen factorization presentation because positive rationals have zero signature.

Multiplication becomes addition:

\[
\boxed{
\Sigma(zw)=\Sigma(z)+\Sigma(w).
}
\]

So the multi-correction endpoint problem is already linear over an integer lattice plus one mod-eight coordinate.

## 3. Reciprocal generators have no inert-prime coordinates

For a reciprocal generator

\[
U_D=[D+i],\qquad D\in\mathbf N_{>0},
\]

its Gaussian norm is

\[
N(D+i)=D^2+1.
\]

If an odd prime \(q\equiv3\pmod4\) divided \(D^2+1\), then

\[
D^2\equiv-1\pmod q,
\]

making \(-1\) a quadratic residue modulo \(q\), impossible for \(q\equiv3\pmod4\).

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
D^2+1
\text{ has prime support only at }2
\text{ and }p\equiv1\pmod4.
}
\]

Thus each reciprocal turn has a finite signature supported exactly on the split primes appearing in \(D^2+1\), plus the torsion coordinate.

This is a useful native sparsity theorem: no analytic angle is needed to know which prime-orientation coordinates a reciprocal turn can move.

## 4. Exact multi-correction endpoint theorem

Take finitely many distinct denominators \(D_1,\ldots,D_m\) and signed integer coefficients \(c_1,\ldots,c_m\). The signed reciprocal-turn word is

\[
W=\prod_{j=1}^m U_{D_j}^{c_j}.
\]

Negative \(c_j\) means the inverse turn \([D_j-i]\).

Write

\[
\Sigma(U_{D_j})
=
(\varepsilon_j,(n_{p,j})_p).
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — affine valuation-lattice certificate

The exact endpoint identity

\[
\boxed{W=\tau}
\]

holds in \(\mathcal T_{\mathbf Q}\) iff

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=1}^m c_j n_{p,j}=0
\quad
\text{for every split prime }p\equiv1\pmod4,
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=1}^m c_j\varepsilon_j\equiv1\pmod8.
}
\]

This is immediate from Theorem 2.1, but it changes the search problem qualitatively: exact multi-correction Machin words are **affine integer-lattice points**, not nonlinear multiplicative guesses.

Equivalently introduce an integer slack variable \(h\) and write

\[
\sum_jc_j\varepsilon_j-8h=1.
\]

Then all endpoint conditions are ordinary integer linear equations.

Freeze:

`MULTI_CORRECTION_ENDPOINT_SEARCH = FINITE_SUPPORT_INTEGER_AFFINE_LATTICE_SEARCH`.

### 4.2 Difference lattice

If \(c\) and \(c'\) are two endpoint certificates, then their difference lies in the homogeneous relation lattice:

\[
\sum_j(c_j-c'_j)n_{p,j}=0,
\]

\[
\sum_j(c_j-c'_j)\varepsilon_j\equiv0\pmod8.
\]

Thus families of Machin-type identities form affine cosets of an exact relation lattice. Support-minimal relations can be analyzed using signed-circuit ideas after the arithmetic signature matrix has been declared; this note does **not** promote a new global circuit-tool family.

## 5. The four Størmer formulas become tiny lattice certificates

Choose

\[
\pi_5=2+i,
\qquad
\pi_{13}=3+2i.
\]

Use coordinates

\[
(\varepsilon,n_5,n_{13}).
\]

The relevant reciprocal generators factor as follows.

### 5.1 Denominator 2

\[
2+i=\pi_5,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\Sigma(U_2)=(0,1,0).}
\]

### 5.2 Denominator 3

\[
3+i=(1+i)(2-i)=(1+i)\bar\pi_5,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\Sigma(U_3)=(1,-1,0).}
\]

### 5.3 Denominator 7

Since

\[
7+i=(1+i)(-i)(2+i)^2,
\]

and \([-i]=\tau^6\),

\[
\boxed{\Sigma(U_7)=(7,2,0).}
\]

### 5.4 Denominator 5

\[
5+i=(1+i)(3-2i)=(1+i)\bar\pi_{13},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\Sigma(U_5)=(1,0,-1).}
\]

### 5.5 Denominator 239

First

\[
(3-2i)^4=-119-120i.
\]

Hence

\[
239+i=(1+i)i(3-2i)^4,
\]

and \([i]=\tau^2\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{\Sigma(U_{239})=(3,0,-4).}
\]

Now all four classical two-term identities are one-line signature cancellations:

#### Euler

\[
\Sigma(U_2)+\Sigma(U_3)
=(1,0,0)=\Sigma(\tau).
\]

#### Hermann

\[
2\Sigma(U_2)-\Sigma(U_7)
=(-7,0,0)\equiv(1,0,0)\pmod8.
\]

#### Hutton

\[
2\Sigma(U_3)+\Sigma(U_7)
=(9,0,0)\equiv(1,0,0)\pmod8.
\]

#### Machin

\[
4\Sigma(U_5)-\Sigma(U_{239})
=(1,0,0).
\]

So the apparently different formulas are all exact cancellations of split-prime orientation coordinates, leaving precisely one diagonal torsion unit.

## 6. Gauss's three-term formula is the first genuinely two-prime lattice example

Consider the classical Gauss identity

\[
\frac\pi4
=12\arctan\frac1{18}
+8\arctan\frac1{57}
-5\arctan\frac1{239}.
\]

At the native endpoint layer it is enough to verify the signatures.

### 6.1 Denominator 18

A direct Gaussian factorization is

\[
18+i
=i(2-i)^2(3-2i)
=i\bar\pi_5^2\bar\pi_{13},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\Sigma(U_{18})=(2,-2,-1).}
\]

### 6.2 Denominator 57

A direct factorization is

\[
57+i
=(1+i)(-i)(2+i)^3(3-2i),
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\Sigma(U_{57})=(7,3,-1).}
\]

Together with

\[
\Sigma(U_{239})=(3,0,-4),
\]

one obtains

\[
12(-2)+8(3)-5(0)=0
\]

in the \(p=5\) coordinate,

\[
12(-1)+8(-1)-5(-4)=0
\]

in the \(p=13\) coordinate, and

\[
12(2)+8(7)-5(3)=65\equiv1\pmod8.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
U_{18}^{12}U_{57}^{8}U_{239}^{-5}=\tau
}
\]

is certified entirely by integer prime-orientation coordinates.

Only after this native endpoint identity is fixed does an arctangent readout yield the displayed Gauss formula, subject to the winding lift below.

This example demonstrates why the valuation lattice is genuinely multi-correction machinery: two independent split-prime coordinates are canceled simultaneously.

## 8. A genuine no-go for cost-free “canonical shortest” language

The affine lattice supplies exact endpoint feasibility, but it does not select an optimization objective.

This distinction is not philosophical; two natural objectives already disagree.

### Theorem 8.1 — unique shortest reciprocal word by native letter count

Restrict to generators \(U_D^{\pm1}\) with \(D\ge2\), and define native word length by the number of reciprocal-generator letters.

There is no length-one word equal to \(\tau\). The unique unordered length-two positive solution is

\[
\boxed{U_2U_3=\tau.}
\]

#### Proof

For two positive letters, suppose

\[
(a+i)(b+i)=\lambda(1+i),
\qquad \lambda>0.
\]

Equality of real and imaginary coordinates gives

\[
ab-1=a+b,
\]

or

\[
(a-1)(b-1)=2.
\]

For integers \(a,b\ge2\), this forces

\[
\{a,b\}=\{2,3\}.
\]

For one positive and one inverse letter,

\[
(a+i)(b-i)=(ab+1)+i(b-a).
\]

If the imaginary coordinate is positive then \(b-a<b\), while \(ab+1> b\); hence it cannot lie on the positive diagonal. Two inverse letters have negative imaginary direction. ∎

Thus the Euler factorization is the unique native shortest word in this letter-count model.

### 8.2 But the analytic completion cost ranks it oppositely

The previous continuation proved that, among the complete four Størmer two-term identities, original Machin

\[
4\arctan(1/5)-\arctan(1/239)
\]

strictly dominates Euler/Hermann/Hutton for every fixed total alternating-series term budget in the declared robust remainder model.

So:

\[
\boxed{
\text{native shortest} = \text{Euler }(2,3),
}
\]

whereas

\[
\boxed{
\text{completion-fastest among the Størmer four}
=\text{Machin }(5,239).
}
\]

Therefore exact endpoint structure alone does not determine a canonical notion of “shortest” or “best”. Any optimization theorem must declare what resource is being minimized.

Freeze:

`EXACT_ENDPOINT_LATTICE != CANONICAL_COST_FUNCTION`.

`NATIVE_WORD_LENGTH != ANALYTIC_COMPLETION_COST`.

This also explains why longer multi-term identities can improve Lehmer's completion measure: extra native factorization complexity can buy smaller analytic parameters.

## 10. New finite-search formulation

For a finite denominator cap \(D\le H\):

1. factor each \(D+i\) in \(\mathbf Z[i]\);
2. build its finite signature column \(\Sigma(U_D)\);
3. solve
   \[
   Ac=0,
   \qquad
   \varepsilon\cdot c-8h=1;
   \]
4. retain the exact signed word and finite fan winding certificate;
5. only afterward evaluate completion-layer costs such as
   \[
   \|c\|_1,
   \quad
   \max D,
   \quad
   \sum_D\frac{1_{c_D\ne0}}{\log_{10}D},
   \quad
   E_T^*(c).
   \]

This is a finite, exact, target-free search architecture. The numerical value of \(\pi\) is never needed to generate a candidate identity.

The integer endpoint equations also admit exact Smith/Hermite normal-form treatment if a future executable checker is warranted. That would be an implementation choice, not an additional native axiom.
