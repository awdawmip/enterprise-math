## Definitions

Let irrational `0<θ<1`, `m,H≥1`, `K=m+H−1`. The historical circle intervals are generated by phases `{zθ}`, `0≤z<m`, and remain labelled by z. For phase x define `A_H(x)[z]` to be the least `r∈{1,…,H}` such that `{x−rθ}` lies in interval z, or empty. This is the **entire labelled vector**, not a scalar count.

R49–R50 show that `A_H(x)` is constant on each right-half-open cell between cuts `{sθ}`, `0≤s≤K`. Let s_r be the index of the r-th largest positive phase among `1≤s≤K`, so that s_1 is R50's last upper Farey record.

Assume a certified rational enclosure `L/D<θ<U/D`, with `0<L<U<D`. Put `M=2D` and `B=L+U`, so the rational midpoint is `β=B/M`.

## Theorem A: a single Stern–Brocot state certifies the whole phase order

Execute the bounded Stern–Brocot neighboring-fraction algorithm on β until `i+j>K`, obtaining `a/i<β<c/j`, `ci−aj=1`. Let `u=iB−aM>0` and `v=cM−jB>0`.

**If `min(u,v)>K(U−L)`, then for every integer `1≤n≤K` and every `α∈[L/D,U/D]`, `floor(nα)=floor(nβ)`. The entire strict sorted order of the phases `{sα}`, `0≤s≤K`, is consequently constant throughout the enclosure.**

*Proof.* The two neighboring vectors are a unimodular basis. If an integer p/q satisfies `1≤q≤K<i+j` and `p/q<β`, the lower one-sided best-approximation inequality gives `qβ−p≥u/M`. If `p/q>β`, the upper inequality gives `p−qβ≥v/M`. For example write `(p,q)=k(a,i)+l(c,j)`; positive upper error with k≤0 forces l≥1 and error≥v, while k,l≥1 forces q≥i+j. The lower case is symmetric. Thus the distance from nβ to **any** integer is ≥min(u,v)/M. Perturbing β to α changes nβ by at most n(U−L)/M≤K(U−L)/M, strictly less than that distance; no integer crossing occurs. For distinct phase indices s,t, reversing their fractional order would force the signed multiple (s−t)α to cross an integer, also excluded for |s−t|≤K. QED.

The certificate has two positive residuals, a determinant and a bound; unlike a full cut atlas it does not enumerate K phases. One bounded continued-fraction/Euclidean run constructs it.

## Theorem B: exact rank and labelled select from Euclidean Floor Sums

For the midpoint β define `g_s=M−(sB mod M)`, `1≤s≤K`. Theorem A makes the g_s distinct and in `{1,…,M−1}`.

For `0≤t≤M`, define `C_n(t)=#{1≤s≤n:g_s≤t}`. Then exactly

`C_n(t)=Σ_(s=1)^n floor((sB+t)/M) − Σ_(s=1)^n floor(sB/M).`  (1)

Both sums are evaluated by the classical Euclidean Floor Sum recurrence without enumerating s. Then

`t_r=min{t∈{1,…,M}:C_K(t)≥r}`,  (2)

`s_r=min{n∈{1,…,K}:C_n(t_r)−C_n(t_r−1)=1}`.  (3)

*Proof.* For each s the difference between the two floors in (1) is 1 precisely when `(sB mod M)+t≥M`, equivalently `g_s≤t`. Thus C is an exact monotone rank oracle. Under Theorem A the g_s are pairwise distinct; binary search on t finds the unique r-th threshold, while prefix-count binary search isolates its sole contributing s. The common order certificate transfers this source identity from β to every θ in the enclosure. QED.

Euclidean Floor Sum uses O(log M) quotient iterations on integers of O(log M+2log K) bits. For any fixed requested r, integer-threshold and prefix binary searches use O(log M+log K) rank evaluations. Enumerating R rightmost labels separately costs O(R(log M+log K)log M) quotient iterations, *plus* enclosure construction, historical source-age classifications, labels/output and validation. This is an exact sublinear-in-K route for fixed R at fixed precision; requesting all K labels necessarily costs Ω(K) output.

## Theorem C: lazy maximal labelled terminal plateau

Compute s_1,s_2,… from Theorem B, and inspect the **complete** vectors `A_H({s_r θ})` in descending-cut order. If the first vector different from `V*=A_H({s_1 θ})` appears at rank r, the maximal terminal contiguous plateau begins at the cut indexed by `s_(r−1)`. Its transfer threshold is exactly

`q_A=ceil(s_(r−1)θ)−s_(r−1)θ`.

*Proof.* Right-half-open cell constancy from R49–R50 means each cut's vector is that of its cell immediately to the right. Traversal is in exact descending cut order and stops precisely at the first differing predecessor; thus the preceding cut is the left boundary of the largest terminal suffix on which the vector equals V*. Every source-to-history comparison at such a cut is controlled by multiples with absolute index ≤m+H−1=K; Theorem A certifies its stability across the enclosing interval. QED.
