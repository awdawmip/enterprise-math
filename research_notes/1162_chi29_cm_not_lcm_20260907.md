## 1. Main result

Let χ(n)=(n/29), the Legendre character modulo 29, and define, for t,z>0,

F(t)=sum_{n>=1} χ(n) exp(-nt)
    =A(exp(-t))/(1-exp(-29t)),    A(X)=sum_{a=1}^{28} χ(a)X^a,

g(z)=F(sqrt(z))/sqrt(z),    h(z)=g(z)/6.

Then:

1. h(0+)=1, and h is strictly completely monotone on (0,infinity).
2. h is not logarithmically completely monotone.
3. For every real 0<r<=1/60, h^r is not completely monotone. In particular h^(1/64) is not a positive-measure Laplace transform.
4. The probability law with Laplace transform h cannot be an m-fold convolution of identical probability laws on [0,infinity) for any integer m>=60.
5. These facts do not obstruct the previous integer polygon-refinement identities: equal convolution roots and refinement with holonomy sectors are different operations.

Here CM means (-1)^k h^(k)(z)>=0 for k>=0. LCM means (-1)^k (log h)^(k)(z)>=0 for k>=1.

## 2. Uniform positive theta certificate

Define

theta(u)=sum_{n>=1} χ(n) exp(-pi*n^2*u/29).

For u>=1 put v=exp(-pi*u/29). We have 0<v<9/10. For example pi>31/10 and the exact inequality 1+31/290+(31/290)^2/2>10/9 prove this bound.

The first five character values are +1,-1,-1,+1,+1. Consequently

theta(u)/v >= f(v)-v^35/(1-v^13),
f(v)=1-v^3-v^8+v^15+v^24.

The tail estimate follows from (6+j)^2-1>=35+13j. It is an absolute bound and does not discard the remaining character signs as positive mass.

Let a=9/10 and C=-8+15a^7+24a^16. Exact rational arithmetic gives C>0 and

f'(v)=v^2[-3+v^5(-8+15v^7+24v^16)]
     <=v^2[-3+a^5 C]<0       (0<v<=a).

In fact -3+a^5 C=-107677651703587922373/125000000000000000000<0. Also

f(a)=126190365171521509863361/10^24 > 1/10,

a^35/(1-a^13)
=2503155504993241601315571986085849/74581341716710000000000000000000000
<1/25.

Thus theta(u)>(3/50) exp(-pi*u/29) for every u>=1.

For completeness the sign of the theta-duality factor need not be assumed. Finite Fourier inversion gives
sum_{a mod 29} χ(a) exp(2pi*i*m*a/29)=χ(m)*tau,
where tau is the Gauss sum. Applying Poisson summation to the Gaussian gives
theta(u)=(tau/sqrt(29))*u^(-1/2)*theta(1/u).
The already-proved theta(1)>0 forces tau/sqrt(29)=1. Hence

theta(u)=u^(-1/2)*theta(1/u),

and, for 0<u<1,

theta(u)>(3/50)u^(-1/2)exp(-pi/(29u)).

This is a global proof, not a finite-grid positivity test.

## 3. Positive probability carrier and strict complete monotonicity

The elementary Gaussian Laplace integral is

exp(-n sqrt(z))/sqrt(z)
=(1/sqrt(pi))*integral_0^infinity s^(-1/2) exp(-zs-n^2/(4s)) ds.

For z>0 absolute convergence permits summation and integration to be interchanged. Therefore

h(z)=integral_0^infinity exp(-zs) mu(s) ds,

mu(s)=1/(6sqrt(pi*s))*theta(29/(4pi*s))>0.

The character sums sum χ(a)=sum aχ(a)=0 and sum a^2χ(a)=348 give g(0+)=348/(2*29)=6. Hence mu has total mass one. Theta duality gives exponential decay of mu as s tends to infinity, and its defining series gives exp(-1/(4s)) decay at zero. In particular differentiation under the integral is valid, and

(-1)^k h^(k)(z)=integral s^k exp(-zs)mu(s)ds>0.

This proves item 1 of the theorem.

## 5. A whole interval of forbidden fractional powers

Define

C(r)=(-1)^39 z0^39/[38! h(z0)^r]*(d^39/dz^39 h(z)^r)|_(z=z0).

Let ell_j be the Taylor coefficients of log h(z0+w)-log h(z0). Then

C(r)=(-1)^39*39*z0^39*[w^39] exp(r*sum_{j=1}^{39}ell_j*w^j)
    =sum_{k=1}^{39} b_k r^k.

The same interval computation proves b_1=C_log<0 and b_k>0 for every 2<=k<=39. Thus Q(r)=C(r)/r is strictly increasing for r>0. Exact interval arithmetic proves

Q(1/60)<-0.000106<0.

It follows that C(r)<0 for every 0<r<=1/60. This proves items 3 and 4. A dyadic example has the certified enclosure

## 6. Divisibility set, and what remains unknown

Let D_29={r>=0 : h^r is CM}. Then 0,1,2,... belong to D_29, D_29 is closed, and r,s in D_29 imply r+s in D_29.

The product assertion follows from multiplication of Laplace transforms or Leibniz' rule. Closedness follows because each derivative sign condition for h^r is continuous in r.

The exclusion (0,1/60] implies that D_29 has a smallest positive element r_* with

rho_39 <= r_* <=1, and in particular r_*>1/60.

No claim is made that D_29 is an interval above r_*, that r_*=rho_39, that h^(1/2) is CM, or that every m<60 is permitted. Arbitrary unequal convolution factorizations are also not ruled out.

## 7. Arithmetic content retained despite non-divisibility

Let S have density mu. For real p>1, positivity and the Mellin integral give

L(p,χ29)=3 Gamma((p+1)/2)/Gamma(p) * E[S^(-(p+1)/2)].

In particular, for n>=1,

L(2n+1,χ29)=3 n!/(2n)! * E[S^(-n-1)].

Thus positive moment methods and Hankel positivity remain valid for the odd L-values even though arbitrary positive convolution subdivision fails. This separates positive-measure certification from infinite divisibility.
