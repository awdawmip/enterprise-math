The **relative canonical-cover map** \`Spec(C_h^can) -> Spec(R_h)\` studied here is distinct from the already computed polynomial-source map \`Spec(C_h^can) -> Spec(R)\` and from the original observer map \`Spec(R_h) -> Spec(R)\`. Thus its flatness is a new question not decided by the source-relative discriminants.

Let \`K\` be any field, \`h>=2\`, \`g=gcd(h,2)\`, \`r=h/g\`, \`R=K[U0,U1,U2,U3]\`, \`A=U1*U2\`, \`B=U0*U3\`. Within the paired ring

\`\`\`text
D_h=R[z0,z1]/(z0^h-B,z1^h-A),
V_j=z0^j*z1^(h-j)   (1<=j<h),
R_h=R[V1,...,V_(h-1)] = D_h^(mu_h),
C_h^can=D_h^(mu_g).
\`\`\`

Here \`mu_d\` is the diagonalizable group scheme assigning character one to both \`z0,z1\`; it is treated as a group scheme, including when \`char(K)\` divides \`d\`. The prior lattice theorem establishes \`[Frac(C_h^can):Frac(R_h)]=r\`.

## Theorem: flatness, torsors, special fibers and socles

For every field \`K\` and integer \`h>=2\`:

1. **Away from the joint-zero locus:** On each of \`D(A)\` and \`D(B)\` in \`Spec(R_h)\`, the map \`C_h^can/R_h\` is a finite free rank-\`r\` Kummer algebra. More precisely there are compatible presentations
   \`\`\`text
   C_h^can[A^(-1)] ~= R_h[A^(-1)][w]/(w^r-A),   w=z1^g,
   C_h^can[B^(-1)] ~= R_h[B^(-1)][v]/(v^r-B),   v=z0^g.
   \`\`\`
   Each map is a \`mu_r\`-torsor for the fppf topology. It is finite étale there exactly when \`char(K)\` does not divide \`r\`. In characteristic dividing \`r\`, the displayed torsor remains finite flat, and its generic field extension is inseparable.
2. **Geometric fiber at the joint-zero locus:** For every scheme point \`s in Spec(R_h)\` with \`A,B in s\`, put \`kappa=kappa(s)\` and \`m=(z0,z1)\`. There is a canonical (exponent-graded) algebra isomorphism
   \`\`\`text
   C_h^can tensor_(R_h) kappa
       ~= [kappa[z0,z1]/m^h]^(mu_g).
   \`\`\`
   The residue-fiber length is
   \`\`\`text
   L(h)= h(h+1)/2, if h is odd;
         (h/2)^2, if h is even.
   \`\`\`
   Its Hilbert series for total \`z\`-degree is \`sum_(0<=d<h, g|d) (d+1)t^d\`. It is an Artin local algebra with socle dimension \`h\` if \`h\` is odd and \`h-1\` if \`h\` is even (for \`h=2\`, dimension one).
3. **Exact flatness locus:** For \`h=2\`, \`C_h^can=R_h\`, hence the canonical-cover map is an isomorphism. For every \`h>=3\`, its flatness locus on \`Spec(R_h)\` is **exactly** \`D(A) union D(B)\`; the non-flat locus is the closed set \`V(A,B)\`. In particular, the finite generic degree \`r\` is strictly smaller than the fiber length at every point of \`V(A,B)\`.

The result has no characteristic restriction, including \`char(K)|h\`.

## Proof

**Step 1: construct the two local monogenic descriptions.** On \`D(A)\`, \`z1\` is invertible and \`w=z1^g\` belongs to \`C_h^can\`, with \`w^r=A\`. The source invariant \`V1=z0*z1^(h-1)\` satisfies \`V1*z1=A*z0\`.

If \`h\` is odd (\`g=1\`), \`w=z1\` and \`z0=V1*w/A\`. Thus the canonical cover \`C_h^can=D_h\` is generated over \`R_h[A^-1]\` by \`w\`.

If \`h=2k\` is even (\`g=2\`), the invariant algebra is generated by \`X=z0^2\`, \`Y=z0*z1\`, \`Z=z1^2=w\`, with \`Y^2=XZ\`. For \`k>=2\`,
\`\`\`text
V1 = Y*w^(k-1),    V2 = X*w^(k-1),
Y  = V1*w/A,       X  = V2*w/A.
\`\`\`
For \`k=1\` the generators already satisfy \`X=B, Z=A, Y=V1\`. In all cases \`C_h^can[A^-1]\` is generated by \`w\`, satisfying \`w^r=A\`.

The resulting surjective homomorphism
\`R_h[A^-1][w]/(w^r-A) -> C_h^can[A^-1]\`
has a free source of rank \`r\`. It is generically an isomorphism because the pinned lattice index gives degree \`r\`; its kernel is torsion inside a free module over the domain \`R_h[A^-1]\`, hence zero. Interchanging \`z0,z1\`, \`A,B\` and \`V_j,V_(h-j)\` gives the second presentation over \`D(B)\`.

Since \`A\`, respectively \`B\`, is invertible on these opens, \`w\`, respectively \`v\`, is a unit. The grading \`w -> w tensor t\`, \`t^r=1\`, identifies the Kummer algebra with the \`mu_r\`-torsor. The Jacobian derivative is \`r*w^(r-1)\`, so the displayed finite free algebra is étale exactly in characteristics not dividing \`r\`.

**Step 2: identify the fiber on \`V(A,B)\`.** For \`1<=j<h\`, the exact invariant identity
\`\`\`text
V_j^h=B^j*A^(h-j)
\`\`\`
holds in \`R_h\`. If \`A=B=0\` in the residue field of a scheme point, every \`V_j\` maps to zero (a field has no nonzero nilpotents). Thus
\`\`\`text
D_h tensor_(R_h) kappa
 = kappa[z0,z1]/
   (z0^h,z1^h, z0*z1^(h-1),...,z0^(h-1)*z1)
 = kappa[z0,z1]/(z0,z1)^h.
\`\`\`
The \`mu_g\`-invariants are the degree-zero direct summand of a \`Z/g\`-graded algebra. Because \`R_h\` lies in degree zero, taking this direct summand commutes with arbitrary \`R_h\`-base change, even for nonreduced group schemes and even in characteristic two. This gives the stated fiber isomorphism.

A homogeneous monomial \`z0^a*z1^b\` survives precisely when \`a,b>=0\`, \`a+b<h\`, and \`a+b=0 mod g\`. At total degree \`d\` there are \`d+1\` such monomials. If \`g=1\`, their total is \`sum_(d=0)^(h-1)(d+1)=h(h+1)/2\`; if \`g=2\`, with \`h=2k\`, the total is \`sum_(j=0)^(k-1)(2j+1)=k^2\`.

The graded fiber is local with maximal ideal its positive-degree part. Its top nonzero invariant degree is \`h-1\` for odd \`h\`, and \`h-2\` for even \`h\`. Every monomial there is killed by the positive-degree ideal, yielding socle dimensions \`h\` and \`h-1\` respectively. No lower homogeneous degree contributes: multiplication by \`z0^g\` remains injective into the next homogeneous piece until the top degree is reached. This proves the socle claims.

**Step 3: flatness locus.** On \`D(A) union D(B)\`, the local Kummer presentations establish freeness. At every point with \`A=B=0\`, the geometric fiber dimension is \`L(h)\`. The finite extension has generic rank \`r\`. A finite locally free module of rank \`r\` must have fiber dimension \`r\` at every point; for odd \`h>=3\`, \`h(h+1)/2>h=r\`, and for even \`h>=4\`, \`(h/2)^2>h/2=r\`. Hence no such point belongs to the flat locus. For \`h=2\`, \`r=1\` and \`C_h^can=R_h\` by the pinned even-height presentation. QED.

## Interpretation and admission boundary

This theorem locates the precise codimension-two base obstruction to *relative canonical-cover flatness*. It also distinguishes two separate ramification phenomena: \`Spec(C_h^can)->Spec(R_h)\` can be finite étale over \`D(A) union D(B)\` whenever the characteristic does not divide \`r=h/g\`, even when the *source-relative* cover \`Spec(C_h^can)->Spec(R)\` is nowhere étale because the characteristic divides \`h\` but not \`r\` (for example \`h=6\`, characteristic two).
