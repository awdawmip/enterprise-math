## Exact model and prior lemma

Let K be any field and h>=2, e|d|h, n=d/e, H=h/d. Put S=K[U_0,U_1,U_2,U_3] with standard grading deg U_i=1; A=U_0 U_3, B=U_1 U_2 and I=(A,B). In D_h=S[z_0,z_1]/(z_0^h-A,z_1^h-B), write C_d for the total-z-degree 0 (mod d) part and M_k for the total-z-degree ek (mod d) character piece, 0<=k<n. Let J_d be generated by all z_0^a z_1^(d-a), 0<=a<=d.

The previously proved normal form, at the *S-module* level, is
E_{k,c}:=M_k/J_d^c M_k ≅ ⊕_{q>=0} (S/I^{m_q})^{N_q}, c>=1,
where
m_q=max(0,ceil((c-q)/H)), r_q=ek+qd,
N_q=#{(a,b): 0<=a,b<h, a+b=r_q}.
Only finitely many q have m_q N_q nonzero.

## Theorem (complete minimal free resolution as an S-module)

For every field K and all parameters above, E_{k,c} has projective dimension exactly 2 over S. Its minimal (ungraded) S-free Betti numbers are

β_0(E)=Σ_{q:m_q>=1} N_q,
β_1(E)=Σ_{q:m_q>=1} N_q(m_q+1),
β_2(E)=Σ_{q:m_q>=1} N_q m_q.

Each S/I^m summand has a minimal standard-graded free resolution
0 -> S(-2m-2)^m -> S(-2m)^(m+1) -> S -> S/I^m -> 0,
where the first differential is the (m+1)×m bidiagonal Hilbert–Burch syzygy matrix with B in row j and -A in row j+1, j=0,...,m-1, and the second differential is the row
(A^m,A^(m-1)B,...,AB^(m-1),B^m).
The signs/column ordering can be chosen compatibly so the composition vanishes. Their direct sum is the minimal S-resolution of E_{k,c}.

The normal-form generator z_0^a z_1^b contributes an *S-grading shift* if one equips E with any compatible weighted grading. The displayed Hilbert series below uses only the intrinsic standard S grading and the choice of the normal-form coordinates as degree zero; it is not a claim that all z-generators have their original degree zero.

Under that intrinsic coordinate grading,

Hilb(E_{k,c};t) = [Σ_{q:m_q>=1} N_q {1-(m_q+1)t^(2m_q)+m_q t^(2m_q+2)}]/(1-t)^4.

Consequently E_{k,c} has Krull dimension 2, is Cohen–Macaulay as an S-module, and its standard S-graded multiplicity is
e_S(E_{k,c}) = 4 Σ_q N_q binom(m_q+1,2).
The multiplicity is four times the generic length at each of the four coordinate-plane components of V(A,B). These conclusions depend only on the S-module structure, not on the C_d-module action.

## Proof

1. A and B form an S-regular sequence because their variables are disjoint: A is a nonzerodivisor in S and B is a nonzerodivisor in S/(A). In particular I is a height-two complete-intersection ideal.

2. For any m>=1, I^m is generated by the m+1 homogeneous polynomials A^(m-j)B^j, j=0,...,m. Relations between adjacent generators are B g_j - A g_(j+1)=0. The (m+1)×m syzygy matrix with these adjacent pairs has maximal minors, up to sign, exactly these m+1 generators. Since the minors have grade 2, Hilbert–Burch gives the exact displayed length-two resolution of S/I^m. Every nonzero matrix entry lies in the homogeneous maximal ideal (U_0,U_1,U_2,U_3), so it is minimal in every characteristic.

3. Apply the pinned S-module decomposition of E to these finite summands and take their direct sum. All differentials remain in the same maximal ideal. This proves exactness, minimality and the Betti sums; q=0 has N_0=ek+1>=1 and m_0>=1, hence β_2>0 and pd_S(E)=2.

4. Alternating the graded free-module Hilbert series gives the rational formula. Auslander–Buchsbaum over regular S yields depth_S(E)=4-2=2. Each summand S/I^m has support V(I), of pure dimension 2; hence E has dimension 2 and is S-Cohen–Macaulay. The multiplicity for S/I^m is 4 binom(m+1,2): I has four generic coordinate-plane components, and at each component the length of S/I^m is binom(m+1,2), since I becomes a two-generated maximal ideal in the 2-dimensional regular local ring at that prime. Sum over N_q to conclude.

## Exact check and worked example

For h=d=4, e=1, k=0, c=3: H=1, (N_0,m_0)=(1,3), (N_1,m_1)=(3,2), all other m_q N_q=0. Thus
E_{0,3} ≅ S/I^3 ⊕ (S/I^2)^3,
(β_0,β_1,β_2)=(4,13,9),
Hilb(E;t)=(4-9t^4+2t^6+3t^8)/(1-t)^4,
e_S(E)=60.

The independent exact coefficient check enumerated all degree-j monomials in S, j=0,...,17, and directly tested membership in I^m for m=1,...,6; all 108 degree/multiplicity pairs agreed with the rational formula. A minimal checker:

```python
from math import comb
for m in range(1,7):
    for degree in range(18):
        observed=0
        for a in range(degree+1):
            for b in range(degree-a+1):
                for c in range(degree-a-b+1):
                    d=degree-a-b-c
                    in_I=any(a>=r and d>=r and b>=m-r and c>=m-r for r in range(m+1))
                    observed+=not in_I
        choose=lambda z: comb(z+3,3) if z>=0 else 0
        expected=choose(degree)-(m+1)*choose(degree-2*m)+m*choose(degree-2*m-2)
        assert observed==expected,(m,degree)
```

This check independently tests the S/I^m Hilbert numerator. The uniform resolution and E-decomposition are mathematical arguments, not inferences from finitely many cases.
