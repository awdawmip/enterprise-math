For any field K and integer h>=2, set g=gcd(h,2), r=h/g and

    D_h=K[U0,U1,U2,U3,z0,z1]/(z0^h-U0U3,z1^h-U1U2),
    R_h=(D_h)^(mu_h),    C_h^can=(D_h)^(mu_g),

where the mu-actions are exponent gradings (also valid in characteristics 2 or dividing h). The cover C_h^can/R_h has generic degree r, but may be nonflat on V(U0U3,U1U2). The assertions below concern normal toric rings and their Weil class groups.

## Theorem

For every K and h>=2,

    Cl(C_h^can) ≅ (Z/hZ)^2 ⊕ (Z/gZ),

where Z/1 is the trivial group. Thus:

    h odd:  Cl(C_h^can) ≅ (Z/h)^2  and |Cl(C_h^can)|=h^2;
    h even: Cl(C_h^can) ≅ (Z/h)^2 ⊕ Z/2 and |Cl(C_h^can)|=2h^2.

The canonical finite toric morphism Spec(C_h^can)->Spec(R_h) induces an exact sequence

    0 -> Z/rZ -> Cl(R_h) -> Cl(C_h^can) -> 0,

where Cl(R_h) ≅ (Z/h)^3 as proved in the preceding note. More precisely, if N_t (0<=t<h) denotes the weight-t rank-one character module from the earlier source, the kernel is exactly the cyclic subgroup generated by [N_(g mod h)] and has order r. In the toric character-lattice presentation it is the quotient of the even-weight (or unrestricted) exponent subgroup by the weight-zero subgroup. The result holds in arbitrary field characteristic; no assertion of etaleness is needed.

## Proof

**Semigroup lattices.** Set a=(1,0,0,1), b=(0,1,1,0), C=R_{≥0}^4. The exponent lattice for the full paired ring is

    L_D=Z^4+Z(a/h)+Z(b/h).

The nonnegative elements of L_D are generated by the four coordinate vectors e_i and the two nonnegative fractional vectors a/h,b/h. Indeed reduce the fractional coefficients to unique i,j in {0,...,h-1}; subtract (i a+j b)/h from any nonnegative lattice point to obtain an element of N^4. Hence D_h=K[C cap L_D] is a normal split toric ring even when h is divisible by char K.

For C_h^can, the invariant monomials have i+j divisible by g. The corresponding full lattice is

    L_g=Z^4 + { (i a+j b)/h : i,j in Z, i+j=0 mod g }.

For h even, the parity condition depends only on (i,j) modulo h; for h odd g=1 and there is no condition. The same fractional-representative argument proves C_h^can=K[C cap L_g], with invariant monomial representatives z0^i z1^j for 0<=i,j<h and g | (i+j).

**Toric boundary divisors.** Both L_D and L_g pair integrally with the primitive coordinate-facet normals h*epsilon_k. For odd h, z0 and z1 already have coordinate values 1/h; for even h, the invariant z0*z1 has coordinate value 1/h on all four coordinates, proving primitiveness also for C_h^can. Thus the standard toric Weil-divisor exact sequence is

    L_g -> Z^4 -> Cl(C_h^can) -> 0,
    x |-> h*x.

In particular

    Cl(C_h^can)=Z^4/(h Z^4 + H_g),

where H_g is the subgroup of Z^4 generated by all i a+j b with i+j divisible by g.

**Smith form without a numerical algorithm.** The four vectors (e0,e1,a,b) form an integral basis of Z^4 (determinant +/-1). Consequently the e0,e1 components survive independently with order h. The a,b quotient is

    Z^2 / (h Z^2 + {(i,j):i+j=0 mod g}).

When g=1 it is trivial. When g=2, h is even and the displayed subgroup is exactly the even-sum sublattice of index 2; adding h Z^2 does not change it. Its quotient is Z/2. This proves (Z/h)^2 ⊕ (Z/g) and the order gh^2. For h=2, the group is (Z/2)^3, as expected because C_2^can=R_2.

**Pullback and kernel.** The inclusion L_R=Z^4+Z(a-b)/h ⊆ L_g is the lattice map of the canonical cover. For both source and target, the four coordinate-facet normals are h*epsilon_i, so their toric height-one divisors have ramification index one. Therefore pullback sends each coordinate prime divisor D_i of R_h to the corresponding D'_i of C_h^can and is represented by the identity on Z^4, modulo the different principal-divisor lattices. It is surjective, and its kernel is

    (hL_g)/(hL_R) ≅ L_g/L_R.

Modulo Z^4, L_g corresponds to the pairs (i,j) in (Z/h)^2 with i+j divisible by g, whereas L_R corresponds to the cyclic subgroup {(t,-t):t mod h}. The sum map (i,j) |-> i+j modulo h has kernel exactly this cyclic subgroup, and image g*(Z/h), a cyclic group of order h/g=r. This proves the exact sequence above.

## Explicit character modules are the pullback kernel

The earlier rank-one module N_t is the total-z-exponent weight-t component of D_h as an R_h-module. Pick the nonzero weight-t monomial z1^t with exponent m_t=t*b/h (0<=t<h). Multiplication by z1^(-t) is an R_h-linear identification of N_t with a fractional monomial ideal of Frac(R_h):

    z1^(-t) N_t
      = direct_sum_(ell in L_R; ell+m_t in C) K*chi^ell.

The toric principal-divisor valuations are val_(D_i)(chi^ell)=h*ell_i. The condition ell+m_t in C is equivalent to

    val_(D_i)(chi^ell) + (t*b)_i >= 0, for all i.

By the standard definition of the rank-one reflexive divisorial module O(D), the displayed ideal is precisely

    Gamma(Spec(R_h), O(t*D_1+t*D_2)).

Consequently the actual class, not merely its order, is

    [N_t] = t*([D_1]+[D_2]) = t*[b] in Cl(R_h).

This agrees with the alternative z0^t trivialization because [a]=[b] in Cl(R_h) by the principal relation [a-b]=0. It also gives the earlier minimal-generator argument an independent toric divisor interpretation.

The class [b] has order h: in the explicit quotient Z^4/(hZ^4+Z(a-b)), b maps to (1,1,0) under Phi and therefore has exact order h. Thus [N_g]=g[b] has order h/g=r (for h=2 interpret N_g as N_0 modulo h). Under the finite toric pullback, [b] maps to zero when g=1, while [2b] maps to zero when g=2; both follow directly from b in H_1 and 2b in H_2. Hence <[N_g]> is contained in the kernel and has its full previously computed order r. Therefore

    0 -> <[N_(g mod h)]> -> Cl(R_h) -> Cl(C_h^can) -> 0

is the exact toric pullback sequence, with an **explicit module-theoretic kernel generator**. No extra class-group hypothesis is needed.
