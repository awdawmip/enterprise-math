Let K be **any field** and h>=2 an integer. Set R=K[U0,U1,U2,U3], A=U1U2, B=U0U3 and

    D_h=R[z0,z1]/(z0^h-B,z1^h-A),
    V_j=z0^j z1^(h-j), 1<=j<h,
    R_h=R[V1,...,V_(h-1)]=(D_h)^(mu_h).

Here mu_h is the diagonalizable group scheme acting with total z-exponent weight one, including when char(K) divides h. This theorem concerns **Cl(R_h)**, not the class group of D_h, the canonical cover, or the polynomial ring R.

## Theorem

As an abelian group, for every h>=2 and every field K,

    Cl(R_h) = (Z/hZ)^3.

More concretely, let D_i (0<=i<=3) be the four torus-invariant height-one prime divisors associated with the coordinate facets in the semigroup lattice described below. Then

    Cl(R_h) = < [D_0],[D_1],[D_2],[D_3] |
                h[D_i]=0 (all i),
                [D_0]-[D_1]-[D_2]+[D_3]=0 >.

The displayed relations are complete. In particular |Cl(R_h)|=h^3. The prior cyclic subgroup {[N_t]:0<=t<h} has order h and thus index h^2 in the full class group. This identifies its size without requiring an unproved identification of N_1 with a specific D_i.

## Proof

**1. Exact saturated semigroup.** In the exponent coordinates of (U0,U1,U2,U3), assign

    exp(U_i)=e_i,
    exp(z0)=(1/h,0,0,1/h),
    exp(z1)=(0,1/h,1/h,0).

Set v=(1,-1,-1,1), L=Z^4+Z(v/h), and C=R_{≥0}^4. The invariant V_j has exponent

    w_j=(j/h,1-j/h,1-j/h,j/h)
       =(0,1,1,0)+(j/h)v.

The semigroup generated by U0,...,U3 and V1,...,V_(h-1) is exactly C cap L. Indeed for x in C cap L choose the unique 0<=j<h with x congruent to (j/h)v modulo Z^4. If j=0, x belongs to N^4. If 1<=j<h, nonnegativity forces each coordinate of x to be at least the corresponding fractional part of w_j, and x-w_j belongs to N^4. Conversely all displayed generators belong to C cap L. Hence

    R_h = K[C cap L]

as a saturated, normal, four-dimensional affine split toric K-algebra. This argument uses integers and monomials, so it does not depend on separability of the mu_h action.

**2. Primitive facet normals.** The dual lattice is N=Hom(L,Z). The dual cone of C has exactly four rays supported on the four coordinate functionals epsilon_i. Since epsilon_i(v/h)=+/-1/h, the smallest positive integral multiple of epsilon_i lying in N is

    n_i=h*epsilon_i.

Each n_i is primitive in N. The standard toric divisor exact sequence for a normal split affine toric variety gives

    L -> Z^{4} -> Cl(R_h) -> 0,
    m |-> (<m,n_0>,...,<m,n_3>)=(h*m_0,...,h*m_3),

where the four coordinate rays correspond to D_0,...,D_3. This sequence follows from generation of Weil divisor classes by invariant prime divisors and the principal-divisor formula div(chi^m)=sum_i<m,n_i>D_i.

**3. Cokernel and Smith form.** The image of that lattice map is exactly

    h Z^4 + Z*v

because hL=h Z^4+Z*v. Therefore

    Cl(R_h) = Z^4 / (h Z^4 + Z*(1,-1,-1,1)).

Define an explicit quotient homomorphism

    Phi: Z^4 -> (Z/hZ)^3,
    Phi(a0,a1,a2,a3)=(a1+a0, a2+a0, a3-a0) mod h.

Phi is surjective (take a0=0); its kernel consists precisely of vectors congruent modulo h to a0*(1,-1,-1,1). Thus ker Phi=hZ^4+Zv, and the first isomorphism theorem proves

    Cl(R_h) ≅ (Z/hZ)^3.

The monomial U_i has principal divisor h D_i, while each V_j gives the corresponding multiple of the v-relation, consistent with the complete presentation. No extra divisor-class relation occurs.

**4. Compatibility with the earlier character-module theorem.** The earlier result established [N_t]+[N_u]=[N_(t+u mod h)] and the pairwise distinctness of [N_t] using their different joint-zero minimal-generator counts t+1. Hence t |-> [N_t] embeds Z/h in Cl(R_h). Combining that theorem with the newly computed cardinality h^3 gives its index h^2. This does not alter the earlier module computations.

## Exact arithmetic crosschecks and scope

The quotient can be checked solely with residue vectors modulo h: v has order h in (Z/h)^4 and the map Phi above is surjective with kernel <v>. Its exact cardinality is therefore h^4/h=h^3. Sample heights:

| h | |Cl(R_h)| | structure |
