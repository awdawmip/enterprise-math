Throughout, K is any field, h>=2 and e|d|h. Let

    S = K[U0,U1,U2,U3],   A=U0*U3,   B=U1*U2,
    D_h = S[z0,z1]/(z0^h-A, z1^h-B).

For every t|h, C_t is the S-subalgebra of elements whose total z-exponent is zero mod t. We consider the finite extension C_d ⊆ C_e, with generic rank n=d/e.

## Theorem — complete nonflat locus, uniformly determined fibers

Define

    M(e,d) = sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (ke+1)
           = n + e*n*(n-1)/2.

Let J_d ⊂ C_d be the ideal generated by the d+1 total-z-degree-d monomials

    z0^u*z1^(d-u),  u=0,...,d.

Then:

1. **Base quotient:** J_d has exactly the same prime support as (A,B) in C_d, and

       C_d/J_d ≅ S/(A,B).

2. **Relative fiber module:** There is a natural S/(A,B)-module isomorphism

       C_e/(J_d*C_e)
           ≅ direct_sum_{0<=k<n, 0<=i<=ke}
                    (S/(A,B))*[z0^i*z1^(ke-i)].

   In particular this module is free of rank M(e,d) over S/(A,B).

3. **Uniform geometric fibers on the closed stratum:** For *every* prime P of C_d with A,B in P,

       dim_{kappa(P)} (C_e tensor_{C_d} kappa(P)) = M(e,d).

   Over D(A) union D(B), every fiber has dimension n, by the pinned monogenic Kummer presentations.

4. If e=d (n=1), the morphism Spec(C_e) -> Spec(C_d) is the identity. If e<d (n>1), then M(e,d)>n and

       NonFlat(C_e/C_d) = NonLocallyFree(C_e/C_d) = V(A,B)

   as sets of primes in Spec(C_d). Consequently, for n>1,

       radical(Fitt_n^{C_d}(C_e)) = radical((A,B)*C_d),

   where Fitt_n denotes the n-th Fitting ideal of the finite C_d-module C_e.

This theorem holds in arbitrary characteristic, including characteristic dividing n. Flatness and separability are separate: on D(A) union D(B) the cover is finite free for every K, and it is étale there exactly when char(K) does not divide n (as proved in the pinned Kummer note).

### Proof

**Step 1: free normal-form module.** The two defining relations of D_h are monic in z0 and z1. Thus D_h is free over S with the monomial basis z0^i*z1^j for 0<=i,j<h. Because t|h, reduction by the defining relations preserves total z-degree modulo t; the subalgebra C_t is exactly the direct sum of those basis terms with i+j≡0 mod t, with coefficients in S.

**Step 2: the ideal J_d and its quotient.** Every reduced normal-form monomial of C_d with positive total z-degree (a positive multiple of d) can be factored by one of the degree-d generators of J_d, since i+j>=d implies that some 0<=u<=d has u<=i and d-u<=j. Also A=z0^h and B=z1^h both belong to J_d, because d|h. The quotient therefore contains only the S-coefficient of the constant monomial, with the relations A=B=0. Conversely, the map C_d -> S/(A,B) sending every positive-z-degree term to zero and sending S to its quotient is a well-defined ring homomorphism: products of positive-degree terms cannot generate a surviving S constant except through factors A or B, which vanish. Its kernel is J_d. Hence C_d/J_d≅S/(A,B). In particular V(J_d)=V(A,B).

**Step 3: quotient of the extension.** Consider the normal-form S-basis of C_e. If a basis monomial has total z-degree i+j>=d, factor a degree-d monomial of J_d as above. The remaining monomial has total degree divisible by e (because both i+j and d are), so lies in C_e, and the original monomial belongs to J_d*C_e. Conversely, every element of J_d*C_e expressed in the reduced S-normal form has, in the coefficient of every monomial with i+j<d, a factor of A or B: the product starts at total z-degree at least d, and reducing an exponent by h to reach total degree <d necessarily introduces A or B. Therefore, modulo (A,B), no linear combination of distinct monomials of total degree <d can lie in J_d*C_e. The classes of exactly those monomials form an S/(A,B)-basis. The allowed total degrees are 0,e,2e,...,(n-1)e; at degree ke there are ke+1 distinct (i,j) pairs, since ke<d<=h. This proves the claimed direct sum and M(e,d).

**Step 4: local fibers and flatness.** Any P containing A,B contains J_d, so the residue-field map C_d -> kappa(P) factors through C_d/J_d≅S/(A,B). Base change of Step 3 yields an M(e,d)-dimensional kappa(P)-vector space. Outside V(A,B), at least one of A or B is a unit, and the pinned Kummer presentations give a free rank-n module. Since C_d is a domain and the generic rank is n, a finite flat localization must be free of rank n. If n>1, M(e,d)-n=e*n*(n-1)/2>0; therefore no point of V(A,B) is flat or locally free. This establishes the complete nonflat locus. Finally, for a finite module of generic rank n, Fitt_n cuts out exactly the locus where more than n generators are required; the same fiber calculation gives the stated radical equality. QED.

## Corollary — exact fiber algebra, Hilbert function, and socle

For any P in V(A,B), with residue field kappa(P), the fiber admits the following **algebra** presentation, not merely a vector-space basis:

    C_e tensor_(C_d) kappa(P)
      ~= [ kappa(P)[x,y] / (x,y)^d ]_{total_degree ≡ 0 (mod e)}.

The right side is the e-th Veronese subalgebra of the truncated two-variable polynomial algebra, including when char(kappa(P)) divides e. It is obtained by the honest Z/eZ graded direct summand, which commutes with base change over the degree-zero algebra; no semisimple group representation argument is needed.

For n=d/e>1 its maximal ideal m consists of the positive Veronese degrees and is generated by x^e,x^(e-1)y,...,y^e. The maximal-ideal filtration is exact:

    m^k / m^(k+1) ~= Sym^(ke)(kappa(P)^2),  0<=k<n,
    dim_kappa m^k/m^(k+1) = ke+1;
    m^n=0,    m^(n-1) != 0.

Here the degree-k term of the ordinary Hilbert-Samuel series is (ke+1)T^k, and the sum of these dimensions recovers M(e,d). Because multiplication by x^e is injective on every nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree ke with k<n-1, the socle has no summands below the top allowed degree d-e. Consequently,

    dim_kappa Soc(fiber) = d-e+1,
    embedding_dimension(fiber) = e+1,
    nilpotence_index(m) = n.

In particular every nontrivial intermediate-cover fiber over V(A,B) is Artinian local, nonreduced and **not Gorenstein**: d-e+1>=2. Therefore it is not a complete-intersection Artin algebra. The identity case e=d has fiber kappa(P), embedding dimension zero and socle dimension one.

**Justification of the algebra isomorphism:** Since A,B and all degree-d base invariants vanish in kappa(P), the fiber multiplication is exactly polynomial multiplication with every monomial of total degree >=d set to zero, restricted to the degrees divisible by e. The monomial normal forms in the main proof give a full multiplicative basis, not just an additive count. The stated maximal-ideal powers follow by factoring each homogeneous degree-ke monomial into k degree-e monomials. Top-degree monomials are annihilated by every positive-degree element. No nonzero element in a lower homogeneous degree is annihilated by x^e before truncation, and distinct total-degree pieces cannot cancel. QED.

The special canonical cover (d=h,e=gcd(h,2)) has socle dimension h when h is odd and h-1 when h is even (except h=2, where d=e and socle dimension one), consistent with the previously saved joint-zero-fiber theorem.
