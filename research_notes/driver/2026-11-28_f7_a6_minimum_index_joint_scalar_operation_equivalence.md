## 2. Joint equivalence, not just operation equivalence

Let rho reflect the new direction. In chart coordinates rho(u,v)=(-v,-u). A reflection sends G to G^-(z)=G(-z). This follows from q composed with rho. The scalar parameter is unique: G(z)=q at chart (2z,0) for +2 or mixed signs, and G(z)=q at chart (-2z,0) for the negative-only type. More generally q determines G within each sign type by its invariant multiplier. For a non-even G, q differs from q composed with rho, as can be seen on n=0,m=z, where q=2G(z). Therefore applying different reflection bits to the two slots cannot map a pair with the same common scalar on both slots to another such pair unless G is even. Slot swap does not change that conclusion. The entire joint equivalence relation in the common-scalar subspace is:
* even G: all eight typed operation gauge elements (G8) are allowed; they fix G.
* asymmetric G: only simultaneous reflection of both slots, slot swap and their product map within the common-scalar subspace (G4); simultaneous reflection exchanges G and G^- and has no fixed G in this sector. For fixed G the residual operation stabilizer is the two-element group consisting of identity and slot swap.
Uniform reflection multiplies every chart channel by -1 in addition to swapping within its slot, so sign type is invariant under G4. In contrast, for independent reflections of only one slot, the signs of cross-slot channels flip, while within-slot signs remain. A split permutation has exactly two cross-slot channels and two within-slot channels. Hence G8 does not preserve the pure sign subsets. For even G use the total G8 count or the invariant partition by whether a full orbit meets a pure-sign subset, not Burnside on a non-invariant subset. Under G4 the type is invariant, so the pure-sign and mixed counts below are valid.

Thus exact orbit counts are finite *per scalar reflection pair*, not a finite total over all scalar functions. If a G is asymmetric and obeys both pure-type laws, it would be even, so these two parameter families do not intersect.
