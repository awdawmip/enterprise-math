## Scope and precise theorem

Hold the three historical signs z=(x,y,t) in {−1,+1}³ **fixed throughout the prediction experiment**. At each future step, a controller chooses either noise-before-Port-selection channel F or noise-after-Port-selection channel P, with public choice label. Conditional on the same hidden z, fresh output words (u,v,w) are independent across steps; policy choices may depend on previously public output words. The initial source prior is any strictly positive distribution on the eight signs.

Reuse the R749SML channels
\[
F_{uvw,z}=\frac{(2+\alpha ux)(2+\beta v z_u)(2+\gamma wyt)}{64},\quad
P_{uvw,z}=\frac{2+\gamma wyt}{128}
\sum_{i=\pm1}(2+ix)(1+\alpha ui)(2+\beta v z_i),
\quad z_+=y,\quad z_-=t.
\]
Consider the exact subfamily gamma=0, with arbitrary real alpha,beta∈[−1,1]. The predictive row space V_H is the span of all probabilities of all publicly labeled experiment/output words of length at most H under the allowed controllers; V_0=span{1}. Let r_H=dim(V_H), c_H=r_H−1. Let V_infty be the union/stable span.

**All-future theorem.** For this fixed-history repeat-observation model,

\[
\boxed{\dim V_\infty=
\begin{cases}
1,&\beta=0,\alpha=0,\\
2,&\beta=0,\alpha\ne0,\\
6,&\beta\ne0,\alpha=0,\\
8,&\beta\ne0,\alpha\ne0.
\end{cases}}
\]

The sharp minimum number of independent affine Walsh source moments for predicting every permitted finite future is therefore 0,1,5,7 respectively. Exact smallest saturation horizon H_* is 0,1,2,2 respectively, **except** the nonzero-beta alpha=1 case, where H_*=3. More explicitly, with beta≠0:

| alpha | r_0 | r_1 (selectable F/P) | r_2 | r_3 | r_infty |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 0 | 1 | 4 | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| 1 | 1 | 4 | 7 | 8 | 8 |
| alpha≠0,1 | 1 | 6 | 8 | 8 | 8 |

For alpha≠0,1, **the fixed non-adaptive two-step sequence F then P already has rank eight**; two identical F steps have rank seven, and three F steps have rank eight. At alpha=1, F=P; two-step rank is seven and three-step rank is eight. At alpha=0, F then P has rank six after two steps and is all-future sufficient.

## Proof: finite source-observer algebra

Each one-step likelihood row is a function on the finite hidden source Ω={±1}³. Since z stays fixed and future observations are conditionally independent, the probability of a fixed publicly labeled output sequence is a pointwise product of its one-step row functions. Allowing all fixed F/P sequences and all outcomes, the all-future predictive space is precisely the *unital commutative real algebra* A generated by the one-step row spaces. Any public-history-dependent policy yields rows that are such products along each path, so all adaptive policies lie in A. Conversely every product of row functions is the likelihood row of its corresponding fixed sequence, so no smaller universal space suffices.

For gamma=0 the w bit is an independent fair coin. If beta=0, both F and P have rows depending solely on 1 and alpha*x; if alpha=0 all rows are constant; otherwise A=span{1,x}. This gives ranks one or two, saturated at H=0 or H=1.

Assume beta≠0. The R749SML one-step analysis gives:
- If alpha≠0, the union one-step span includes 1,x and the feedback functions (2+alpha*x)y and (2−alpha*x)t.
- If alpha=0, the union one-step span is exactly span{1,y,t,x(y−t)}.

For alpha≠0, multiply x by the two feedback functions. Since x²=1, the coefficient pairs (2+alpha*x)y and x(2+alpha*x)y span {y,xy} (determinant 4−alpha²>0), and similarly the t functions span {t,xt}. Products of y and t give yt; then multiplication by x gives xyt. Consequently A contains all eight independent Walsh characters {1,x,y,t,xy,xt,yt,xyt}. Thus dim A=8 for every nonzero alpha, including alpha=1.

For alpha=0, let q=x(y−t). The one-step generated algebra is generated by y,t,q, with relations y²=t²=1, q*y=x(1−yt), q*t=−x(1−yt), q*(y+t)=0, q²=2(1−yt). It equals
\[
A=\operatorname{span}\{1,y,t,yt,x(y-t),x(1-yt)\}.
\]
Closure under multiplication follows directly from these identities (or from the fact that y,t determine the four (y,t) sectors, while q distinguishes x only in the two sectors y≠t). These six characters/combinations are linearly independent on Ω: four functions depend only on (y,t), and the two x-odd combinations have disjoint nonzero supports y≠t. Thus dim A=6. In the y=t sectors, the two x states always have identical likelihood for every future policy; the other four source states remain separate.

## Sharp finite horizons

For alpha≠0 and beta≠0, each single-channel one-step row span has dimension four. Put a=(2+alpha*x)y, b=(2−alpha*x)t. The F-only two-step products generate
\[
\operatorname{span}\{1,x,y,xy,t,xt,yt\},
\]
because ab=(4−alpha²)yt and x times a,b separates y/xy,t/xt. This is dimension seven, with xyt absent. Three F steps multiply x by ab and yield xyt, so FFF has rank eight.

For the two-step mixed F→P experiment, the P row space for nonzero alpha contains c=(2+x)y, d=(2−x)t and 1,x. The mixed products include
\[
ad=(2-\alpha+(2\alpha-2)x)yt,\qquad
bc=(2-\alpha+(2-2\alpha)x)yt.
\]
Their coefficient determinant is nonzero exactly when alpha≠1, because 2−alpha>0 on [−1,1] and 2(alpha−1)≠0. The remaining six Walsh characters are already generated by products involving 1,x,a,b. Thus for alpha≠0,1, FP has rank eight at horizon two. When alpha=1, F=P and horizon two has rank seven, horizon three eight.

At alpha=0 and beta≠0, the F rows span {1,y,t}, while the P rows span {1,H}, H=2(y+t)+x(y−t) up to a nonzero scale. Two-step FP products include yH, tH. From yH+tH one obtains (1+yt), and from yH−tH obtains x(1−yt), so the six-dimensional all-future algebra is already spanned at H=2. At H=1 the joint rank was four. This finishes the exact saturation classification.

For sharp *minimal affine* memory, every observation family is column-stochastic and has the constant function in its row span. Its row space rank r has affine quotient rank r−1 over the strict interior probability simplex. If fewer expectation coordinates are retained, a nonzero zero-mass difference direction invisible to those coordinates changes at least one future probability. Small signed perturbations of the uniform prior make the two competing priors strictly positive. QED.

\[
