## Theorem 1: for every positive concentration N the continuous Beta average has at most s zeros, counting multiplicity
For p in (0,1), define B_N(p)=E[h(X)] with X~Beta(Np,N(1−p)). Then the number of real zeros of B_N on (0,1), with multiplicity, is at most s.

Proof. Write a=Np and t=log(x/(1−x)). The unnormalized transform is
F_N(a)= integral_0^1 h(x) x^(a−1)(1−x)^(N−a−1) dx
       = integral_R H(t) e^(a t) dt,
where H(t)=h(e^t/(1+e^t))/(1+e^t)^N.
The Beta normalizing integral is strictly positive and real analytic for a in (0,N), so multiplication/division by it and the affine reparameterization preserve zeros and multiplicities. H has precisely s sign changes. All derivatives of the integral converge absolutely locally uniformly in a, including after multiplying H by any fixed polynomial in t: at t→−infinity the integrand is O(poly(|t|)e^(a t)); at t→+infinity it is O(poly(|t|)e^(−(N−a)t)). Thus F_N is real analytic (indeed holomorphic on the open strip).

More generally for such a continuous nonzero integrand with s sign changes and the same exponential integrability, the transform has at most s zeros counted with multiplicity. Induct on s. For s=0, the kernel is positive and the integrand has one weak sign, with strict sign on a set of positive measure; the integral has strict sign. For s>0 choose a sign-change point tau. Multiplication by t−tau removes exactly that change and preserves all other sign changes (the multiplier has constant nonzero sign on each side), yielding a nonzero integrand with s−1 changes and the same local integrability. Its transform is G=F_N'−tau F_N=e^(tau a)(e^(−tau a)F_N)'. If F_N has a finite selection of distinct zeros of total multiplicity r, the derivative of e^(−tau a)F_N has at least r−1 zeros with multiplicity: each repeated root contributes its multiplicity minus one, and Rolle gives one zero between each pair of distinct roots. The induction bound on G implies r<=s. If F_N were identically zero then G would be identically zero, contrary to the induction hypothesis (ultimately the base case). An infinite zero set would allow arbitrary finite r and is excluded. This completes the proof. This is a self-contained variation-diminishing argument for the inherited scalar observer, not a claim of a new general transform theory.

## Theorem 3: sign variations of integer rows are monotone, bounded, and eventually exact
For integer N>=2 let V_N be the number of strict sign changes of the finite sequence B_N(j/N), j=1,...,N−1, after deletion of exact zeros. Then
0<=V_N<=V_(N+1)<=s, and V_N=s for all sufficiently large N.
In particular every observed row with r changes certifies at least r actual sign-changing spectral roots. Every negative entry in any row forces a negative entry in each later row. Since endpoints are eventually positive, an observed negative entry forces at least two true sign-changing roots. A row exhibiting r separate negative blocks separated by strictly positive entries and bracketed by positive entries proves s>=2r, not just the existence of a negative integral.

Proof. Between alternating nonzero samples of B_N lies a distinct continuous root, so V_N<=s by Theorem 1. To prove monotonicity use the exact positive-weight refinement law
B_N(j/N)=(1−j/N) B_(N+1)(j/(N+1))+(j/N) B_(N+1)((j+1)/(N+1)).
This follows by writing the Beta(j,N−j) density as the sum of its (1−x) and x multiples, whose normalizing factors are 1−j/N and j/N respectively. A nonzero entry in the coarser row has at least one strictly same-sign child at index j or j+1. Choose a strictly alternating subsequence of coarse entries of length V_N+1. Child indices in this subsequence can be chosen independently and are nondecreasing because distinct parent indices increase by at least one. They must be strictly increasing since equality at consecutive chosen children would require both opposite signs at the same entry. Thus the finer row has an alternating subsequence of the same length. This also proves propagation of a negative entry, recursively. Finally the s+1 ordered alternating witness points used in Theorem 2 can be approximated by distinct grid means j/N; uniform convergence of B_N and continuity of h imply that the signs of these grid samples agree with those witnesses for all sufficiently large N. Therefore V_N>=s eventually. QED.

The result gives exact recovery of the number of sign changes by refining integer rows, but no effective stabilization index or upper bound on s beyond the actual spectral zero information. It distinguishes sign-change intervals from isolated even-order zeros; a negative region split only by a zero without a positive intervening segment is not counted twice as a sign change.
