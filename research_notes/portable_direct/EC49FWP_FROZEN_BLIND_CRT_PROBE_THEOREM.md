## Precise observer and theorem

Let \(\mathcal F\) be the 50 distinct ten-bit supports generated by the frozen 44-cell P10 decoder over 72 primitive directions. Let \(q\ge3\) be coprime to 10, \(V\ne\varnothing\) be an arbitrary **unknown** Boolean support in \(\mathbb Z/q\), and \(U\in\mathcal F\) also be unknown. Under the indexed CRT identification, put \(W=U\times V\subseteq\mathbb Z/(10q)\).

The principal observation is the *entire indexed* reflection-defect array
\[
\Delta_W(a,b)=|W\triangle((a,b)-W)|,\quad (a,b)\in\mathbb Z/10\times\mathbb Z/q.
\]
A local probe is a fixed physical Boolean membership reading \(1_W(a,b)\); its position may be chosen either before or after reading the entire array. The objective is to recover the **total support mass** \(N=|W|\), and therefore the EC49FWO factor sizes, odd-factor support/hole locations, and shorter-factor indexed pair-sum vector. Original signed membership of U is *not* the requested output.

**Theorem.** On this frozen family, the minimum number of **fixed, nonadaptive** local probes sufficient for all unknown nonempty V is exactly **2**, for every such q. A fixed pair is sufficient iff one probe has first CRT coordinate in \(\{2,7\}\) and the other in \(\{3,8\}\); second coordinates are arbitrary. Thus there are exactly \(4q^2\) optimal unordered two-probe sets. For \(q=11\), there are **484**, with physical sites \(t=22\) and \(t=33\) as one example.

If the probe may be chosen *after* observing \(\Delta_W\), the worst-case minimum is **1**. On any instance with \(V\ne\mathbb Z/q\), the full array alone uniquely fixes N (zero local probes). These statements are exact for the declared static product family, with arbitrary unknown V, not merely a fixed one-hole model.

## Proof: analytic reduction

For a cyclic Boolean support S define \(K_S(c)=|\{(x,y)\in S^2:x+y=c\}|\). Its defects satisfy \(\Delta_S(c)=2(|S|-K_S(c))\). CRT gives \(K_W(a,b)=K_U(a)K_V(b)\).

Suppose two eligible products W=U×V and W'=U'×V' have equal indexed defects but **different** masses N,N'. Set d=N'−N ≠0. Then
\[
K_{U'}(a)K_{V'}(b)=K_U(a)K_V(b)+d
\]
at every (a,b). The left matrix has rank one. For any two row and two column indices the 2×2 determinant on the right equals, up to sign,
\[
d\,[K_U(a_1)-K_U(a_2)] [K_V(b_1)-K_V(b_2)].
\]
Hence either \(K_U\) or \(K_V\) is constant. A nonempty Boolean support S in a finite cyclic group has constant K_S only when S is the full group: the Fourier relation \(\widehat K_S=\widehat{1_S}^{\,2}\) forces all nontrivial Fourier coefficients of 1_S to vanish, so 1_S is constant, and nonempty Boolean constancy means 1.

The frozen \(\mathcal F\) contains only supports of sizes 4,5,6,7,8, never the full ten-cycle. Therefore **V is full**. Equality forces \(K_{V'}\) also constant, so V' is full. It follows that \(\Delta_W=q\Delta_U\) and \(\Delta_{W'}=q\Delta_{U'}\). Consequently a different-mass ambiguity is *exactly* a different-cardinality equal-\(\Delta_U\) pair inside the finite frozen family, lifted by full V.

| first ten-bit mask | second ten-bit mask | cardinalities | symmetric-difference positions |
|---|---|---|---|
| 23 | 287 | 4, 6 | \(\{3,8\}\) |
| 833 | 965 | 4, 6 | \(\{2,7\}\) |

Masks use bit t for first-coordinate position t. The two difference supports are disjoint. If zero probes are used, either pair with V full is a counterexample. A **single fixed** physical probe misses at least one of these two disjoint supports, so fails. Two physical probes meeting both difference supports distinguish every possible different-mass pair, by the analytic reduction. They are precisely the characterized optimal sets.

For an **adaptive** probe, the complete array identifies which of the two distinct ambiguous defect signatures (if either) occurred, so one reading in its corresponding first-coordinate pair distinguishes its two masses. One is necessary by either actual full-V collision.

When V is not full, the analytic reduction already rules out any different-mass alternative, so no local reading is needed for N. Recovery of the factor data then uses the mass-augmented EC49FWO theorem. Nothing here assumes that an arbitrary future observer or a native state update factors through these static statistics.
