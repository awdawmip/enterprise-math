# EC49FWX — Independent left/right phase drift: exact two-channel activation costs

**Date:** 2026-11-22. **Frozen scientific pin:** `BRC16943`. **Parents:** EC49FWV at `e2a255177cb437976b356e1a6ba21f3a4fb862e0` and EC49FWW at `61af64c051725e4caafd186efaeb6de7d23268ca`. **Status:** AUTHOR_PROVED / EXACT_INTEGER_CROSSCHECK / PARENT_DEPENDENT / NOT_REVIEWED.

## Observer and quantifier order

This is an exact *finite auxiliary Farey/CRT observer* over \(\mathbb Z/110\), with a known reference word
\[
w(t)={\bf1}_U(t\bmod10)\,{\bf1}_V(t\bmod11).
\]
\(U\) ranges over the 50 distinct frozen P10 support masks generated by 72 primitive directions; \(V\) is either all of \(\mathbb Z/11\) or has one missing residue. For one reference word \(w\), left and right sensors have **independent** unknown phases \(s_L,s_R\in\mathbb Z/110\):
\[
L_{s_L}(t)=w(t-s_L),\qquad R_{s_R}(t)=w(-t-s_R).
\]
These phases are not constrained to be negatives of one another, in contrast to EC49FWW. The two *complete indexed reflection-defect spectra* are available. Each hardware activation at \(t\in\mathbb Z/110\) yields two **separately sampled** Boolean channel readings \((L_{s_L}(t),R_{s_R}(t))\). A fixed layout is chosen universally before \(U,V,s_L,s_R\) and spectra are known. An adaptive layout may depend on the known reference and both supplied spectra. The decoded target is the observed pair of full membership words, not the underlying absolute phase or a native X6 state.

In each common-pair-of-spectra fiber, every two different paired response vectors must have channel-bit Hamming distance at least \(r\). This means \(r=1\) exact recovery, \(r=2\) tolerance of one known-channel erasure, \(r=3\) correction of one unknown-channel-bit error; the unit of cost is a **paired physical activation**, not a single Boolean channel sample. Only \(1\le r\le20\) is considered, as a one-hole state with two disagreeing P10 rows has precisely 20 differing positions in either channel.

## Exact theorems

Write
\[
h(r)=\begin{cases}r+1&1\le r\le10,\\r+2&11\le r\le20.\end{cases}
\]
For the independently drifting two-channel observer:
\[
\boxed{N_{\rm fixed}^{\rm independent}(r)=5h(r)},\qquad
\boxed{N_{\rm adaptive}^{\rm independent}(r)=2r}
\quad (1\le r\le20).
\]
In particular, **fixed** costs for exact recovery / one known erasure / one unknown erroneous bit are \(10,15,20\); **adaptive** costs are \(2,4,6\).

The two supplied spectra have up to four candidate *paired words* (two possible observed words for each independently drifting side). More precisely, the inherited EC49FWV spectral-alias lemma shows two distinct membership words per side in this frozen family, so there are exactly four candidate paired words when both phases vary freely. This counting assumes the known reference and separately free phases; it is not a statement about hidden anchors with extra coupling constraints.

## Proof: spectra and disagreement

For a cyclic shift \(s\), \(K_{w_s}(c)=K_w(c-2s)\) and hence its indexed defect spectrum is \(\Delta_{w_s}(c)=\Delta_w(c-2s)\). Thus \(s\) and \(s+55\) have identical indexed spectra. For the right observer, replacing \(s_R\) by \(s_R+55\) has the same property. EC49FWV proves that these exhaust the two distinct word-level choices for each one-hole factor, and the full-factor spectrum likewise leaves precisely the two ten-residue words differing by half a ten-period.

Let \(D(U)=\{i\in\mathbb Z/10:U(i)\ne U(i-5)\}\). The inherited exact P10 census has \(|D(U)|\in\{2,4,6,8\}\), with \(D(U)\) a nonempty union of opposite row pairs \(P_i=\{i,i+5\}\) for \(0\le i<5\). A pair of phase-aliased left words disagrees precisely on a translation of \(D(U)\times V\) under CRT. The corresponding right disagreement has the same cardinality, reflected and independently translated. All possible one-channel disagreements contain at least 20 physical positions.

## Proof: fixed-installation lower bound

The frozen family has masks with \(D(U)\) consisting of one opposite pair; the variable phase shifts this pair to any chosen \(P_i\), independently of the position of the single excluded modulo-11 column. Let \(S\) be any fixed activation set, \(m_i=|S\cap\{t:t\bmod10\in P_i\}|\), and \(c_{ij}\) be its site count in column \(j\in\mathbb Z/11\) and rows \(P_i\).

Consider two **independent-phase paired states** with the same right phase but different left phases \(s_L,s_L+55\). They share both spectra, and their paired response Hamming distance is exactly the number of installed positions on the chosen \(P_i\) excluding the adversarial missing column. This is
\[
m_i-\max_{j}c_{ij}.
\]
Hence every \(i=0,\ldots,4\) must satisfy \(m_i-\max_jc_{ij}\ge r\). For \(1\le r\le10\), a nonempty block requires \(m_i\ge r+1\). For \(11\le r\le20\), if \(m_i\le r+1\), the pigeonhole principle forces some column to contain at least two readings, implying \(m_i-\max_jc_{ij}\le r-1\); therefore \(m_i\ge r+2\). The five \(P_i\) partition the activation locations, so \(|S|\ge5h(r)\). The independent right channel cannot repair this witness because it is identical in the two compared states.

## Proof: fixed upper bound and exact construction

For each \(P_i\), if \(r\le10\) install one site in row \(i\) in each of columns \(0,\dots,r\): \(h=r+1\) sites with maximum column occupancy one. If \(r\ge11\), install sites in row \(i\) across all eleven columns and another site in row \(i+5\) in columns \(0,\dots,r-10\): \(h=r+2\) sites, maximum occupancy two.

Any authentic alias disagreement contains an entire translated opposite row pair apart from at most one excluded column, so the left-channel distance is at least \(r\). Reflecting the coordinates sends \(P_i\) bijectively to another \(P_j\) and column \(c\) to \(-c\), preserving the same occupancy bounds. The **same physical installations** therefore achieve distance at least \(r\) for every possible right-channel alias. For two paired candidates differing on both channels, total channel-bit distance is the sum of the two nonnegative channel distances, so at least \(r\). Thus \(5h(r)\) installations attain the lower bound.

## Proof: adaptive upper and explicit sharp counterexample

After both indexed spectra are known, the known reference determines the two candidate membership words in each channel. Their respective difference supports \(A,B\subseteq\mathbb Z/110\) each have cardinality at least 20. For \(1\le r\le20\), activate any \(r\) sites of \(A\) and any \(r\) sites of \(B\). The union uses at most \(2r\) activations. The four candidate pairs differ in at least one channel; their observed distance is therefore at least \(r\).

For sharpness take the genuinely frozen 10-bit mask \(U=299\), whose one-residues are \(\{0,1,3,5,8\}\), the one-hole factor \(V=\mathbb Z/11\setminus\{0\}\), and baseline independent phases \(s_L=s_R=0\). The left half-turn-alias difference support \(A\) occupies rows \(\{1,6\}\bmod10\); the right half-turn-alias difference support \(B\) occupies rows \(\{4,9\}\bmod10\). Both have exactly 20 points, and \(A\cap B=\varnothing\). The two phase choices on each channel generate **four** distinct paired words with identical two indexed spectra. Any paired activation hits at most one of the two difference supports, whereas discriminating the pairs differing only on the left and those differing only on the right requires at least \(r\) sites in each support. Thus at least \(2r\) activations are needed after seeing the spectra. This is a genuine finite-family witness, not a hypothetical mask.
