## 1. Germ capacity theorem

For every clipped open active interval with distinct positive-projective closure rays a,b, keep two directed germs (a,b,k) and (b,a,k), with support tag k. A germ is the open interval sufficiently close to its first endpoint, represented by (1-e)a+e b for arbitrarily small positive e. For any homogeneous linear form q its eventual sign is lexsign(q·a,q·b). If this is zero, q vanishes on the whole interval (both endpoint rays are annihilated). Unlike R228, a query passing through an endpoint cannot erase a germ transversely. Preserve distinct component incidence and both directions, even when endpoints coincide across tags.

For a finite tree, finitely many ancestor tests have eventual signs simultaneously on some nonempty initial subinterval. Consequently every retained germ forces its tag into the strict-path wall capacity of that node. If S is the retained germ set, c(S) counts distinct tags, and remaining depth d, c(S)<=2^d-1 is necessary. For each query q split S by its lex signs, discarding only zero germs. The exact finite relaxed recursion tests all attainable unordered strict child masks, with G(S,0) iff S empty. If c(S)<=d, success is legitimate in this relaxation: query each distinct support once, removing every germ of its tag and maintaining capacity. Downward monotonicity holds. Any actual output tree induces a feasible germ tree. R228 endpoint point-witness relaxation is implied by germ feasibility since an endpoint with strict path signs has its corresponding germ survive; the converse need not hold. Example: one nonzero transverse q at a point annihilates a point witness but not the incident germ. No claim that germs alone certify continuous-wall or complete output feasibility.

## 2. Complete finite query library

Let P be the global bank of endpoint rays for one root side. If P spans R^3, consider its central hyperplane arrangement in the auxiliary coefficient space q·p=0. Each face cone is pointed (lineality would annihilate span P); every nonempty face has a vertex ray v at an intersection of at least two independent endpoint equations. Up to overall sign enumerate all such v=p_i×p_j. Include v itself. For each p with v·p=0, use w=v×p, and include lex sign tuples (v, ±w) and (v, ±w, ±p) on every endpoint. These tuples are actual signs of v+epsilon (±w)+epsilon²(±p) for sufficiently small positive epsilon (or omit the last term). In the transverse 2D quotient at v, incident hyperplanes become lines, whose directed rays are ±w. The 1D sectors are these rays; every 2D sector is incident to at least one such ray and is reached by a sufficiently small perturbation to one side, represented by ±p. Thus every face containing v in closure is represented, including strict full-dimensional faces, 2D faces, and the ray itself. The global sign reversal swaps strict children, so only one orientation of v is required. A germ sign is determined exactly from the two endpoint signs, hence this bank is complete for the fixed germ relaxation at all recursive depths. If global P is rank deficient, a nonzero normal annihilates every endpoint and germ, subject to the initial cap. Deduplicating equal unordered masks is safe; do not replace full sign patterns by extreme rays only, which would be unsound for lex signs.
