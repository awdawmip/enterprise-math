## Theorem (affected reverse region)
Let finite resources C have fixed nonnegative integer capacities, distinct exclusive targets with fixed neighborhoods, and zero-capacity edges normalized away. For a maximum matching M of the current targets, write W for the largest Hall-deficiency maximizer and R=C minus W. By GBC, R is the reverse reachability set of unused resource slots F under directed arcs j->k when an occupant of j may use k. Keep original labels and neighborhoods. Insert one new exclusive target u. If its neighborhood is contained in W, rank and W stay unchanged by GBC insertion corollary. Otherwise there exists an augmenting path using only R: choose a neighbor in R and follow a simple directed resource path to F, moving the distinct old occupants back along the path and inserting u. Rank increases by one; old W stays blocked by GBC monotonicity.

Let P be the resource vertices of any such simple augmenting path, and A be the old reverse reachability set of P within R (including P). Put O=R minus A. In the new matching, only outgoing arc rows at P change, and free status changes at most at the terminal resource. In the new graph restricted to R, define seeds S=(new free resources in A) union {j in A: j has a new outgoing arc to O}. Let K be the reverse reachable set from S within A, using the new arc graph. Then the exact new live set is O union K, and W_new = W union (A minus K).

Proof: every old path starting in O to a free resource avoids P, since otherwise its starting vertex would belong to A. All its outgoing arcs and terminal free status are unchanged; so O remains live. The old W remains blocked by monotonicity, which follows directly from d_new(V)=d(V)+1_{E(u) subset V} and integer slack, as proved in GBC. A vertex in A is newly live iff it has a new path to a new free resource inside R. If the path stays in A it reaches a free seed; otherwise its first exit from A goes to O and the preceding vertex is a seed. Conversely every seed has a path to free capacity, and reverse reachability preserves that property. Thus K describes precisely the live part of A. This proves the update for every prefix; it does not assume the graph is connected or generic. The maintained matching remains maximum after each successful augmentation, and previously blocked occupants are never moved.

Corollary: a direct insertion into a free resource which remains free adds outgoing arcs without removing old arcs or free vertices, hence W stays unchanged and no reachability update is needed. A direct insertion that consumes the final free slot need not satisfy this shortcut. For example c_a=c_b=1 with old target {a,b} assigned at a; insertion of {a} causes an augmenting path to b and makes both resources blocked. For a simpler direct last-slot example, insert {a} into an empty unit slot a.

Second characterization: after contracting W by GBB, let slack s(V)=c(V)-f'(V) on remaining resources, which is at least 1 for every nonempty V. Let E'=E(u) intersect R. If E' is nonempty, the increment in W equals the union of the V with E' subset V and s(V)=1 (empty union means no change). Indeed the old reduced maximum deficiency is zero with largest maximizer empty. After insertion the new deficiency is still zero and the nonempty maximizers are exactly these slack-one cuts. The characterization is independent of the chosen augmentation and connects the affected reverse graph to the previous cut-based coordinates.

The implementation retains matching occupants, original target masks and cached outgoing and reverse adjacency bitmasks; only changed rows are rebuilt. Removed resources are excluded from all future traversals. This is safe for insert-only fixed-capacity observers, including every prefix rank and largest cut; by GBB each current cut also preserves every future shared-subset capacity at that fixed exclusive prefix. It does not justify discarding physical labels, deleting targets, changing capacities, or changing existing adjacency without a new proof. The update visits A rather than all live resources for reachability, but search, changed-row rebuilding, integer bit width, retained data and output still incur costs. No universal running-time improvement is asserted. For dense connected graphs A can equal R. For 400 independent unit slots inserted one by one, sum |A|=400 versus sum |R|=80200; this is an affected-region count, not total operation complexity.
