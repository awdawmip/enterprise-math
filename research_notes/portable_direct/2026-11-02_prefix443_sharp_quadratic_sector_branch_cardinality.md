## Exact definition

Fix integers q>=1 and n>=0. A source bag contains m **distinct** ordered legal n-step words over (+e1,-e1) with unit positive weight, embedded in full signed-six-axis X6. A word with k negative steps has endpoint x_k=(n-2k)e1, with C(n,k) distinct realizations. Let c_k be the number of selected words at endpoint k, 0<=c_k<=C(n,k). Declare the q residue-sector quadratic observer

  T_(r,j)(c)=sum_{0<=k<=n, k mod q=r} c_k (n-2k)^j,
  0<=r<q, 0<=j<=2.

Two bags are distinct for this theorem when their terminal endpoint multiplicity histograms differ (not merely path identity within one endpoint). Define b_q(n) as the least equal mass m of two such bags with identical complete T-observation but different endpoint histograms, or infinity if none.

## Proof of optimality

Within each residue class r, columns are evaluations at distinct x_k=n-2k of [1,x,x^2], hence the block rank is min(3,#K_r), K_r={k in [0,n]: k mod q=r}. Total rank is min(n+1,3q) (PREFIX-442). For n<3q every block has at most three columns and zero kernel: endpoint histograms are uniquely determined at every mass.

When 3q<=n<4q, every block has at most four columns. A nonzero kernel of a four-column block is generated by the **primitive integer** vector (1,-3,3,-1), with increasing k. Any nonzero integral histogram difference has an integer multiple of this vector as its nonzero block, so both its positive and negative parts contain at least four atoms. Adding other blocks or common atoms cannot lower the bag mass. The displayed four-word bags attain this lower bound.

When n>=4q, two words remain uniquely recoverable in each sector from its first three moments: for two atoms at u,v, their sum M1 and product (M1^2-M2)/2 determine their unordered pair, and M0 handles zero/one atoms.
