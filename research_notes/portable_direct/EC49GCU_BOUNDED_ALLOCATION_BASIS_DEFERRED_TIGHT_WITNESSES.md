## 1. One-unit reserve theorem
Ports P have positive integer capacities c_p; nonempty supports E_j carry legal prefix counts v_j, so h_v(U)=c(U)-sum_{E_j subset U}v_j>=1 for every nonempty U. Available class i has m_i>0 remaining labeled candidates. Define lambda_i(v)=min_{U superset E_i} h_v(U). A batch of a of those candidates is legal iff 0<=a<=m_i and a<=lambda_i(v)-1. This follows by separating the cuts containing E_i (decrement by a) from all other cuts (unchanged). Thus maximal batch a_i=min(m_i,lambda_i(v)-1).

Retain GCR's root-independent integer allocation A, with row sums v_j, column sums at most c_p, support E_j; z_p=c_p-sum_j A_jp. The uncompressed quotient has port->class capacity A_jp, class->supported-port capacity M=1+sum c, port->sink capacity z_p; omitted zero-count classes/arcs are zeros. Add source->root ports M. Its max flow is lambda_i(v) by GCR (cut identity on all port sets). Solve only until value m_i+1 or no augmenting path. The obtained integer flow value F=min(lambda_i(v),m_i+1)>=1. Roll back exactly one unit on the last successful residual augmenting path, reversing its actual residual updates, including reverse arcs. This is legitimate since the last integral augmentation had size >=1. The remaining feasible flow g has value F-1=a_i.

Transport g directly to the new common allocation:
 A'_jp=A_jp-g(p,j)+g(j,p)+1_{j=i}g(s,p).
Arcs not present have zero flow. Each entry is nonnegative because g(p,j)<=A_jp. All terms respect E_j, and conservation at class nodes gives row sums v_j+1_{j=i}a_i (including when class i was already selected in the prefix). Conservation at each port gives increment of its column load exactly g(p,t)<=z_p. Thus A' is a full feasible allocation of the new legal prefix. There is no separate repair or cold recomputation for that prefix. Strict Hall legality follows from the maximal-batch formula, not merely ordinary assignment feasibility. The residual augmenting path is an execution certificate; no physical meaning is assigned to the rollback unit.

If a_i<m_i, then F=lambda_i(v)<=m_i, and the exhausted flow's source reachability BEFORE rollback gives the exact least rooted minimum port cut U_i. All cuts containing E_i decrease by the same a_i, hence U_i is the least rooted tight cut after insertion. By GCO it stays the least such cut after every subsequent legal addition. When a_i=m_i the solver can stop without obtaining lambda_i(v) or a least cut; it has certified the entire class is inserted. Do not report the truncated F as exact credit in this case.

For k active classes, b cache-hit classes below, and final greedy residual rank r=sum_i a_i, successful bounded-query augmentations are at most r+k-b: each noncached query has F=a_i+1 and each successful augmentation increases F by at least one. If the initial prefix S needs cold allocation, this costs at most |S| further successful augmentations. This is an integer-value operation bound, not a strongly polynomial bit-complexity or elapsed-time claim. Standard integral flow algorithm and explicit labeled supports remain available.

## 2. Exact credit cache, deferred structure
If a previous tight U contains E_i, then lambda_i(v)=1, since v is legal and h_v(U)=1. Therefore a_i=0 and no rooted flow or allocation repair is needed. Every old tight cut remains tight under legal additions (its credit cannot increase and cannot fall below one); keep an exact witness for this decision. However a containing tight U need not equal the least rooted tight U_i. The algorithm records the blocked root i, not an unproved exchange relation. Every positive-count auxiliary node reaches a port and every port reaches the sink by strict Hall; otherwise its nonempty reachable port side would have zero outgoing capacity, contradicting the quotient cut identity. Each deferred root has credit 1 at the final state, since its cached tight container persists. GCT identifies the smallest containing unit-bridge region and recovers the least optimal port cut by reachability after deleting that bridge. This is exactly the least cut at initial blocking by GCO persistence. Retain the final cut U_i and original support labels; use GCN/GCO base exchange E_j subset U_i for selected basis labels only (not prefix labels). This is a sequential greedy-basis execution improvement, not a replacement for the full future minor observer under arbitrary deletions.

Counterexample to a tempting shortcut: two unit-capacity ports a,b, one selected class with E={a,b} and count 1 allocated at a. Both U={a,b} and {a} are tight for root {a}. Deleting the unique outgoing arc of the larger tight container makes both ports reachable from a, while the least tight cut is {a}. ## 3. Unbounded operation-count separation
For GCN/GCO's distinct-support uniform family, d>=5 unit ports, 2d classes with one label each: first d supports omit distinct individual ports, next d supports omit each cyclically consecutive pair. It is U_{d-1,2d}. Use that order, zero prefix. The first d-1 classes are inserted and class d blocks with least tight cut P; the remaining d classes hit the cached P. Every sink edge in the cold rooted graph has unit capacity, so every augmentation adds exactly 1. At the first class the cold rooted flow has value d-1; all subsequent classes have cold rooted flow value d (for the first d classes compare their own omitted-port support and P, then after rank d-1 use tight P). For the last d classes the rooted credit is one and prefix count d-1. Total cold successful augmentations are exactly 2d^2-1. The new bounded solver needs two augmentations per first d-1 class and one at class d; all later classes are cache hits. Total 2d-1, plus one final index and witness reconstruction. Unit capacity also bounds each quotient augmentation by one. This proves an unbounded factor (2d^2-1)/(2d-1) in successful augmentation count, not a claim that the whole algorithm is linear or that witness cost is negligible. For large d repeated reachability of nearly identical final cuts dominates candidate edge scans; witness interning or batched SCC/closure queries are the next actual bottleneck.
