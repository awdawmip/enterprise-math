## R39.1 — Model and future observer

Let M=(S,Σ,{Δ_a},o) have a finite nonempty state set S, completely named physical event labels a∈Σ, nondeterministic partial successor sets Δ_a(s)⊆S, and a finite exact public readout o:S→O. Labels must retain source and signed Port whenever those are observable. From initial s the observer reads o(s); after each successful a-transition to t it reads the pair (a,o(t)). Define Γ=Σ×O and, for X⊆S,

\[
D_{(a,v)}(X)=\{t\in S:o(t)=v,\ \exists u\in X\text{ with }t\in\Delta_a(u)\}.
\]

Let D_w denote successive application of these set maps. The exact successful observed-future language is

\[
L(s)=\{w\in\Gamma^*:D_w(\{s\})\ne\varnothing\}.
\]

Two decorated states have the same declared observed future precisely when their initial outputs and their respective L-languages are both equal. This observer records *existential support*, not the number or weights of different paths producing one observed word.

## R39.2 — Exact finite equivalence, cutoff and erasure certificate

**Theorem.** For two finite systems A,B with the same output/label alphabets and respective initial states p,q, let m_A(p),m_B(q) count the distinct reachable nonempty subset states generated by D_w from {p},{q}.

1. The complete observed futures agree iff o_A(p)=o_B(q) and the nonemptiness predicates D_w({p})≠∅, D_w({q})≠∅ agree for every observed word w.
2. If initial readouts agree but full futures differ, there is a shortest distinguishing observed word of length at most m_A(p)+m_B(q)−1. Breadth-first search over pairs of reachable subsets constructs one. An immediate readout disagreement has witness depth zero.
3. In particular, a finite positive-depth witness has length at most 2^{|S_A|}+2^{|S_B|}−3.
4. A proposed state-erasure relation P⊆S×S is safe for *this exact observed trace-support semantics* iff each pair it identifies agrees in initial public output and full L-language. The greatest such equivalence yields a trace-support-preserving nondeterministic quotient. It may be strictly coarser than strong bisimulation.

**Proof.** Induction on word length proves that D_w({s}) is exactly the set of possible endpoints of paths emitting observed word w. Hence its nonemptiness is exactly the success predicate for w.

The powerset construction is a deterministic partial automaton whose reachable nonempty subsets are accepting states, where the empty subset is one shared absorbing reject sink. It has exactly L(s) as its success language. For the disjoint union of the two reachable subset automata, there are m_A(p)+m_B(q) accepting states and the single reject sink. The length-zero accepted-language partition has two blocks. Refining at each round by the successor block for every γ∈Γ stabilizes after at most m_A(p)+m_B(q)−1 strict rounds: no stable partition can have more than m_A(p)+m_B(q)+1 blocks; once a round makes no split, all later rounds are identical. At stability, the blocks are exactly full accepted-language equivalence. This proves the cutoff. The bound on m follows because a finite n-state system has at most 2^n−1 nonempty subsets.

For the erasure claim, necessity follows by contrasting the observed futures. For sufficiency, a class C of fully future-equivalent states has one initial output, and any representative r∈C has the same L. For each observed symbol γ, the union of suffix languages of all γ-successors from any such r is exactly the right derivative γ^{-1}L. Merging C and collecting successors from every representative therefore leaves this derivative unchanged. Induction on the suffix length establishes exactly the same supported words from C in the quotient; equivalence is maximal because any safe identification must preserve o and all observed futures. QED.

## R39.3 — Strict separation from branching bisimulation

Use six states p,q,u,v,z,t, identical readout o=0, and Σ={a,b,c}, with only these edges:

- p --a--> u and p --a--> v (two branches);
- q --a--> z;
- u --b--> t, v --c--> t;
- z --b--> t, z --c--> t; t terminal.

For both p and q the entire future trace-support language is exactly {ε,a,ab,ac}. Thus p and q are equivalent under R39, but not strongly bisimilar: z offers b and c, whereas neither u nor v can match both. A strong bisimulation's back-and-forth condition fails. An explicit greatest-bisimulation refinement and full successful-language enumeration are in the companion verifier. If individual a-branch source identities are observed, the original unlabelled branching example is not a valid source-erasure certificate under that richer alphabet.

## R39.4 — Exact computation

The Python 3 companion verifier uses integer bitset subset dynamics and two distinct finite tests: (i) a pair-product BFS for the minimum disagreement, and (ii) direct enumeration of every observed word up to the pair's own calculated cutoff. It performs:

- all 1,024 two-state, two-event, binary destination-readout nondeterministic machines (4^4 transition choices × 2^2 readouts), testing all 4 ordered initial pairs for each: 4,096 checks;
- 2,196 deterministically selected pairs from different machines, checking the same equivalence and minimum witness depth;
- the exact six-state trace-equivalent/non-bisimilar branching counterexample.

Every assertion returned PASS. Same-machine pairs: 2,448 equal; 1,024 first distinguished at initial readout; 624 first distinguished after one event. Selected cross-machine pairs: 837/1,177/58/2 first distinguished at depths 0/1/2/3, respectively, and 122 equivalent. The largest observed pair-specific cutoff was five. One explicit cross-machine witness has depth three, with alphabet-symbol sequence ((1,1),(0,1),(0,1)) and cutoff three. Those are finite computations; the symbolic theorem applies to any declared finite systems satisfying the hypotheses.
