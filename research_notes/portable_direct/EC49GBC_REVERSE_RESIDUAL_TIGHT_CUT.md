## Theorem and proof
For a finite bank of resources C of nonnegative integer capacity c, target set U and neighborhoods E(u), let M be any maximum capacitated matching of size h. Delete zero-capacity edges for the observer (zero-capacity coordinates may remain in the universe). For each resource j draw a directed arc j -> k whenever a target matched at j has k in its neighborhood. Let F be the resources with unused capacity. Let R be all resource vertices that can reach F along these arcs, including F itself. Then the largest maximizer W* of d(W)=#{u:E(u) subset W}-c(W) equals C minus R. This is independent of the chosen maximum matching.

For a maximizer W, Hall gives d(W)=|U|-h. Let f(W) count confined targets. At least f(W)-c(W) of them must be unmatched, and there are exactly |U|-h unmatched in total. Equality forces all unmatched targets to be confined to W, exactly c(W) confined targets to be matched there, all slots of W filled, and each occupant of W confined to W. Thus W contains no free vertex and is closed under outgoing arcs. No vertex of W can reach F, so W is contained in C minus R.

Conversely set W0=C minus R. Every resource in W0 is saturated and its matched occupants have no edge outside W0, by closure. Every unmatched target has its whole neighborhood in W0: otherwise a path from a neighbor to F would supply an augmenting path (take a simple resource path, use a distinct matched occupant for each arc, move them in reverse order, then insert the unmatched target). This contradicts maximality of M. Confined targets are exactly the c(W0) matched occupants plus the |U|-h unmatched targets, since a confined matched target must be assigned within W0. Hence d(W0)=|U|-h and W0 is the largest maximizer. This handles isolated targets, empty banks and zero capacities. In the zero-edge normalized convention, zero-capacity resource vertices are simply included in W0.

For fixed U_i and capacities in the GBB bank observer, use this exact W_i* in the GBB reduction. It inherits the full equality H(Q)=sum_i c_i(W_i*)+H'(Q) for every shared subset Q, not just the tested threshold; it is not permission to delete physical labels or arbitrary future edges. The construction is a residual-network realization of classical maximum matching/minimum cut structure, not a claim of novel general graph theory. Reuses the GBA capacitated matching and GBB supermodular cut observer, with exact integer labels.

After maximum matching, explicit reverse adjacency construction and reachability require O(E+m) graph operations, E=sum |E(u)|, m=|C|, with no slot expansion; deduplication optional. An augmenting DFS implementation is O(n(E+m)) graph operations in the worst case, n=|U|, since each resource is visited once per augmentation and its occupants' adjacency lists are scanned at most once. The bitmask implementation additionally pays bit width and Python integer/list overhead. In contrast the GBB subset zeta enumeration needs O(m*2^m+n) integer operations and O(2^m) storage. This is a polynomial alternative for wide banks, not a measured speedup at small m.

## Insertion corollary (proof, not separately tested)
For a new exclusive target u with neighborhood E, d_new(W)=d(W)+1_{E subset W}. If E is contained in W*, the maximum grows by 1 and its new largest maximizer is exactly W*: attaining the new maximum forces d(W)=delta and E subset W, so every such W lies in W*. If E is not contained in W*, no old maximizer contains E, and integer slack implies the new maximum remains delta; W* is still a maximizer and the new largest cut contains W*. Thus the blocked set is monotone under insertion of exclusive targets at fixed capacities. The rank increment is zero exactly in the first case. A successful insertion may grow the blocked set: one initially free unit slot becomes tight after insertion of a dedicated target. This is an exact update principle; an incremental implementation is a future question.
