## Fiber homothety: analytic proof

The law \(\sigma\) has mass \(1/4\) at \(k\in\{0,3,12,15\}\) \(the four (A=B=+1) atoms\) and zero elsewhere. At \(t=s=r\), regroup the 16 atom inequalities by the four pairs of \(A,B\in\{\pm1\}\). Nonnegativity throughout the \(A=B=-1\) class gives \(h\ge 2r-1\). Nonnegativity of the \((A,B)=(+,-)\) and (\(-,+\)) classes gives, respectively,

$$
|u+v|+|w-z|\le 1-h,\qquad |u-v|+|w+z|\le1-h.
$$

In the \(A=B=+1\) class each atom therefore obeys

$$
16\mu(k)\ge1+2r+h-|u+v|-|w+z|
\ge 2r-1+3h\ge4(2r-1).
$$

Thus for **every** \(\mu\in F_{r,r}\), \(\mu\ge(2r-1)\sigma\) coordinatewise. When \(r<1\),

$$
\nu=\frac{\mu-(2r-1)\sigma}{2(1-r)}
$$

is a nonnegative probability law in the same seven-character Fourier family with retained moments \(t=s=1/2\). Conversely, mixing any \(\nu\in F_{1/2,1/2}\) with \(\sigma\) in these proportions produces an element of \(F_{r,r}\). At \(r=1\) the same inequality forces \(F_{1,1}=\{\sigma\}\). This proves the claimed identity for all real (r).
