## Theorem 1: sharp minimal path-branch cardinality

For fixed nonnegative integer n, let b(n) be the least m for which two m-word bags with DIFFERENT TERMINAL ENDPOINT HISTOGRAMS have identical M0,M1,M2; let b(n)=infinity if none. Distinct ordered words sharing the same endpoint are the same atom for this particular endpoint-histogram question; path-provenance distinctions are separately typed. Then

    b(n)=infinity (0<=n<=2); b(3)=4; b(n)=3 (n>=4).

Proof. For n<=2 there are n+1<=3 possible terminal coordinates, and the matrix of powers 0,1,2 at these distinct coordinates has full column rank. At n=3 the moment-map kernel on terminal multiplicities is generated by (1,-3,3,-1) in k=0,1,2,3 order. Its positive and negative parts each have mass four, so an integer-multiplicity witness needs at least four words per side. The bags A3=(0,2,2,2), B3=(1,1,1,3) realize that threshold with four legal distinct ordered words on each side and common (M0,M1,M2)=(4,0,12), but M3(A3)-M3(B3)=48.

For arbitrary n, any two-word bag is uniquely determined as an unordered endpoint pair by M1 and M2: if the endpoints are u,v, their sum is M1 and their product is (M1^2-M2)/2, hence they are exactly the roots of a fixed quadratic. The one-word case is immediate. For all n>=4 choose

    A_n=(0,3,3),    B_n=(1,1,4),

where the entries are numbers k of negative steps. Each side is realized by three *distinct* native signed-step words: choose one all-positive and two different 3-negative words on A, and two different 1-negative and one 4-negative words on B. Binomial capacities suffice for every n>=4. Their k sums are both 6, and squared k sums both 18. Therefore, exactly,

    (M0,M1,M2)=(3,3n-12,3n^2-24n+72).

Their k-cube sums are 54 and 66. Expanding (n-2k)^3 yields

    M3(A_n)-M3(B_n)=(-2)^3*(54-66)=96.

Thus three paths suffice and two are insufficient. QED.
