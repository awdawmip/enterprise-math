## Exact dynamic observation and necessity
Let C be the fixed finite labeled resource set, c nonnegative integer capacities, A the current multiset of labeled unit-demand targets (possibly dependent or empty-neighbor), E(a) subset C. H(A) is the maximum matching count. Write f_A(V)=#{a:E(a) subset V}, delta_A=|A|-H(A)=max_V(f_A(V)-c(V)). Define s_A(V)=c(V)-f_A(V)+delta_A and g_A(S)=min_{V superset S}s_A(V). Thus s_A>=0, g_A(empty)=0, g_A is monotone. Since f_A is supermodular, s_A is submodular. Given minimizing supersets V,W for S,T, V union W and V intersection W are feasible for S union T and S intersection T, so g_A is also submodular. This uses only the fixed matching model.

For any finite collection Q of new labels, Hall gives H(A+Q)-H(A)=|Q|-max_V(f_Q(V)-s_A(V)). Replacing s_A(V) by g_A(V) preserves this max: for any V choose a minimizing superset W; f_Q(W)>=f_Q(V), and s_A(W)=g_A(V). The reverse inequality uses g_A<=s_A. Consequently the complete future batch-rank observer is
H(A+Q)=H(A)+|Q|-max_S(f_Q(S)-g_A(S)).
All original resource identities and future masks are interpreted on C. No matching witnesses are encoded by this observer.

For q identical new masks E (distinct fresh labels), the increment is min(q,g_A(E)): cuts containing E contribute q, others zero, and the normalized maximum is max(0,q-g_A(E)). Taking q=c(C) recovers g_A(E) (since g_A(E)<=s_A(C)=c(C)-H(A)). Thus, for fixed C and capacities, two states have identical all-future insertion rank increments iff their g tables coincide. If absolute ranks are observed, also retain H(A). This is minimal as an observational equivalence class, not a lower bound on bit length. It applies to arbitrarily long insertions of fresh targets; arbitrary deletion, edge edits, old-target subset queries interleaved with new insertions, and witnesses are not covered. For old-label subsets retain GBI's static observer and original masks as needed.

## Exact one-mask update
For insertion of mask E let h=1_{g_A(E)>0}. Then H'=H+h and delta'=delta+1-h. Hence s'(V)=s_A(V)+1-h-1_{E subset V}. Minimizing gives
g'(S)=min(g_A(S)+1-h, g_A(S union E)-h).
If h=0, g_A(E)=0; submodularity and monotonicity imply g_A(S union E)=g_A(S), so g'=g_A. If h=1, g'(S)=min(g_A(S),g_A(S union E)-1). Therefore successful insertions consume a boundary slack unit and unsuccessful insertions leave the entire future increment observer unchanged, not just the present rank. No target-level graph rebuild is needed to apply this algebraic update.

## Sparse maximal equal-level envelope
Define P(g)={(W,g(W)): every strict superset V of W has g(V)>g(W)}; checking immediate supersets suffices by monotonicity. For every S there is a maximal superset W with g(W)=g(S), hence g(S)=min_{(W,v) in P, W superset S} v. Every listed W is necessary in this particular upper-envelope representation using actual g-valued masks, since no other superset can supply g(W). It is not necessarily a globally shortest representation; with no old targets and positive capacities g=c is modular but this envelope has 2^|C| entries.

For h=1 reweight each old pair by v'=v-1_{E subset W}; for h=0 keep P. The new table is the min over the reweighted superset pairs: distributing the min over witnesses for g(S) and g(S union E) gives the update formula above. Prune any pair dominated by a strict superset with no larger reweighted value. Any remaining pair (W,v') has actual g'(W)=v' and all strict supersets have greater g', since otherwise their minimizing witness would dominate W. Conversely every canonical g' mask must be present among the old witnesses or its value would be achieved by a strict superset. Thus support(P(g')) subset support(P(g)); no new mask is needed during fixed-capacity insertion. Current implementation expands then prunes with a min-zeta pass O(m 2^m) where m=|C|, and scans the sparse pairs for a single increment.
