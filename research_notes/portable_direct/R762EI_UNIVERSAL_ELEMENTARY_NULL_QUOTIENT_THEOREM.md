## Proof

### 1. Exact 8q-column representation

The two cyclic directions of each K_J span a subgroup of order q². The subgroup generated by three times the first direction and the entire second direction has order q²/3. Every distinct translate of each annihilator is therefore specified by a fiber \(\ell_j=t\pmod q\) and stripe \(m_j=b\pmod3\), with

| j | \(\ell_j(x,y,z)\) mod q | \(m_j(x,y,z)\) mod 3 |
|---|---|---|
| A | x | y+z |
| B | y | x-z |
| C | z | x+y |
| D | x-y+z | y+z |

Write \(L_{j,t}=1_{\ell_j=t}\) and \(H_{j,t,b}=1_{\ell_j=t,m_j=b}\) as integer coefficient functions on G. The actual translated integer annihilator is

\[
A_{j,t,b}=3H_{j,t,b}-L_{j,t}.
\]

Since \(\sum_{b=0}^2 A_{j,t,b}=0\), the 8q columns with b=0,1 generate precisely I over the integers. Let A denote the q³×8q integer matrix with these columns.

Modulo 3 each column equals \(-L_{j,t}\). The sum of four families of coset indicators has rank exactly 4q-4: a relation \(f(x)+g(y)+h(z)+k(x-y+z)=0\) has four degrees of freedom, comprising three free constants (the fourth determined by their sum) and one shared additive homomorphism \(Z/q\to F_3\). This follows by shifting x,y,z and observing their constant differences. Thus

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{F_3}(A\bmod3)=4q-4. \tag{1}
\]

### 2. First 3-adic Smith residual

For an integer matrix A, let \(V_0=\operatorname{im}(A\bmod3)\). Any integer column combination v with Av divisible by 3 defines the well-defined class \((Av/3)\bmod3\) in \(F_3^{q^3}/V_0\). In Smith normal form the dimension of its image is exactly the number of nonzero invariant factors with 3-adic valuation ONE: factors prime to 3 contribute to V_0 and valuation-at-least-two factors vanish at this first level.

The difference of two original columns is

\[
A_{j,t,0}-A_{j,t,1}=3(H_{j,t,0}-H_{j,t,1}).
\]

At each stripe residue s=0,1,2, the function \(1_{s=0}-1_{s=1}\) takes values 1,-1,0, which equals \(1+s\) in F3. Hence the original mod-3 column space together with these first Smith residuals spans exactly

\[
W_q=\left\{\sum_{j=A}^D [f_j(\ell_j)+\bar m_j g_j(\ell_j)] :
f_j,g_j:Z/q\longrightarrow F_3\right\}. \tag{2}
\]

The decisive lemma is

\[
\boxed{\dim_{F_3}W_q=8q-12\text{ for every }q=3^a.} \tag{3}
\]

### 3. Twelve-dimensional base case q=3

All maps F3→F3 are polynomials of degree at most two. Put

\[
U=x(y+z),\quad V=y(x-z),\quad
B_A=x^2(y+z),\quad B_B=y^2(x-z),\quad B_C=z^2(x+y).
\]

The following twelve functions are a basis of W_3:

\[
1,x,y,z,x^2,y^2,z^2,U,V,B_A,B_B,B_C. \tag{4}
\]

The A/B/C families generate these twelve. The D family adds nothing: for t=x-y+z and s=y+z, polynomial identities in F3 are

\[
\begin{aligned}
t^2&=x^2+y^2+z^2-U-V,\\
st&=U-y^2+z^2,\\
st^2&=B_A+B_B-B_C+y+z.
\end{aligned}
\]

The polynomials (4) are independent as functions because reduced monomials (each exponent at most two) form the standard basis of functions on F3³, and the quadratic and cubic leading terms displayed above are independent. Thus dim W_3=12.

### 4. Every relation for q>=9 descends to q=3

Consider any relation among the eight q-indexed function families of W_q:

\[
F_A(x)+sG_A(x)+F_B(y)+bG_B(y)+F_C(z)+cG_C(z)+F_D(t)+sG_D(t)=0,\tag{5}
\]

where \(t=x-y+z\bmod q\), \(s=\overline{y+z}\), \(b=\overline{x-z}\), \(c=\overline{x+y}\). Let Δ be an argument shift by 3 modulo q.

Shift x by 3. Holding x and t fixed while varying z makes \(s=\overline{x-t+2z}\) take all three values. Therefore \(\Delta F_A=d,\Delta F_D=-d,\Delta G_A=e,\Delta G_D=-e\) for constants d,e∈F3. Shifting y by 3, and using the independent variation of b=x-z and s=y+z at fixed y, yields \(\Delta G_B=0,e=0,\Delta F_B=-d\). Shifting z by 3 gives \(\Delta G_C=0,\Delta F_C=d\).

Thus all four G functions depend only on their argument modulo 3. For canonical representatives 0<=r<q the F functions have the forms

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_A(r)&=f_A(r\bmod3)+d\lfloor r/3\rfloor,\\
F_B(r)&=f_B(r\bmod3)-d\lfloor r/3\rfloor,\\
F_C(r)&=f_C(r\bmod3)+d\lfloor r/3\rfloor,\\
F_D(r)&=f_D(r\bmod3)-d\lfloor r/3\rfloor.
\end{aligned} \tag{6}
\]

Since q/3 is divisible by 3, write x=x0+3X, y=y0+3Y, z=z0+3Z (x0,y0,z0∈{0,1,2}) and substitute (6) into (5). The result is

\[
H(x_0,y_0,z_0)-d\kappa(x_0,y_0,z_0)=0,\quad H\in W_3,\tag{7}
\]
where \(\kappa(x_0,y_0,z_0)=\lfloor(x_0-y_0+z_0)/3\rfloor\bmod3\). Wraparound by q changes the floor by a multiple of q/3, hence does not affect this F3 identity.

Define the five-point functional on F3³,

\[
\mathcal L(H)=-H(0,0,1)+H(0,2,2)+H(1,0,2)+H(1,2,1)+H(1,2,2).
\]

Direct substitution in all twelve polynomials (4) proves \(\mathcal L(W_3)=0\), while \(\mathcal L(\kappa)=1\). Applying it to (7) forces d=0. Consequently every relation at q is the pullback of a q=3 relation: its kernel dimension equals 12. The eight function families have domain dimension 8q. This proves (3) for q>=9 and completes the universal lemma.
