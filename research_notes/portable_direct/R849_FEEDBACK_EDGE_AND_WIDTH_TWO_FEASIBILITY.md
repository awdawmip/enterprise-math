## 1. Positive theorem: feedback-edge conditioning

Use exactly the R848 finite edge-list model: each edge of the bipartite scope/anchor incidence graph has an explicit list of original witnesses (k,p), with nonnegative integer k and rational p>=1. Scope resource is b+sum k<=B, anchor resource Q=P0 prod p<=U<=2-eta, P0>=1. Objective C=sum phi(Q), phi(Q)=(Q-1)/(2-Q). Let F be any feedback edge set, r=|F|, D=max list size. A spanning forest gives F by deleting all nonforest edges, r=E-V+components (isolated vertices included).

Enumerate the product of the lists on F. For each assignment, add its k to the incident scope's base b and multiply its p into the incident anchor's base P0. Keep all vertices, ceilings and the anchor objective exactly once, including isolated nodes. Remove F and apply R848's forest solver with relative objective error eps. Reject an invalid base or an infeasible forest; recover the forest witness and reattach the fixed edge witnesses. Select the minimum exact total cost among all feasible outputs.

Proof: for each fixed assignment on F, the forest and the original conditioned feasible set are in bijection. All original constraints and the objective agree exactly. The optimal original assignment belongs to one of the enumerated branches; the R848 theorem gives a feasible output of cost <=(1+eps) OPT in that branch. Every returned output is originally feasible. Thus feasibility is decided exactly and the global approximation ratio is unchanged, including zero optimum. All products in updated bases have polynomial bit length (each deleted factor appears once), and the forest solver's positive cost lower bound and safe upper bound remain computable in polynomial bits. Total work is <=D^r poly(input length,1/eps), using R848's per-branch bound; no extra error factor or radius loss beyond R848. The result is polynomial for fixed r, and FPT in the combined parameter (r,D); it is not claimed FPT in r alone for unbounded D. In particular unicyclic components can each be handled with one edge choice independently, with total time the sum of their solve times, not their product. More generally disconnected components optimize independently, and the max component feedback count suffices in the exponent. No new end-to-end implementation of the cut solver is claimed here.

## 2. Negative theorem: treewidth two is not enough for exact feasibility

In the explicit finite edge-list model, deciding feasibility is NP-complete already on K_{2,n}, with two analytic anchors and n scope vertices, two choices per edge, common R=2, fixed eta=1/2 and fixed ceilings 3/2. All edge choices can be generated by actual single-label pendant activity factors and dyadic Kraft lengths. This is a weak NP-hardness proof from PARTITION, not a strong NP-hardness claim or a native Port realizability claim.

Take positive integer PARTITION weights a_1,...,a_n of even total W=2T (multiply all original weights by 2 if needed). Set M=16(W+1)^2; this M is a scaling denominator, NOT the Kraft depth. Define x_i=a_i/(M-a_i)>0 and b_i=x_i/8. Each scope i has two private labels, one at each anchor, and fixed boundary consumption 1 in depth-2 units. Each private label has lengths 1 or 2. At R=2, activity b_i R^(L+2) is x_i or 2x_i. The two list options are
 short: k=2, p=1+x_i=M/(M-a_i);
 long: k=1, p=1+2x_i=(M+a_i)/(M-a_i).
 Scope budget B=4, boundary b=1. Both short is forbidden (1+2+2=5); one long or both long is allowed. If length 0 is also allowed it cannot be feasible with the other label and the boundary. These options are genuine positive rational pendant factor witnesses with polynomial bit length.

Set P=prod_i M/(M-a_i). Relative to short on all edges at an anchor, each long choice multiplies its payment by (1+a_i/M). Let theta=1+(T+1/2)/M and U=P theta. Choose the same fixed base P0=(3/2)/U at both anchors. Then actual ceiling Q<=3/2 is exactly equivalent to
 prod_{i long at this anchor}(1+a_i/M)<=theta.
 We check P0>=1 below.

For any subset I, with sum s, write u=s/M<=W/M. The expansion into elementary symmetric polynomials gives 1+u<=prod(1+a_i/M)<=1+u+u^2/(1-u); the degree-j elementary sum is bounded by u^j, hence the bound. With M=16(W+1)^2 the tail is strictly <1/(2M), since 2W^2 < M-W. Therefore the product ceiling is equivalent to s<=T for integer s: if s<=T the upper bound is <theta, if s>=T+1 the lower bound is >theta.

To verify P0>=1, note prod(1-a_i/M)>=1-W/M by induction. Hence P<=1/(1-W/M). Also theta<=1+(W+1)/(2M), so U<= [1+(W+1)/(2M)]/[1-W/M]<3/2 (equivalent to 4W+1<M). Thus P0>1 and common ceiling 3/2 is valid. All bases and ceilings are positive rationals of polynomial bit length: the product has O(n log M) bits.

Every scope forces at least one long. If the network is feasible, the total weight of long choices across the two anchors is >=W. Each anchor has weight <=T, so total is exactly W; every positive-weight item has exactly one long, giving a partition. Conversely a partition gives one long at the selected anchor per item and a short at the other; both anchor ceilings and all Kraft constraints hold. This is a polynomial many-one reduction. Membership in NP follows by exact product/sum comparisons with polynomial-length rational arithmetic. For n>=2 the incidence graph has treewidth 2: bags {A1,A2,S_i} form a path decomposition; it contains a cycle and cannot have width 1. The result applies also with a fixed number (two) of analytic anchors. It rules out a polynomial exact-feasibility FPTAS in input length and 1/eps on this class unless P=NP, even though the ordinary objective-only error is allowed. It does not rule out algorithms with controlled capacity relaxation, promise gap, or radius slack. The exact-feasibility threshold has exponentially small possible gap in bit length; no stronger inapproximability is inferred. This also explains why independently keeping just one resource at a width-two separator is insufficient as a general proof strategy.
