## Exact model and observation contract

There are `p` named binary sources `x_i in {0,1}`, with provenance-retaining state `x=(x_0,...,x_{p-1})` and injective integer readout `U(x)=sum_{i=0}^{p-1} 2^i x_i`, `0<=U<2^p`. In each heartbeat at most one named bit flips; no-op is allowed. The initial complete state is unknown. At consecutive heartbeats the observer reads `c_t(S+U_t) mod q_1`, then `c_{t+1}(S+U_{t+1}) mod q_2`, with known baseline `S`, `c_t` a power of 2, and both moduli odd. Therefore modular inversion of `c_t` reduces observations exactly to `U_t mod q_1` and `U_{t+1} mod q_2`. We require unique **terminal complete named state** for every legal trajectory, not reconstruction of the earlier state or path.

## Theorem 858-A (exact two-reading orbit criterion)

Set `N=2^p`, `q_2=N-1`, and let `q_1>1` be odd. Put
`A_p(q_1)={0} union {2^i mod q_1:0<=i<p}` and `r=(N-1) mod q_1`.
The ordered two-reading scheme `(q_1,N-1)` identifies the terminal source vector for all legal trajectories **if and only if**
`r notin A_p(q_1)+A_p(q_1) (mod q_1)`.

**Proof.** Two distinct terminal values in `[0,N-1]` can share a residue modulo `N-1` only if they are exactly `0` and `N-1`. The set of permissible first-step predecessors of terminal `0` is `{0,2^0,...,2^{p-1}}`; that of terminal `N-1` is `{N-1,N-1-2^0,...,N-1-2^{p-1}}`. Their residues modulo `q_1` are `A_p(q_1)` and `r-A_p(q_1)`, respectively. These are disjoint exactly when `r` is absent from the pairwise modular sumset `A_p+A_p`. If disjoint, the first observation resolves the only terminal collision left by the second. If not, the matched first predecessors and the two distinct terminals form a legal indistinguishable trajectory pair. All steps retain the named bits and their legal one-flip relation. QED.

## Theorem 858-B (infinite Mersenne-orbit construction)

For **every integer `k>=4`** and `p>=k` satisfying `p congruent k-1 (mod k)`, the ordered scheme
`q_1=2^k-1`, `q_2=2^p-1`
has exact terminal recovery. The first read is `k` bits, and the second read is `p` bits.

**Proof.** Since `2^k=1 mod q_1` and `p congruent k-1 mod k`, the first predecessor residue set is `A={0,1,2,4,...,2^{k-1}}`, while `r=2^{k-1}-1`. The integer `r` has `k-1>=3` consecutive 1s in binary. The sum of two elements of `A` is either zero, one power of two or the sum of two powers of two, hence cannot equal `r`. Further, every such nonzero sum is at most `2^k`; the next integer congruent to `r` is `r+q_1=3*2^{k-1}-2>2^k` for `k>=4`. Thus `r` is absent from `A+A mod q_1`; apply Theorem 858-A. QED.

## Theorem 858-C (exact four-bit family and conditional optimality)

For every `p>=4`, the ordered pair `(15,2^p-1)` succeeds **if and only if `p congruent 3 (mod 4)`**. In particular, for all `p>=7` with that congruence, a constant four-bit first observation followed by a `p`-bit observation cold-starts the terminal complete named state.

For the same fixed second modulus `q_2=2^p-1`, **15 is the least eligible odd first modulus** for all `p>=7, p congruent 3 (mod 4)`.

**Proof.** For `p>=4`, `A_p(15)={0,1,2,4,8}` and `r` cycles through `0,1,3,7` for `p mod 4=0,1,2,3`. The sumset `A+A` is `{0,1,2,3,4,5,6,8,9,10,12}` modulo 15; among the four possible `r`, only 7 is absent. For minimality, test the smaller odd choices `q=3,5,7,9,11,13`. For `p>=12`, their sets `A_p(q)+A_p(q)` cover every residue: `2` generates all nonzero residues for `q=3,5,11,13`; for `q=7`, `{0,1,2,4}+{0,1,2,4}` covers all seven; for `q=9`, `{0,1,2,4,5,7,8}` already has full modular sumset. The two remaining admissible `p` values below 12 are 7 and 11; direct residue witnesses for all six smaller odd moduli are generated by the exact checker. QED.

## Example, relation to PREFIX-857, and costs

For `p=7`: `(q_1,q_2)=(15,127)`; first predecessor residues for terminal 0 are `{0,1,2,4,8}`, and for terminal 127 are `{3,5,6,7,14}`. They are disjoint. Thus exact cold-start terminal state is recovered with **4+7=11 raw bits** across two observations, versus the elementary `7+7=14` construction from Theorem 858-B using `k=7`; for `p=11,15,19,...`, the first width remains 4 bits.

The predecessor PREFIX-857 proves that for `p=5`, an unrestricted-time four-bit controller cannot cold-start, and the unique admissible late-odd two-reading pair with both moduli <32 is `(17,31)`. The present `p=7 mod 4` family is a different, infinite arithmetic configuration and does not revise that `p=5` result.

The second modulus `2^p-1` always costs `p` bits, and at least two observations are needed with this second modulus because it alone confuses terminal 0 and `N-1`. This family optimizes the **first modulus subject to the fixed second modulus**, not the joint width or total-bit objective over all two-read schemes.
