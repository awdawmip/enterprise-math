## 1. Definitions

Let G=(U,V,E) be a finite **named bipartite multigraph**; parallel edges have distinct names. All edges are oriented row-to-column; B is the signed vertex-edge incidence matrix. Fix capacities K_f in Z_{\ge 0}, signed integral margin vector b, and a nonempty bounded integer fiber

\[
 F(b,K)=\{x\in\mathbb Z^E: Bx=b,\ 0\le x_f\le K_f\}.
\]

For a feasible seed x, its directed residual graph D_x contains named arc u→v when x_f<K_f, and v→u when x_f>0. Both directions are possible. A zero-capacity edge has no residual arc.

Pin one named edge e at the *feasible* count c=x_e. The pinned fiber is F_{e=c}={y in F(b,K): y_e=c}; its residual graph at x, for permissible *movable* coordinates, is D_x^{-e}, obtained by deleting **both** residual arcs of e. The pinned incidence term B_e c is subtracted from the movable margin vector.

For any integer observer matrix A in Z^{k×E}, its **difference subgroup** is

\[
 L_A(F)=\langle A(y-z):y,z\in F\rangle_{\mathbb Z}\subseteq\mathbb Z^k.
\]

This subgroup does not claim that every congruent observation value is *attained*.

## 2. Exact residual cycle-lattice lemma

**Lemma 813.1.** Let C run over the SCCs of D_x. Let H_C be the undirected *named* multigraph of positive-capacity edges whose endpoints both lie in C. With Z_1(H_C;Z)=ker_Z(B_{H_C}) its integral incidence cycle lattice, embedded by zeros into the full edge space,

\[
 \boxed{L_A(F)=A\left(\bigoplus_C Z_1(H_C;\mathbb Z)\right).} \tag{813.1}
\]

Consequently any spanning forest of each H_C supplies a finite integral fundamental-cycle basis whose A-images generate the exact observer-difference subgroup.

*Proof.* For any feasible y, d=y-x satisfies Bd=0. Its positive edge differences use forward available residual arcs; negative differences use reverse available arcs. These nonnegative arc multiplicities form a directed integer circulation on D_x, decomposable into directed cycles. Every cycle lies inside one SCC, proving inclusion in the RHS.

Conversely, fix an integral incidence cycle d on H_C. Each positive-capacity undirected edge has at least one residual arc in D_x. Select one available arc for every edge and represent d as an integral *signed* arc circulation f. Because C is strongly connected, each selected arc lies on some directed cycle. The sum T of one such directed cycle per selected arc is a nonnegative circulation **positive on every selected arc**. For sufficiently large integer M, f+MT is nonnegative. Decompose f+MT and MT separately into directed cycles; their difference expresses f as an integer linear combination of directed cycles. A simple directed residual cycle gives an admissible unit move from x, because its required increases/decreases have slack and its incidence sum vanishes. The harmless two-arc reversal of a single named edge gives zero net change; a two-cycle on distinct parallel edges is a genuine move. Thus the image of every integral cycle belongs to the group generated by feasible table differences. The incidence cycle lattice has the integral spanning-forest fundamental-cycle basis even for named parallel edges. QED.

**Exact scalar formula.** For w in Z^E, choose a spanning forest of the H_C and assign signed tree-path potentials p_v, adding +w_f in the row-to-column direction and -w_f in reverse. For every non-tree internal SCC edge f=(u,v), its fundamental-cycle weight is w_f+p_u-p_v. Thus

\[
 \boxed{g_w(F)=\gcd\{|w_f+p_u-p_v|:\ f\text{ a non-tree internal SCC edge}\}},\tag{813.2}
\]

with gcd(empty)=0 and L_w(F)=g_w(F) Z. All arithmetic is integral; g=0 means the zero subgroup.

## 3. One-pin local repair theorem

**Theorem 813.2.** For a feasible seed x and a named edge e=(u,v) pinned to x_e:

1. D_x^{-e} has an SCC partition **refining** the old partition; it can split but never merge SCCs.
2. If u,v are in **different** old SCCs, then e already has constant count on all of F (PREFIX-812); hence the pin is redundant, F_{e=x_e}=F, the observer-difference lattice remains identical, and no SCC changes.
3. If u,v are in the **same** old SCC C, only C can split. Every other old SCC remains strongly connected. The new observer lattice is obtained by retaining all unchanged SCC fundamental-cycle contributions, replacing the old contributions from C with the fundamental-cycle contributions from the new SCCs within C, with e deleted from the movable edge set.
4. For every integral A,
\[
 \boxed{L_A(F_{e=x_e})\subseteq L_A(F).} \tag{813.3}
\]
For scalar w, old g divides new g, allowing new g=0; if old g=0 then new g=0.

*Proof.* Arc deletion cannot create a path, so it cannot merge SCCs. A path inside an old SCC that exits and re-enters that SCC would induce a directed cycle in the old condensation DAG; therefore all original within-SCC paths can be chosen internally. Deleting an edge wholly inside C leaves every other old SCC intact, and deleting an inter-SCC edge cannot split any SCC. PREFIX-812 proves that an edge joining different original SCCs has the same count throughout the entire original feasible fiber, establishing redundancy.

For an internal pin, the movable graph is exactly D_x with the pinned arcs deleted; all internal graphs outside C are unchanged. Apply Lemma 813.1 separately to the old and the new feasible fibers. The corresponding direct-sum cycle generators differ only in the affected C contribution, which must be replaced by its new internal-SCC generators. Inclusion (813.3) also follows immediately from F_{e=x_e} being a subset of F. Subgroups of Z are gZ, yielding scalar divisibility, with g=0 handled as the zero subgroup. QED.

**Algorithmic consequence:** after computing an initial feasible seed, SCCs and named-cycle generators, a pin between distinct old SCCs requires no lattice/SCC repair. For a pin internal to C, perform SCC and fundamental-forest processing only in the old C and recombine its new lattice generators with cached outside contributions. Local graph work is O(|V_C|+|E_C|), not including integer lattice-normalization/bit costs, initialization, observer output recovery, or native event-admission checks. No universal wall-clock speedup is asserted.

## 4. Two exact witnesses

**813.A — SCC unchanged yet lattice strictly refines.** One row and one column joined by three distinct named parallel edges a,b,c, all with capacity 2 and total margin 3. Take seed (1,1,1), observer W=x_a+2 x_b. The unpinned fiber has difference group Z because exchange a↔c changes W by 1. Pin a=1: b↔c exchanges change W by 2, so its new difference group is 2Z. Both old and new residual graphs have **one SCC** because b,c still admit both arc directions. Thus unchanged SCC partition is not a sufficient test for unchanged observer lattice: the local fundamental-cycle generators matter.

**813.B — SCC split after pin.** Complete 2×2 unit-capacity graph with row/column margins 1 and seed x=(1,0,0,1), row-major named edge order. The old fiber has x and y=(0,1,1,0), and one residual SCC. Pin edge (0,0) to 1, leaving only x and four singleton residual SCCs. With W=x_00+x_11, the old difference group is 2Z and the pinned difference group is {0}. This is a change of fiber, not a contradiction to PREFIX-812's invariance *inside* one fixed fiber.
