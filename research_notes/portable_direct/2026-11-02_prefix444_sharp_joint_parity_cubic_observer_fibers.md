## The precise question and observer

A source bag consists of m **distinct** ordered native n-step words in the alphabet (+e1,-e1), each with unit positive weight. The number k of negative steps determines its terminal chart-relative displacement x=n-2k, with capacity 0<=c_k<=binomial(n,k). Bags are different only when their terminal multiplicity histograms differ. All their paths lie in native signed-X6, without introducing a new spatial axis or claiming a specific heartbeat-time dynamics.

Define the parity-sector quadratic BRC observer
\[
T_{r,j}(c)=\sum_{\substack{0\le k\le n\\k\equiv r\ (2)}}c_k(n-2k)^j,\quad r=0,1,\quad j=0,1,2,
\]
and one ordinary global cubic observer
\[
U_3(c)=\sum_{k=0}^n c_k(n-2k)^3.
\]
Let J=(T_{0,0..2},T_{1,0..2},U_3). Write b_J(n) for the least m such that two m-word legal bags with distinct endpoint histograms have the same J; put b_J(n)=infinity if no such bags exist.

## Main theorem — exact sharp thresholds at every length

For all integers n>=0,
\[
\boxed{b_J(n)=\begin{cases}
\infty&0\le n\le6,\\
8&n=7,8,\\
6&9\le n\le13,\\
4&n\ge14.
\end{cases}}
\]
Each finite threshold is attained with genuine distinct native signed-step words and unit positive branch weights. The conclusion concerns the specified observer and admissible finite source bags, not all native X6 observables, all possible future operations, or physical residual attribution.

### n<=6: complete observer

In each parity sector, the quadratic observer has evaluation columns (1,x,x²). There are at most three distinct k-values in either sector when n<=5, hence it is injective. At n=6, the even sector has exactly four k-values and a one-dimensional third-finite-difference kernel while the odd sector has only three. The global cubic on four equally spaced distinct x-values has nonzero third finite difference, so U3 eliminates the one-dimensional kernel. J is injective on all histograms for n<=6.

### n=7: primitive rank-one integer kernel

For n=7, both parity sectors have four endpoints. Each sector's quadratic kernel is the primitive integer vector (1,-3,3,-1) on increasing k. The cubic response of either such kernel is +384, since consecutive k in the sector differ by two and x differs by -4. The joint kernel is generated by
\[
h=(1,-1,-3,3,3,-3,-1,1)
\]
indexed by k=0,...,7, with positive and negative masses both equal to 8. Its first coordinate is 1, forcing every integral joint-kernel element to be a multiple of h. Therefore no smaller positive-unit source bags collide. The explicit eight-word witness is
\[
A=(0,3,3,3,4,4,4,7),\qquad
B=(1,2,2,2,5,5,5,6).
\]
Entries specify negative-step counts, not repeated path identities; capacities binomial(7,k) allow all required words.

### n=8: complete three-kernel analysis

The even-sector quadratic kernel has integer basis
\[
v_0=(1,-3,3,-1,0),\quad v_1=(0,1,-3,3,-1)
\]
on k=0,2,4,6,8, and the odd sector kernel has generator w=(1,-3,3,-1) on k=1,3,5,7. Every integral quadratic-kernel element has unique coefficients a,b,c in Z. Each basis vector's U3 response is +384; thus J requires a+b+c=0. Set s=a+b=-c. The even vector equals
\[
(a,\ s-4a,\ 6a-3s,\ 3s-4a,\ a-s).
\]
If c=0 and the vector is nonzero, b=-a and its positive mass is 8|a|. If |c|>=2, the odd-sector positive mass alone is 4|c|>=8 and the even vector must be nonzero. If |c|=1, the odd positive mass is 4 and s=+/-1. For any integer a,
\[
|a|+|a-s|\ge1,\quad |6a-3s|\ge3,\quad
|s-4a|+|3s-4a|\ge4.
\]
The even vector has sum zero, so its positive mass is half its L1-norm, at least 4. Every nonzero integral joint-kernel vector therefore has positive mass >=8. The n=7 witness remains legal at n=8 by appending one +e1 step to every word. Hence b_J(8)=8.

### 9<=n<=13: exact bounded integer moment lemma

For two bags sharing T, each parity sector has the same branch count, first and second moments. Bags of at most two branches in a sector are uniquely determined from these moments (the unordered two endpoints are determined by their sum and sum of squares). Thus if both sectors differ, there are at least three words in each, and m>=6.

Suppose exactly one sector differs and m<=5. Since the other is identical, equality of U3 gives equality of that sector's third moment. Translate its k-grid to l=(k-r)/2. At n<=13, l belongs to the finite grid {0,...,6}. The affine substitution x=n-2r-4l is invertible on the first four power sums, so equality of sector moments up to cubic is equivalent to equality of the first three power sums of l and the branch count. On that finite grid all multisets of mass m<=5 have distinct triples of power sums. A complete exact integer enumeration examines respectively 7, 28, 84, 210 and 462 combinations-with-repetition for m=1,...,5, without a collision; the accompanying executable certificate independently checks this finite lemma. Consequently J distinguishes all bags with m<6.

For n>=9, the six-word pair
\[
A=(0,3,3,6,6,9),\qquad B=(1,2,2,7,7,8)
\]
has equal parity-sector quadratic moments and equal global cubic. Both bags are legal at n=9, and remain so after appending common positive steps for every n>=9. Thus b_J(n)=6 for 9<=n<=13.

### n>=14: cubic sparse-source boundary

If a collision had mass m<=3, either both parity sectors differ, requiring at least three words per sector and hence m>=6, or exactly one sector differs. In the latter case the common zero-to-third moments determine the multiset of at most three endpoints by Newton's elementary-symmetric formulas. This proves injectivity at m<=3 for every n. For n>=14,
\[
A=(0,6,8,14),\qquad B=(2,2,12,12)
\]
are legal four-word bags, all in the even sector, with identical moments through degree three. At n=14 their T_even=(4,0,400), T_odd=(0,0,0), U3=0. Appending +e1 steps preserves all equalities at all subsequent n. Therefore b_J(n)=4.

## Two observers are strictly incomparable before joining

At n=7, four-word bags A=(0,3,4,7), B=(1,1,6,6) share **ordinary global moments through degree three** (4,0,100,0), but parity-sector quadratic moments differ. Conversely A=(0,4,4,4), B=(2,2,2,6) share parity-sector quadratic moments ((4,4,52),(0,0,0)), while their global cubic moments differ by exactly +384. These two witnesses remain valid for every n>=7 upon common positive-step extension. Thus the additional coordinate of J genuinely preserves an otherwise lost joint relation; adding only the total cubic does not make every parity-sensitive observer redundant.

At n=7, exact rational evaluation ranks are: parity-quadratic 6; global moments through cubic 4; joint J 7; parity-sector cubic 8 (the full endpoint-count dimension). This is linear information rank, separate from the positive integer sparse-source threshold of 8.

\[
T'_{r,j}=\sum_{t=0}^j {j\choose t}
\bigl(\alpha T_{r,t}+\beta(-1)^{j-t}T_{1-r,t}\bigr),\quad j\le2,

\[
U'_3=\sum_{t=0}^3{3\choose t}
(\alpha+\beta(-1)^{3-t})M_t.
