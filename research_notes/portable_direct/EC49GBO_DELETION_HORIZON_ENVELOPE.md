## 1. Exact fixed-support deletion horizon
Let C be a finite fixed labeled port set, c_i nonnegative integer capacities, A a finite labeled target family with neighbor masks E_x subset C, and f_D(W)=number of x in D with E_x subset W. Write raw cut weight s(W)=c(W)-f_A(W). For any deletion set D subset A and net newly inserted target family Q disjoint from A, put s_D,Q(W)=s(W)+f_D(W)-f_Q(W). The minimum over W determines Hall deficit and H=|A|-|D|+|Q|+min_W s_D,Q(W); the normalized port observer is min_{W superset S}s_D,Q(W)-min_W s_D,Q(W). Labels remain available for deletion and physical witness recovery.

For a raw weight t, let P(t) be masks W such that t(W)<t(T) for every strict superset T. The canonical sparse support of the suffix minimum is exactly P(t): if W is strictly better than all its strict supersets, the suffix minimum there equals t(W) and is strictly below every strict-superset suffix minimum. Conversely if this fails there is a strict superset attaining a value no larger than t(W), so W is redundant. Each omitted row has a dominating strict superset and finite ascent reaches a retained row.

For d>=0 define R_d=union_{D subset A, |D|<=d} P(s+f_D). This is the unique minimum fixed *raw-mask subset* preserving every suffix-minimum readout for all such D and arbitrary finite Q under raw reweighting (not an information-theoretic byte lower bound). Proof: for a given D, every W not in P(s+f_D) is dominated by a retained strict superset T with no greater weight. Since f_Q(T)>=f_Q(W), the same dominance persists after arbitrary net insertion. Thus R_d suffices. Necessity follows by taking Q empty and the deletion set witnessing W in P(s+f_D); only W realizes the strict minimum at query S=W. All these statements remain valid for nonnegative capacities including zero, duplicate masks, empty neighborhoods and empty deletion. Mixed sequences with at most d original labels absent simultaneously and arbitrary inserted labels present/absent are represented by net D,Q, provided the net label operations and counts are retained; do not destructively discard R_d after a transient query.

General exact witness criterion for W in R_d: there exists D of <=d original labels outside the class E_x subset W such that for every strict superset T of W,
    |{x in D: E_x subset T}| >= 1+s(W)-s(T).
Targets already contained in W cancel in the difference and may be omitted. Nonpositive right sides impose no condition. This is a finite simultaneous multicover condition; no efficient general algorithm is asserted. Safe (potentially larger) envelope: retain W if no strict superset T has s(T)<=s(W)-d. For any removed W, such T still dominates after any allowed D and Q, since f_D(T)-f_D(W)<=d; finite ascent terminates. The d=0 case recovers GBJ/GBK.

## 2. Complete one-deletion classification and efficient construction
Let u(W)=min_{T strictly superset W}s(T), +infinity for full mask. If s(W)<u(W), retain W. If s(W)>u(W), integer weights and a single deletion imply at least one superseding row still has <=weight, so W never revives. In the tied case s(W)=u(W), let K(W)=intersection of all strict supersets attaining u(W). Then W revives exactly when there is an original label x with E_x subset K(W) and E_x not subset W. Indeed all tied supersets must gain one more unit than W. All originally strictly larger supersets remain strictly larger by monotonicity of f_D. This is both necessary and sufficient. Thus compute R_1 directly from a suffix min/intersection transform and a scan of distinct original neighbor masks; no need to enumerate a separate new observer per deleted label for construction. Straightforward cost O((n+p)2^n) integer/mask operations for n ports, p distinct masks, plus raw cut construction; width/memory O(2^n) rows, not polynomial in n. Validation independently enumerates all distinct deletion masks.

## 3. Unrestricted old-label deletion requires the original effective adjacency relation
For fixed port capacities and known original label set, equality of all possible matching-capacity observations after arbitrary old-label deletions and future insertions implies equality of every original label's neighborhood restricted to positive-capacity ports. Proof: delete all other original labels, leave x. For any positive-capacity port i, append singleton-neighborhood probes filling exactly every capacity except one slot at i (total c(C)-1 probes). The final matching capacity is c(C) iff i in E_x, and c(C)-1 otherwise, because probes can all be matched and filling all slots requires all probes and x. If total capacity zero the effective adjacency is empty. Conversely effective adjacency and known labels suffice by Hall. Consequently arbitrary old-label removal cannot be supported solely by the insertion-only normalized observer. Example: two unit ports, two labels x,y, each assigned its own port; swap labels between ports. Both systems have H=2 and g identically zero, but delete x and insert a singleton on the first port to distinguish. This is an operation-typed information boundary, not a claim about physical states or native coordinates.
