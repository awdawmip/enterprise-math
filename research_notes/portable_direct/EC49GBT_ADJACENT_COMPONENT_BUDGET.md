## Exact adjacent cut factorization
Fix W, residual ports R with positive integer capacities, finite labeled projected masks (empty masks impose no constraints and are retained at zero deletion cost), finite nonnegative real weights. Move i in R into W. Define S as the labels with current mask exactly {i}. Form components B_j of R minus i using the nonempty projected masks E_x minus i as hyperedges; isolated vertices count. Let X_j be the labels whose nonempty projected mask is in B_j. These are disjoint groups. Retain all original labels/masks for other observers.
For I subset of retained labels, let t=|I intersect S|, I_j=I intersect X_j. For V subset B_j let f'_j(V) count I_j labels whose projected mask lies in V, and f^0_j(V) count those whose original mask (not containing i) lies in V. Define q_j=max_{V subset B_j}(f'_j(V)-c(V)), including empty V so q_j>=0. This is the ordinary Hall unmatched count for the projected labels (not the strict Hall deletion threshold).
Claim: original I is strict Hall feasible iff (a) every nonempty V subset B_j has f^0_j(V)<=c(V)-1 and (b) t+sum_j q_j<=c_i-1.
Proof: For a nonempty cut not containing i, split it across B_j; f is the sum of f^0_j of its pieces. Individual strict inequalities imply the global one, and restriction gives necessity. For a cut containing i, write it as {i} union disjoint V_j. Its count is t+sum f'_j(V_j) and capacity is c_i+sum c(V_j). The maximum over independent choices V_j is t+sum q_j. This proves equivalence, including empty remaining R. All nonempty projected labels retain identities.
After moving i, singleton labels S become irrelevant and all other groups are strictly feasible independently on B_j. Define G_j as maximum retained weight of X_j under this projected strict Hall. Define F_j(b), b a nonnegative integer, as maximum retained weight of X_j in the original local masks on B_j plus a copy of i with capacity b+1. By the equivalence this exactly imposes local (a) and q_j<=b. Therefore the original weighted retained optimum (ignoring initially empty masks) equals max of sum F_j(b_j) plus retained singleton weights subject to sum b_j+t<=c_i-1. Each retained subset of S with fixed size t is optimally chosen by highest weights. After projection the optimum is sum G_j+sum_{x in S} w_x. Unaffected groups not containing any mask incident to i satisfy F_j(b)=G_j for all b and cancel in the adjacent difference.

## Two-sweep capacity rank law
At any positive weight layer (or any fixed subset of X_j), let r(b) denote maximum cardinality under local capacity b+1. GBP packing theorem: deletion count is maximum sum of [1+f(V)-c_b(V)] over disjoint nonempty cuts; empty packing permitted. In any such packing at most one cut contains i. Thus the objective is a constant or constant minus b, with coefficient determined only by whether the packing contains i. Taking maxima separately gives rho(b)=max(A,B-b) where A is the best no-i packing (>=0), B the best i-containing packing evaluated at b=0 (possibly negative). For b>=m where m is the local label count, i-containing constraints are automatically satisfied (c_i=b+1), so rho(infinity)=A. It follows exactly that
r(b)=r(0)+min(b,r(infinity)-r(0)).
Use finite b=m to compute infinity. Applies also to empty groups and arbitrary capacities at other ports. This is a rank law, not a claim that the same selected labels stay optimal as b changes.
GBR weight-layer identity now yields for each local group, with r_t the rank of labels of weight >=t, d_j(t)=r_t(infinity)-r_t(0):
F_j(b)=F_j(0)+sum_{k=1}^b mu_{j,k}, mu_{j,k}=integral_0^infty 1_{d_j(t)>=k} dt.
Each sequence mu is nonnegative and nonincreasing in k, has at most m_j nonzero terms, and is computable from two descending-weight greedy sweeps using GBQ oracle (capacity 1 and m_j+1). Finite distinct weights turn the integral into a finite sum. The code uses every prefix, with zero weight differences on tied internal prefixes.
Pool the finite marginal sequences and all weights of S, take largest c_i-1 entries, or all if fewer, and call their sum T. T is achievable with prefix selection per component, by breaking equal ties by increasing marginal index, and arbitrary subset of singleton labels. Any budget allocation selects at most c_i-1 such entries so cannot improve it. Hence old optimum is sum F_j(0)+T. The adjacent difference has the exact signed decomposition
rho_w(W+i)-rho_w(W) = sum_j(F_j(0)-G_j) + T - sum_{x in S}w_x.
Each F_j(0)-G_j>=0: a projected strict-feasible selection lifts to old capacity-1 i with (a) and q_j=0. Thus there are three distinguishable mechanisms: loss of local strict slack, loss of shared capacity credit, and relief from disappearing singleton labels. GBS star example has k independent single-port groups, F_j(0)=1, G_j=0, no singleton labels, T=0, giving k even though c_i=1. A single port with two singleton labels of weight 1 and capacity 1 gives difference -2. No capacity-only signed bound. If i has no incident labels, exact difference is zero.
Computational construction: connected components, preserve labeled masks, two old capacity sweeps and one projected sweep per group, evaluate marginals, sort pool, select budget. Naive marginal integration O(sum m_j^2), pool size <= total labels, sort O(m log m). Each sweep uses capacity-level double augment (no capacity-slot expansion), with naive mask scanning and occupants O(m_j^2(|B_j|+1)) in unit-cost mask/occupancy arithmetic; include bit cost, mask projection, component building and output recovery. Current Python code performs simple scans; no general paired speedup claim. Local groups unaffected by i are mathematically reusable, but persistent across-cut caching and all-edge complexity benchmarks are not implemented.
