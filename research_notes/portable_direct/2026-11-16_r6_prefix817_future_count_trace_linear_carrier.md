## 1. Exact named-source dynamics

Let \`m>=1\`, \`0<=q<=m\`, with nonnegative integer weights \`alpha_i\`, \`beta_i\` for i=1,...,m. The legal named states are subsets \`S\` of \`[m]\` with \`|S|<=q\`, exactly those in the PREFIX-815 named hub fiber. The weighted adjacency matrix \`Q\` is indexed by such states and assigns \`Q[S,S∪{i}]=alpha_i\` for inactive i when |S|<q, and \`Q[S,S\{i}]=beta_i\` for active i. A path's weight is the product of its step weights and alternatives sum, retaining exact integer multiplicities. The count readout matrix \`C[S,k]=1_{|S|=k}\` has columns \`k=0,...,q\`. The entire length-h terminal-count histogram from S is the S-row of \`G_h=Q^h C\`. Here \`h\` is a formal number of toggles, not physical time.

## 2. Theorem 817.1: a finite certificate for all future count observations

Let \`N=sum_{k=0}^q binom(m,k)\` and define the integer observability matrix

\`O_{N-1}=[C | QC | ... | Q^(N-1) C]\`.

For two legal named states S and T, their terminal-count histograms agree for **every** \`h>=0\` if and only if their rows in \`O_{N-1}\` agree. Moreover, \`V=span_Q{columns of C,QC,...,Q^(N-1)C}\` is Q-invariant, and its dimension \`d=rank_Q(O_{N-1})\` is the smallest possible dimension of a linear, exact future-count realization that supports arbitrary initial rational (hence integer) row masses. A rational-basis realization of dimension d exists; a basis-free sufficient realization retains the complete \`N\` states.

**Proof.** Equality of all horizons implies the finite equalities. By the Cayley–Hamilton theorem for the N×N integer matrix Q, \`Q^N\` is a rational/integer linear combination of lower nonnegative powers through \`Q^(N-1)\`. Multiplying this recurrence by every higher power and by C proves that zero differences through horizon N−1 remain zero at every horizon. The same recurrence gives Q-invariance of V. Choose a basis matrix \`E\` for V; because \`QE=ER\` for some d×d rational R and \`C=EC_*\`, we have \`Q^h C=E R^h C_*\` for every h. Every initial row mass µ therefore maps to the d-coordinate row µE and produces precisely µQ^h C. Conversely, any linear realization of dimension r factoring every initial mass-to-entire-future-output map has image rank at most r. The latter map's rank is d by its finite observability columns, so r>=d. QED.

An exact incremental stopping certificate is available: if the column spans \`V_t=span{C,QC,...,Q^t C}\` and \`V_{t+1}\` have the same rational dimension, then \`V_t=V_{t+1}\` and \`QV_t⊆V_t\`; all later columns lie in that same space. This can stop earlier than the general N−1 bound. Linear compressed states should be distinguished from a positive **named-state partition** quotient: a rational basis may have signed coordinates. The explicit q=1 construction below is nonnegative and integral.

## 3. Theorem 817.2: exact three-moment BRC carrier at hub capacity one

Assume q=1, arbitrary m>=1 and arbitrary nonnegative integral named weights. Write a row of initial path mass as \`µ=(µ_empty, µ_{ {1} },...,µ_{ {m} })\`. Define

\`p0=µ_empty\`, \`p1=sum_i µ_{ {i} }\`, \`pβ=sum_i beta_i µ_{ {i} }\`,
\`A=sum_i alpha_i\`, \`B=sum_i alpha_i beta_i\`.

Then the exact one-toggle propagation of these **three** integers is

\`(p0',p1',pβ')=(pβ, A*p0, B*p0)\`.

At every horizon h, the aggregate terminal-count histogram is \`(p0^(h),p1^(h))\`. This holds for any initial integer mass vector and hence all named initial states, with no floating-point or approximation. The \`pβ\` coordinate records the source-removal moment, while \`B\` retains an alpha–beta joint interaction not reconstructible from A and an unweighted total alone.

**Proof.** From q=1, every singleton {i} has precisely one outgoing named edge: removal to empty with weight beta_i; empty can only add i with weight alpha_i. Thus \`µ'_empty=sum_i beta_i µ_i\`, and \`µ'_i=alpha_i µ_empty\`. Summing the latter both with and without beta_i proves the recurrence. The two exposed count totals are p0 and p1. Induction proves all-horizon equality. QED.

**Minimal linear dimension.** If m>=2 and the beta_i are not all equal, then \`d=3\`. If they are all equal, or if m=1, \`d=2\`. At q=0, \`d=1\`. Indeed, the column span generated by C includes the empty-state indicator \`e0\` and the all-singleton indicator \`u\`. Applying Q to e0 yields the singleton beta-vector \`v\`; and \`Qu=A e0\`, \`Qv=B e0\`. This span is invariant. If beta is nonconstant, e0,u,v are independent and all occur in the observability span by h=1; otherwise v is a multiple of u. At q=0 there is one state. These give both matching upper and lower bounds, by Theorem 817.1. In particular at q=1 the minimal linear realization is certified by horizons 0 and 1.

## 4. Theorem 817.3: minimal state-partition quotient at q=1

A nonnegative weighted **partition** of named states respecting current count and all future terminal-count histograms must keep the empty state separate. Two singleton states {i},{j} can occupy one class if and only if \`beta_i=beta_j\`. Thus the unique coarsest such partition has \`r+1\` classes, where \`r\` is the number of distinct beta values, even when alpha values are arbitrary.

**Proof.** If beta_i differs from beta_j, the horizon-one weight from the respective singleton to count zero differs, so the states must be separated. If beta_i=beta_j=b, both singleton states send exactly b aggregate weight to the unique empty class, and no other positive-weight outgoing class; hence the partition grouped by b is weighted equitable. The empty state sends weight \`A_b=sum_{i:beta_i=b}alpha_i\` to class b. Multiplication of its explicit nonnegative quotient adjacency preserves every count terminal histogram at every horizon. QED.

The minimal **linear** dimension and minimal **state-partition** cardinality are distinct exact resources. For example with m=6, q=1, alpha=(1,2,3,4,5,6) and beta=(0,1,2,3,4,5), the named graph has 7 states, the minimal state-partition quotient still has 7 classes, while the integral BRC moment recurrence has 3 coordinates, with A=21 and B=70. Its input transform from named µ costs O(m) integer operations and O(1) additional storage after weights are known. Each subsequent formal toggle costs O(1) integer arithmetic operations. The arithmetic bit length grows with the horizon and weights; no universal bit-complexity or native physical speedup is asserted.

## 5. A positive-weight later-horizon witness at q=2
