## 1. One-scan bridge marking theorem
Let G be a finite directed multigraph with positive integer arc capacities, sink t, and all vertices able to reach t. Reverse all arcs (preserving individual arc identity) to obtain a flow graph H rooted at t. In its dominator tree T let S(v) be the subtree of v, including v. A vertex v dominates x iff every t-to-x path in H passes v. This is the usual dominator tree; the immediate-dominator routine here is the established Cooper–Harvey–Kennedy iterative RPO algorithm (not a new discovery), see Keith D. Cooper, Timothy J. Harvey and Ken Kennedy, A Simple, Fast Dominance Algorithm, Rice TR-06-33870, https://www.cs.tufts.edu/~nr/cs257/archive/keith-cooper/dom06.pdf . The new step is its exact use for the GCS rooted credit and least cut observer.

For every nonroot v, every arc entering S(v) from outside must end at v. Indeed if a->b enters at a proper descendant b, since a is outside S(v) there exists a t-to-a path avoiding v; appending that arc reaches b without v, a contradiction. There is at least one entering arc by reachability. Thus count q(v) of such arcs is exactly the number of incoming arcs a->v with a outside S(v).
If q(v)=1, its sole arc e is necessary to reach every x in S(v); any x outside S(v) admits a path avoiding v and hence avoiding e. So deleting e disconnects exactly S(v). Conversely if deleting e=(a,v) disconnects any vertex x, every t-to-x path contains e and thus v; a simple such path shows v also becomes disconnected (otherwise append its suffix), and a is outside S(v) (its prefix avoids v). If there were another incoming arc a'->v from outside, t reaches a' without v and thus without e, reaching v without e; contradiction. Therefore e is a bridge iff q(v)=1 and a is the sole entering tail. Mark v only if this arc has capacity 1. This characterizes all GCS nonempty unit bridge domains exactly, including parallel arcs. In original orientation the bridge is v->a.
Euler entry/exit times characterize subtree membership in constant time. After dominators, a single scan of arcs computes all q(v) and sole edge IDs; no edge subdivision, per-edge deletion, or stored domain masks are required. Per-edge deletion below is an independent experimental audit only.

## 2. Two exact query implementations
By GCS, a nonempty root E has credit 1 iff it is contained in some marked subtree. In that case the smallest containing marked subtree is unique and its sole edge e* gives the least optimal cut as forward reachability from E in G-e*. Otherwise credit>=2; use exact GCR maxflow for the value and least witness.
Version A: take the LCA of all vertices of E in T, then its nearest marked ancestor. Binary lifting gives O(|E| log N) query for edge selection, O(N log N) table and build, plus witness scan O(N+M).
Version B: use tin extrema lo and hi of E. A subtree is an interval [tin,tout) and contains E iff it contains both extrema. Start at the nearest marked ancestor of lo, ascend marked ancestors (nearest[par[v]]) until the interval contains hi, or the chain ends. The first match is precisely the smallest containing domain. This avoids binary lifting entirely. Index storage is O(N) words beyond adjacency and the dominator computation; edge selection O(|E|+b_depth), where b_depth is the number of marked ancestors examined. A forward reachability scan still reconstructs the exact least cut when needed. No claim of an entire-state quotient: keep original labelled support, capacities, allocation and higher-credit observer; revalidate/rebuild after state changes. This is structural, not physical residual attribution.

## 3. Complexity and implementation
N vertices, M positive arcs, B marked domains. GCS explicit domains use B*N boolean entries and O(b*(N+M)) deletion reachability work (b unit arcs). The new bridge marking after computing dominators is O(N+M); variant B total auxiliary index storage O(N) plus graph O(N+M), and variant A has the above table cost. The actual iterative dominator solver uses RPO sweeps with ancestor-path intersections; report its measured sweeps, predecessor scans and intersect steps rather than claim linear total time. For P sweeps a safe simple bound O(P*(N+M*N)) covers path climbs; no worst-case improvement of dominator algorithm claimed. GCS correctness theorem provides least witness; direct reference below separately checks the full rooted observer. For a unit chain of n non-sink vertices, deleting each arc entails sum_{i=1}^n i = n(n+1)/2 reverse adjacency visits (including the skipped arc), whereas the new solver needs one reverse DFS n scans, two RPO sweeps 2n predecessor scans, one bridge scan n, zero intersection steps; tree work O(n). This is an exact operation-count separation, not elapsed time. For n=500 the formula gives 125250 versus 2000 adjacency/predecessor/bridge scans, plus linear tree operations. Variant B does not store the n*(n+1) explicit boolean entries of this chain. Both variants verified chain domains independently at n=100 and n=500.
