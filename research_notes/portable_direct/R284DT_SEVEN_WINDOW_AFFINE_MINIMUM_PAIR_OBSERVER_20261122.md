### Proof

For a fixed arc (a,w), 0<=a<P and 0<w<P, let z=(x-a) mod P. The original directed-arc distance equals 0 for z<=w and otherwise min(z-w,P-z). Unwrapping at the circular boundary gives at most four half-open nonzero affine pieces per arc, each slope +1 or -1, together with the zero plateau. Exactly the same integer breakpoints are produced by the frozen R284DD/R284DS construction: for k=-1,0 put b=a+w+kP, e=a+(k+1)P, m=floor((b+e)/2), and clip the intervals [b,m+1), [m+1,e+1) to [0,P).

For a claimed window [L,U] construct the finite verification vertex set consisting of L,U and, for every distinct breakpoint t with L<t<=U, both t-1 and t. Between successive vertices within any one event slab, the distance of every individual original arc, as well as the claimed s*x+c, is integer affine. Consequently the two inequalities d(x,A_l)=s*x+c and s*x+c>d(x,A_j) for each j != l hold at every integer interior position if they hold at all such vertices. This follows because their differences are affine on each slab, hence their minimum on its closed integer endpoints is attained at an endpoint.

The exact checker evaluates all 64 original arc distances at respectively 2,7,2,5,6,5,5 vertices in the listed seven windows, totaling 32 vertices / 2,048 point-to-arc operations. At every vertex, the claimed label equals the original direct argmax, and the exact minimum competing margin is the positive integer in the table. These finite integer checks therefore certify the **entire seven integer intervals**, rather than only the 512 constructed endpoints. No floating-point tolerance is used.
