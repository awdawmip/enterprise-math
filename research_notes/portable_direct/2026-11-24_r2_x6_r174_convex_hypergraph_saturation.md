## Theorem: arity-three convex-hull obstruction, exact leaf relaxation

Any binary linear-comparison leaf (strict and complementary nonstrict halfspaces) has a convex normalized parameter region. A necessary condition for points S to share a leaf outputting o is conv(S) subset R_o on that normalized simplex. The condition is sufficient for the existence of a convex subset containing S on which o is legal, not for realizability as a leaf of the permitted finite query tree.

Every failure of this condition has a witness involving at most three endpoints. Proof: choose r in conv(S) outside R_o. In the normalized affine parameter domain of dimension two, write r as a convex combination with a minimal number k of positive coefficients. If k>3, those points have an affine dependence with coefficients c_i summing to zero. Vary the combination coefficients along this dependence up to the first zero coefficient while keeping all coefficients nonnegative; the represented point stays r, contradicting minimality. Hence k<=3. Equivalently in unnormalized parameters, positive combinations of at most three endpoints suffice, since the output is scale invariant. This covers strict boundary points without changing their status. For k=1 the endpoint itself disallows o; k=2 gives the prior segment obstruction; k=3 captures a genuine triangle obstruction not implied by edges.

Thus for endpoints with a common unique legal output, define a hypergraph of invalid singleton/pair/triple hulls (discard hyperedges containing smaller hyperedges). Different singleton outputs require disjoint leaves, so add the group counts.
