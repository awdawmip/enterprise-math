## 1. Factorwise exchange without global product
Under GBJ/GBK/GBL, let g(S)=sum_l g_l(S intersect C_l), each normalized monotone integer submodular port function with canonical sparse min-envelope P_l. A successful new target E has g(E)>0. Restrict to D, the union of active factors (those with g_l(E intersect C_l)>0), and replace E by E intersect D; all other factors stay unchanged. On D, g'(S)=min(g(S),g(S union E)-1). For a nonnegative integral virtual-probe multiplicity z with z_i<=c_i, define
a_l(z)=min_{(W,v) in P_l} [v-z(W)],
d_l(z)=min_{(W,v) in P_l, E intersect C_l subset W} [v-z(W)].
Then z is independent for g' iff sum_l a_l(z)>=0 and sum_l d_l(z)>=1.
Proof: independence is z(S)<=g'(S) for every S. Expand g and g(S union E) by their sparse min-envelopes. For each envelope term on a global support W, nonnegativity of z implies the strongest inequality over S subset W occurs at S=W. Thus the first family is z(W)<=sum_l v_l for all choices of W_l; the second is z(W)<=sum_l v_l-1 for all choices with E intersect C_l subset W_l. The two respective minima separate across factors. Empty E_l has no filter. In particular a_l<=0 because g_l(empty)=0 and z>=0. No Cartesian product or dense table is required to check the inequalities. The same unshifted test suffices to construct the initial decomposition from one sparse support.

Compute a greedy basis b' by querying g' on prefixes (two sums of old factor queries). For each pair i!=j with b'_i>0 and b'_j<c_j, use the above feasibility test on z=b'-e_i+e_j. GBL's virtual-probe fundamental-exchange theorem then gives the unique finest additive partition of whole ports. Edges already internal to a union-find component can be skipped. This recovers the new factors before constructing their supports. It also handles zero-capacity ports and rank-zero singleton factors. With m ports and L=sum_l |P_l|, a naive scan of all exchanges costs O(m^3 L) scalar operations (mask summation costs O(m)), besides union-find and bit costs. Greedy queries cost O(m L) mask operations. No polynomial bound on the eventual support lengths is asserted.

## 2. Reconstruct only new local factors
For a new factor F within D, project each old mask W_l to W_l intersect F. For branch 0 retain minimum weight on duplicate masks then prune dominated masks. For branch 1 first restrict old masks to those containing E_l, then project and prune. The two branch functions are represented by the Cartesian products of these projected local supports, adding their weights, and subtracting 1 from the second branch. Combine by minimum on identical masks and discard dominated strict subsets. This is exactly g' restricted to F: for each S subset F the eligible projected supersets are precisely those old masks that contain S intersect C_l (and E_l for branch 1). Minima separate before the outer minimum. Dominated terms cannot lower any query. If g'(F)=0, monotonicity gives the single coordinate (F,0), with no product. For one active factor use GBK direct sparse reweighting and GBL split, as implemented. Because the partition is proved additive and the reconstruction preserves every restricted query, the complete observer remains exact for any future insertion sequence by induction (H updated by 1 or 0 each time). Products may still be large inside a new coupled factor; no universal speed or storage improvement. No global dense-table expansion occurs inside push; initialization and independent verification do use dense reference. The counter named global_product_avoided counts the potential active support Cartesian size summed over multi-active updates, not a measured number of saved operations. local_product_pairs counts intermediate products in both branches; they can exceed the former counter.
