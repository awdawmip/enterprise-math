## Theorem 1: port factorization from one greedy base
Let C be labeled ports with integer capacities c_i>=0, A fixed old targets, H their maximum matching rank, and g the GBJ normalized port function, a normalized monotone integer submodular function. For fixed A and arbitrary future target insertions g is sufficient with H by GBJ/GBK. Write P for GBK's canonical maximal equal-level support.
Expand each port i into c_i distinct virtual singleton probe labels. The rank of a subset of probes of multiplicities z is the increment of matching rank when adding those singleton targets. This is a matroid rank: the capacitated matching matroid on old plus probe labels is transversal after slot expansion, and contraction by A (not necessarily independent) gives the incremental matroid. GBJ yields rank(z)=min_W [g(W)+sum_{i outside W} z_i]. In particular z independent iff z(W)<=g(W) for every W; it suffices to check W in P because each other W has a support superset with the same g. Also g(S) is exactly rank of all virtual copies in S: choosing W=S yields <=g(S); the reverse bound follows from g(S)<=g(W)+c(S minus W), by submodularity and g({i})<=c_i. The latter inequality follows by extending a zero-slack minimizing cut for the empty set by i. Thus no virtual copies need be instantiated.

Choose any port ordering and let b_i be greedy increments g(prefix_i)-g(prefix_{i-1}). By submodularity b is nonnegative, b_i<=c_i, b(W)<=g(W) (sum the marginal bounds along W), and b(C)=g(C). Select b_i virtual copies in each port as a basis B. Compute tight support family T={W in P:b(W)=g(W)}. For i!=j, b_i>0, b_j<c_j, one exchange b-e_i+e_j is feasible iff every W in T containing j also contains i. Indeed all other inequalities either stay fixed or decrease or have at least unit slack. Equivalently the bitset signature of j in T is contained in that of i. Connect i and j by an undirected edge for every such exchange; retain isolated ports (including zero capacities).
Then the resulting connected components C_l are the unique finest partition of whole ports for which g(S)=sum_l g(S intersect C_l) for all S. Proof: on virtual labels the fundamental circuit graph of any matroid basis has components equal to its finest direct-sum factors, as in GBH: each component is spanned by its basis portion, ranks of whole components add to total, and submodularity implies every subset rank adds. Every additive partition must keep every circuit together, hence every fundamental exchange edge stays within a part. Identify copies of each port (join the fundamental components containing them); cross-port edges are exactly the tests above, since copies at a port have identical exchange feasibility. A port partition additive for all unions of port copies is also additive for every individual-copy subset: equality of full component ranks implies all-subset additivity by submodularity. This proves necessity and sufficiency and independence from the chosen greedy order. Zero-capacity ports contribute rank zero and are isolated.
The restriction to a component D is obtained by projecting each support W to W intersect D, keeping minimum duplicate weight, then removing dominated projections. Indeed min over projected supersets is the original g on D. Conversely the global maximal equal-level support is exactly the Cartesian product of each factor support (all strict extensions increase at least one factor). Global support count is product, separate count sum; factor storage can exceed global storage for small/zero factors, so select representation by measured cost. This is a scope-typed dynamic insertion observer, not an arbitrary old-target subset/deletion observer.

## Theorem 2: insertion coupling locality
Given the above partition and fresh target E, let J be the indices with g(E intersect C_l)>0 and D the union of their factors. If J empty, h=0 and g unchanged. Otherwise h=1. For any inactive factor, g(T union (E intersect C_l))=g(T) by monotonicity/submodularity and zero singleton-set rank. Substitution into GBJ gives
g_new(S)=g(S minus D)+min(g(S intersect D),g((S union E) intersect D)-1).
Hence unchanged factors remain exactly separable and only D needs recomputation and possible repartition; no global product is needed outside D. If E touches at least two live factors, any partition of the old active factors into two nonempty groups has a cross-group coupling witness: the two disjoint E portions have positive old ranks, each keeps its rank alone after the insertion (the other contributes >=1), whereas the union loses 1. This rules out retaining the old active split; it does not require every old port in D to stay in one new factor. E.g. a rank-two uniform port matroid on three unit ports contracts a singleton probe, leaving that port a loop and a rank-one factor on the other two. Future repartition may split old factors.
The current code validates the locality identity but does not implement an end-to-end incremental factored updater. Theoretical exchange tests use O(m*L) scalar mask work for greedy queries and tight sums plus O(m^2) bitset subset tests (charge L bits/signature), where L is current support size. Current code intentionally uses dense cut_costs(b), O(2^m), and quadratic projection dominance, plus dense reference and exhaustive verification. No actual runtime advantage asserted.
