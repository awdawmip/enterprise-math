## R50.1 — Exact correlated-error certificate

Fix a candidate integer q>=1. Write v_q=(qS,(q-1)S,2(q^2-1)P,2qP). Define the rational/real closed half-plane polygon

  K_q(z,eta) = {(S,P): S>=0, P>=0, |(z_i-z_j)-(v_q(S,P)_i-v_q(S,P)_j)|<=2eta for all 0<=i<j<=3}.

**Theorem (necessary and sufficient).** The measured z is generated by some positive-real alpha,beta and common b with that q if and only if K_q contains a point with

  S>0, P>0, S^2>=4P.                         (1)

**Proof.** Let residuals r_i=z_i-v_q(S,P)_i. There exists b satisfying |r_i-b|<=eta for all i exactly when the intervals [r_i-eta,r_i+eta] have nonempty intersection, equivalently max r_i-min r_i<=2eta. The six pairwise differences in K_q express precisely this condition. Positive alpha,beta exist exactly when S>0,P>0,S^2>=4P, because they are the two positive real roots of t^2-St+P=0. The shared offset may be chosen from the common interval, e.g. b=(max r+min r)/2; epsilon_i=r_i-b. QED.

**Exact finite vertex test.** For q>=1 the polygon K_q is bounded whenever nonempty: x-y bounds S, and d-h bounds P because 2(q^2-q-1) is a nonzero integer for every positive integer q. The polygon is the intersection of twelve rational half-planes and S,P>=0. Let V be its complete vertices, computed by pairwise boundary-line intersections, retaining exactly those satisfying every inequality. Then (1) holds if and only if either

(A) some vertex (S,P) has S>0, P>0, S^2>=4P; or
(B) there is a vertex with S>0,P=0 and another vertex with P>0.

For proof, g(S,P)=S^2-4P is convex; its maximum on a compact polygon is attained at a vertex. A strictly positive feasible point with g>=0 implies a nonnegative-g vertex. Such vertices can be positive-positive (A), or be at P=0 with S>0, or the origin. An origin-only nonnegative-g maximum gives no positive-positive feasible point because every nontrivial convex combination with g-negative vertices is strictly negative. At an S>0,P=0 vertex, joining any P>0 vertex supplies small positive P with S>0 and g>0, proving (B). Conversely (A),(B) explicitly construct a feasible point. This covers degenerate segments and singleton polytopes. All comparisons can be exact rationals for rational z and eta. In case (B), choose a sufficiently small positive rational interpolation weight; strict g>0 then also permits choosing rational positive source weights close to the corresponding real roots.

## R50.2 — Finite exhaustive Port bound when the X-Y gap is separated from noise

Let s=x-y, k=x-h, e=d-h. Assume s>2eta. For any feasible q>=3, exact underlying values obey

  S>=s-2eta, 0<P<=(e+2eta)/(2(q^2-q-1)),
  qS-2qP <= k+2eta.

Thus E=e+2eta must be positive, and since q/(q^2-q-1)<=3/5 for q>=3,

  q <= [k+2eta+(3/5)E]/(s-2eta).            (2)

Consequently, testing q=1,2 plus every q>=3 up to floor of the right side is a terminating exact decision procedure for **all** admissible Port numbers. No arbitrary numerical Port search cutoff is needed. The six pairwise constraints above, rather than three independent difference error boxes, retain the shared-error correlation. The bound is sufficient, not asserted optimal.

## R50.3 — No universal finite Port cutoff without a separated gap

For every eta>0, the exact zero readout z=(0,0,0,0) is feasible for **every** positive integer q: choose t>0 with 2t<=eta and 2t^2<=eta, alpha=beta=t/q, b=0. The unperturbed masses become

Hence a universal finite-q bound for arbitrary noisy measurements is false. The gap hypothesis in R50.2 is a constructive sufficient route, not a necessary requirement for every dataset to be finitely identifiable.
