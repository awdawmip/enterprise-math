## Theorem 2 — universal antipodal-correction criterion

1. `|M|=3` and, writing `M={x,y,z}`, one choice of distinguished `z` satisfies `x+y-2z≡5 (mod 10)`; or

**Proof.** An antipodal mismatched pair `{z,z+5}` is a two-cycle of an *odd* reflection center c. Its other four two-cycles must each be entirely occupied or entirely empty; hence `|M|∈{1,3,5,7,9}`. Cardinalities 1 and 9 always admit a perfect reflection. For cardinality 5, translate to `c=5` and the sole occupied antipodal point 0. The remaining occupied points must be two of the four pairs `(1,4),(2,3),(6,9),(7,8)`. In lexicographic pair-index order 12,13,14,23,24,34, the six five-site words admit perfect reflection centers respectively `4,0,8,2,0,6`. Therefore a **minimizing** antipodal defect-2 correction requires weight 3 or 7.

For weight 3, the reflection exchanging x and y has center `c=x+y`; its remaining mismatch pair is `{z,x+y-z}`, antipodal exactly when `x+y-2z=5 mod 10`. This cannot coexist with a perfect reflection: if `y+z=2x` also held, subtracting gives `3(x-z)=5 mod 10`, implying `x=z+5` and `y=z`, contradictory to distinctness. The other possible fixed points are symmetric by relabeling. For a count, each z has exactly five unordered solutions `{x,y}` to `x+y=2z+5`, one containing z; hence four admissible pairs per z. A triple cannot have two such distinguished z, since subtracting the two conditions forces them equal (3 is invertible modulo 10). Thus 10·4=40 triples; complementation preserves every symmetric-difference reflection defect. QED.
