## 1. Exact global continuous optimum and sharp universal integer threshold

Take a finite incidence system of nonempty labeled factors f and groups B, with positive rational activities a_f=2^{s_f} delta_f lambda_f as in R825; zero-activity factors may be removed only for this observer. Write p_f=2^{-L_f}. Define M=max_B sum_{f incident B} sqrt(a_f). The relaxed continuous optimization is
 V_cont = min_{p_f>0, sum_{f incident B}p_f<=1 for every B} max_B sum_{f incident B} a_f/p_f.
Then V_cont=M^2 exactly, even when the groups overlap arbitrarily. Proof: Cauchy-Schwarz gives each group lower bound W_B^2, hence M^2. A single globally consistent choice p_f=sqrt(a_f)/M satisfies every Kraft constraint and has group load M W_B<=M^2. Empty factor system is interpreted as V_cont=0. In particular independent optimization of the separate groups is unnecessary for the continuous minimax value.

For integer nonnegative lengths, V_cont<=V_int<=2 V_cont. Round p_f down to the largest dyadic reciprocal power at most p_f. The Kraft constraints remain valid, and each load increases by at most a factor 2. Thus M<=1/4 suffices for the R825 beta<=1/8 certificate.

This sufficient uniform threshold is SHARP over positive auxiliary activity systems, not merely an artifact of a loose bound. For any real C>1/4 choose positive rational t,u with 1/4<t<C, t+u<C (and t<sqrt(1/8) if desired). Take one group containing two factors of activities a_1=t^2,a_2=u^2; each can also touch a private singleton group to have arity two. Every valid finite integer code has L_1,L_2>=1, since L_1=0 consumes the entire Kraft budget and a second codeword has positive budget. Consequently beta>=2t^2>1/8 although max W_B=t+u<C. This proves no larger constant than 1/4 can guarantee the integer certificate for every system. With u/t tending to zero, V_int=2(t^2+u^2) and V_int/V_cont tends to 2. This is a sharp supremum, not a hardness or physical realizability assertion. The continuous necessary threshold 1/sqrt(8) is attainable for balanced dyadic profiles, but cannot replace 1/4 as a uniform sufficient threshold.

## 2. Exact local optimizer via exponential-cost binary merging

For a single group with m positive rational activities w_i (other incidence constraints absent), minimize sum_i w_i 2^{L_i} subject to binary Kraft. A prefix code exists by Kraft. If m>=2 an optimum can be taken as a full binary tree: contract any unary internal node, shortening all its descendant codewords and strictly reducing cost. A full binary tree has at most m-1 depth; existence of an optimum follows. If m=1 use length zero. For m=0 use zero load.

Choose two smallest weights x,y. An optimal full tree can place them at two sibling leaves of maximum depth: such a sibling pair exists since a deepest leaf's sibling must also be a leaf, and exchanging smaller weights toward greater depth cannot increase sum w_i 2^{L_i}. Contract the sibling pair into a single leaf of weight 2(x+y), since at parent depth d its contribution equals (x+y)2^{d+1}. Conversely expanding that leaf into x,y children recovers the cost. Thus the exact recurrence is to repeatedly merge the two smallest current weights into 2(x+y), not x+y. By induction this gives the exact minimum and reconstructs a minimizing tree. Priority-queue implementation uses O(m log m) rational operations; with common denominator of bit length at most total input bit length, every intermediate weight is bounded by 2^{m-1} times total starting mass, so bit complexity is polynomial. This is the exponential-cost Huffman-type rule specialized to R825; no novelty claim about coding theory is made. It provides a polynomial-time local impossibility witness whenever a group's optimum exceeds 1/8. For overlap, optimizing groups separately need not give a globally consistent length.

## 3. Exact-cover obstruction for overlap, and a sharp four-group example

Consider a finite incidence hypergraph where every group is incident to exactly three positive factors, all with a_f=1/80. For any feasible integer certificate beta_B<=1/8 the three lengths at B must be a permutation of (1,2,2). Proof: no length zero; at most one length one by Kraft. If none is one, the load is at least 12/80. If exactly one is one, the smallest possible sum of powers is 2+4+4=10; the bound forces equality and hence the profile. Conversely (1,2,2) satisfies Kraft with equality and load 1/8.

Consequently the global certificate exists if and only if there is a subset of labeled factors covering every group exactly once (select precisely those with L_f=1; assign L_f=2 to all other factors). This is an exact reduction to an exact-cover feasibility question for this incidence class, not a complexity-hardness claim for Port physics.

Explicit counterexample: four groups B_0,...,B_3 and four factors f_0,...,f_3, with f_i incident to all groups except B_i. Every group individually passes with optimum 10/80. All groups have W_B=3/sqrt(80), strictly between 1/4 and 1/sqrt(8). Continuous optimum is 9/80, attained at p_f=1/3. No exact cover exists: every selected factor covers three groups, so the total coverage count would have to be both 3|selected| and 4. Hence no integer certificate at 1/8. In fact V_int=12/80=3/20: any smaller maximum load would be <=10/80, since powers 2^{L_f} are even integers for L_f>=1, which is impossible. All L_f=2 attain 12/80. This exhibits a global consistency gap even when every individual group passes. The model is auxiliary and its activity values are not attributed to any native road generator.

## 4. Joint continuous optimization of asymmetric road envelopes and lengths

In R824, for a retained road e=uv joining two shared vertices, choose positive envelope factors theta_eu,theta_ev<=1 with theta_eu>=m_u, theta_ev>=m_v and theta_eu theta_ev>=m_uv. Single shared endpoint constraints use the actual R824 internal-endpoint bound. For fixed coefficients delta and incidences, each a_f has the form A_f prod_{(e,v) in P_f}theta_ev, where A_f=2^{s_f}delta_f and P_f is the multiset of retained incident port payments (including all groups contributing to f). No exact joint moment is changed.

Set x_ev=-log(theta_ev). The feasible domain for finite x is the polyhedron x>=0, x_eu<=-log(m_u), x_ev<=-log(m_v), x_eu+x_ev<=-log(m_uv), omitting an upper inequality when the corresponding m is zero. The one-shared-endpoint bound similarly gives a linear upper bound. For each group,
 W_B(x)=sum_{f incident B} sqrt(A_f) exp(-1/2 sum_{(e,v) in P_f} x_ev).
Every term is an exponential of an affine function; W_B and max_B W_B are convex. By section 1 minimizing the continuous global code load jointly over theta and p is precisely minimizing max_B W_B(x), followed by p_f=sqrt(a_f)/max_B W_B. This is a convex minimax formulation, not an assertion that an optimizer or an exact comparison at the threshold has already been computed. For positive lower bounds the feasible relevant coordinates are compact; when a bound is zero, finite positive envelopes suffice for approximating any zero-envelope limit by continuity (use theta=max(theta,epsilon), preserving all lower/product inequalities). A chosen rational envelope can be certified by the original inequalities and R825's rational code construction; no logarithmic numerical approximation is needed in final acceptance checks. At M<1/4 strict slack gives room for rational approximation, while equality should be checked by exact certificate. This decouples the continuous optimization from the hard integer consistency issue in section 3.

For a single road with fixed other coefficients and weighted sum objective C_u theta_u+C_v theta_v, C_u,C_v>0, write m=m_uv. If m_u m_v>=m the optimum is (m_u,m_v). Otherwise optimum lies on theta_u theta_v=m, with theta_u in [max(m_u,m),min(1,m/m_v)] (use upper 1 if m_v=0). The derivative of C_u x+C_v m/x vanishes at sqrt(C_v m/C_u); clamp to the feasible interval, then theta_v=m/x. This solves the exact one-road weighted objective; it does not claim a coordinate descent solves the general integer minimax problem.
