## Question and precise semantics

Let S ⊆ Z/mZ be a binary word, with reflection r_c(t)=c-t and Hamming distance |S Δ r_c(S)|. Let C be a **nonempty specified set of centers**, especially the set of **all** centers attaining the minimum original reflection defect. Define the *simultaneous exact repair cost*

\[
J_m(S;C)=\min\{|S\triangle T|:\ r_c(T)=T\text{ for every }c\in C\}.
\]

The target T must preserve every originally optimal symmetry at once. In particular, every originally equal reflected pair is also constrained; it is generally incorrect to minimize only a vertex cover of the union of mismatch edges.

## Theorem 1: exact orbit-majority formula, every finite cyclic modulus

Let K_C be the permutation group generated by the reflections r_c, c ∈ C, on Z/mZ. Write its orbits as O. Let k_O=|S ∩ O|. Then

\[
\boxed{J_m(S;C)=\sum_{O\in(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)/K_C}\min(k_O,|O|-k_O).}
\]

Every minimizing T is constant on each K_C-orbit. For a strict majority it has the majority bit; for a tie it may take either bit. Therefore the exact number of distinct minimizing target words is

\[
\boxed{\#\operatorname{argmin}J=2^{\,\#\{O:2k_O=|O|\}}.}
\]

Choose c0∈C and set H=⟨c-c0:c∈C⟩ ≤ Z/mZ. The orbits of K_C are precisely the cosets x+H when r_c0 fixes that coset and otherwise the pairs of cosets (x+H)∪(c0-x+H). This gives an algorithm independent of graph-forest assumptions.

**Proof.** A binary word is invariant under all r_c iff it is constant on each orbit of their generated permutation group. There are exactly two allowed bits on each orbit, with costs k_O and |O|-k_O, respectively. Minimization separates over disjoint orbits; ties account for the power of two. Products r_c r_c0 are translations by c-c0, so they generate H. Together with r_c0 they generate K_C, giving the stated coset classification. QED.

This theorem applies to any S and any nonempty C. A valid optimization of all previously equal reflection pairs is obtained without enumerating target words.

## Theorem 2: exact CRT lifting of joint repair for the frozen certificate

Use exactly the parent BRC16943 certificate:
\[
W(t)=U(t\bmod10)V(t\bmod11),\qquad t\in\mathbb Z/110.
\]
U is the frozen 44-cell P10-direction word. V is either the complete 11-site word (F=11) or the complete 11-site word with precisely one hole z (F=10). Let C10 be **all** the optimal reflection centers of U, and C110 all optimal centers of W. Define
\[
j_{10}=J_{10}(U;C_{10}),\quad j_{110}=J_{110}(W;C_{110}).
\]
Then, without requiring d10(U)=2,

\[
\boxed{j_{110}=Fj_{10},\qquad F\in\{10,11\}.}
\]

**Proof, F=11.** Parent CRT theorem gives C110=C10×Z11. Reflections with identical mod10 center and arbitrary mod11 centers generate the whole translation group of Z11. Therefore a simultaneously invariant T is constant in the mod11 coordinate and induces precisely a C10-invariant 10-word. An orbit O of the ten-coordinate group lifts to O×Z11; both occupancies and sizes multiply by 11. Theorem 1 gives j110=11j10. Its number of minimizing targets is exactly the number of ten-word minimizers, because each lifted orbit makes only one common choice.

**Proof, F=10.** Parent CRT theorem gives C110=C10×{2z}. The missing mod11 fiber z is identically zero and its reflection orbit is disjoint from all allowed fibers; changing any bit there cannot decrease the minimum and no minimizing T changes it. The ten allowed mod11 residues form five unordered reflection pairs {u,2z-u}. For one such pair, translations generated by C10 act separately on each fiber; r_(c0,2z) switches the two fibers while applying the base reflection. If a K_C10 orbit is one reflection-stable H-coset A, the corresponding pair-orbit has 2|A| sites and 2|S∩A| occupied sites, contributing 2min(k_A,|A|-k_A). If a base orbit is a pair A∪B of distinct H-cosets, the lifted pair splits into two equal-size orbits, each with k_A+k_B occupied positions, contributing twice the original orbit's minimum. Thus each of five mod11 reflection pairs costs exactly 2j10, giving j110=10j10. All equal-pair constraints are included. QED.

The number of optimal repairs is not generally 2^F or 2 raised to the number of base ties; it is computed from the **full group orbits** via Theorem 1. The attached verifier reports the complete tie exponents.
