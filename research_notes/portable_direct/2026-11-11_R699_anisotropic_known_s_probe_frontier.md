## 2. New sharp anisotropic theorem

For any fixed `1<=s<=59`, every possible noisy pair (`hat T3,hat T8`) with independent absolute errors `<=eps3,<=eps8` uniquely identifies its input among all `X_s` **if and only if**:

| Exact count s | Sharp condition |
| --- | --- |
| 1 or 59 | `a<4 AND (a<1 OR b<1)` |
| 2 or 58 | `a<3 AND b<1` |
| 3 through 57 | `a<1 AND b<1` |

For `s=60` the known sum determines `v=N`, so no probe is needed.

The quantifiers cover **all** valid integer states and **all** errors in the corresponding closed, axis-aligned error rectangles. At equality of any excluded threshold there is a witness of ambiguity; all inequalities are strict. The theorem describes a guaranteed nearest-feasible-signature decoder with coordinate tolerances `(10s eps3,5s eps8)`, not an assumption of random noise.

Equivalently, if `D_s` is the set of unordered distinct pairs of signatures and `P_s` denotes its coordinatewise-minimal absolute difference pairs `(|Delta U|,|Delta V|)`, the complete Pareto sets are

\[
P_s=\begin{cases}
\{(1,1),(4,0)\},&s\in\{1,59\},\\
\{(0,1),(3,0)\},&s\in\{2,58\},\\
\{(0,1),(1,0)\},&3\le s\le57.
\end{cases}
\]

The error rectangles of two states intersect exactly when `|Delta U|<=a` and `|Delta V|<=b`, explaining the iff.

## 3. Proof

(1) **Injectivity.** R697's primitive integer kernel of the map `v -> (s,U,V)` is generated by `(3,-4,-7,8)`. Since the c-coordinate difference of any two legal histograms is between -6 and 6, the only admissible multiple of that generator is zero. Thus two distinct states cannot have `(Delta U,Delta V)=(0,0)`.

(2) **Middle totals.** For every `3<=s<=57` there exists an integer starting histogram in the componentwise interval `[0,1,2,0]..[23,18,6,10]` with total s. Adding `(1,-1,-2,2)` yields another valid state with `(Delta U,Delta V)=(1,0)`. For every `2<=s<=58`, choose a starting histogram with total s in `[0,1,1,0]..[23,18,6,11]`; adding `(1,-1,-1,1)` yields `(0,1)`. Every integer total between the sums of the lower and upper endpoints occurs, since each independent interval has consecutive integer values. Since all nonzero signature differences are integral, the exhibited pairs are the entire Pareto frontier for `3<=s<=57`.

(3) **Two remaining interior-edge totals.** At `s=2`, list the two-atom multisets of four histogram classes. Their signatures group by equal V as follows: V=0: U={10}; V=1: U={6}; V=2: U={2,5,9}; V=3: U={1,5}; V=4: U={0,4,8}. Thus the smallest nonzero `|Delta U|` with `Delta V=0` is 3. The preceding `(0,1)` witness exists. All pairs with nonzero V difference have `|Delta V|>=1`, so `P_2={(0,1),(3,0)}`. Since `v -> N-v` maps `X_s` bijectively to `X_{60-s}` and reverses both signature differences, `P_{58}=P_2`.

(4) **Extreme totals.** At `s=1`, the four possible signatures `(U,V)` are `(4,2),(5,0),(0,2),(1,1)`. The minimum `|Delta U|` for fixed V is 4; U is injective on these four states; the pair for c vs z gives absolute differences `(1,1)`. So `P_1={(1,1),(4,0)}`. The same complement map gives `P_{59}=P_1`.

(5) **Sharpness.** Every Pareto vector has a pair of feasible histograms realizing it. The midpoint of the corresponding two probe readings satisfies the closed error bounds for both histograms at or above the listed threshold, so no decoder can distinguish both. Conversely when the inequalities hold, all distinct feasibility rectangles are disjoint. This proves necessary and sufficient uniform recovery.
