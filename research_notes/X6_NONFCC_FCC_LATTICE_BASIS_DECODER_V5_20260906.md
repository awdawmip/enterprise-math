## 2. FCC parity lattice

Using the standard integer representatives

`AB=(1,1,0)`, `AC=(1,0,1)`, `AD=(0,1,-1)`,

`BC=(0,1,1)`, `BD=(1,0,-1)`, `CD=(1,-1,0)`,

## 3. Rank-3 basis theorem

For every FACE or PATH chart, V4 gives

`|det M_S|=2`.

But every column already lies in `L_FCC`, itself an index-2 sublattice. Hence the two lattices are equal:

`M_S Z^3 = L_FCC`.

So:

`FACE_OR_PATH <=> SELECTED_CARRIER_LINES_FORM_A_Z_BASIS_OF_L_FCC`.

`C_S : Z^3 -> L_FCC`,

`C_S(n)=M_S n`.

Because `M_S Z^3=L_FCC`, a carrier integer point `y` has an integer coordinate decode in any FACE/PATH chart iff

`y_1+y_2+y_3 = 0 mod 2`.

`n=M_S^{-1} y`

## 6. STAR is the opposite information regime

For a STAR chart, one can orient its three carrier rays so their pairwise carrier Euclidean dot products are `-1`. The oriented carrier vectors then satisfy

`v_1+v_2+v_3=0`.

This is a carrier relation only, never a native Enterprise vector identity.

It means the STAR map from raw signed native slice coordinates `Z^3` into the planar FCC carrier has a one-dimensional integer kernel. This is exactly why the centered-X6 rebase had to retain a common-depth/repair coordinate rather than identify raw native points by the carrier kernel.
