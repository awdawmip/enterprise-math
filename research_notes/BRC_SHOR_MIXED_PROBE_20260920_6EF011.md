## Exact scope and inexpensive character oracle

Uniform exponent state Q^(-1/2) sum_x |x>|a^x mod N>, unit a, Q=2^m, ideal QFT, unconditioned second register, numeric output K with bit reversal already corrected. For 0<d<Q,

    Q E[exp(2*pi*i*d*K/Q)] = (Q-d)[a^d=1 mod N]+d[a^(Q-d)=1 mod N].

Proof: Fourier orthogonality restricts the path-pair difference to -d moduloQ. The nonwrapped and wrapped classes have Q-d and d members; unit cancellation gives the two return tests. Thus the exact character expectation chi(d) is a nonnegative real rational and needs at most two modular powers, not an order oracle. This is the inherited cyclic collision identity used as a new observer, not a claim of novel orthogonality.

## Sampleable Fourier observer and fixed-k guarantee

For bit j=0 indicator, period T=2^(j+1), its Fourier coefficients are c(0)=1/2, c(u)=(1-i*cot(pi*u/T))/T for odd u, and0 for nonzero even u. Bit-one flips only nonzero coefficients. With v=min(u,T-u), |c(u)|<=1/(2v).

For j=0, use the uniform proposal on0,1. For j>=1, put q(0)=1/(j+2); each of u1,T-1 also has1/(j+2). In bin 2^h<=v<2^(h+1), h1..j-1, each odd signed frequency has probability1/((j+2)2^h). The probabilities sum1, are generated by integer tickets/sign/offsets, and satisfy |c(u)/q(u)|<=Z_j, where Z_0=1 and Z_j=(j+2)/2. No exponentially long support is enumerated.

For distinct positions j_i, draw their frequencies independently and set d=sum_i u_i*2^(m-j_i-1) modQ. The real part of the product of c/q times chi(d) is an unbiased estimator of the requested joint cell. Distinct nonzero local frequencies have distinct 2-adic valuations after lifting, so d=0 only for the all-zero tuple. Remove that contribution and add its exact expectation2^(-k) analytically. All2^k cells use the same sample with pattern-dependent signs. The nonconstant sample is bounded by Z=product Z_j<=((m+1)/2)^k.

Hoeffding and a union bound give O(Z^2 epsilon^(-2) log(2^k/delta)) draws for simultaneous additive error epsilon. For FIXED k this is polynomial in m,logN,1/epsilon,log(1/delta), including polynomial-precision phase arithmetic. It is not a polynomial full-output algorithm when k grows, nor an exponentially precise probability oracle. The implementation has explicit sample/register/phase-width limits rather than pretending every budget succeeds.

## Integer numerical and probabilistic error control

Reuse stage6.marginal.Dyadic unchanged. Signed complex coefficients are pairs of directed integer intervals, not positive BRC mass. Each estimator sample is enclosed at grid2^bits with width<=2 units, midpoint error<=2^(-bits), and bounded by A=ceil(Z). A positive denominator and final enclosure width must be certified or the call fails.

For n fixed draws, grid D, integer sample sum S and squared sum S2, use H=ceil(log2(8*2^k/delta)), an upper bound for the required natural logarithm. The simultaneous radius in grid units is the smaller of

    ceil(sqrt(2*A^2*D^2*H/n))
    ceil(sqrt(2*H*(n*S2-S^2)/(n^2*(n-1))))+ceil(14*A*H*D/(3*(n-1)))

plus1 for deterministic rounding. The second line is the rescaled Maurer-Pontil empirical Bernstein bound. The union bound covers both inequalities, both tails and all cells. Its confidence assumes independent exact proposal draws; fixed PRNG seeds are reproducibility mode, not an IID proof. No optional stopping is used. A fresh larger predetermined run is labelled separately. Failure to meet requested radius returns PRECISION_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED with wider intervals.

Clip negative estimated cells and normalize integer weights to obtain an actual probability law. Since raw estimates sum1, a conservative total-variation bound is the sum of coordinate radii, capped at1. Samples from this law are approximate SELECTED-BIT samples, not full or exact Shor outcomes. ## Exactly fair versus almost fair

For original r=2^v*u<Q, u odd, Q'=Q/2^v,

    P_(N,a,Q)(k)=2^(-v) P_(N,a^(2^v),Q')(k modQ').

Equal-output pairs must have x=z modulo2^v; summing their common low residue proves the identity. The top v bits are independent fair bits, independent of the rest. Put h=floor(log2u). A single bit j is exactly fair iff j<h or j>=m-v. For a remaining j, the reduced marginal has odd order u<L=2^(j+1). Symmetry gives its top-bit bias P(0)-P(L/2). Writing L=q*u+w, its numerator is

    (u-w)q^2+w(q+1)^2-(u-w)(q mod2)-w((q+1) mod2)>0.

Therefore every collection of individually EXACTLY fair bits is jointly uniform within this specific state family. This is not a general probability principle. Near-fairness does not imply small correlations.
