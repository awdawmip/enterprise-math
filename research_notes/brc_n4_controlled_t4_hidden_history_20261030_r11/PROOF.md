# BRC N=4: controlled time-four sufficiency and a sharp hidden-history obstruction

Frozen predecessor: [PR #1738](https://github.com/awdawmip/enterprise-math/pull/1738), commit c1501f7f4863df0446db3fa1a33892976026e213. This is an **external, finite, positive-weight two-law BRC model**, not a theorem about native P000 X6/time dynamics.

## Original model and the precise question

There are 16 strictly positive leaves (s,j,k), s in {AU,AV,BU,BV}, j,k in {0,1}, with Q mass q_s/4 and P mass (q_s/4)r_{s,j}. The source probabilities q are (1/6,1/3,1/4,1/4), and the stage-three ratios r are AU=(9/10,11/10), AV=(1/4,1/4), BU=(1/5,9/5), BV=(2,2). This is exactly the predecessor source, not a fresh fit.

Under the predecessor's lawful G0 strategy, AU/BU continue to T3 and AV/BV stop at T2. The coarse terminal observer O'=(T,Decision,Z_BU,Z_AU) has identical P and Q laws; the BU coarse atom has mass 1/4 under each law. The previous result proved that adding the BU child bit j is sufficient for **passive** downstream readouts on the original stage-four bit k, since P(k|BU,j)=Q(k|BU,j)=(1/2,1/2). We ask what extra assumptions are required when a controller may select a future transition affected by hidden history.

## Theorem 1: exact action-wise Blackwell/factorization criterion

Let J be a finite observed state, H finite hidden pre-intervention history, and P,Q two laws with P(J=j),Q(J=j)>0. A controller uses the same J-measurable action-selection policy under both laws. A physical output Y is generated by a **common law-independent** stochastic kernel K_a(y|j,h), for action a. A common randomized decoder D_a(y|j) that preserves the actual J and reproduces each full joint (J,Y) law separately for P and Q exists for all individually selectable actions exactly when

    for every j,a,y:
        sum_h [P(h|j)-Q(h|j)] K_a(y|j,h) = 0.               (1)

For a single fixed policy, test only actions it uses at each j. For **all** hidden-history readout kernels, (1) is equivalent to equality of the complete conditional laws P(H|J)=Q(H|J), since the identity query Y=H is allowed. For a restricted action family, (1) requires only that each conditional signed contrast vector lie in the nullspace of each admissible kernel.

**Proof.** For law L and action a, the conditional output distribution at observed j is M_L^a(y|j)=sum_h L(h|j)K_a(y|j,h). If the joint J marginal is preserved, a common decoder must equal M_P^a and M_Q^a at every j of positive mass. This holds iff (1). Conversely their common mixture is a decoder and its composition with any J-measurable randomized action policy remains exact. Selecting each allowed action deterministically proves necessity for the full class. Choosing the identity hidden-state readout gives the final statement. This is algebraic and applies to *all* finite stochastic kernels, not merely sampled inputs. QED.

In particular, with no additional hidden history beyond the original BU (j,k), any law-independent downstream Markov kernel depending on j and the original fair k is reproducible from j. **The original 16-leaf source alone does not furnish a counterexample.** A failure must specify a changed operation, extra relevant history, or another changed assumption.

## Theorem 2: exact rational counterexample in a declared 20-leaf extension

Split **only** each of the four BU leaves (BU,j,k) by a binary latent H produced before the time-four action; conditional on BU,j, let H and the original fair k be independent and set

    P(H=1|BU,j)=3/4,   Q(H=1|BU,j)=1/4     for j=0,1.    (2)

AU/AV/BV remain unsplit. This gives 12+8=20 strictly positive leaves under each source law. Summing out H returns **every original P and Q leaf mass** exactly; the 16-leaf marginal is unchanged. H is a new, explicit positive-weight BRC history variable, not a previously demonstrated component of the old source.

The T3 controller may choose a in {0,1} based on observed j, not on H or future k. The shared controlled T4 physical output rule is

    K_0(Y=1|j,H)=1/2  (original passive fair k),
    K_1(Y=1|j,H)=1[H=1]  (new history-sensitive transition). (3)

Both rules are identical under P/Q: only the prior hidden-history masses differ. For action 1 the signed contrast in (1) equals (3/4-1/4)=1/2, hence **j alone is not statistically sufficient** for the controlled extension, even though it remains sufficient for action 0.

Under G0 the BU j-atom masses are

    P_j = (1/40,9/40),    Q_j = (1/8,1/8).                 (4)

All other O' atoms have deterministic output and remain equal under P/Q. For the *same refined observer* (O',j_BU,Y), the two actions give exact total variations

    TV(P_a0,Q_a0)=1/10,
    TV(P_a1,Q_a1)=11/80,  strict increase = 3/80.        (5)

Indeed on the four (BU,j,Y) atoms ordered (0,1),(0,0),(1,1),(1,0), the P masses in units of 1/160 are (3,1,27,9), and Q masses are (5,15,5,15). Their absolute differences sum to 44/160, giving TV=11/80.

## Theorem 3: sharp common-decoder minimax radius for controlled action

For fixed action a=1 and target (O',j_BU,Y), require one law-independent decoder which **preserves the actual (O',j_BU)** and reconstructs Y stochastically. Then

    inf_D max(TV_P(reconstruction), TV_Q(reconstruction)) = 5/112. (6)

**Proof.** Reconstruct non-BU atoms exactly. On each BU j, choose theta_j = D(Y=1|BU,j). Both error terms are exactly

    e_P=(1/40)|theta_0-3/4| + (9/40)|theta_1-3/4|,
    e_Q=(1/8)|theta_0-1/4|  + (1/8)|theta_1-1/4|.        (7)

Clipping theta_j to [1/4,3/4] cannot increase either error. Write theta_j=1/4+t_j/2, 0<=t_j<=1. The exact convex dual certificate for **all real** t is

    (5/14)e_P+(9/14)e_Q = 5/112 + t_0/28 >= 5/112.    (8)

The maximum of the two errors dominates their convex combination. Equality is realized at t_0=0,t_1=5/7, i.e.

    theta_0=1/4, theta_1=17/28; e_P=e_Q=5/112.        (9)

Thus this is the exact global law-independent stochastic reconstruction radius, not a numerical upper bound. QED.

For the **different, coarser target** (O',Y) reconstructed from O' alone, the sharp binary output radius is 1/16 by midpoint theta=1/2 on the BU atom of mass 1/4. Since the target observer differs, these two radii should not be conflated. Retaining one additional latent bit H beyond j makes both actions exactly reconstructible at law level: for a=1 output H; for a=0 sample a fresh fair k. Zero added bits cannot suffice by (1), so this extra bit is minimal for the specified two-action family. Law-level resampling does **not** reproduce the actual previously realized k on the same path.
