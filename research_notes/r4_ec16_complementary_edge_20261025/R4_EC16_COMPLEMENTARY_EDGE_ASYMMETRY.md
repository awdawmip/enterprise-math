## 1. Exact transducer and rational input

Control q belongs to {0,1,2}. The two input edges (q',eta,b) are:

| q | bit 0 | bit 1 |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | (2,-1,-1) | (2,+1,-1) |
| 1 | (0,-1,-1) | (0,+1,-1) |
| 2 | (1,+1,+1) | (2,+1,-1) |

The orientation e updates as e'=eta e and the signed grade as t'=t+e'b=t+e(1-2a) for bit a. Source r=0 is clean without trailing 0, r=1 is clean with trailing 0, and r=2 means the word already contains 00. Initially (q,r,e,t)=(0,0,+1,0). Every ordered word has exact mass one. Put j=(n-t)/2. The signed-grade word is triangularly bijective to the input word, whence the unrestricted grade-j shell contains exactly binom(n,j) paths. Let C[n,j] count the words avoiding 00, and define C[n,j]=0 outside 0<=j<=n.

The exact eight-clean-state generating identity established and author-checked in EC13–EC15 is

\[
F(x,u)=\sum_{n\ge0}\sum_{j=0}^n C[n,j]x^n u^j
=\frac{N_-(x,u)}{\Delta(x,u)},\quad
N_-=(1-ux^2)(1-ux^3),\quad
\Delta=1-(1+u)x+2u^2x^4-u^3x^6. \tag{1}
\]

It is a formal Q[[x,u]] identity (and a convergent analytic identity in the domains used in Section 3). The included verifier separately reconstructs the eight-state transition graph and checks its exact coefficients.

## 2. Theorem A: the complete finite-depth reflection defect

Define H[n,k]=C[n,n-k] and D[n,k]=H[n,k]-C[n,k]. For **every** n,k, the following full formal-series identity holds:

\[
\boxed{\sum_{n,k}D[n,k]x^n u^k
=\frac{u(1-u)x^3(1-ux^2)}{\Delta(x,u)}.} \tag{2}
\]

**Proof.** The reflection H is generated by F(ux,1/u), a formal power series with nonnegative exponents because 0<=j<=n. Exact algebra gives Delta(ux,1/u)=Delta(x,u) and numerator N_+=(1-ux^2)(1-u^2x^3). The difference N_+-N_-=u(1-u)x^3(1-ux^2). Subtracting the rational functions proves (2) for all coefficients without an asymptotic assumption. QED.

Expanding (2) at u=0 gives exact small-edge formulas:

\[
\boxed{D[n,1]=1\quad(n\ge3),\qquad
D[n,2]=n-5\quad(n\ge5),\qquad
D[n,3]=\frac{(n-9)(n-6)}2\quad(n\ge6).} \tag{3}
\]

For example, D[7,3]=-1: there is **no universal high-edge dominance at every finite n,k**, despite the positive moving-edge asymptotic in Section 3.

There is also a fixed-k polynomial theorem. Expand the denominator as

\[
\frac1\Delta=\frac1{1-x}\sum_{r\ge0}
\left(\frac{ux-2u^2x^4+u^3x^6}{1-x}\right)^r.
\]

For each fixed k>=1, [u^k] of (2) is P_k(x)/(1-x)^k with an integer polynomial P_k and P_k(1)=1. Indeed the unique pole of order k at x=1 uses the leading numerator term ux^3 and exactly k-1 factors ux/(1-x), producing x^{k+2}/(1-x)^k; every other contribution has pole order <=k-1. Coefficient extraction therefore gives, for n sufficiently large relative to fixed k,

\[
\boxed{D[n,k]=\frac{n^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}+O_k(n^{k-2}).} \tag{4}
\]

The analogous expansion of F yields C[n,k]=n^k/k!+O_k(n^{k-1}). Hence, for every fixed k>=1,

\[
\frac{C[n,n-k]}{C[n,k]}=1+\frac{k}{n}+O_k(n^{-2}). \tag{5}
\]

The constants in (4) and (5) may depend on fixed k; they are **not** uniform moving-k conclusions.

## 3. Theorem B: uniform complementary-edge asymptotics

Let alpha=k/n. There exist common absolute constants alpha0>0, J0>=1 and K>0 (depending only on rational function (1)) such that, whenever J0<=k<n and 0<alpha<=alpha0, both clean counts are positive and

\[
\boxed{\left|
\log\frac{C[n,n-k]}{C[n,k]}
-\left(\alpha+\frac32\alpha^2-\frac23\alpha^3\right)
\right|
\le K\left(\alpha^4+\frac1k\right).} \tag{6}
\]

As a consequence, for every integer sequence sqrt(n)<<k<<n,

\[
\boxed{\log\left(\frac{C[n,n-k]}{C[n,k]}\right)
=(1+o(1))\frac{k}{n}>0.} \tag{7}
\]

Because binom(n,n-k)=binom(n,k), (6) and (7) equally compare the **grade-conditioned source-clean probabilities**, without conflating grade with the full affine effect or future-word observer.

**Proof.** Use exactly the isolated pole and uniform Fourier mechanism proved in R4-EC15, now with the reflected numerator. At u=0 the denominator has one simple finite root rho(0)=1. For complex u in one small fixed disk, Rouche's theorem on |x|=3/2 isolates an analytic root rho(u); the other poles lie outside that circle. Write lambda=1/rho and f=log lambda. Let g(u)=u f'(u), and define the positive saddle u_alpha by g(u_alpha)=alpha. The EC15 exact series and positivity are

\[
u_\alpha=\alpha+5\alpha^2+22\alpha^3+86\alpha^4+O(\alpha^5),\quad
\sigma^2(u)=u g'(u)>0,\quad n u_\alpha\asymp k.
\]

The EC15 power-series angular estimate

\[
\Re\{f(ue^{i\theta})-f(u)\}
\le-\frac u2(1-\cos\theta)
\]

holds uniformly on the same small disk; it uses the coefficient f'(0)=1 and the inequality 1-cos(r theta)<=r^2[1-cos theta]. For either numerator N_±, subtract the dominant pole: its remainder is uniformly bounded on |x|=3/2, and its analytic nonzero residue amplitude is

\[
A_\pm(u)=-\frac{N_\pm(\rho(u),u)}
{\rho(u)\,\partial_x\Delta(\rho(u),u)},\qquad A_\pm(0)=1.
\]

Coefficient extraction on |u|=u_alpha and a Gaussian expansion within width (n u_alpha)^(-1/2) give a **uniform relative** local limit for *each* numerator,

\[
\begin{aligned}
C[n,k]&=\frac{A_-(u_\alpha)\lambda(u_\alpha)^n u_\alpha^{-k}}
{\sqrt{2\pi n\sigma^2(u_\alpha)}}(1+O(k^{-1})),\\
C[n,n-k]&=\frac{A_+(u_\alpha)\lambda(u_\alpha)^n u_\alpha^{-k}}
{\sqrt{2\pi n\sigma^2(u_\alpha)}}(1+O(k^{-1})).
\end{aligned} \tag{8}
\]

For clarity about uniformity: the Fourier tail is exp[-c n u_alpha(1-cos theta)] and therefore exponentially small outside a k^{-2/5} arc; within that arc odd cubic and odd first-amplitude terms integrate to zero, and the first surviving corrections are O(1/(n u_alpha))=O(1/k). The second numerator is a fixed polynomial with nonzero analytic amplitude, so EC15's proof applies without changing any spectral hypothesis. The pole-subtracted remainder is exponentially small relative to the saddle. After raising J0, the relative error is <1/2 and both coefficients are strictly positive. No fixed-alpha large-deviation limit is substituted for this uniform analysis.

Dividing (8) cancels the entire exponential, saddle, and Gaussian terms. The amplitudes satisfy the **exact** rational ratio

\[
\frac{A_+(u)}{A_-(u)}
=\frac{1-u^2\rho(u)^3}{1-u\rho(u)^3}. \tag{9}
\]

Put L(u)=log[(1-u^2 rho(u)^3)/(1-u rho(u)^3)]. The EC15 verified algebraic root series lambda=1+u-2u^2+7u^3-29u^4+O(u^5) yields by exact formal expansion

\[
L(u)=u-\frac72u^2+\frac{37}3u^3+O(u^4).
\]

Substituting the saddle gives

\[
\boxed{L(u_\alpha)=\alpha+\frac32\alpha^2-\frac23\alpha^3+O(\alpha^4).} \tag{10}
\]

Equations (8)–(10) prove (6). If sqrt(n)<<k<<n, then alpha->0 and (1/k)/alpha=n/k^2->0, so (6) implies (7). QED.

Combining with the existing EC15 left-edge rate I(alpha) and amplitude B(alpha), the right-edge clean probability satisfies

\[
\log\frac{C[n,n-k]}{\binom nk}
=-nI(\alpha)+B(\alpha)+L(u_\alpha)+O(k^{-1})
=-2\frac{k^2}{n}-3\frac{k^3}{n^2}+\frac{k}{n}
+O\left(\frac{k^4}{n^3}+\frac{k^2}{n^2}+\frac1k\right). \tag{11}
\]

This **right-edge correction** is invisible to the symmetric exponential rate function I(alpha)=I(1-alpha). The stronger output is the ratio law (6), not a claim that all finite source counts have fixed sign.
