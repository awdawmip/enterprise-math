## 3. Geometric frontier and exact equivalence

Define F_H(V,x) to be the old Cells v in V for which there exists a native path x=y_0,...,y_m=v, 1<=m<=H, such that all intermediate Cells y_1,...,y_(m-1) are pairwise distinct and outside V. The final Cell may equal the starting x; other repeated vertices are excluded by these conditions. Thus the witness path has no future self-contact before v and makes v its first old contact. F_0 is empty. This is a finite subset of the coordinate-reachability ball B_H(x).

Theorem 1. For two finite old sets V,W containing the same current Cell x, all absorbing first-hit responses through horizon H agree if and only if

  F_H(V,x)=F_H(W,x).

Necessity. For v in F_H(V,x), take its witness word. Its first hit for V is exactly the last step m. Equality of the first-hit response forces W to survive every earlier step and stop at m. The witness word has no earlier self-repeat, including the possible final return to starting x, so the terminal hit must be old membership v in W. Its intermediate Cells are outside W, proving v in F_H(W,x). Reverse V,W for equality.

Sufficiency. Suppose the first-hit responses differ for a word and let j be the earlier of the two stopping times. If that stop were a future-prefix repeat, both models would stop at j unless the other had already stopped, contradicting the choice of j. Hence the earlier stop is at an old Cell v, with a simple, old-free prefix. Thus v is in that model's F_H, hence in the other model's old set by frontier equality. The other response must also stop no later than j, a contradiction. This proves equality for every admitted word, not just for selected shortest probes.

This is a coarsest semantic quotient on the declared old-set population: two different attainable frontier values can be separated by an admitted witness word. It is not a claim that individual frontier-coordinate bits are all independent or that its literal set encoding is the unique shortest binary code.

A useful representative theorem is stronger than merely naming the quotient. Put

  R=F_H(V,x) union {x}.

Then replacing V by R preserves every absorbing response through H. Indeed R is a subset of V. Before the first old contact of any word, no self-repeat has occurred; that old contact, when it precedes a self-repeat, is in F_H and was retained. No earlier new hit is introduced by taking a subset. Adding x retains the known current-Cell fact. In particular shadowed old Cells can really be removed for this language without reconstructing them later.

## 4. Safe online update

At remaining horizon r>=1, retain the frontier and the known current Cell. A unit step to x' is an immediate old contact exactly when x' belongs to F_r(V,x), because every old neighbour is a visible one-step contact. On a hit emit the event and enter the absorbing terminal state.

On a safe step, define R=F_r(V,x) union {x}. The new exact frontier is

  F_(r-1)(V union {x'}, x') = F_(r-1)(R union {x'}, x').

Proof. V and R have the same absorbing responses through r by the representative theorem. Their first step to x' is safe in both. Prefixing that common safe step to any word of length at most r-1 proves equality of future responses from the new current Cell; prefix-visited x' is now part of both new old sets. Theorem 1 gives equality of the displayed frontiers. This explicitly applies the existing T6 prefix/descent law; it does not introduce a new general quotient calculus. R need not be an actual connected past trace: it is a scope-certified analytic representative, not a new native physical history.

## 6. Shadow compression does not imply surface-order worst-case memory

Theorem 2. Despite the strict quotient above, the worst-case fixed-capacity history-memory order for this absorbing-future language is still Theta(H^6), with current Cell, clock and H free. This is a scope extension of the prior realizable-history construction, not a second general capacity method.

For the absorbing lower bound, H>=5, let q=floor((H-5)/12)+1 and K=q^6. Reuse the actual K-bit past family from the original paired construction with targets c_z=e1+2z, attachments a_z=c_z+e2, dummies d_z=a_z+e3, z in {0,...,q-1}^6. The old shortest-word separation is NOT sufficient here: it may encounter a common visited Cell first and become absorbed. A new contact-free probe is required. The following parity check supplies it.

Record a Cell's parity as the set of its odd coordinate indices. Every fixed non-target Cell of the old construction has parity among

  empty, {2}, {1,2}, {1,2,3}, {1,2,4}, {1,2,5}, {1,2,6}, {1,3}.

Targets have parity {1}. This list follows directly from the entrance/exit, two-step index connectors, the coordinate2 detour through axis3, and fixed dummy excursions. It includes the anchor and all connector interiors, not just attachments. No set on the list contains both4 and5; when the first coordinate is even, only empty or {2} can occur.

For a chosen target c_z, use this future probe:

1. From0 take e4, then e5.
2. Keeping coordinate1 equal to0, take 2z_j positive axis-j steps for j=2,3,4,5,6, in that order.
3. Take 1+2z_1 positive axis1 steps last, arriving at c_z+e4+e5.
4. Take -e5, then -e4, arriving at c_z.

During step2 the first coordinate is even and at least one of coordinates4,5 remains odd; it therefore avoids every fixed old Cell and every target. During step3 both4,5 are odd, again avoiding every fixed old Cell and target. The penultimate Cell c_z+e4 has parity{1,4}, also safe. The first coordinate reaches its positive final value only at the end of step3. Hence the final two Cells have not appeared earlier in the probe; the positive-coordinate initial segments also have no self-repeat. This checks future self-contact, not merely avoidance of the old trace.

Thus every prefix Cell before the final target is fresh for every member of the old K-bit family. The probe length is

  5+2 sum_i z_i <=5+12(q-1)<=H.

Its outcome is a first hit exactly at its final step when the selected target bit is1, and SURVIVE through that step when the bit is0. Every two bit strings therefore have different absorbing signatures, despite a common Cell and clock. There are at least2^K retained machine states. A fixed-capacity m-bit register requires m>=K; q>= (H-4)/12 gives the sixth-power asymptotic lower bound. Arbitrary variable-length code conventions do not alter the state-cardinality statement.

The proof does not assume that all subsets of a ball are real visited histories, that the absorbing frontier is always an outer shell, or that a smaller semantic quotient must have a smaller worst-case exponent.
