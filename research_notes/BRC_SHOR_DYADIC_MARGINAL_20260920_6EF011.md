## Exact observer and theorem

For a unit a modulo N, use the ideal state Q^(-1/2) sum_x |x>|a^x mod N>, then a Q-point QFT on the first register. The second register is unconditioned. Let P_{N,a,Q}(k) be the resulting probability for the numeric output k; convert any circuit bit reversal first.

For L dividing Q, h=Q/L and b=a^h mod N, prove

    sum_{j=0}^{h-1} P_{N,a,Q}(y+jL) = P_{N,b,L}(y).

Expand P_Q(k) as Q^(-2) sum_{x,z}[a^x=a^z] exp(2*pi*i*k*(x-z)/Q). The frequency-coset sum vanishes unless h divides x-z. Write x=c+hv,z=c+hw. Unit cancellation removes a^c, and h choices of c plus the character-sum factor h change Q^(-2) to L^(-2). This is the reduced distribution. Equivalently cyclic collision counts satisfy C_Q(h*j)=h*C_L^(b)(j), an integer identity checked directly.

This is finite Fourier orthogonality at a phase readout, NOT arbitrary cancellation by positive BRC weights. At Q=2^m,L=2^t it describes the lowest t numeric output bits, not high bits, arbitrary measured wires, selected second-register outcomes or arbitrary input states.

## Uniform horizon and first informative layer

Writing s=ord_N(b), the marginal is uniform iff s>=L: K_L=L precisely when all L modular outputs differ; otherwise P(0)=K_L/L^2>1/L. For original order r=2^v*u with u odd and r<Q, the first nonuniform low-bit depth is floor(log2(u))+1. Earlier low-bit marginals are exactly uniform, not merely approximately flat.

At the first informative layer with t>=2 and odd L/2<u<L,

    P(y)=1/L+2*(L-u)/L^2*cos(2*pi*u*y/L).

Only C(0)=L and C(u)=C(L-u)=L-u are nonzero. N=71,a=2 gives uniform five low bits and a six-bit distribution (64+58*cos(2*pi*35*y/64))/4096; P(0)=61/2048.

Set c=(L-u)/L. Exact Parseval gives chi_squared(P||Uniform)=2*c^2. For n independent samples the product divergence is (1+2*c^2)^n-1, and total variation is at most half its square root. Equal-prior binary discrimination with error<=1/3 therefore requires n>=log(13/9)/log(1+2*c^2), or Omega((L/(L-u))^2) in the weak-signal regime. This is ONLY a measurement-only testing bound for these two distributions; it is not a factoring/order lower bound, does not account for other arithmetic side information, and does not cover full Shor samples. The self-inverse t=1 case is excluded.

## Executable interface and integer phase bounds

Otherwise the strictly decreasing model K_L(s)=L+2*sum_{j>=1}(L-j*s)_+ permits inherited integer binary inversion for the exact transformed order. Its minimality comes from the true coefficient, not merely checking b^s=1.

The coefficient costs B+ceil(L/B)-1 modular multiplications, one inverse and O(B) keys, plus preprocessing/decoding. This is O(m+2^(t/2)) group-scale work, NOT polynomial in t; no Q-entry statevector is built. Budget failure is explicit. The descriptor retains original N,a,m because finer marginals cannot be generated by independently appending bits to a uniform prefix.

## Stronger observations preserve the hard problem

For n=bit_length(N), Q=2^(2n), L=2^n, b has the odd part u of the original order. A certified zero-prefix probability p0 determines K=L^2*p0 and then u by integer inversion. Repeated squaring of a^u restores the power-of-two component. Absolute error<1/(2L^2) is sufficient for exact K rounding. Thus a polynomial strong marginal evaluator at this accuracy would yield polynomial original-order recovery; our square-root coefficient engine does not. The 7741 reduction checks use reference-derived masses, not evidence that those masses are cheap to obtain.

High-bit counterexample: N=7,a=2,Q=64 has uniform low bit but Pr[k<32]=1365/2048. For odd r<Q, symmetry and fibre parity prove |Pr[k<Q/2]-(1/2+1/(2r))|<=r/(2Q^2). Conditioning the same example on second-register output1 changes its two-low-bit distribution. Do not generalize the uniformity theorem to these different observers.
