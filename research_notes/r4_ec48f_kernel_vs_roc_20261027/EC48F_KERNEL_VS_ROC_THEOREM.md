### Proof 1: four memories are necessary for an interior microscopic kernel

Any factorization W=pi*u using K memory states places the row vectors W(h,*) in the convex hull T of at most K decoder points u_m in the unit square [0,1]^2. For 0<s<1, the four rows (s,0),(1,s),(s,1),(0,s), cyclically ordered, form a *genuine convex quadrilateral* D_s. A shoelace calculation gives

    2 Area(D_s) =
    |(s*s + 1*1 + s*s + 0*0) - (0*1 + s*s + 1*0 + s*s)| = 1.

Every triangle contained in the unit square has area <=1/2. Indeed twice signed triangle area is a determinant separately affine in its three vertices; the maximum of its absolute value over the Cartesian product of three unit squares is attained at square corners, where its largest absolute value is one. (The same bound holds for a degenerate triangle or at most two points.)

If K<=3, D_s is contained in a (possibly degenerate) triangle T inside the unit square. Thus 1/2=Area(D_s)<=Area(T)<=1/2. Full-dimensional compact convex sets with containment and equal area must coincide, but D_s is a genuine quadrilateral and T has at most three vertices: contradiction. Hence mu(W_s)>=4 for all 0<s<1. Encoding each h separately with K=4 gives equality.

At s=0 the distinct binary row vertices are 00,10,01; at s=1 they are 10,11,01. A 0/1 cube vertex cannot be expressed as a convex mixture of distinct points in that cube. Thus K>=3 at the endpoints; storing the distinct row profiles realizes each with K=3. This proves the exact jump.
