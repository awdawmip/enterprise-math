## 2. Hasse unit index of the quartic CM field

The quartic field \(F\) contains no imaginary-quadratic subfield, so its roots of unity are

\[
\mu(F)=\{\pm1\}.
\]

For any unit \(u\in\mathcal O_F^{\times}\), the quotient \(u/\bar u\) has absolute value one in every complex embedding and is therefore a root of unity. Hence

\[
\frac u{\bar u}\in\{1,-1\}.
\]

If \(u/\bar u=-1\), then \(\bar u=-u\), so \(u^2\in F^+\) is a totally negative unit and

\[
F=F^+(u)=F^+(\sqrt{u^2}).
\]

A quadratic extension generated by the square root of a unit cannot be ramified at an odd prime. But

\[
N_{F^+/\mathbf Q}(\mathfrak d_{F/F^+})=24
\]

shows that \(F/F^+\) is ramified at a prime above three. This is impossible. Therefore every unit is real:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal O_F^{\times}=\mu(F)\mathcal O_{F^+}^{\times}
=\mathcal O_{F^+}^{\times},
}
\]

and the CM Hasse unit index is

\[
\boxed{Q_F=1.}
\]
