    sum_{[g]∈Ω(y,d)/E} T_g diag(w0(a),w1(a),1) f_j(r).

Choose one transform per right E-coset. Reject a proposed transition if ||y.z||_1 exceeds the remaining final-horizon steps. Every coefficient is a nonnegative integer; the algorithm creates no per-state compiled matrix, expands no source orbit, and exactly reproduces the full unquotiented state histogram after canonical lift at *every layer*. Thus all 4×3 terminal observation coordinates are preserved at the fixed horizon.

For a chosen edge (r,a), the transforms sending its target y to d form Ω(y,d); two transforms yield the same incoming edge iff they differ by an element of E. Summing one per E-coset counts every incoming edge once, including the target stabilizer multiplicity, without averaging or division. ## Theorem 2: operator-memory bound

At depth t≤n, at most 9(2t+1)^6 typed states exist. Each weighted count is bounded by 36^t, since there are at most 12^t path words and each has weight at most 3^t; hence each channel has O(n) bits. The six signed coordinates take O(log(n+1)) bits each. Retaining only two complete canonical layers takes O(n^7) abstract bits. The streaming transfer enumerates at most 12 letters and four group transforms using O(n) additional transient arithmetic bits, and maintains **zero historical outgoing-operator cache**. A coarse schoolbook upper bound remains O(n^8) bit operations. This is a memory improvement, not an asserted asymptotic time-exponent improvement. A 512-entry pure-function LRU is also mathematically valid but incurs eviction work.

## Exact counterexample: terminal return pruning is not future-append sufficient

Compare path multisets U=empty and V={p}, p=(+E3,+E3), at depth 2. Their closed-return-only projections are identically empty, since p ends at 2E3. Append the same suffix (-E3,-E3). U still contributes zero; V contributes at displacement zero exactly (weighted0, weighted1, count)=(9,9,1). Therefore future append does not factor through the fixed-horizon terminal return projection. Preserving the typed K-orbit *within a fixed horizon* is exact; it does not excuse discarding off-return provenance for a larger future horizon.
