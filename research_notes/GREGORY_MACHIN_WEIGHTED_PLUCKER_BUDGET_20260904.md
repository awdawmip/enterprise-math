\[

## 2. Weighted free length of one rational atom

For a primitive strict rational atom

\[
z=b+ai,\qquad 0<a<b\le H,\qquad\gcd(a,b)=1,
\]

write its free Gaussian valuation vector

\[
v(z)=(v_p)_{p\equiv1(4)}.
\]

As proved in the support-two generator theorem, the odd free part of the norm is

\[
M(v)=\prod_p p^{|v_p|}
\]

and

\[
a^2+b^2\in\{M(v),2M(v)\}.
\]

Define the weighted free `L1` length

\[
\boxed{
X(v)=\sum_p|v_p|\ln p=\ln M(v).
}
\]

Since `a^2+b^2<2H^2`, every atom in the height box satisfies

\[
\boxed{
X(v)<L_H:=\ln(2H^2).
}
\]

Also every prime in its free support satisfies `p<2H^2`.

## 3. Primitive Plücker state of a rank-two valuation plane

Let `v,w` be two independent free valuation columns belonging to atoms of height at most `H`.

Their exterior product has coordinates

\[
(v\wedge w)_{pq}=v_pw_q-v_qw_p,
\qquad p<q.

Let

\[
g=\gcd\{|(v\wedge w)_{pq}|:(v\wedge w)_{pq}\ne0\}

and define the primitive exterior state

\[
\boxed{
\Omega=\operatorname{prim}(v\wedge w)=\frac1g(v\wedge w),

with one overall sign convention.

`Omega` is independent of the chosen integer basis of the same saturated rational plane.  It is the primitive Plücker coordinate of that plane.

## 4. Weighted Plücker height theorem

Define

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P(\Omega)
=\sum_{p<q}|\Omega_{pq}|\ln p\,\ln q.
}
\]

### Theorem 4.1

Every rank-two free valuation plane generated by two rational atoms with `b<=H` satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P(\Omega)<\ln^2(2H^2).
}
\]

### Proof

Primitive normalization can only decrease coefficient magnitudes, so

\[
\mathcal P(\Omega)
\le
\sum_{p<q}|v_pw_q-v_qw_p|\ln p\ln q.
\]

By the triangle inequality,

\[
|v_pw_q-v_qw_p|
\le |v_p||w_q|+|v_q||w_p|.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P(\Omega)
&\le
\sum_{p<q}
\left(|v_p||w_q|+|v_q||w_p|\right)
\ln p\ln q\\
&=X(v)X(w)-\sum_p|v_p||w_p|(\ln p)^2\\
&\le X(v)X(w)\\
&<L_H^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves the claim. ∎

The bound is **polylogarithmic in atom height** even though the previous no-go family can force every lattice basis to contain a generator of free multiplicative norm `Theta(H^2)`.

Only finitely many integer tensors satisfy these conditions.  Thus the possible rational rank-two planes are finite before any atom enumeration.

For a nonzero decomposable primitive `Omega`, define

\[
\boxed{

=\{x\in\mathbf Z^{(P)}:x\wedge\Omega=0\}.

Over `Q`, the equation `x wedge Omega=0` says exactly that `x` lies in the two-plane represented by `Omega`.  Intersecting with `Z^(P)` gives its saturated integer lattice.

Thus `Omega` determines the full free-valuation plane without choosing a potentially huge lattice basis.
