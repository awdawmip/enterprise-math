## 1. Canonical result

For the four-dimensional Prym variety

\[
P_{46}=\operatorname{Prym}(C_{46}/E),
\]

previous exact certificates give

\[
\operatorname{End}_{\overline{\mathbf Q}}(P_{46})=\mathbf Z[i],
\qquad
\operatorname{End}^0_{\overline{\mathbf Q}}(P_{46})=K=\mathbf Q(i),
\]

and Hodge signature \((3,1)\).  Let \(V=H^1(P_{46},\mathbf Q)\), let \(h\) be the induced \(K/\mathbf Q\)-Hermitian form, and set

\[
L=\operatorname{GU}_K(V,h).
\]

The verified conclusions are

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{MT}(P_{46})=L,}
\]

\[
\boxed{
G_\ell^\circ=L_{\mathbf Q_\ell}
\quad\text{for every prime }\ell,
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Hg}(P_{46})=\operatorname{U}_K(V,h),
\qquad
\operatorname{Hg}(P_{46})^{\mathrm{der}}=\operatorname{SU}_K(V,h).
}
\]

Thus the Mumford–Tate conjecture holds for \(P_{46}\) at every \(\ell\).

All Hodge classes on every power \(P_{46}^n\) are generated by divisor classes.  The same is true for \(\ell\)-adic Tate classes after finite base extension.  Hence the Hodge and Tate conjectures hold for every self-power of \(P_{46}\).

## 3. Group-theoretic upgrade

Over an algebraic closure,

\[
L\simeq GL_4\times G_m.
\]

A connected reductive subgroup \(H\subset GL_4\) of rank four with scalar commutant must equal \(GL_4\): irreducibility and Schur's lemma force center rank at most one, so the derived rank is at least three; the only faithful irreducible four-dimensional semisimple type of rank at least three is \(A_3\), and the remaining scalar center yields all of \(GL_4\).

Faltings' endomorphism-centralizer theorem supplies the scalar commutant.  The maximal Frobenius torus supplies rank five.  This proves

\[
G_\ell^\circ=L_{\mathbf Q_\ell}
\]

for every \(\ell\).  Deligne's absolute-Hodge inclusion together with \(\operatorname{MT}(P_{46})\subseteq L\) then gives equality of the Mumford–Tate group with \(L\).

## 4. Hodge and Tate cycles

After complexification,

\[
V_{\mathbf C}=W\oplus W^\vee,
\qquad
\operatorname{Hg}(P_{46})_{\mathbf C}\simeq GL(W),
\qquad \dim W=4.
\]

The first invariant theorem for \(GL(W)\) says that tensor invariants are generated by pairwise contractions \(W\otimes W^\vee\to\mathbf C\).  On cohomology of powers these are precisely the degree-two classes supplied by polarizations and \(K\)-endomorphisms.  Therefore all Hodge classes are generated by divisors.  Full connected \(\ell\)-adic monodromy gives the identical Tate-invariant calculation, and Faltings identifies the degree-two Tate classes with algebraic divisor classes.

The determinant line

\[
\bigwedge_K^4H^1
\]

has Hodge types \((3,1)\oplus(1,3)\) and transforms by the determinant character, so there is no exceptional Weil-type middle Hodge class.
