## 2. The return interface must include the arriving edge

For a fixed incoming i, the first outgoing neighbour cannot be i. There are two choices, and the second step has two choices outside b. Hence there are four possible two-edge prefixes. After these two steps, avoiding b forces the walk along one of the two directed three-cycles of the other three vertices. At every subsequent step, one allowed choice returns to b and the other continues along that cycle.

Consequently Pr(L=l)=2^{-(l-2)} for l>=3, E[L]=4, and Pr(L>M)=2^{-(M-2)} for M>=2. This law holds for each incoming i, including after arbitrary earlier excursions.
