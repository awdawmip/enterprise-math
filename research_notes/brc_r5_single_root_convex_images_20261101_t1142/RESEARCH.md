## Theorem A — convexification of TWO-law downstream terminal flows

For a source-separated downstream tree with complete-history controls, its simultaneously attainable P- and Q-terminal event-mass vectors form a compact convex set. For rational exogenous kernels and terminal observations the image is a rational polytope generated by deterministic policies, so each K_h is convex.

Proof. At history v and policy π write beta_P(v), beta_Q(v) for law-specific products of nature transition probabilities, and s_pi(v) for the product of all relevant ancestor CONTINUE probabilities. Arrival under law L is beta_L(v)s_pi(v), with the SAME survival factor under both laws. For policies π1,π2 and any λ∈[0,1], define s(v)=λs_1(v)+(1−λ)s_2(v). For s(v)>0 set the new STOP probability
p(v)=[λs_1(v)p_1(v)+(1−λ)s_2(v)p_2(v)]/s(v);
if s(v)=0 choose any p(v). Then s(child)=s(v)(1−p(v))=λs_1(child)+(1−λ)s_2(child) at every labelled nature-child history. Inductively this realizes the λ-mixture of BOTH law-specific terminal flow vectors, including all STOP/CONTINUE leaf masses. Since the policy cube is compact and the observation map is continuous, the image is compact. A no-revisited-key policy output is multiaffine in its separate decision parameters, so it lies in the convex hull of finitely many deterministic outputs. Conversely the image is convex and contains these vertices, proving equality with that convex hull. QED.
