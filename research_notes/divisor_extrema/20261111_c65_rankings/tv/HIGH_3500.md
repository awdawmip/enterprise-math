# A 3,500-fold TV separation for high defects

For $k\ge65$, if at least one internal edge at $r\ge64$ is noncanonical, then $R_b=\Delta_b/\Delta_A>3500$. This refinement of the [C64 high-1000 proof](https://github.com/awdawmip/enterprise-math/blob/8db83d05fa1e5c507ff3f88697f79d1f00bc9c27/experiments/divisor_extrema/20261111_c64/supplement/high_1000.md) also uses the [C64 off-diagonal cut bound](https://github.com/awdawmip/enterprise-math/blob/127f70da9f81b2fd66a512117cc97a3b7ed256e9/research_notes/divisor_extrema/20261111_c64/HIGH_AND_TAIL.md).

Write $B=21/10$, $h=377/378$, $d=99/100$, $P=W_{\rm start}\le1$, and let $p\ge1$ count noncanonical high edges. A high edge has surplus $s=c-r-1$ and successor $s'=s+1-m$. For each high defect let $Z=L/(G_r Q)>B$, using the notation of C63. If $D_{\rm cut}$ is any valid lower bound for its suffix and $U$ the canonical upper slope, the gluing bound is
$$
 R_b\ \ge\ h\,(W_{\rm cut}D_{\rm cut}/U)\,P^{-1}\prod_{\rm defects}Z.
$$
At every stock a valid weighted suffix bound exceeds $d$. At $2\le c_{\rm cut}\le96$, $c_{\rm cut}\ne64$, it exceeds 600. At $65\le c_{\rm cut}\le70$ it exceeds 49508, by [the C64 finite proof](https://github.com/awdawmip/enterprise-math/blob/8db83d05fa1e5c507ff3f88697f79d1f00bc9c27/research_notes/divisor_extrema/20261111_c64_low_ranks_and_stabilization.md).

1. For $p\ge12$ use $h d B^{12}>3500$. For $b_0\ge4$ use $P\le1/4096$ and $h d B\cdot4096>3500$. In what follows, $p\le11$ and $s_{\rm start}\in\{-2,-1,0,1\}$.
2. Any noncanonical edge is preceded by at most ten distinct defects, so its initial surplus is at most 11. Thus $c\le r+12$ and $c/(2r+1)\le76/129<3/5$. For any $m\ge2$, the unchanged near-inventory bound from the high-1000 proof yields $\rho\le4913/8192<3/5$ and $7\cdot16^{-m}\le\tau\le63(17/92)^m<3$. Indeed the local factors of $H_0/H_2$ are at most $17/92$, and the two rate ratios at the end are bounded by 3. The minimum of $t/(t+1)^2$ on this interval exceeds $6\cdot16^{-m}$ (check its endpoints). Hence $L>(12/5)16^{-m}$, and $G_r\le1/(100r^2)$, $Q\le16^{2-m}$ yield $Z>(15/16)r^2\ge3840$. Now $R_b>h d\cdot3840>3500$.
3. Suppose all high $m\le1$ and $s_{\rm start}<0$. The first edge is a defect. If $m=0$, C64 gives $Z>15r^2/256\ge240$, with $P\le1/16$, implying $R_b>h d\cdot16\cdot240>3500$. If $m=1$, C64 gives $Z>50r/46\ge 50\cdot64/46$. For $p\ge3$ combine this with two factors exceeding $B$ and $P\le1/16$. The resulting bound exceeds 4,800. For $p\le2$, the first edge preserves its negative surplus and at most one further unit can be gained. If $s_{\rm cut}<0$ then $62\le c_{\rm cut}\le63$ and $R_b>h\cdot600\cdot16B>3500$. If $s_{\rm cut}=0$ the second defect must be $m=0$ from $s=-1$. At this edge apply the same local $Z>15r^2/256\ge240$; gluing again gives $R_b>3500$. Positive cut surplus is impossible here.
4. Suppose all high $m\le1$ and $s_{\rm start}\ge0$. Surplus is nondecreasing and a high defect occurred, so $1\le s_{\rm cut}\le12$, hence $65\le c_{\rm cut}\le76$. For $p\ge3$, use $h B^3\cdot600>3500$. For $p\le2$, $c_{\rm cut}\le67$ and $h B\cdot49508>3500$.

The conclusion holds uniformly for every pair of distinct input endpoints induced by the histories. Combined with the universal readout comparison $\log H>(53/37)\Delta$, it gives $\log H>5013\,\Delta_A$ for these competitors. The latter strict integer threshold follows from $(53/37)3500>5013$.
