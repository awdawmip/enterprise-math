## 1. Motivation and admissibility

After the user correction that microscopic/high-order differentiation is not automatically stable under the project's rough finite-resolution relation, replace analytic derivative jets by exact finite polynomial coefficients. Polynomial coefficients of a declared finite matrix are finite algebraic observables and require no interpolation between retained states.

Set circumference L=1 and

A_N=N^2(2I-R_N-R_N^*).

Define the normalized finite determinant polynomial

Delta_N(u)=det(A_N+uI)/N^(2N).

Since the zero eigenvalue is simple and the product of the nonzero A_N eigenvalues is N^(2N),

Delta_N(u)=u prod_(k=1)^(N-1) (1+u/lambda_(N,k)).

Write

Delta_N(u)=u sum_(j=0)^(N-1) e_j(N) u^j,

where e_j(N) is the j-th elementary symmetric polynomial in the nonzero inverse eigenvalues (with e_0=1).

## 2. Exact finite coefficient formula

The exact Chebyshev identity is

Delta_N(u)=2[T_N(1+u/(2N^2))-1].

Coefficient extraction in the finite polynomial gives, for 0<=j<N,

`e_j(N)= 2/(2j+2)! * product_(r=1)^j (1-r^2/N^2)`.

Equivalently,

Delta_N(u)=sum_(n=1)^N [2/(2n)! * product_(r=1)^(n-1)(1-r^2/N^2)] u^n.

This is an exact finite identity; no u-derivative or N-interpolation is required. Coefficients may be obtained by exact determinant/polynomial arithmetic.

For fixed j, integer refinement gives the coefficient limit directly:

`e_j(infinity)=2/(2j+2)!`.

## 3. Exact polynomial refinement composition

For integer q>=2,

Delta_(qN)(u)=Delta_N(u_(q,N)),

where

u_(q,N)=2N^2[T_q(1+u/(2q^2N^2))-1].

For q=2,

Delta_(2N)(u)=Delta_N(u+u^2/(16N^2)).

Thus all finite coefficient transport is polynomial composition. In the dyadic case this yields the explicit coefficient recursion

`e_j(2N)=sum_(r=0)^floor((j+1)/2) binom(j+1-r,r) (1/(16N^2))^r e_(j-r)(N)`.

The binomial factor is the path-matching/Lucas coefficient generated by the quadratic child split.

## 4. Local branch algebra for power moments

For one nonzero dyadic parent inverse mass x=lambda_(N,k)^(-1), its two child inverse masses x_+,x_- satisfy

x_+ + x_- = x,

x_+ x_- = c_N x,

c_N=1/(16N^2).

Hence for integer m>=1,

x_+^m+x_-^m
=
sum_(j=0)^floor(m/2)
(-1)^j * m/(m-j) * binom(m-j,j) * c_N^j x^(m-j).

The zero fiber contributes one nonzero Nyquist child with inverse mass c_N. Therefore, with

P_m(N)=Tr'(A_N^(-m)),

`P_m(2N)=c_N^m + sum_j A_(m,j)c_N^j P_(m-j)(N)`

with the same finite Lucas coefficients. This is the derivative-free triangular moment refinement law.
