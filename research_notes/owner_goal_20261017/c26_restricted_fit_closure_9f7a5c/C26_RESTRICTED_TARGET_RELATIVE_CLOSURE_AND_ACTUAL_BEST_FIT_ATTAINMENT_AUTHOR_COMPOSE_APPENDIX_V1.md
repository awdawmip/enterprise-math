## 2. Exact restricted image and projective coordinates

## 3. Fixed-T positive strip compactness, fully paid

The needed extra obligation is that a bounded h cannot hide targets
escaping to a zero coordinate. This is qualitative fixed-T compactness,
not a pairwise quantitative inverse estimate or T-uniform modulus.

Let m=min_(y,z)T_yz>0 and M=max_(y,z)T_yz. Mean1 gives

 mN<=(TK)(y)<=MN.

If max_y|h_T(K)(y)|<=B with B>=0, then

 K(y)/K(y0)=[(TK)(y)/(TK)(y0)] exp(-h_T(K)(y)).                  (7)

With q=(m/M)exp(-B)>0, every such ratio lies in [q,1/q], including
the basepoint ratio1. Summing the upper bounds and using sum K=N gives
K(y0)>=q, so every K(y)>=q^2. Every coordinate is also at most N.
The strip {K in Pplus:max|h_T(K)|<=B} is consequently a closed subset
of the compact box q^2<=K(y)<=N with sum K=N: continuity of (5) is
available throughout that positive box. Hence this strip is compact.
No compactness of the whole open domain Pplus, and no global compactness
of F, was assumed. Strict positivity of the fixed T is used here.

## 4. Closure identity without enlarging F by zero targets

Assume the good schedule. Suppose S_n in S_F converges to S in G.
Each S_n is in the closed E0, so S remains there. Its unique coefficients
give a_n to a and h_n to h; in particular h_n is bounded. Choose the actual
K_n in F representing S_n. Section3 gives a subsequence K_nk converging
to a strictly positive mean-one K. Since each K_nk is in F, K is in
barF. Continuity gives h=h_T(K), and
S=V a+b h_T(K) belongs to S_barF by the exact construction in section2.
Thus closure(S_F) is contained in S_barF.

Conversely, if K in barF and S=V a+b h_T(K), select K_n in F tending to
K. Continuity gives V a+b h_T(K_n) to S, and every approximating element
belongs to S_F with its actual positive reconstructed nuisances.
Therefore

 closure_G(S_F)=S_barF.                                        (8)

The isometry (4) proves statement (3a). Applying the same argument to a
relatively closed nonempty F proves S_F is closed in G.

For the converse, if F is not relatively closed, choose a positive
K_* in barF minus F. The element S_*=b h_T(K_*) with all a=0 is in
S_barF=closure(S_F). If it also belonged to S_F, uniqueness of H's
coefficient decomposition would force h_T(K)=h_T(K_*) for some K in F.
Positive target uniqueness would give K=K_*, a contradiction. Hence S_F
is not closed. This proves

 F relatively closed in Pplus iff S_F closed in G.              (9)

This implication would fail without the actual good-schedule hypothesis;
the bad case does not identify the target and is handled in section7.

## 5. Actual best fit and the exact universal quantifier

The family barF is nonempty and relatively closed. Its image S_barF is
closed by (9). For A=g(P), choose a minimizing sequence S_n in S_barF.
The infimum is finite because this image is nonempty. Residual norms can
be kept bounded, and
nu(S_n)<=nu(A)+nu(A-S_n) bounds the sequence. The finite-coordinate norm
bounds in (4) give a convergent subsequence. Closedness and continuity
produce S_star in S_barF attaining the distance. Its inverse softmax is
positive and normalized, and section2 returns an actual K in barF,
positive Q,D and all Z_j. No parameter-space compactness was used.

Distance to a set equals distance to its closure here directly: one
inequality is inclusion; for the other, approximate the nearest S_star
in closure(S_F) by points of S_F and use continuity of nu(A-S).
Together with (8) this gives

 delta_F(P)=min_(R in M_barF)d(P,R).                            (10)

If F is relatively closed, (10) proves actual attainment in M_F for
EVERY complete positive P. Conversely, if F is not relatively closed,
use S_* from section4 and P_*=softmax(S_*). It is a complete positive
table in closure(M_F) but outside M_F, so delta_F(P_*)=0. Attainment
would give R in M_F with d(P_*,R)=0; normalized positive rows then have
equal ratios and are equal in full, forcing R=P_*, a contradiction.
This proves all three equivalences in (3c), with its universal quantifier.
It does not say that every P fails when F is not closed.

For a fixed P, (10) also proves the exact attainment statement preceding
section2: a nearest table from M_barF belongs to M_F precisely when its
good-schedule recovered target belongs to F. Multiple nearest tables
may exist and their targets may differ. No algorithm or decidability
of this membership condition is asserted.

## 6. Fully positive nonattainment example and strict value gap

Take Y={0,1}, y0=0, T the2 by2 all-ones matrix, d=0, L=2,
positions (0,1), labels (0,1). H has rows (1,0),(1,1), so the schedule
is good. Let

 K_t=(1+t,1-t), 0<t<1/2, F={K_t:0<t<1/2}.

All targets are strictly positive of mean1. Let P_1=P_2=(1/2,1/2).
For Q=(1/2,1/2) and no drift factors, the actual model rows are
R_1=K_t/2 and R_2=(1/2,1/2), since TK_t=(2,2). Therefore

 d(P,R)=log((1+t)/(1-t)) to0 as t decreases to0.                 (11)

The infimum in F is0. If P were produced by any K_t in F, the b=1 row
would equal Q and force Q uniform. The b=0 row would then be K_t/2;
its equality to the uniform row would force t=0, which is excluded.
Thus no actual best fit exists. The missing target (1,1) is itself
strictly positive: this failure is not caused by zero data, zero T,
a singular target boundary, unknown labels or omitted normalization.

A different obstruction remains even for closed F. In ANY good schedule,
if F is a nonempty proper relatively closed subset of Pplus, take a
positive K_* outside F and any actual model table P built from it.
Good-schedule uniqueness gives P outside M_F, and M_F is closed.
Its distance delta_F(P) is then strictly positive and attained, whereas
delta_Pplus(P)=0. Hence the unrestricted finite signed certificates
cannot generally compute the original restricted distance. Closedness
fixes attainment, not the hidden target-family membership/value gap.

Relative closedness really suffices without global compactness: in the
same two-state domain, {K_t:1/2<=t<1} is relatively closed in Pplus
but not compact in the ambient mean-one hyperplane. The theorem still
gives actual best fits for all positive complete data; section3 prevents
a bounded-error minimizing sequence from escaping to t=1.
