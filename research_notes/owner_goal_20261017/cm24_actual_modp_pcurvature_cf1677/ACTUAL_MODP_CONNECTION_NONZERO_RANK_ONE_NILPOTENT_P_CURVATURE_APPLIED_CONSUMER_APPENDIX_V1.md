## 2. Literal reduction and L-linearity of p-curvature

We give the integral reduction bridge instead of reducing an
unspecified characteristic-zero weak function. By the integral
finite-generator weak completion, h in R0 has a presentation

 h=sum_(l>=0) p^l P_l(r,f^-1,q^-1),
 P_l with O-coefficients, generator degree <=C(l+1).           (6)

R0 is the weak subring of the separated p-adic completion of A0
specified by these presentations. A0 is p-torsionfree and
A0/pA0=A. Its p-adic completion is also p-torsionfree: if p h=0,
then modulo p^(n+1) a lift a of h satisfies p a in p^(n+1)A0.
Cancellation in A0 gives a in p^n A0. Thus h is zero modulo
every p^n, and separatedness gives h=0. In particular p is a
non-zero-divisor on R0.

The reduction of (6) is P0 modulo p, an element of A. Conversely
every element of A lifts in A0, so this map is onto. If its
reduction vanishes, P0=pQ0 in A0, and

 h/p=Q0+sum_(l>=1) p^(l-1) P_l

is again integral weak: shifting the index keeps a linear
generator-degree bound, and the one finite initial term is harmless.
Consequently its kernel is exactly pR0, and

 R0/pR0=A.                                                   (7)

In particular 1 is nonzero modulo p, so p is not a unit in R0.
Differentiation preserves the weak presentation: differentiating a
generator introduces integral integer factors and a bounded degree
increase. It therefore induces the displayed partial on A and L.

The old substituted matrix sigma(D)(r^p) in (5) really is integral
weak. Here the coefficients of D are integral on the indicated
localization, and f(r^p),q(r^p) are congruent to f^p,q^p modulo p.
The inverses of their factors 1+pU have integral geometric series
with degree growing linearly with the geometric index and valuation

not a localization/strong-completion exchange.

In characteristic p, partial^p is a derivation by the iterated

It is L-linear. Let Psi in Mat2(L) be its row matrix, so that

## 3. An actual horizontal row modulo p and nilpotence

Reduce the ACTUAL integral identities (5) using (7). Their right
side is divisible by p in the original weak ring. Hence

 bar(F)'+bar(F)bar(D)=0, det bar(F)=0.                        (8)

The entire matrix bar(F) cannot vanish. If it did, (7) would give
F=pG for some G in Mat2(R0). Taking determinants in (5) would give
p=p^2 det G. Cancellation of p gives 1=p det G, which reduces to
the contradiction 1=0 in A. Therefore bar(F) is a nonzero matrix
of generic rank one over L.

In particular one row v of bar(F) is a nonzero element of L^(1x2)
and is actually horizontal by (8). Thus psi(v)=0 and

 det Psi=0.                                                  (9)

This step only needs some nonzero horizontal row, not a previously
chosen second row or a determinant shortcut for a p-primitive row
in every Frobenius iterate. It does not invoke any cokernel dimension.

The original D is traceless, including after reduction. The induced
determinant connection on the exterior square of this row module
is consequently the ordinary derivation partial. Repeated exterior
Leibniz, proved by induction, gives in characteristic p

 nabla_det^p(u wedge w)
     =nabla^p(u) wedge w+u wedge nabla^p(w).

The intermediate binomial coefficients vanish. On the constant
standard basis wedge, its left side is zero; its right side is
tr(Psi) times that nonzero wedge. Therefore

 tr Psi=0.

For a two-by-two matrix the characteristic identity, directly
valid over the field L, now gives from (9)

 Psi^2-tr(Psi)Psi+det(Psi)I=Psi^2=0.                         (10)

Thus the actual p-curvature is nilpotent. This conclusion uses
the actual integral Frobenius reduction and determinant connection,

## 4. Local highest-pole coefficient proves nonvanishing

It remains essential to exclude Psi=0. We do so by a finite local
recurrence in the ORIGINAL frame, not by reducing a gauge with
uncontrolled denominators.

At r=1, S(1)=1 and P'(1)=-6. Since p is neither 2 nor 3,
the literal rational matrix gives, with x=r-1,

 bar(D)=N/x+D_reg(x), D_reg in Mat2(k[[x]]),

where N is exactly (4). Direct multiplication gives N^2=0,
whereas N is nonzero over k.

For a constant standard row e_i, define the actual local matrices

 A_0=I, A_(j+1)=A_j'+A_j bar(D);
 nabla^j(e_i)=e_i A_j.

Induction shows A_j has pole order at most j. Write C_j for its
coefficient of x^-j, allowing that coefficient to be zero. The
highest term in the derivative is -j C_j x^(-j-1), and the
highest product term is C_j N x^(-j-1). Lower terms and D_reg
cannot reach that order. Hence

 C_0=I, C_(j+1)=C_j(N-jI),
 C_p=product_(j=0)^(p-1)(N-jI)=N^p-N.                       (11)

The last equality is the polynomial T^p-T over the prime field,
substituted into the single matrix N; all its factors commute.
Since N^2=0 and p>=2, N^p=0. Thus

 C_p=-N!=0.                                                  (12)

On constant rows A_p is precisely the matrix Psi of nabla^p.
Equation (12) is therefore its actual x^-p coefficient. The pole
bound already proved shows its order is exactly p. In particular
Psi is nonzero in Mat2(k((x))) and hence in Mat2(L). Combined with
(10), a nonzero nilpotent two-by-two matrix has rank exactly one.
This proves every part of (3)-(4).
