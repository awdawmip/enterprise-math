# A Structural Feasibility Criterion and a Unique 66-State Nine-Source Configuration in a Restricted Four-Coordinate Boolean Sumset Family

**Research memorandum — full author-side manuscript, 22 October 2026.** This paper is a computer-assisted, finite-domain result, not a Driver-reviewed or formally admitted Enterprise Math theorem.

## Abstract

We study finite sumset bases in the integer simplex \(\Delta_8^4\), with a fixed 45-point support-at-most-two skeleton \(R\), nine support-three points \(Q\), and freely chosen support-four completion points \(W\). A novel exact feasibility criterion shows that the existence of *any* completion \(W\) is equivalent to covering only twelve specific support-three targets by \((R\cup Q)+(R\cup Q)\); this is proved without numerical search. In a frozen family of \(\binom{75}{3}=67,525\) nine-point sets generated by removing indices \(\{1,2,64\}\) from a fixed seed, independent exact integer enumerations find 66,879 configurations not completable even using all 35 support-four points, and 646 potentially completable configurations. Exhaustive exact solvers show that only one of the latter 646 admits completion with at most twelve support-four points. We exhibit and independently check the resulting 66-state sumset basis for all 969 degree-sixteen targets. Four-coordinate symmetries enlarge the previously certified restricted family to 2,331,754 configurations with a common 66-state necessary bound; the combined restricted family also attains that bound. No unrestricted lower bound for \(\beta_4(8)\) or fidelity theorem for the P000 native six-axis model is asserted.

## 2. A non-full-support feasibility theorem

**Theorem 1.** For an arbitrary \(Q\subseteq X_3\), some \(W\subseteq X_4\) completes the sumset if and only if \((R\cup Q)+(R\cup Q)\) contains every support-at-most-three member of \(\Delta_{16}^4\). Whenever this necessary condition holds, choosing \(W=X_4\) suffices.

**Proof.** A sum involving an \(X_4\) point has all four coordinates positive. Hence only \((R\cup Q)+(R\cup Q)\) can represent lower-support targets. For sufficiency, we prove every full-support target \(y\) lies in \((R\cup X_4)+(R\cup X_4)\). If all coordinates of \(y\) are at least two, split \(y=x+(y-x)\) with both summands positive in degree eight: one chooses \(x_i\in[1,y_i-1]\) with sum eight, possible because the allowed sum interval is \([4,12]\). Otherwise sort the four positive coordinates as \(1=a\le b\le c\le d\). Since \(b+c+d=15\) and \(b\le c\le d\), we have \(c+d\ge10\). Remove a degree-eight vector \(r\), supported on the two largest coordinate positions and bounded by \(c-1,d-1\); then \(y-r\in X_4\). The only vector disallowed in \(R\) is \((4,4,0,0)\). If it is the sole candidate for \(r\), the two selected capacities must be exactly \((4,4)\), so \(y\) is a permutation of \((1,5,5,5)\); choose another pair among its three coordinates equal to five. This avoids the excluded vector. Therefore every full-support target is covered independently of \(Q\), proving the theorem. \(\square\)

Among the 514 support-at-most-three degree-sixteen targets, \(R+R\) already contains 502. The twelve remaining targets, in explicit lexicographic order, are

\[(4,9,0,3),(4,9,3,0),(4,10,0,2),(4,10,2,0),(4,11,0,1),(4,11,1,0),\]
\[(9,4,0,3),(9,4,3,0),(10,4,0,2),(10,4,2,0),(11,4,0,1),(11,4,1,0).\]

Thus Theorem 1 reduces arbitrary-\(W\) feasibility to twelve Boolean conditions involving just \(R+Q\) and \(Q+Q\). In particular, no failure of these conditions can be repaired by adding more support-four states.

## 3. Census and sharp twelve-point boundary

The frozen 67,525 input configurations are enumerated without replacement in lexicographic order, each recorded as nine index bytes followed by eighteen protocol padding bytes. An independent Python program computes exact support-at-most-three coverage using integer bit masks for the 514 targets; a separate C++ program constructs all producer clauses and detects those with no producer. Both find **66,879 impossible configurations** and **646 completable configurations**. The missing-hard-target distribution is 5,898 with one missing condition, 17,301 with two, and 43,680 with three; it is independently reproducible from the frozen input.

For any \(Q\), with \(B=R\cup Q\), each residual target \(t\notin B+B\) can be represented by a singleton or pair of indices of \(X_4\), depending on whether \(t=b+w\) for a fixed \(b\in B\), or \(t=w+w'\). The residual coverage condition is therefore a finite monotone Boolean disjunction-of-producers, one clause per residual target. Complete branch-and-bound chooses an unmet clause with fewest feasible producers and tries each missing producer in turn; an admissible disjoint-alternative lower bound may prune a branch. Integer bitmasks and exact subset arithmetic suffice; overflow, float tolerances, and heuristic final verdicts are avoided.

Two independently compiled C++17 implementations of the same mathematical recursion exclude \(|W|\le11\) for **all 67,525** configurations, with no YES/UNKNOWN outcomes or abnormal exits. On the 646 support-unobstructed configurations, both implementations exclude \(|W|\le12\) for exactly **645**, and return YES for exactly **one**. The principal twelve-point search visits 724,727 branch nodes in aggregate; its maximum of 6,266 for one configuration is well below the declared 200,000 node cap. Both programs completed normally. This constitutes author-side exact finite proof evidence, not mathematically independent review.

## 5. Symmetry closure and scope

The coordinate permutation subgroup preserving \((4,4,0,0)\) has order four, preserves \(R,X_3,X_4\), and commutes with pairwise addition. It therefore carries every impossibility certificate for a particular \(Q\) to each of its four images. The present 67,525 direct configurations have 270,100 distinct images. Direct set equality and deduplication against the preceding author-checked PREFIX-179 union of 2,061,662 configurations finds overlap eight and a new union size **2,331,754**.
