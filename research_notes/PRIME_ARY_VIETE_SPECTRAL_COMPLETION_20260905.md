## 1. Prime-ary half-phase states

Fix a prime `p`.  Define

\[
\boxed{
y_j:=\frac{\tau}{2p^j},
\qquad
s_j:=S(y_j),
\qquad j\ge0.
}
\tag{PAV-1}

Then

\[
s_0=S(\tau/2)=1,
\]

and

\[
y_j=p y_{j+1}.
\]

Because `0<y_j<=tau/2`, the internal cosine companion is positive and `S` is strictly increasing on this interval.  Hence every backward refinement state is the unique positive identity-near inverse branch of the `p`-fold phase law.

## 2. Odd-prime multiple-angle polynomial

For odd `p`, repeated use of the internal addition laws eliminates `C^2=1-S^2` and gives an integer odd polynomial `A_p` such that

\[
\boxed{S(py)=A_p(S(y)).}
\tag{PAV-2}

It is exactly the sine multiple-angle polynomial, but here it is generated from the internal power-series rotation law rather than assumed as classical circle input.

The first examples are

\[
\boxed{A_3(s)=3s-4s^3,}
\]

\[
\boxed{A_5(s)=5s-20s^3+16s^5.}
\]

More generally

\[
A_p(s)=p s+O(s^3)
\]

and all coefficients are integers.

Therefore the prime-ary states obey the exact algebraic refinement recurrence

\[
\boxed{s_j=A_p(s_{j+1}),\qquad s_0=1.}
\tag{PAV-3}

Each `s_(j+1)` is algebraic over the field generated by `s_j`; no value of classical `pi` is used.

## 3. Layer factor from one refinement step

The prime-adic Euler-mode layer theorem gives at `x=tau/2`

\[
\mathcal L_{p,j}(\tau/2)
=\frac{S(\tau/(2p^j))}
{pS(\tau/(2p^{j+1}))}.
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{
c_{p,j}:=\frac{s_j}{p s_{j+1}}.}
\tag{PAV-4}

Then

\[
\boxed{
c_{p,j}
=\prod_{\substack{k\ge1\\v_p(k)=j}}
\left(1-\frac1{4k^2}\right).
}
\tag{PAV-5}

Using (PAV-3),

\[
\boxed{
c_{p,j}
=\frac{A_p(s_{j+1})}{p s_{j+1}}.
}
\tag{PAV-6}

For odd `p`, `A_p(s)/(ps)` is a rational polynomial in `s^2` with constant term one.

## 5. Completion product

\[
\prod_{j=0}^{J}c_{p,j}
=\frac{s_0}{p^{J+1}s_{J+1}}.

\[

\[
\boxed{
