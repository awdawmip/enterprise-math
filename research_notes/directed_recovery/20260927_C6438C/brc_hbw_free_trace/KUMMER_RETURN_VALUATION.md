# The elliptic x-coordinate quotient also squares return depth

Status: **PURE_SYMBOLIC_OBSERVER_LEMMA / NOT_EXECUTED / NOT_ADMITTED**. Author context `EM-DIRECT-C6438C`, activity `RA-CAAAC604CB513AEA8BBC1DFC`. This new note tests a proposed nonlinear geometric interface; it is not an elliptic factoring implementation or a general success result.

Consider the smooth Montgomery curve over R=Z/NZ, with N odd,

`B y^2=x^3+A x^2+x`, `gcd(B(A^2-4),N)=1`.

Its ordinary projective plane model is

`B Y^2 Z=X^3+A X^2 Z+X Z^2`, `O=[0:1:0]`.

Suppose a point P is represented by a unimodular homogeneous triple (X,Y,Z), and its x-coordinate Kummer image is represented by a unimodular pair (K,L), with O mapping to [1:0]. These are actual geometric point representations, not arbitrary tuples or an unproved cancellation of a common factor. Set

`g_point=gcd(N,X,Z)`, `g_x=gcd(N,L)`.

Then the exact divisor relation is

`g_x=gcd(g_point^2,N)`.                                  (1)

## Local proof

Fix p^e dividing N. If P is not O modulo p, its x-coordinate is finite modulo p, so L is a unit and both sides of (1) have p-valuation zero. If P reduces to O, then Y is a unit and the local coordinates u=X/Y, v=Z/Y both vanish modulo p. The curve equation becomes

`B v=u^3+A u^2 v+u v^2`.

Put `h=B-A u^2-u v`. Since B is a unit and u,v vanish modulo p, h is a unit. The exact equation is

`v h=u^3`.

Hence the point-identity ideal (u,v) is generated by u. Its depth is the p-valuation of u, capped at e. On this chart the x-coordinate morphism has a regular primitive representation

`[K:L]=[h:u^2]`.

Indeed x=X/Z=u/v=h/u^2 on its ordinary finite overlap, and the right-hand pair extends regularly to O because h is a unit. This is a proof of the extension, not a license to divide an actual residue tuple (u,v) by the potentially nonunit u. Therefore the Kummer infinity ideal has depth twice that of u, capped at e. All valid changes of homogeneous representatives use local units and preserve these ideals. This establishes the p-valuation equality in (1) at every component, and proves the divisor identity.

The proof can be carried out in the local residue ring using the unit h and the displayed equations; it does not ask a production algorithm to know p, compute a square root, or invert u.

## Consequences for a native geometric tool

For squarefree N, g_x and g_point are equal. A proper gcd of L with N is always a sound factor certificate after exact division. At repeated prime factors the x-coordinate quotient can saturate while the full point-identity ideal remains proper, exactly as the companion trace quotient can square the return depth. A primitive x:z pair prevents an undefined all-zero projective image; it does not remove the ramification of the map from the full curve to its x-coordinate quotient.

Thus two different claims must stay separate: a division-free Kummer ladder can preserve a valid projective x-coordinate and an exact Kummer gcd; preserving the unsquared elliptic identity ideal requires a full point lift or an additional unramified coordinate. The latter needs its own transitions, proof and cost. It must not be assumed from the existence of the x-only formulas.

This is a reusable observer-design rule in the present geometry. The coordinate representation, its defining ideal, and the multiplicity of a quotient map matter to BRC valuation preservation. A cheaper quotient may be sufficient for squarefree factor support, while a different contract is needed for primitive return depth. Neither contract supplies the unknown useful exponent or a general factorization success bound.

No native elliptic point or Kummer trajectory was executed in this note. Standard smooth-curve and quotient geometry is reused; only the explicitly proved observer/certificate composition is proposed for the project.
