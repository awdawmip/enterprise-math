## 2. Conjugacy cannot change a complete return ideal

For a matrix over `R=Z/NZ`, let `I(A)` be the ideal generated by all its entries. If `S` is invertible over R, then

`I(S A S^-1)=I(A)`.                                      (1)

Every entry on the left is an R-linear combination of entries of A; multiplication by `S^-1` and S gives the reverse inclusion. Consequently, for either sign and every nonnegative integer E,

`gcd(N, entries((S M S^-1)^E - epsilon I))`

`= gcd(N, entries(M^E - epsilon I))`.                      (2)

The assertion includes all prime-power depths. It does not require diagonalization, an eigenvalue oracle, or a field. A cyclic mark may replace the full matrix only when its certified source already proves equality with this complete ideal. A single arbitrary coordinate is a different observer and need not obey (2).

A singular candidate change of basis is outside (1). Its determinant gcd must be paid and retained: a proper gcd is already a factor; gcd N is an unresolved degeneracy, not permission to invert the matrix. Geometric language does not remove this check.

There is a coordinate-free version. For a smooth pointed curve over R and a genuine point Q, define its identity divisor `g_O(Q)` as the largest divisor d of N for which Q reduces to O over `Z/dZ`. Equivalently it is the gcd of a complete set of local generators for the pulled-back identity ideal. In an ordinary primitive Weierstrass triple it is `gcd(N,X,Z)`. A pointed group-scheme isomorphism and its inverse commute with all these reductions, so they preserve `g_O([E]P)` exactly. A translated chart must transport the distinguished target as well; changing the target is not an isomorphism-invariant version of the old event.

## 3. Trace reparameterization and cross clocks stay in the old torus

Use `M(k)=[[0,1],[-1,k]]`, `V_m=tr(M(k)^m)`. In this section only, define `U_0=0, U_1=1, U_(m+1)=k U_m-U_(m-1)`; this is not the alternative degree-indexed convention used in the companion spectrum note. Cayley-Hamilton induction gives

`M(k)^m=U_m M(k)-U_(m-1) I`,

`V_m^2-4=(k^2-4) U_m^2`.                                (3)

For the fixed cyclic vector `v=(0,1)`, `det[v,M(k)^m v]=-U_m`. Thus when U_m is a unit, the cyclic basis identifies `M(k)^m` with the companion for trace V_m, up to a fixed unit basis convention. Replacing k by V_m therefore replaces E by mE and preserves the complete signed ideal. It does not create a new local torus. Its discriminant character is unchanged by (3).

If U_m is not a unit and `k^2-4` is a unit, at a residue prime its vanishing is precisely `lambda^m=lambda^-m`, or `lambda^(2m)=1`. The determinant failure is a paid Lucas/return witness. Calling it a new conjugacy does not avoid the clock that produced it.

Cross-clock trace collisions in the same regular torus satisfy the exact identity

`V_e-V_f=lambda^-e (lambda^(e-f)-1)(lambda^(e+f)-1)`.      (4)

This follows by expanding the product. It can be interpreted in the finite etale quadratic algebra without supplying lambda to an implementation. Such a collision mixes the two old return clocks `e-f` and `e+f`; valuations may add or saturate. It can still reveal a proper factor, but no new local group size is created. An adjacent primitive mark is needed if exact return depth rather than a product collision is the requested output.

These statements do not exclude arbitrary nonlinear native tools, noncommuting dynamics, or alternative observers. They exclude only the claimed escape obtained by an invertible recoding of this same full return event.

## 4. Isogeny sandwich: useful refinement without new cardinality

Let C and C' be smooth elliptic curves over R. Assume an actual R-defined isogeny `phi:C->C'` and a certified dual `psi` satisfy `psi phi=[d]`, with all required charts defined. The dual identity is part of the certificate; a finite-field existence theorem alone is not an implementation over a composite ring. Then

`g_O([E]P) | g_O(phi([E]P)) | g_O([dE]P)`.               (5)

Proof: for every divisor c of N, a return of `[E]P` over `Z/cZ` implies a return of its image; a return of the image implies a return after applying psi. Apply this at each prime power. The argument is about the complete identity ideal, so it is not invalidated by squared x-coordinate vanishing.

Over a residue field, if P has order r, the image has order `r/|<P> intersect ker(phi)|`. The removed factor divides the separable kernel size and hence d. A chain of known degrees can only remove factors of their product from the transported point order. When d is a unit in R, an isogeny and its dual are etale; near a kernel section their local identity parameter has a unit linear term. They preserve the primitive depth at that section. Nonunit degree or an undefined chart needs its own retained factor/degeneracy branch.

The two outer divisors in (5) may be 1 and N while the middle is proper. Therefore an isogeny refinement is not dismissed as useless. It selects which kernel point was reached and can recover information obscured by a saturated multiplied clock. It does not, however, change the finite-field cardinality: curves isogenous over a finite field have the same Frobenius polynomial and the same number of rational points. This precise distinction is the reason to examine a cheap rational kernel label before constructing an entire second curve.

## 5. Positive tool: obtain a two-torsion mark from the same adjacent pairs

Return now to the frozen Montgomery convention

`C: B y^2=x^3+A x^2+x`, `gcd(B(A^2-4),N)=1`, N odd.

Let `T=(0,0)`, the known nonzero rational two-torsion section. Fix the existing admitted affine mark `P=(a,b)` with b a unit and a paid curve-equation certificate. The equation implies that a is a unit too. Suppose the actual ladder has supplied **primitive, genuinely adjacent** Kummer pairs

`(X0:Z0)=x(Q)`, `(X1:Z1)=x(Q+P)`.                        (6)

Define two divisors

`g_O = gcd(N, Z0, X1-a Z1)`,

`g_T = gcd(N, X0, a X1-Z1)`.                             (7)

Then g_O is the exact primitive depth of Q at O, and g_T is the exact primitive depth of Q at T, at every prime power. In particular

`g_T = g_O(Q+T)`, `gcd(g_O,g_T)=1`.                       (8)

### Proof with no nonunit inversion

For a generic affine point R, the chord through R and T gives

`x(R+T)=1/x(R)`, `y(R+T)=-y(R)/x(R)^2`.

The induced map on the full Kummer line is the regular projective involution

`(X:Z) -> (Z:X)`.                                       (9)

This interchanges O and T and needs no inversion of an actual residue x. Its matrix has unit determinant -1. Equality of the rational maps extends globally; alternatively it is the x-coordinate map of the everywhere-defined translation by T on the smooth curve.

Apply the already proved translated-Kummer theorem to `Q'=Q+T` and the same fixed P. Commutativity gives `Q'+P=(Q+P)+T`. The supplied adjacent pairs for these points are therefore exactly `(Z0:X0)` and `(Z1:X1)`. That theorem returns

`g_O(Q')=gcd(N,X0,Z1-a X1)=g_T`.

Signs do not change an ideal. Formula (7) for g_O is the original theorem. Since `(X0,Z0)` is primitive, no residue prime divides both g_O and g_T. This proves (8), including nilpotent neighborhoods and exact O/T endpoints. Arbitrary or commonly nonunit-scaled pairs do not satisfy the premise.

For interpretation, the quotient isogeny by `{O,T}` is etale of degree 2. Its zero fiber is the disjoint union of these two sections. Near either section its local parameter is a unit multiple of the section parameter. Thus its primitive return divisor is

`g_phi(Q)=g_O g_T`, and `g_O | g_phi(Q) | g_O([2]Q)`.      (10)

The product is an ordinary integer divisor of N because the two factors in (8) are coprime. Neither an actual quotient curve nor the product is required merely to emit a proper g_T. Computing either as validation is additional paid work.

## 6. Strict extra success when the doubled return saturates

There is a particularly transparent special case. For this paragraph allow the more general model

`y^2=x(x^2+alpha x+beta)`,

with `2 beta (alpha^2-4 beta)` a unit. Here beta is the constant quadratic coefficient; it is **not** B from the preceding Montgomery convention. Let Q be a certified point, affine in every component, and suppose the complete group certificate proves `[2]Q=O` over R.

Because Q=-Q and 2 is a unit, its affine y-coordinate is exactly zero. Put x=x(Q), `F=x^2+alpha x+beta`. The curve equation gives `x F=0` in R. At any residue prime, x and F cannot both vanish since beta is a unit. Consequently at each prime-power component one of x,F is a unit and the other is zero in that whole component. Thus

`d=gcd(N,x)`, `e=gcd(N,F)`, `gcd(d,e)=1`, `d e=N`.        (11)

For a primitive ordinary projective tuple with unit Z, use `X` and `X^2+alpha XZ+beta Z^2`; multiplication by powers of Z changes neither divisor. The second polynomial is optional validation, not necessary to obtain d.

Under this exact two-torsion premise, x does not suffer partial-depth ambiguity: its local valuations are either zero or capped at the full modulus exponent. Without the premise, x has a double zero at T and is not the general primitive observer; (7) is the safer general theorem. A trace, a lone Kummer denominator, or a malformed all-zero pair cannot replace the complete `[2]Q=O` certificate.

### CRT witness of strict extra success

Suppose `N=p^a q^b` and the admitted curve has an additional rational two-torsion point T' different from O,T in the q component. Smoothness makes the torsion sections distinct, and etaleness lifts T' uniquely from q to `q^b`. Let the p component of Q be T and the q component be T'. CRT supplies a genuine global point Q. It is nowhere O, while its double is O everywhere. Then

`g_O(Q)=1`, `g_O([2]Q)=N`, `g_T(Q)=p^a`.                 (12)

This is a strict containment of success sets for observers on certified points. The same conclusion holds for an actual `Q=[E]P` whenever its local endpoints have these labels. **The CRT construction is a proof of existence, not an algorithm for choosing P,E without factors.** It does not promise that a factor-blind ladder reaches these points; it is not fed back into the blind experiment. If the only local rational two-torsion is `{O,T}`, a nowhere-O two-torsion endpoint has label T everywhere and this particular selector saturates. If Q is not two-torsion, (7) remains valid but (11) does not apply.

For a fixed cyclic source point, reaching any nonzero two-torsion point requires its local order to divide 2E but not E. Thus this refinement cannot on its own remove an unknown odd part. It can distinguish labels after that prerequisite happens, including cases where the old doubled identity readout lost the distinction.

## 7. What would genuinely change the available local group sizes?

On a residue field the regular companion's group is a split or nonsplit one-dimensional torus. Changing its trace parameter can change element order and split type; its ambient possibilities remain `p-1` and `p+1`. A new successful torus parameter need not be an escape from this ambient restriction.

For an elliptic curve C over `F_p`, write `#C(F_p)=p+1-t_p`. An `F_p`-isomorphism leaves the point group unchanged; an `F_p`-isogeny leaves the cardinality unchanged although it can change point order by the kernel mechanism above. To change this cardinality one must leave that local isogeny class. Different j-invariants are not sufficient: nonisomorphic curves can be isogenous. The statement is local; an algebraic change of coefficients over `Z/NZ` does not certify that the unknown local traces actually changed.

A quadratic twist gives a limited genuine alternative. For `y^2=f(x)` and a local nonsquare d, the twist `d y^2=f(x)` has

`#C(F_p)+#C^(d)(F_p)=2(p+1)`.

Proof: for every x the two affine counts are `1+chi(f(x))` and `1-chi(f(x))`, with the convention chi(0)=0; each curve contributes one point at infinity. Thus the trace changes sign. If t_p=0 even this changes no cardinality. If the public d is locally square it gives an isomorphic curve instead; a single global Jacobi label need not reveal which local case occurred. A rational point on the original curve also does not freely become a rational point on its nonsquare twist. Point construction, admission and any nonunit denominator event must be charged.

Randomizing admitted curves/points and using an exponent assembled from small prime powers is precisely the ECM mechanism, not a new native theorem. The unresolved requirement is a factor-blind policy that produces useful asymmetric local annihilation with an honestly costed probability bound. Nothing here proves such a policy, a uniform positive rate, or polynomial-time Shor closure.
