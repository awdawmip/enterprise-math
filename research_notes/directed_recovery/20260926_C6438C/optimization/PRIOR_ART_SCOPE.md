# External primary-source context for the optimization pass

Scope: metadata and abstracts inspected on 2026-09-26, not full-paper
replication or an exhaustive novelty review. External quantum models remain
external models; these citations do not modify P000 or replace actual BRC
execution with ideal numerical phases.

- Nadav Yoran and Anthony J. Short, *Classical simulability and the
  significance of modular exponentiation in Shor's algorithm* (2007),
  [arXiv:0706.0872v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.0872v1), published in Physical
  Review A 76, 060302(R). The abstract identifies efficient simulation of
  modular exponentiation with specified product inputs and general product
  output measurements as sufficient for efficient Shor simulation. This is
  relevant to our distinction between cheap evaluation of individual modular
  columns and the harder coherent conditional-probability calculation.
- Aidan Dang, Charles D. Hill and Lloyd C. L. Hollenberg, *Optimising Matrix
  Product State Simulations of Shor's Algorithm*,
  [arXiv:1712.07311v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.07311v4), Quantum 3, 116
  (2019). The abstract reports improved entanglement mapping, resource
  dependence on factors of the order, and a 60-qubit simulation instance.
  It motivates representation-sensitive cost accounting; neither that
  reported instance nor our small fixtures imply uniform polynomial cost.

The collision formula, lazy integer compiler, and exact six-coordinate codec
are established here against this project's existing native sources. No
claim is made that their general mathematical ideas are new to the literature.
The implicit Gram prototype is evaluated as a concrete computational route,
including its query-closure cost, rather than as a novelty or efficiency claim.
