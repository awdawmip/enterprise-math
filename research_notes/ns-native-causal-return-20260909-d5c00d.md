## 5. Why eight packets cannot realize the stated return (all times)

This is an ordinary discrete proof, not extrapolation from a finite search.

A color with at most four particles can have at most one collision, by the distinct-line argument of Section3. A first alternating return therefore needs at least five particles in its first color and at least three in the other. N<=7 is excluded. At N=8 these numbers must be exactly5 and3.

    Lw-(L-k)a,  Lw-(L-k)b.                                (5.2)

These two particles meet only at tick L. In the first-event-veto control there can be no earlier new first-color triple: two original first-core trajectories never meet, and the two extras do not meet before L. Thus their ballistic positions really are valid in the relevant counterfactual as well.

The first event changes only its old +/-e2 trajectories to +/-e3. For it to change the middle decision, at least one such difference must enter an ELIGIBLE neighbor score, not a score along the middle core's own incoming axes.

**New pair3 cannot change an eligible middle score.** Combine (5.1) with adjacency to k*(+/-e3).
