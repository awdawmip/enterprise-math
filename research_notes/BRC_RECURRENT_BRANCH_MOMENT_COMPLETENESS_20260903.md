## 1. Question

The branch-moment lift defines an infinite family

\[
P_m(Q)=\sum_{q\in Q}q^m,
\qquad m=0,1,2,\ldots
\]

for every finite explicit positive branch multiset `Q`. Is the whole family genuinely infinite information?

No. Once branch count is known, a finite prefix is complete up to permutation of parallel branches.

Newton identities and power-sum reconstruction are classical algebra. No generic novelty claim is made. The BRC contribution proposed here is the finite exact matrix-level compression of the entire branch-moment tower.

## 2. Local branch-weight completeness

Let

\[
Q=\{q_1,\ldots,q_r\}
\]

be a multiset of positive rationals. Define

\[
p_k=\sum_{i=1}^rq_i^k.
\]

Then `p_0=r`.

Let `e_k` be the elementary symmetric functions in `q_1,...,q_r`, with `e_0=1`. Newton identities give, for `1<=k<=r`,

\[
\boxed{
ke_k
=
\sum_{i=1}^k(-1)^{i-1}e_{k-i}p_i.
}
\]

Therefore `p_1,...,p_r` recursively determine `e_1,...,e_r` exactly in `Q`.

The monic polynomial

\[
\boxed{
f_Q(t)
=t^r-e_1t^{r-1}+e_2t^{r-2}-\cdots+(-1)^re_r
}
\]

has precisely the branch weights as its roots with multiplicity.

### Candidate BRC-MC1

\[
\boxed{
(p_0,p_1,\ldots,p_r)
\text{ uniquely determines the positive-rational branch-weight multiset }Q.
}
\]

No root approximation is required for the uniqueness theorem; the exact root polynomial itself is a complete certificate.

## 3. Higher moments are generated by recurrence

Every root `q_i` satisfies its characteristic polynomial. Summing the resulting identities over all roots gives, for every `m>r`,

\[
\boxed{
p_m
-e_1p_{m-1}
+e_2p_{m-2}
-\cdots
+(-1)^re_rp_{m-r}
=0.
}
\]

Hence the finite prefix `(p_0,...,p_r)` generates the entire infinite power-sum sequence exactly.

Thus the moment tower is **finitely generated per source-target branch cell**.

## 4. Matrix-level finite completeness

For explicit positive-rational multigraph branches define

\[
W^{(m)}_{uv}=\sum_{e:u\to v}q_e^m.
\]

Let

\[
R=\max_{u,v}\#\{e:u\to v\}
\]

be maximum parallel multiplicity.

Then `W^(0)` gives every local branch count `r_uv`, and the entries

\[
W^{(1)}_{uv},\ldots,W^{(r_{uv})}_{uv}
\]

recover the local root polynomial by Newton identities.

### Candidate BRC-MC2

\[
\boxed{
W^{(0)},W^{(1)},\ldots,W^{(R)}
}
\]

uniquely determine the explicit weighted directed multigraph **up to permutation of parallel branches sharing the same ordered endpoints**, provided branch identity contains no further metadata beyond endpoint and weight.

They also determine every higher moment matrix `W^(m)` by the local recurrences above.

This is stronger than retaining only total mass `W^(1)` and strictly weaker than retaining full labeled provenance.

## 5. Relation to CWM

For one source-target cell, the finite signature begins with

\[
p_0=C_{\rm one-step},
\qquad
p_1=W_{\rm one-step}.
\]

Higher moments restore the branch-weight shape erased by `(C,W)` alone. Dominant one-step weight is then exactly the largest positive rational root of the recovered root polynomial.

Thus the moment-complete signature refines the CWM local data:

```text
(C,W,M)
<- coarse readout of ->
finite exact moment/root-polynomial signature.
```

It still does not recover labels, timestamps, semantic provenance or other metadata attached to equal endpoint/weight branches.

## 6. Why lower prefixes can fail

### Two branches

The families

\[
\{1/3,2/3\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\{1/4,3/4\}
\]

both have

\[
p_0=2,
\qquad
p_1=1,
\]

but different `p_2`. Thus `(p_0,p_1)` is not complete for two branches.

### Three branches

The integer triples

\[
\{1,5,6\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\{2,3,7\}
\]

satisfy

\[
p_0=3,
\qquad
p_1=12,
\qquad
p_2=62,
\]

but have different products and hence different `p_3`. Thus even `(p_0,p_1,p_2)` can fail for three branches.

The theorem claims sufficiency of the full prefix through order `r`; it does not claim every family requires all `r` nonconstant moments.

## 8. Boundary with Schur/port collapse

Total-mass recurrent Schur collapse retains only the `m=1` projection. It is therefore not moment-complete in general.

To make a recurrent port collapse safe for the entire branch-moment language, one would need a richer interface that tracks enough moment-lifted port matrices — or equivalently another exact representation of the explicit branch-weight multiset behavior.

This is a new future research target; no moment-safe recurrent Schur quotient is asserted here.
