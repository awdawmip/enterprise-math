## 4. Two-prime support-splitting theorem

Let `p != r` be distinct primes and define the torsion-blind scalar

`q_{p,r}(n,a) = 1/2 * (1_{p does not divide n} + 1_{r does not divide n})`.

It obeys M1 and M2:

- `q(0)=0`,
- `q(e)=1`,
- it ignores torsion,
- it is even in the free coordinate,
- `R` and `S` do not change its value.

For a prime `l`, let

`h_l(n)=1_{l does not divide n}`

and

`H_l(x,y)=h_l(x)+h_l(y)`.

### Lemma 4.1 — support-count isometries

For `G in GL_2(F_l)`,

`H_l(Gv)=H_l(v)` for every `v`

iff `G` is monomial.

Proof: `H_l(e1)=H_l(e2)=1`. Hence each column of an isometry has exactly one nonzero coordinate. Invertibility forces the two nonzero positions to be different. The converse is immediate.

### Theorem 4.2 — exact fixed-scalar free-block classification

For `A in GL_2(Z)`, exact marked conservation of `q_{p,r}` together with balanced elementary splitting holds iff:

1. `A mod p` is monomial;
2. `A mod r` is monomial;
3. the two monomial reductions have opposite permutation type.

Necessity of separate `p`- and `r`-support conservation follows by testing inputs divisible by the other prime: inputs `r v` isolate the `p` support term, while inputs `p v` isolate the `r` support term.

Opposite permutation type is exactly what makes both entries of the first column carry one, and only one, of the two support contributions, so both have scalar `1/2`.

Thus, for fixed `(p,r)`, the entire free-block survivor stratum is

`S_{p,r} = { A in GL_2(Z) : A mod p and A mod r are monomial of opposite permutation type }`.

This is an exact infinite congruence classification, not a finite-box inference.
