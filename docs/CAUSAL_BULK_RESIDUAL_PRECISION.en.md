## 1. Residual is a fiber, not an inverse

Let the actual bulk composition law be

\[
\boxed{b\odot r=t.}
\]

For current bulk `b`, define left translation

\[
L_b(r)=b\odot r
\]

and residual fiber

\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_b(t)=\{r:b\odot r=t\}.}
\]

A singleton fiber gives a uniquely recoverable residual. A non-singleton fiber is not automatically a defect.

If future evolution continues by the same associative law on the right and

\[
b\odot r=b\odot r',
\]

then for every later increment `u`,

\[
(b\odot r)\odot u=(b\odot r')\odot u.
\]

The distinction has already become causally irrelevant. Traditional left cancellation is merely the special regime in which all reachable residual fibers are singletons.

## 4. BR-03 — Commutative accumulation coarsens precision

Assume `odot` is associative and commutative and new bulk is causally accumulated as

\[
b'=b\odot u.
\]

If `b odot r=b odot r'`, then commutativity lets the additional bulk move outside the old collision:

\[
(b\odot u)\odot r=u\odot(b\odot r)=u\odot(b\odot r')=(b\odot u)\odot r'.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{P_b\preceq P_{b'}.}
\]

Every collision coordinate is nondecreasing:

\[
\boxed{J_k(L_{b'})\ge J_k(L_b).}
\]

Resolution becomes coarser because the operation itself creates larger fibers.

## 5. Residual-resolution family

Whenever `P_b` refines `P_b'`, there is a canonical quotient map

\[
\boxed{\Phi_{b\to b'}:[r]_b\mapsto[r]_{b'}.}

## 6. Noncommutative negative boundary

Associativity alone guarantees that an **already realized** collision remains collapsed under later right extension. It does not imply that residual partitions for two different left contexts are ordered.

A finite transformation-semigroup counterexample in the executable tests has `b odot r=b odot r'` but `(b odot u) odot r != (b odot u) odot r'`. Thus left/context precision can change non-monotonically in noncommutative worlds. Commutativity, or another proved kernel-stability condition, is genuinely needed for the monotone bulk-precision theorem.

## 7. Minimal examples

### Addition

`b odot r=b+r`: all residual fibers are singletons, so higher collision coordinates vanish and accumulating bulk does not reduce residual precision.

### max

### Boolean OR

Once bulk is True, both future residuals False and True produce the same current state; if future continues only by OR they need not be retained separately.
