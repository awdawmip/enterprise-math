# Open Problems

These problems are deliberately small enough to attack independently. A contributor does not need to accept the physical hypotheses of Enterprise Math.

## P001 — Characterize multiplicativity of integer roots

Difficulty: medium  
Good first research problem: yes

Find necessary and sufficient conditions for

\[
R_p(ab)=R_p(a)R_p(b).
\]

Start with \(p=2\).

## P002 — Sharp collapse-gap bounds

Difficulty: easy to medium  
Good first research problem: yes

Define

\[
G_p(n)=n-C_p(n).
\]

Derive the sharp upper bound for \(G_p(n)\) inside a basin and express it using only integers.

## P003 — Commutation of collapse operators

Difficulty: medium

For which \(p,q\) and \(n\) does

\[
C_p(C_q(n))=C_q(C_p(n))
\]

hold? Determine whether any nontrivial global commutation law exists.

## P004 — Fixed points of collapse compositions

Difficulty: medium

Classify the fixed points of

\[
C_{p_m}\circ\cdots\circ C_{p_1}.
\]

## P005 — Scale algebra beyond one base

Difficulty: medium

Define compatible refinement and projection when two integer scale bases \(a\) and \(b\) coexist. Determine when the order of scale conversion matters.

## P006 — Signed-state extension

Difficulty: medium

Construct a signed extension that preserves the integer-only rule and makes clear which root operations are total, partial, or sign-sensitive.

## P007 — General division without hidden fractions

Difficulty: hard

Design the smallest useful division structure that remains closed in explicit discrete states without treating an unrepresented rational value as the hidden true answer.

Compare quotient-only, quotient-with-state-transition, and residuated approaches.

## P008 — Minimal algebraic structure

Difficulty: hard

Identify the weakest established algebraic or order-theoretic structure that contains the v0.1 root and collapse laws without adding unwanted continuum assumptions.

## P009 — Collapse semigroup classification

Difficulty: hard

Study the semigroup generated by a finite family of collapse operators and scale maps on a finite state domain. Classify attractors, fixed points, and possible cycles.

## P010 — Strict growth of merged-history multiplicity

Difficulty: medium

T012 gives

\[
M_{t+1}(x)\ge M_t(x).
\]

Find necessary and sufficient conditions for strict inequality.

## P011 — Alternative integer irreversibility observables

Difficulty: medium  
Good first research problem: yes

Construct integer-valued observables other than raw preimage cardinality that are monotone under broad classes of many-to-one forward maps.

Do not assume logarithms.

## P012 — Discrete geometry without hidden Euclidean distance

Difficulty: hard

Propose an integer-valued distance or relation compatible with the finite-resolution philosophy, then prove which metric axioms survive.

## P013 — Lean formalization of T001 and T005

Difficulty: easy to medium  
Good first formalization problem: yes

Formalize integer-root characterization and collapse idempotence in Lean, preferably reusing established natural-number order lemmas without importing a real-number square root.

## P014 — Lean formalization of scale compatibility

Difficulty: medium

Formalize T010 for general \(p\), integer base \(b\ge2\), and scale level \(s\).

## P015 — Prior-art map

Difficulty: accessible  
Good first research problem: yes

Build a precise comparison table covering integer roots, Euclidean division, interior operators, Galois connections, residuated structures, irreversible semigroups, preimage entropy, and discrete geometry.

For every connection, state both the similarity and the exact point where Enterprise Math currently makes a stronger or different interpretation.

## P016 — Physical falsification criteria

Difficulty: hard

List observations that would contradict the stronger hypothesis that finite-resolution many-to-one state collapse is fundamental rather than merely an effective coarse-grained description.

This problem must separate mathematical inconsistency from physical falsification.

## How to take a problem

Open a GitHub issue with the problem ID in the title, or comment on an existing issue for that ID.

A valid contribution can be a proof, disproof, smaller counterexample, computation, prior-art identification, formalization, or sharper problem statement.
