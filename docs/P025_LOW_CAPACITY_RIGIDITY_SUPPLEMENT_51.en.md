## 1. Why the unit slice is now a capacity problem

For

\[
1+b=c,
\]

Supplement 49 gives the exact projective resource

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{\rm proj}
=
\max\left\{
\frac{m(c)}{C(b)},
\frac{m(b)}{C(c)}
\right\},
}
\]

where

\[
C(n)
=
\sum_{p\mid n}
 v_p(n)\frac{\operatorname{rad}(n)}p.
\]

Thus large unit-slice projective pressure requires a large multiplicity residual on one consecutive integer together with small derivative capacity on the other.

The present supplement shows that bounded derivative capacity is arithmetically rigid.

## 2. P025-T118 — exponents are bounded by capacity

Write

\[
n=\prod_{i=1}^k p_i^{e_i},
\qquad
R=\prod_{i=1}^k p_i.
\]

Then

\[
C(n)
=
\sum_i e_i\frac{R}{p_i}.
\]

Every factor `R/p_i` is a positive integer, so every summand is at least `e_i`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
e_i\le C(n)
\quad\text{for every support prime }p_i.}
\]

Hence a fixed capacity horizon bounds every prime-power exponent.

## 3. P025-T119 — if at least two primes occur, every support prime is bounded by capacity

Assume

\[
k=\omega(n)\ge2.
\]

Fix any support prime `p_i`. Choose another support prime `p_j`. The `j`th summand in `C(n)` is

\[
e_j\frac{R}{p_j}.
\]

Since `e_j>=1` and `R/p_j` contains `p_i` as a factor,

\[
e_j\frac{R}{p_j}\ge p_i.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
p_i\le C(n)
\quad\text{for every }p_i\mid n
\quad\text{when }\omega(n)\ge2.}
\]

This is the decisive rigidity absent from one-prime blocks.

## 4. P025-T120 — bounded-capacity non-prime-powers form a finite universe

Fix an integer

\[
H\ge1.
\]

Suppose

\[
C(n)\le H
\]

and `n` is not a prime power. Then `omega(n)>=2`, so by P025-T118–T119:

\[
p\le H
\quad\text{for all }p\mid n,
\]

and

\[
v_p(n)\le H.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
 n
\mid
Q_H
:=
\prod_{\substack{p\le H\\p\text{ prime}}}
p^H.
}
\]

The set of such `n` is finite and effectively enumerable from `H` alone.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
C(n)\le H
\Longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
 n=p^e,\ 1\le e\le H, &\text{prime-power branch};\\
 n\mid Q_H, &\text{finite non-prime-power branch}.
\end{cases}}
\]

The only infinite bounded-capacity families are prime powers with bounded exponent.

## 5. P025-C07 — low capacity below five is exactly prime-power behavior

If `omega(n)>=2`, the two smallest possible distinct support primes are `2,3`, and

\[
C(n)
\ge
\frac{6}{2}+rac{6}{3}
=5.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
C(n)<5
\Longrightarrow
n=p^e.
}

For a prime power

\[
n=p^e,
\]

one has

\[
C(n)=e.
\]

Hence the first capacity shells are completely classified:

- `C=1`: prime;
- `C=2`: prime square;
- `C=3`: prime cube;
- `C=4`: prime fourth power.

This explains why the hard unit examples naturally expose prime-power neighbors.

## 7. Consequence for infinite hard families

Fix `H`. Any infinite sequence of unit relations

\[
1+b_j=c_j
\]

with

\[
\min\{C(b_j),C(c_j)\}\le H
\]

must, after discarding finitely many terms coming from the divisors of `Q_H`, contain a prime-power low-capacity side

\[
\boxed{p^e,
\qquad
1\le e\le H.}
\]

Thus persistent low-capacity unit pressure is forced toward bounded-exponent prime-power Diophantine families.

This does not solve those families. It identifies their exact arithmetic type.

## 8. Connection to the classical consecutive-perfect-power theorem

If **both** sides of a sufficiently large unit relation have bounded capacity, the finite-core / prime-power dichotomy forces both sides, outside a finite set, toward prime powers of bounded exponent.

The classification of consecutive perfect powers is classical deep prior art: Mihăilescu proved Catalan's conjecture, whose unique positive solution with both exponents greater than one is

\[
3^2-2^3=1.
\]

P025 does not re-prove or claim this theorem. Its possible role here is only as an external boundary result after the project-specific capacity reduction.
