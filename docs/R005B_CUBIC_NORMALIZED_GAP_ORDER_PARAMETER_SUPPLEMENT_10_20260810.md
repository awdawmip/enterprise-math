## 1. Result

The asymptotic cubic full-forcing frontier can be organized by one classical
prime-gap order parameter

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L
=
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{p_{n+1}-p_n}{p_n^{1/3}}.
}
\]

The two R005-B critical constants are

\[
\boxed{\frac32\quad\text{and}\quad3.}
\]

They separate three asymptotic regimes:

### Regime I — sub-opening

If

\[
\boxed{\mathcal L<\frac32,}
\]

then every sufficiently large cubic candidate prime witness is forced.  In
other words,

\[
\boxed{\text{cubic full forcing is eventually true}.}
\]

### Regime II — upper-only transition

If

\[
\boxed{\frac32\le\mathcal L<3,}
\]

then the lower PRE_HORIZON cofactor-gap mechanism eventually disappears.
Any remaining full-forcing failure must lie in the upper horizon cap.
Supplement 07 proves that this upper-cap failure set has natural density zero.
Hence

\[
\boxed{
\#\{k\in[K,2K]:\text{cubic full forcing fails}\}=o(K).
}
\]

This does not imply finite failure.

### Regime III — supercritical lower amplification

If

\[
\boxed{\mathcal L>3,}
\]

then there is some fixed `eta>0` and infinitely many consecutive prime gaps

\[
g\ge(3+\eta)a^{1/3}.
\]

Supplement 09 amplifies each sufficiently large such gap into many fully
non-forced lower-band cubic witnesses.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{\text{cubic full forcing fails infinitely often}.}
\]

The exact boundary cases

\[
\mathcal L=\frac32,
\qquad
\mathcal L=3
\]

remain unresolved by this classification.

## 2. Why one order parameter is enough

Every candidate divisor q belongs to exactly one of the two e=1 regimes from
Supplement 02:

\[
qF\le A
\quad\text{(cofactor-gap / PRE side)},
\]

or

\[
qF>A
\quad\text{(horizon-gap / upper side)}.
\]

For cubic basins,

\[
A=k^3,
\qquad
F\asymp k^{3/2}.
\]

Thus a consecutive cofactor prime gap at scale `a` enters the cubic system
when

\[
a\asymp k^{3/2},
\qquad
k\asymp a^{2/3}.
\]

The lower activation threshold from Supplement 08 is

\[
g_{\rm crit}(a)=3a^{1/3}+O(1).
\]

The upper opening threshold from Supplement 05 is

\[
g_0(k)=\frac32\sqrt{k}+o(\sqrt{k}),
\]

which at the same scale is

\[
g_0=\frac32a^{1/3}+o(a^{1/3}).
\]

So both bands are controlled by the same normalized consecutive-prime gap

\[
\frac{g}{a^{1/3}},
\]

but at two different constants.

## 3. B45 — `L<3` kills the lower cofactor band eventually

Assume

\[
\mathcal L<3.
\]

Choose a fixed `delta>0` such that eventually every consecutive prime gap
satisfies

\[
g\le(3-\delta)a^{1/3}.
\]

Supplement 08 gives

\[
g_{\rm crit}(a)=3a^{1/3}+O(1).
\]

Therefore, for every sufficiently large prime left endpoint a,

\[
g<g_{\rm crit}(a).
\]

Hence no large consecutive prime gap can open even a **real** PRE_HORIZON
reciprocal interval

\[
\left(\frac Ub,\frac Aa\right].
\]

Every lower-band e=1 failure would have to be generated by such a consecutive
cofactor gap through R005-A T-A16.  Thus every sufficiently large lower-band
candidate has an e=1 exclusive cofactor certificate and is forced.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L<3
\Longrightarrow
\text{eventual lower-band full forcing}.
}
\]

This implication is internal once T-A16 and Supplement 08 are accepted; it
uses no almost-all prime theorem.

## 4. B46 — `L<3/2` also kills the upper horizon cap

\[
\mathcal L<\frac32.

Consider the consecutive primes

\[

\qquad

\[

\[

\[

\le

Every cubic candidate above

## 7. Hardness corollary

Taking the contrapositive of B48 gives

\[
\boxed{
\text{eventual cubic full forcing}
\Longrightarrow
\mathcal L\le3.
}
\]

Thus any proof that every sufficiently large cubic basin has all divisor
candidates forced would simultaneously establish the classical prime-gap bound

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{p_{n+1}-p_n}{p_n^{1/3}}\le3.
}
\]

This is not merely a statement that R005-B currently lacks enough computation.
It is a reduction from eventual cubic full forcing to an all-gaps theorem at
the cube-root scale.

The established Baker–Harman–Pintz all-interval exponent `0.525` is far above
`1/3`; it therefore does not settle this implication.

## 8. One-gap excursion picture

For a fixed gap write

\[
c=\frac{b-a}{a^{1/3}},
\qquad
s=\frac{F_3(k)-a}{a^{1/3}}
\]

while the factor horizon lies inside the gap.

The remaining post-horizon gap is

\[
(b-F_3(k))
=(c-s)a^{1/3}.
\]

At leading order the upper state-machine boundaries are therefore

\[
\boxed{c-s=3}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\boxed{c-s=\frac32}.
\]

As the horizon moves right, `s` increases.  Hence one fixed gap follows the
scaled excursion

\[
\text{SATURATED if }c-s>3,
\]

\[
\text{EXPANDING if }\frac32<c-s\le3,
\]

\[
\text{CLOSED if }c-s\le\frac32,
\]

before retirement at `s>=c`.

Its PRE_HORIZON precursor exists asymptotically only if

\[
c>3.
\]

Thus `3` is the bifurcation at which a short upper pulse acquires a long lower
precursor, while `3/2` is the opening/closing threshold for the post-horizon
pulse itself.

This scaled picture is a limit description; exact finite boundaries remain
those in Supplements 05 and 08.
