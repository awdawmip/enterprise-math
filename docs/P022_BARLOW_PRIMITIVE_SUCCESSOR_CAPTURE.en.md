# P022 — Primitive Franel Events Are Captured Within One Defect Step

Status: `PROVED WIP / EXACT TRIANGULAR CAPTURE THEOREM`  
Owner: `program/p022-geometry-v2`  
Depends on: primitive Franel defect criterion; canonical central-binomial elimination  
Prior-art boundary: primitive divisors, twin primes, and elementary factor bounds are classical; P022 owns the exact defect-capture statement.

## 1. Setup

Let `p` be a primitive prime divisor of the Franel sequence at rank `r>=2`:

\[
p\mid F_r,
\qquad
p\nmid F_j\quad(1\le j<r).
\]

Write

\[
z=v_p(F_r)>0.
\]

The existing primitive-defect theorem handles the case in which `r` itself is a composite odd-boundary index. The missing case is when

\[
2r-1
\]

is prime, so `D_r` does not exist.

## 2. P022-LI45 — immediate capture at a composite current boundary

If

\[
2r-1\text{ is composite},
\]

then the pure Franel defect `D_r` exists. Primitive divisibility gives

\[
\boxed{v_p(D_r)=z.}
\]

Every earlier defect uses only Franel indices `<r`, so the same valuation row is zero on all earlier defect columns.

This is the existing primitive-defect pivot, retained here as the first half of the dichotomy.

## 3. P022-LI46 — negative successor capture

Suppose instead

\[
2r-1\text{ is prime}
\]

but

\[
2r+1\text{ is composite}.
\]

Then `D_(r+1)` exists.

The canonical relation for

\[
A_{r+1}=\binom{2r+2}{r+1}
\]

has the form

\[
A_{r+1}
=
A_r\prod_{j<r}A_j^{\beta_j}.
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{\alpha_r=1.}
\]

Why can no other part of the canonical elimination create another `A_r` term?

- the expansion of the integer `r+1` uses central-binomial indices at most roughly half of its odd prime factors, hence strictly `<r` for `r>=3`;
- because `2r+1` is odd composite, its largest prime factor is at most `(2r+1)/3`, so every central-binomial index generated by its prime-halving expansion is also strictly `<r`.

Thus `A_r` occurs exactly once and every other Franel factor in the denominator has index `<r`.

Primitive divisibility makes every such earlier factor a p-unit. Also `F_(r+1)` is a p-unit: in the legal range a Franel zero cannot persist to an adjacent term.

Therefore the only p-adic contribution is the denominator factor `F_r`, and

\[
\boxed{v_p(D_{r+1})=-z.}
\]

Again this row is zero on every earlier defect column, because `p` is primitive at rank `r` and `D_r` does not exist.

## 4. P022-LI47 — one-step capture / twin-prime deferral dichotomy

Combining LI45 and LI46:

\[
\boxed{
\text{a primitive Franel event at rank }r
\text{ is captured at }D_r\text{ or }D_{r+1}
}
\]

unless both neighboring odd numbers are prime:

\[
\boxed{2r-1\text{ and }2r+1\text{ are prime}.}
\]

That exceptional geometry is exactly a twin-prime pair centered at `2r`.

Thus the only obstruction to this **immediate** structural capture is a twin-prime center.

This is a deferral statement, not a permanent noncapture theorem. A primitive row may re-enter at a later composite defect.

## 5. The deferral is real, not merely a proof gap

Take

\[
r=6.
\]

Then

\[
2r-1=11,
\qquad
2r+1=13
\]

are twin primes. The Franel number `F_6` has primitive prime divisors `13` and `73`.

For either valuation row:

- there is no `D_6` or `D_7`;
- the next available composite defect `D_8` still has valuation zero.

So the immediate-capture failure is genuine arithmetic deferral, not only the absence of a theorem statement.

Both rows later re-enter at

\[
\boxed{v_{13}(D_{11})=v_{73}(D_{11})=1.}
\]

No universal delay length is claimed here.

## 6. Existing examples are reinterpreted

### `p=157`

The prime `157` is primitive at `r=16`.

\[
2r-1=31\text{ prime},
\qquad
2r+1=33\text{ composite}.
\]

Hence LI46 gives

\[
\boxed{v_{157}(D_{17})=-1.}
\]

This explains why the later exact cancellation

\[
v_{157}(D_{78})=0
\]

does **not** mean that `157` is useless to the global defect lattice. Its valuation row was already captured at `D_17`.

### `p=369581`

This prime is primitive at `r=8`, where `2r-1=15` is composite, so

\[
\boxed{v_p(D_8)=1.}
\]

At the much later forced midpoint `m=184790`, the same row has

\[
v_p(D_m)=-1.
\]

The sign reversal is therefore a later holonomy jump of an already useful valuation row, not a loss of the row.

### Positive- and negative-unit target examples

- `p=518220701` is primitive at `r=50`, and `D_50` captures it with valuation `+1`;
- the Pocklington-certified prime `8895267426781770496852703` is primitive at `r=49`, with `97=2r-1` prime and `99=2r+1` composite, so `D_50` captures it with valuation `-1`.

Thus even the enormous half-index analysis is not needed to establish that these prime rows already belong to the defect lattice.

## 7. Consequence for global low-order identifiability

The old sufficient strategy

> find a primitive Franel prime directly at every composite-boundary index `n`

is stronger than necessary.

A primitive event at the preceding rank `n-1` can supply the pivot for `D_n` whenever

\[
2n-3\text{ is prime}
\]

and `2n-1` is composite.

This produces a two-rank primitive coverage criterion, developed separately.
