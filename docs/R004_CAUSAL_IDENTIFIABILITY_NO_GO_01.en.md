## 1. Result and scope

For every fixed finite horizon, unrestricted hidden-state extension can compile either

- a finite action-indexed relation/support process, or
- a finite **total rational** action-indexed stochastic process,

into ex-ante deterministic counterfactual masters whose declared finite-horizon visible behavior is exactly the same.

For total rational kernels, one policy-independent rational measure over those deterministic masters reproduces both every literal action-word law and every visible-history law generated by a deterministic history-dependent intervention policy through the compiled horizon.

Therefore finite branching, finite rational randomness, multivalued support, and deterministic adaptive intervention syntax do **not by themselves** certify ontic online generation.

This is a finite operational identifiability no-go. It is not a hidden-variable ontology and does not adopt a physical axiom.

## 3. R004-CI-T01 — finite relation-support completion

Let `X` be a finite state set, `A` a finite declared action set, and `R_a subseteq X x X` a finite relation for each action.

A depth-`H` deterministic counterfactual master rooted at visible state `x` stores, for every declared action at every contingent node,

- explicit disabledness when that action has no successor, or
- one chosen successor together with a complete child master.

The master family is built recursively by taking the Cartesian product of all action-indexed branch choices.

For every literal action word `w` with `|w| <= H`,

`raw_support(x,w) = { target(m,w) : m is a compiled master and target(m,w) is defined }`.

The proof is induction on the word length. At the first action, compiled branch choices range over exactly the raw relation successors; applying the induction hypothesis to the suffix and taking the union reproduces ordinary relational composition. Disabled first actions yield the empty support on both sides.

Thus finite visible multivalued support can be represented by a family of ex-ante deterministic contingent masters without changing the raw A4 wordwise support language.

## 4. R004-CI-T02 — one policy-independent rational master measure

Now assume every action is a **total rational stochastic kernel** `K_a(y|x)` on a finite state set.

At each master node and independently for every counterfactual action `a`:

1. sample one successor `y` with exact weight `K_a(y|x)`;
2. sample a child master recursively from the distribution rooted at `y`;
3. take the product over all action-indexed branches.

This produces one rational probability measure `mu_(x,H)` on finite deterministic masters before the future action word or policy is known.

For every literal word `w` with `|w| <= H`, pushing `mu_(x,H)` through deterministic execution of `w` gives exactly the ordinary kernel law `delta_x K_w`.

Unused counterfactual branches marginalize to total mass one, while the selected branch has exactly the required kernel marginal. Induction on word length completes the proof.

## 5. R004-CI-T03 — deterministic adaptive policies use the same master measure

Let the next action be an arbitrary deterministic function of the visible history observed so far.

The same ex-ante measure `mu_(x,H)` from T02 reproduces the exact complete visible-history law generated by the original kernels under that policy through horizon `H`.

Conditioned on a visible history, the policy selects one action. That action's child branch was already sampled with the correct kernel conditional law for the current visible state; unused action branches remain marginalized out. Induction over history depth gives equality of the full history laws.

Therefore deterministic visible-history adaptivity does not, by itself, escape finite counterfactual pre-sampling.

## 6. R004-CI-C01 — closure implies non-identifiability

Let an admissible finite operational model class be closed under adjoining the corresponding hidden counterfactual master while preserving the declared visible projection.

Then, in the relation/support and total-rational-kernel classes above, every target finite-horizon behavior has an admissible ex-ante completion with the same declared finite-horizon operational behavior.

Hence no experiment expressible solely in that declared finite-horizon language can distinguish online generation from ex-ante completion.

The steward-verified logical direction is:

`closure under the relevant latent master extension -> non-identifiability`.

Consequently, **failure of that closure is necessary to escape this guaranteed no-go**.

Bare nonclosure is **not sufficient** for identifiability. A model class may forbid some master extensions while still admitting another presampled representative with the same declared operational behavior. Any stronger positive identifiability theorem requires an independently justified admissibility restriction that excludes all relevant observationally equivalent presampled completions, or an equivalent separating-observable theorem.

This supplement therefore does **not** call latent-extension nonclosure an iff-style identifiability criterion.

## 7. Candidate primitive verdicts

The verified no-go gives the following narrow verdicts.

- **Intervention-local response ownership:** insufficient alone if all local counterfactual response subtrees may coexist hidden ex ante.
- **State-extensional visible accessibility:** insufficient alone if hidden master coordinates may refine the retained visible state.
- **Bounded latent capacity:** potentially obstructive because the capacity bound itself forbids otherwise valid completions; it is an additional resource/ontic assumption, not a consequence of finite operational syntax.
- **Composition/factorization locality:** potentially obstructive only when the factorization or independence rule substantively forbids joint counterfactual masters. The restriction itself supplies the additional causal content.

Bell-locality plus measurement independence is a prior-art example of a substantive restriction, not a Foundation axiom adopted here.

No unique weakest physical axiom is selected by this no-go.
