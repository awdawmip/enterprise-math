## 1. A pointwise safe region exists before any exceptional-set counting

Consider a primitive non-unit triple

\[

## 2. P025-T137 — squarefree `c` forces the subunit basin

Assume `c` is squarefree. Then

\[
m(c)=1,
\]

so the c-oriented term is strictly below one because its denominator is a positive integer greater than one.

For the a-oriented term,

\[
\rho_a=\frac{m(a)}{K_{bc}}.
\]

Since `b>1`, its block capacity is positive, and the denominator contains the term

\[
R_c C(b)=cC(b)\ge c.
\]

But

\[
m(a)\le a<c.
\]

Therefore

\[
\rho_a<1.
\]

The b-oriented term is identical. Hence

\[
\boxed{
c\text{ squarefree}\Longrightarrow\sigma_{\rm proj}<1.}
\]

So every activated non-unit state necessarily has a nonsquarefree output component `c`.

## 3. P025-T138 — two squarefree input sides also force the subunit basin

Assume now that `a,b` are both squarefree.

Then

\[
m(a)=m(b)=1,
\]

so neither side-oriented term can reach one.

For the c-oriented term, the exact block capacity becomes

\[
K_{ab}
=
R_bC(a)+R_aC(b)
=
b\,a'+a\,b'
=(ab)',
\]

because `a,b` are squarefree and coprime.

For every squarefree integer `n>1`,

\[
\frac{n'}n
=
\sum_{p\mid n}\frac1p
\ge
\frac1n,
\]

so `n'>=1`. Consequently

\[
(ab)'
=b a'+a b'
\ge
b+a
=c.
\]

But for nonsquarefree `c`,

\[
m(c)<c,
\]

and for squarefree `c`, P025-T137 already applies. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
a,b\text{ both squarefree}\Longrightarrow\sigma_{\rm proj}<1.}
\]

## 4. P025-C14 — activation needs repeated-prime structure in at least two components

Combining P025-T137–T138:

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{\rm proj}\ge1
\Longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
c\text{ is nonsquarefree},\\
\text{at least one of }a,b\text{ is nonsquarefree}.
\end{cases}}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\text{at most one nonsquarefree component}
\Longrightarrow
\sigma_{\rm proj}<1.
}
\]

Thus non-unit activation requires repeated-prime structure in at least two distinct components, and one of them is always `c`.

This is pointwise and elementary; no density theorem is used.
