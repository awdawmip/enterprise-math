# P022 — Prime-Factor Ceiling for Half-Defect Support

Status: `ACTIVE RESEARCH NOTE / PROVED SUFFICIENT CERTIFICATE`  
Owner: `program/p022-geometry-v2`  
Depends on: central-binomial basis reduction, Franel half-index zero, rank of apparition  
Boundary: this is a sufficient support-safety theorem, not a characterization.

## 1. Canonical A-basis support is much lower than the midpoint

For a positive integer `x`, let `E(x)` be its canonical exponent vector in the central-binomial generators

\[
A_j=\binom{2j}{j}.
\]

For an odd prime `q`, put

\[
h=(q+1)/2.
\]

The exact recurrence

\[
q=\frac h2\frac{A_h}{A_{h-1}}
\]

gives

\[
E(q)=E(h)-E(2)+e_h-e_{h-1}.
\]

Hence the only new indices introduced by the prime `q` are the adjacent pair

\[
h-1,h,
\]

and the recursion continues only inside the strictly smaller integer `h`.

Let `P_odd(x)` be the largest odd prime factor of `x`, with value `1` when `x` has no odd prime factor. Then every index in `E(x)` is at most

\[
\boxed{
C(x)=
\begin{cases}
1,&P_{\rm odd}(x)=1,\\[2mm]
(P_{\rm odd}(x)+1)/2,&P_{\rm odd}(x)>1.
\end{cases}}
\]

This follows by induction down the prime-halving recursion.

## 2. Apply the bound to the composite-boundary half-index family

Take

\[
p\equiv5,23\pmod{24},\qquad p>5,
\]

and put

\[
m=(p-1)/2.
\]

Then `p|F_m` by the forced-midpoint theorem, while

\[
2m-1=p-2
\]

is composite. The canonical A-elimination relation is

\[
A_m=\prod_{j<m}A_j^{\alpha_j}.
\]

From

\[
E(A_m/A_{m-1})=E(2)+E(p-2)-E(m)
\]

we obtain the exact support envelope

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)
\subseteq
\{m-1\}\cup\{1,\ldots,B_p\},
}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
B_p=\max(C(m),C(p-2)).
}
\]

The point `m-1` is isolated from the low support. It is automatically Franel-nonzero modulo `p`, because `F_m=0` and two adjacent Franel terms cannot both vanish modulo a prime in the legal recurrence window.

## 3. P022-LI33 — rank-ceiling support certificate

Let

\[
r_p=\min\{j>0:p\mid F_j\}
\]

be the Franel rank of apparition.

If

\[
\boxed{r_p>B_p,}
\]

then every left-half Franel zero lies strictly above the low support ceiling, while the remaining support point `m-1` is nonzero. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)\cap Z_p=\varnothing.
}
\]

Consequently every earlier Franel factor used by the canonical A-elimination is a p-adic unit and

\[
\boxed{
v_p(D_m)=v_p(F_m).}
\]

This conclusion still does not prove that the midpoint zero is simple; that is the separate Franel--Wieferich / transverse-lift problem.

## 4. Why this improves the previous support-avoidance search

The old pressure test checked every support index against the Franel zero table. LI33 replaces that by two much smaller pieces of information:

1. the largest odd prime factors of `m` and `p-2`;
2. the single scalar rank `r_p`.

For the target primes below `5000`, the rank-ceiling criterion alone certifies 145 of 167 cases. The remaining cases are not counterexamples. They merely have an early zero below the coarse ceiling and therefore require the actual sparse prime-halving support tree.

Examples:

- `p=149`: `m=74`, `r_p=50`, `B_p=19`, so support avoidance is automatic even though the midpoint is not primitive;
- `p=239`: `m=119`, `r_p=94`, `B_p=40`, again automatically safe;
- `p=173`: `m=86`, `r_p=4`, `B_p=22`; the rank certificate is silent, but the exact support is still safe. This is the required negative boundary showing the condition is sufficient rather than necessary.

The finite counts are executable pressure-test evidence only; the theorem itself is the exact support envelope and the implication `r_p>B_p => support-safe`.

## 5. Next frontier

The remaining problem is now sharply localized:

> when `r_p<=B_p`, can a Franel zero digit lie on the actual canonical prime-halving DAG generated by the odd prime factors of `m` and `p-2`?

The universal integer midpoint companion converts the zero side into divisibility of one fixed recurrence `H_d`; the central-binomial basis converts the support side into one sparse recursive prime-halving DAG. The next theorem should study their incidence rather than returning to full Franel tables or large determinant certificates.
