# E001/E002 — Contact Action-Semigroup Boundary, Supplement 02

Status: `ACTIVE CROSS-ROUTE ENGINEERING NOTE`

Parent: `docs/E001_E002_CONTACT_ACTION_FAMILY_SUPPLEMENT_01.en.md`

## 1. Correction of the apparent symmetry requirement

The preceding action-family supplement proved the gcd contact coordinate for a family containing paired `+a_j/-a_j` actions. That symmetry is sufficient, but it is stronger than necessary.

The real algebraic boundary is whether the **signed action monoid is one-sided or two-sided**.

- with only separating actions (or only closing actions), future cumulative motion is a one-sided additive semigroup and can contain finite holes above its gcd;
- once at least one positive and one negative gap action are available, the additive action monoid equals the full subgroup generated by the signed actions, namely `g Z`.

This distinction changes the coarsest Boolean-contact precision.

## 2. One-sided separating language

Let

\[
A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}\subset\mathbb N_{>0}
\]

and allow only

\[
x\mapsto x+a_j.
\]

For horizon `h`, let

\[
M_h
\]

be the set of distinct cumulative sums of words of length at most `h`, including zero. Let

\[
M=\bigcup_hM_h
\]

be the generated numerical semigroup.

For a current contact gap `0<=x<d`, define remaining distance

\[
r=d-x\in\{1,\ldots,d\}.
\]

After a word with cumulative displacement `s`, contact remains true exactly when

\[
s<r.
\]

## 3. E001/E002-T40 — Exact one-sided semigroup partition

Two current-contact gaps are horizon-`h` future-equivalent exactly when no value of

\[
M_h\cap\{1,\ldots,d-1\}
\]

lies between their remaining-distance coordinates.

Equivalently, the scalar rank

\[
\boxed{
\rho_h(x)
=\#\{s\in M_h:0<s<d-x\}
}
\]

is the coarsest horizon-`h` predictive state inside the contact fiber.

The exact class count is

\[
\boxed{
C_h
=1+|M_h\cap\{1,\ldots,d-1\}|.
}
\]

For arbitrary finite future words,

\[
\boxed{
C_\infty
=1+|M\cap\{1,\ldots,d-1\}|.
}
\]

### Proof

For every reachable cumulative displacement `s`, the Boolean future bit is the nested threshold predicate `s<r`. Each distinct positive reachable `s<d` contributes exactly one new boundary between `r=s` and `r=s+1`; unreachable displacements create no future observation and therefore cannot split a fiber. Ordered threshold predicates are completely determined by how many reachable thresholds lie below `r`, giving the rank and count above. ∎

## 4. Closing-only separated shell

For a separated shell

\[
x=d+j,\qquad0\le j<R,
\]

with only closing magnitudes `a_j`, a cumulative closing displacement `s` reaches contact exactly when

\[
s>j.
\]

The same threshold argument gives

\[
\boxed{
C^{\rm close}_h
=1+|M_h\cap\{1,\ldots,R-1\}|,
}
\]

and the analogous arbitrary-future formula with `M`.

Thus one-sided motion on either side of the contact boundary is controlled by the **actually reachable semigroup thresholds**, not by the group completion alone.

## 5. E001/E002-T41 — Exact criterion for when gcd is already minimal

Let

\[
g=\gcd(a_1,\ldots,a_m).
\]

The sufficient gcd coordinate would retain

\[
\left\lceil\frac dg\right\rceil
\]

classes in the contact fiber.

It is also **minimal** for the one-sided separating language if and only if every positive multiple of `g` below `d` is actually reachable:

\[
\boxed{
kg\in M
\quad\text{for every }1\le kg<d.
}
\]

Equivalently, there are no numerical-semigroup holes among the relevant gcd multiples below the contact boundary.

### Counterexample

Take

\[
d=7,
\qquad
A=\{4,6\}.
\]

Then `g=2`, so gcd refinement retains

\[
\left\lceil\frac72\right\rceil=4
\]

classes.

But below `d`, the only positive reachable cumulative sums are

\[
4,6;
\]

`2` is a semigroup hole. Therefore

\[
\boxed{C_\infty=1+2=3.}
\]

The gcd coordinate is safe but strictly over-refined.

This is the promised counterexample to treating gcd as automatically minimal after removing bidirectionality.

## 6. Two-sided signed action language

Now let

\[
D=\{\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_m\}\subset\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}
\]

contain at least one positive and at least one negative action. Physical motion is

\[
x\mapsto\max(0,x+\delta_j).
\]

Let

\[
\boxed{g=\gcd(|\delta_1|,\ldots,|\delta_m|).}
\]

## 7. E001/E002-T42 — Bidirectionality restores the gcd-minimal quotient

The nonnegative-word additive monoid generated by `D` is exactly

\[
\boxed{g\mathbb Z.}
\]

Consequently the exact coarsest arbitrary-future Boolean-contact coordinate is again

\[
\boxed{
K_{d,D}(x)
=\left\lceil\frac{d-x}{g}\right\rceil.
}
\]

For a positive action `delta>0`,

\[
K\mapsto K-\delta/g.
\]

For a negative action `delta<0`,

\[
K\mapsto
\min\left(K+|\delta|/g,\left\lceil d/g\right\rceil\right).
\]

And

\[
x<d\iff K\ge1.
\]

### Why the action monoid equals `g Z`

The integer group generated by `D` is `g Z` by Bezout. It remains to show that negative coefficients are unnecessary once both signs occur.

Fix one positive generator `p` and one negative generator `-q`. For any positive generator whose Bezout coefficient is negative, add enough copies of the positive zero relation

\[
\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(a,q)}a\,a
+
\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(a,q)}q\,(-q)
=0
\]

to make that coefficient nonnegative. For any negative generator with a negative coefficient, use the analogous zero relation with `p`. Repeating this converts an arbitrary integer representation of any multiple of `g` into a representation using only nonnegative word counts. Hence the action monoid is all `g Z`.

### Coarsestness

Exact quotient transport proves that equal K-values are behaviorally identical. Since every integer K-shift is realizable by a finite signed-action word, distinct K-values can be shifted so that one finishes at `K=1` and the other at `K<=0`. Execute all separating moves before closing moves; the coordinate then rises only to the final boundary value, avoiding unintended lower-gap clipping. Thus distinct K-values are finitely distinguishable. ∎

## 8. Corrected boundary

The earlier sufficient paired-action theorem remains valid, but its strongest interpretation is corrected:

\[
\boxed{
\text{paired symmetry is sufficient, not necessary;}\quad
\text{two-sided signed reachability is the real gcd-minimal condition here.}
}
\]

One-sided action languages expose numerical-semigroup holes and may admit a strictly coarser state than gcd refinement.

This correction should supersede any relay wording suggesting that paired `+a_j/-a_j` symmetry itself is essential.

## 9. Compiler checks

The generic predictive quotient compiler independently validates both sides of the classification:

- separating-only finite worlds reproduce `1+|M_h cap [1,d-1]|` classes inside the current contact fiber;
- bidirectional but non-paired examples such as `{+6,-10}` and `{+9,-15}` produce stable compiler partitions equal state-by-state to the gcd coordinate K.

The compiler is not given the numerical-semigroup or gcd formulas.

## 10. Interpretation

This result gives an engineering meaning to the difference between a semigroup and its group completion:

- **one-sided future operations** leave unreachable threshold holes, so those distinctions are not part of the minimal predictive state;
- **two-sided future operations** fill the entire gcd subgroup behaviorally, forcing every gcd phase that can affect the Boolean boundary to remain distinguishable.

The amount of required precision therefore depends not only on numerical step sizes but on the directional operation language available to the future world.
