## 1. Three distinct witness quantities

The previous stages now separate three certificate quantities:

1. the first radius containing **any** non-degenerate witness,
   \[
   \mu
   =\min\{\|x\|_\infty:x\in T\setminus T^\circ\};
   \]
2. the minimum possible arithmetic absorption redundancy,
   \[
   \eta_{\min}
   =\min\{\eta(x):x\in T\setminus T^\circ\};
   \]
3. the first radius at which that arithmetic optimum is actually reachable.

Define the third quantity by

\[
\boxed{
\nu
=\min\{\|x\|_\infty:
 x\in T\setminus T^\circ,
 \eta(x)=\eta_{\min}\}.
}
\]

Then define the **absorption access delay**

\[
\boxed{
\delta_{\rm abs}=\nu-\mu\ge0.
}
\]

This quantity is zero when the first available certificate is already absorption-optimal, and positive when extra geometric search is required to reach the arithmetic floor.

## 2. P025-T21 — zero access delay iff the two-cost Pareto frontier is a singleton

Let

\[
\mathcal P
=\operatorname{Min}_{\preceq}
\{(\|x\|_\infty,\eta(x)):
 x\in T\setminus T^\circ\}
\]

be the absorption-aware Pareto frontier from Supplement 04.

Then

\[
\boxed{
\delta_{\rm abs}=0
\iff
\mathcal P=\{(\mu,\eta_{\min})\}.
}
\]

### Proof

If `delta_abs=0`, some witness simultaneously realizes radius `mu` and absorption `eta_min`. Every other witness has norm at least `mu` and absorption redundancy at least `eta_min`, so that point dominates every other cost pair; the Pareto frontier is the singleton shown.

Conversely, suppose the frontier is a singleton `(r,e)`. There exists a witness of radius `mu`, whose cost is dominated by some Pareto-minimal point, so `r<=mu`; minimality of `mu` gives `r=mu`. Likewise a witness attaining `eta_min` forces `e=eta_min`. Hence the singleton is `(mu,eta_min)` and there is a witness realizing both minima, so `nu=mu`. ∎

### Meaning

A scalar witness radius is complete for the two-threshold language exactly when geometric accessibility and arithmetic absorption optimality align at the first witness layer.

## 3. P025-T22 — rank-one witness lattices have no norm/absorption tradeoff

Assume

\[
\omega(abc)=2,
\]

so there are exactly two distinct prime coordinates. Then

\[
\boxed{
\delta_{\rm abs}=0
}
\]

and the complete two-cost Pareto frontier is a singleton.

More explicitly, write the primitive additive normal as

\[
\widehat\alpha=(u,v),
\qquad
\gcd(u,v)=1.
\]

Then

\[
T=\ker_{\mathbb Z}\widehat\alpha
=\mathbb Z\,(v,-u).
\]

Let

\[
t=(v,-u).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mu=\|t\|_\infty=\max(|u|,|v|).
}
\]

If `d` is the positive generator of the Wronskian image on `T`, then

\[
|W(t)|=d,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\eta(t)=\eta_{\min}=d/M.
}
\]

Every non-zero witness is `k t` for an integer `k!=0`, and therefore

\[
\left(
\|k t\|_\infty,
\eta(k t)
\right)
=
|k|
\left(
\mu,
\eta_{\min}
\right).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P
=\{(\mu,\eta_{\min})\}.
}
\]

### Proof

A primitive row `(u,v)` has integer kernel generated by `(v,-u)`. Supplement 04 identifies the positive generator of the Wronskian image as the absolute determinant of the additive and Wronskian rows; evaluating the Wronskian on `(v,-u)` gives that determinant up to sign. All non-zero lattice points are integer multiples of this primitive generator, and both costs scale by `|k|`. ∎

## 4. P025-C01 — at least three prime coordinates are necessary for a genuine Pareto tradeoff

From P025-T22:

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal P|>1
\Longrightarrow
\omega(abc)\ge3.
}
\]

The converse is false.

For example,

\[
1+5=6
\]

has three prime coordinates `(2,3,5)` but exact frontier

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P(1,5,6)=\{(1,1)\}.
}
\]

Thus ambient witness rank at least two merely makes a tradeoff **possible**; it does not force one.

## 5. Explicit tradeoff delays

### `2+3=5`

Supplement 04 found

\[
\mathcal P(2,3,5)
=\{(1,2),(2,1)\}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\mu=1,
\qquad
\eta_{\min}=1,
\qquad
\nu=2,
\qquad
\boxed{\delta_{\rm abs}=1}.
\]

### `2+7=9`

The exact frontier is

\[
\mathcal P(2,7,9)
=\{(1,3),(4,2),(5,1)\}.
\]

Hence

\[
\mu=1,
\qquad
\eta_{\min}=1,
\qquad
\nu=5,
\qquad
\boxed{\delta_{\rm abs}=4}.
\]

This example has no arithmetic absorption obstruction at the floor (`eta_min=1`) but still requires four extra lattice-radius levels to reach perfect absorption.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\text{absorption obstruction and absorption access difficulty are independent axes.}
}
\]

A state may have `eta_min>1` because the Wronskian image itself has a nontrivial index, or it may have `eta_min=1` but large `delta_abs` because the optimal image generator is geometrically expensive to realize in the chosen norm.

## 6. Four-way certificate decomposition

The P025 witness architecture can now distinguish:

1. **existence geometry** — `mu`;
2. **arithmetic obstruction** — `eta_min` / its local prime spectrum;
3. **optimal-access geometry** — `nu` or `delta_abs`;
4. **intermediate tradeoffs** — the full Pareto frontier `P`.

No one scalar determines the other three in general.

This is more precise than saying merely that “witness cost is multidimensional.” It identifies exactly where the different costs enter.
