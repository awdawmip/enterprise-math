# P022 — Primitive Franel rows re-enter after twin-prime deferral

## 1. The remaining immediate exception

Let `P` be a primitive Franel prime at rank `r>3`:

\[
P\mid F_r,
\qquad
P\nmid F_j\quad(1\le j<r),
\]

and write

\[
z=v_P(F_r)>0.
\]

The existing one-step capture theorem handles every primitive event except the
case

\[
2r-1,\quad2r+1
\]

both prime.  Thus `r` is the center of a twin-prime pair.

Every twin center above three satisfies

\[
\boxed{3\mid r.}
\]

The current note asks where the valuation row first has a forced opportunity to
re-enter after this genuine one-step deferral.

## 2. P022-TR01 — the delayed column D_(2r-1) always exists

Put

\[
q=2r-1.
\]

The odd boundary attached to segment `q` is

\[
2q-1=4r-3.
\]

Since `3|r`,

\[
4r-3\equiv0\pmod3.
\]

For `r>3`, this is a nontrivial composite.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{D_{2r-1}\text{ always exists}.}
\]

So a twin-prime center does not create an indefinitely missing defect column;
it creates a finite structural deferral.

## 3. P022-TR02 — exact high support of the canonical A relation

The canonical relation at segment

\[
q=2r-1
\]

is

\[
A_q=\prod_{j<q}A_j^{\alpha_{q,j}}.
\]

The universal relation formula is

\[
\alpha_q
=e_{q-1}+e_1+\beta(2q-1)-\beta(q),
\]

where `beta(v)` is the canonical central-binomial exponent vector of the
integer `v`.

Because `q=2r-1` is prime,

\[
\beta(q)=\beta(r)-e_1+e_r-e_{r-1}.
\]

All support of `beta(r)` is strictly below `r`.

On the other side,

\[
2q-1=4r-3
\]

is divisible by three.  Every prime factor of `4r-3` is at most

\[
\frac{4r-3}{3},
\]

so every central-binomial index generated by its prime-halving expansion is
strictly below `r`.  Consequently `beta(2q-1)` has no support at or above `r`.

Hence the complete high-index support is

\[
\boxed{
\{(r,-1),(q-1,+1)\}.
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\alpha_{q,r}=-1,
\qquad
\alpha_{q,2r-2}=1,
}
\]

and every other nonzero `alpha_(q,j)` has `j<r`.

This explains the empirical first re-entry at `D_11` for primitive rows born at
rank `6`: it is a structural central-binomial fact, not a coincidence of the
primes `13` and `73`.

## 4. P022-TR03 — exact delayed valuation formula

Since `P` is primitive at `r`, every Franel value with index `<r` is a
`P`-unit.  Pairing the relation of Section 3 against the `P`-adic Franel
valuation field therefore gives

\[
\boxed{
 v_P(D_{2r-1})
 =
 v_P(F_{2r-1})
 -v_P(F_{2r-2})
 +v_P(F_r).
}
\]

Writing

\[
w_-=v_P(F_{2r-2}),
\qquad
w_+=v_P(F_{2r-1}),
\]

this is simply

\[
\boxed{v_P(D_{2r-1})=w_+-w_-+z.}
\]

## 5. P022-TR04 — the second failure is an equal-depth collision

Franel zero digits cannot be adjacent.  Since all three relevant indices are
strictly below the primitive modulus `P`, if

\[
w_->0,
\]

then necessarily

\[
w_+=0.
\]

There are therefore only two cases.

### No predecessor zero

If `w_-=0`, then

\[
v_P(D_{2r-1})=z+w_+>0.
\]

The row is captured.

### Predecessor zero

If `w_->0`, then `w_+=0` and

\[
v_P(D_{2r-1})=z-w_-.
\]

Thus delayed capture fails **if and only if**

\[
\boxed{
P\mid F_{2r-2}
\quad\text{and}\quad
v_P(F_{2r-2})=v_P(F_r).
}
\]

This is much narrower than the original phrase "twin-prime deferral".  The
twin geometry only postpones the row; a second exact equal-depth Franel zero is
required to erase it again.

For a simple primitive event `z=1`, the exceptional condition is precisely a
simple second zero at `2r-2`.

## 6. P022-TR05 — reflection forces the one-third threshold

Assume the dangerous predecessor zero exists:

\[
P\mid F_{2r-2}.
\]

Because

\[
P\ge2r+1,
\]

this is a single base-`P` digit zero.  Jarvis--Verrill reflection supplies
another zero digit at

\[
P-1-(2r-2)=P-2r+1.
\]

But `r` is the first zero digit.  Therefore

\[
P-2r+1\ge r,
\]

which rearranges to

\[
\boxed{P\ge3r-1.}
\]

Consequently every primitive twin-center prime in the interval

\[
\boxed{2r+1\le P<3r-1}
\]

is unconditionally captured at `D_(2r-1)`.

The equality case

\[
P=3r-1
\]

is exactly

\[
r=\frac{P+1}{3},
\]

the one-third Franel index isolated independently by the Bailey/fixed
hypergeometric route.  At equality, reflection sends `r` exactly to `2r-2`,
so the dangerous second zero is forced whenever the one-third zero occurs.

Thus the one-third route is not an unrelated special-value experiment: it is
the exact boundary at which the first structural delayed-capture certificate
can lose its reflection-based size protection.

## 7. Midpoint-coordinate interpretation of the strict region

Let

\[
m=\frac{P-1}{2},
\qquad
r=m-d.
\]

Reflection of the dangerous second zero `2r-2` gives

\[
P-2r+1=2d+2.
\]

Primitiveness says

\[
2d+2\ge r.
\]

Equality is again the one-third line

\[
2d+2=r
\iff
P=3r-1.
\]

For `P>3r-1`, an actual delayed-capture cancellation therefore requires two
distinct zeros in the relevant universal midpoint-offset sector.  This points
to the existing `midpoint_integer_companion` / complementary reflection-sector
recurrences as the next tool for attacking the strict inequality region.

## 8. Exact examples

At `r=6`, the primitive primes `13` and `73` are genuinely deferred because
`11` and `13` are twin primes.  The new theorem gives

\[
\boxed{
v_{13}(D_{11})=v_{73}(D_{11})=1.
}
\]

Further exact primitive rows show the same unit re-entry:

\[
(r,P)=(9,937),(9,1409),(15,31),(15,179),(21,3019),(30,1361).
\]

In every listed case

\[
(v_P(F_r),v_P(F_{2r-2}),v_P(F_{2r-1}))=(1,0,0),
\]

so the delayed pivot equals `+1`.

The examples are evidence for the breadth of the theorem, not an assertion
that the equal-depth collision never occurs.

## 9. Pressure-test boundary

An independent recurrence scan of primes below `20000` found only two cases in
which a first zero `r_P` also satisfies

\[
P\mid F_{2r_P-2}:
\]

\[
(P,r_P)=(5,2),\qquad(149,50).
\]

Both lie exactly on

\[
P=3r_P-1.
\]

No strict `P>3r-1` example was found in that finite range.  This is only a
pressure test.  It motivates, but does not prove, the sharper candidate

\[
r_P=r,\ P\mid F_{2r-2}
\stackrel{?}{\Longrightarrow}
P=3r-1.
\]

That candidate is now a clearly isolated zero-spacing problem rather than an
unstructured primitive-divisor conjecture.

## 10. Consequence for the infinite Barlow route

The capture hierarchy is now:

1. `3 does not divide r`: capture at `D_r` or `D_(r+1)`;
2. `3|r` but the rank is not a twin center: the same one-step theorem applies;
3. twin center: capture at `D_(2r-1)` unless an equal-depth second zero occurs;
4. such a second zero forces `P>=3r-1`, with equality exactly the one-third
   hypergeometric boundary.

So the unresolved primitive-row loss mechanism has been compressed from
"arbitrary twin-prime deferral" to a **left-third zero-spacing/equal-depth
collision problem**.

If that final collision can be excluded (globally or on an infinite primitive
subfamily), the already-proved infinitude of primitive Franel events will
immediately produce an infinite family of nonzero Barlow defect valuation
rows.
