## 1. A3 exterior map as a structural target

Fix an integer capacity vector `m=(m_1,...,m_d)`. The A3 weighted relation field is

`E_m(c)=m wedge c`,

with coordinates

`(E_m(c))_(i,j)=m_i c_j-m_j c_i`.

This is a linear target map. Therefore every Structural Target Cut theorem from Supplement 20 applies directly once m is fixed.

## 2. Exterior Smith law

Let `g=gcd(m_1,...,m_d)` and assume `m != 0`. Write `m=g u` with primitive integer vector u.

A primitive integer vector extends to a unimodular basis, so after a unimodular domain change u becomes `e_1`; the induced change on `wedge^2 Z^d` is also unimodular. Thus the matrix of `c -> m wedge c` is Smith-equivalent to

`g * (c_2,...,c_d,0,...,0)`.

Hence the nonzero Smith invariants are exactly

`g,g,...,g`  (`d-1` times).

This strengthens the earlier determinant-token identity into a complete integer invariant-factor description.

## 3. p-adic relation-field profile

Work over `R=Z/p^K Z`. Let

`s=min_i nu_p(m_i)`, capped at K.

If `s=K`, the relation field is zero. Otherwise write

`m=p^s u`

with u primitive mod p. Then

`ker(E_m)=R u + p^(K-s) R^d`,

and

`im(E_m) ~= (Z/p^(K-s) Z)^(d-1)`.

Therefore the exact p-group exponent profile of the A3 relation field is

`(K-s,...,K-s)` (`d-1` entries),

with integer exponent mass

`(K-s)(d-1)`.

For an integer capacity vector, `s=nu_p(gcd(m_i))`. Hence the common p-adic content of the capacities reduces relation depth but not the number `d-1` of relation directions.

Define the capped A3 relation-digit deficit

`Gamma_(p,K)^A3(m)=(d-1)*min(K,nu_p(gcd(m_i)))`.

Primitive normalization `m/gcd(m_i)` removes every common prime-axis deficit simultaneously.

## 4. Equal profile does not identify relation semantics

If u and v are primitive over R, both exterior fields have the same exponent profile

`(K,...,K)` (`d-1` entries).

Yet `E_u` factors through `E_v` iff

`ker E_v subseteq ker E_u`.

Since both kernels are primitive rank-one lines, this holds iff

`R v=R u`,

or equivalently

`v=alpha u` for a unit `alpha in R^x`.

Thus relation rank and exponent profile quantify representation capacity but do not determine its projective direction or typed semantics.

## 5. Projective agreement depth

For primitive u,v define

`rho_(p,K)(u,v)=min(K, min_(i<j) nu_p(u_i v_j-u_j v_i))`.

Then `rho>=r` iff u and v determine the same primitive projective line modulo `p^r`.

Proof: choose a unit coordinate of u. If all 2x2 minors vanish mod `p^r`, every coordinate of v is the same unit multiple of the corresponding coordinate of u mod `p^r`; the converse is immediate. Because equality of primitive projective lines mod `p^r` is transitive, the relations `rho>=r` form a nested family of equivalence relations.

Define the integer projective defect distance

`d_(p,K)(u,v)=K-rho_(p,K)(u,v)`.

Then

`d(u,w)<=max(d(u,v),d(v,w))`.

So `d_(p,K)` is an integer ultrametric on primitive projective directions modulo `p^K`.

## 6. Target-defect distance equals projective distance

Take current observation `E_v=v wedge -` and target `E_u=u wedge -`.

Because v is primitive,

`ker E_v=R v`.

The target variation inside one current fiber is

`E_u(R v)=R (u wedge v)`.

The cyclic submodule generated by `u wedge v` has exponent depth

`K-rho_(p,K)(u,v)`.

By Supplement 20's defect/guard duality, the missing-target module has the same profile. Therefore

`Delta(E_v -> E_u)=d_(p,K)(u,v)`.

Thus the p-adic precision that must be repaired when replacing one primitive A3 relation representation by another is exactly their projective ultrametric distance. This metric is not externally imposed; it is the compiler's own target-defect mass.

## 7. Guard-image duality

Let A be a current linear coarse observation and W an A3 guard matrix over R. Put

`K_A=ker A`.

Supplement 20's missing-target module for exact guard-score visibility is

`D_(A,W)=(Row(A)+Row(W))/Row(A)`.

Under the perfect finite pairing,

`Row(A)=Ann(K_A)`

and

`Row(A)+Row(W)=Ann(K_A cap ker W)`.

Hence D is the finite dual of

`K_A/(K_A cap ker W)`.

By the first isomorphism theorem,

`K_A/(K_A cap ker W) ~= W(K_A)`.

Therefore

`D_(A,W) ~= W(K_A)^vee`

in the finite-dual sense, and the two modules have exactly the same invariant p-group exponent profile.

This identifies the Structural Target defect module with the dual representation of A3's existing guard-image lattice.

## 8. What this bridge does and does not solve

The bridge unifies the **amount and type of hidden guard freedom**:

- guard descends exactly when D=0;
- rank-one hidden guard corresponds to a cyclic nonzero defect profile;
- full-rank hidden guard corresponds to a defect module with full guard-direction rank;
- p-adic depth inside each hidden direction is retained rather than collapsed to rank alone.

However piecewise program exactness is not reduced to D=0. Existing A3 results already show that a hidden guard can be safely erased when all reachable branches induce the same coarse effect. Branch-effect equality, reachable sign patterns and threshold orthants remain typed piecewise semantics owned by A3.

The defect module measures hidden guard degrees of freedom; it does not decide what the program does with them.
