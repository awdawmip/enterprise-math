## 1. Goal

The previous P025 stages produced two complementary views of the same relation-conditioned witness precision `mu`:

1. Supplement 05 turns multiplicity demand into a necessary norm budget;
2. Supplements 01--02 reconstruct the witness lattice and its degeneracy hyperplane from a compact generator signature `(alpha,beta)`.

This supplement combines them without solving a shortest-vector problem in full.

The result is an exact finite bracket

\[
\boxed{\lambda_{abc}\le \mu\le U_2}
\]

whose lower endpoint is forced by arithmetic demand and whose upper endpoint is supplied by an explicit two-prime-coordinate non-degenerate witness read directly from a nonzero generator minor.

If the endpoints meet, `mu` is certified exactly without enumerating the witness lattice.

## 2. Residual-normalized derivative weight

For a positive integer `n`, put

\[
A(n)
=
\sum_{p\mid n}\frac{n}{p}v_p(n)
\]

and recall

\[
m(n)=\frac{n}{\operatorname{rad}(n)}.
\]

Every term of `A(n)` is divisible by `m(n)`, and exact cancellation gives

\[
\boxed{
\widehat A(n)
:=\frac{A(n)}{m(n)}
=
\sum_{p\mid n}
\frac{\operatorname{rad}(n)}{p}v_p(n)
\in\mathbb N_0.
}
\]

This coordinate removes the repeated-power bulk that is already carried by `m(n)` while retaining the exponent labels needed by the arithmetic derivative coefficients.

## 3. P025-T19 — normalized complementary capacity

For two positive integers `x,y`, define

\[
\boxed{
K_{x,y}
=
\operatorname{rad}(x)\widehat A(y)
+
\operatorname{rad}(y)\widehat A(x).
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
K_{x,y}
=
\operatorname{rad}(x)\operatorname{rad}(y)
\left(
\sum_{p\mid x}\frac{v_p(x)}p
+
\sum_{p\mid y}\frac{v_p(y)}p
\right),
\]

but the first formula makes integrality explicit.

Let `H_(x,y)` be the norm-budget coefficient mass from Supplement 05:

\[
H_{x,y}
=xA(y)+yA(x).
\]

Because `x=m(x)rad(x)` and `A(x)=m(x)\widehat A(x)`, one gets the exact factorization

\[
\boxed{
H_{x,y}=m(x)m(y)K_{x,y}.
}
\]

Now let `(x,y,z)` be an orientation of the primitive abc triple. Supplement 05 gives

\[
m(x)m(y)m(z)\le H_{x,y}\mu.
\]

Cancel the positive factor `m(x)m(y)`:

\[
\boxed{
m(z)\le K_{x,y}\mu.}
\]

This is a useful normalization: the target's repeated-prime demand is controlled by the complementary pair's normalized coefficient capacity times the global minimum witness precision.

## 4. P025-T20 — triple-only arithmetic demand floor

For target `z` with complementary pair `(x,y)`, define

\[
\lambda_z
=
\left\lceil\frac{m(z)}{K_{x,y}}\right\rceil.
\]

Whenever the non-degenerate witness family exists, `K_(x,y)>0`, and P025-T19 gives

\[
\boxed{\lambda_z\le\mu.}
\]

Combine all three target orientations:

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{abc}
=
\max\{\lambda_a,\lambda_b,\lambda_c\}
\le\mu.
}
\]

`lambda_abc` is important because it is computed from the arithmetic triple alone. It does not require enumerating or optimizing any witness vector.

### Interpretation

`lambda_abc` is the **arithmetic-demand precision floor**: below this norm radius, no relation-adapted non-degenerate certificate can possibly carry all target multiplicity demands, regardless of lattice geometry.

It is only a lower bound. It is not a complete replacement for `mu`.

## 5. Negative boundary — arithmetic demand does not determine witness precision

For

\[
1+7=8,
\]

one obtains

\[
\lambda_{abc}=4,
\]

while the exact relation-conditioned witness lattice has

\[
\mu=12.
\]

The primitive additive normal on prime coordinates `(2,7)` is

\[
\alpha=(12,-1),
\]

so the additive kernel is generated by `(1,12)` up to sign. The arithmetic demand floor sees only part of this relation slope.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\text{multiplicity demand}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{complete witness precision}.
}
\]

The missing cost is genuinely relation/lattice information, not more radical support bookkeeping.

## 6. P025-T21 — explicit two-coordinate non-degenerate witness

Let

\[
\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_s)
\]

be the primitive additive normal and

\[
\beta=(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_s)
\]

a degeneracy normal for the arithmetic Wronskian, on the same prime-labelled coordinate set.

For `i<j`, define the two-row minor

\[
\omega_{ij}
=
\alpha_i\beta_j-\alpha_j\beta_i.
\]

Suppose

\[
\omega_{ij}\ne0.
\]

Let

\[
g_{ij}=\gcd(|\alpha_i|,|\alpha_j|)
\]

and define the sparse vector `x^(ij)` by

\[
x_i=\frac{\alpha_j}{g_{ij}},
\qquad
x_j=-\frac{\alpha_i}{g_{ij}},
\]

with every other coordinate zero.

Then

\[
\alpha\cdot x^{(ij)}=0.
\]

Moreover

\[
\beta\cdot x^{(ij)}
=
\frac{\beta_i\alpha_j-\beta_j\alpha_i}{g_{ij}}
=-\frac{\omega_{ij}}{g_{ij}}
e0.
\]

Therefore `x^(ij)` is an explicit non-degenerate relation-adapted witness.

Its `L_infinity` cost is

\[
\boxed{
U_{ij}
=
\frac{\max(|\alpha_i|,|\alpha_j|)}
{\gcd(|\alpha_i|,|\alpha_j|)}.
}
\]

Because non-degeneracy of the full witness flag means `alpha,beta` are not proportional, at least one `omega_ij` is nonzero. Hence define

\[
\boxed{
U_2
=
\min_{\omega_{ij}\ne0}U_{ij}.
}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\mu\le U_2.}
\]

This is an elementary sparse kernel construction. P025 does not claim the two-row determinant/Pluecker algebra as new.

## 7. P025-T22 — exact finite witness-precision bracket

Combining P025-T20 and P025-T21:

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{abc}\le\mu\le U_2.
}
\]

The interval width

\[
\Delta_\mu=U_2-\lambda_{abc}
\]

is an exact finite uncertainty budget before a full lattice optimization is performed.

Most importantly,

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{abc}=U_2
\Longrightarrow
\mu=\lambda_{abc}=U_2.
}
\]

So some witness-precision questions can be closed by matching one arithmetic lower certificate with one generator-based sparse upper certificate, with no witness-ball enumeration.

### Examples

- `1+2=3`: `lambda_abc=1=U_2`, hence `mu=1` exactly.
- `1+8=9`: `lambda_abc=2=U_2`, hence `mu=2` exactly.
- `1+7=8`: `lambda_abc=4`, `mu=12`, `U_2=12`.
- `1+36=37`: `lambda_abc=6`, `mu=12`, `U_2=24`; both sides remain genuinely nontrivial.

## 8. P025-T23 — high abc quality forces a witness-precision horizon

Recall the earlier P025 result. Fix positive integers `u>v`. If

\[
c^v>\operatorname{rad}(abc)^u,
\]

then

\[
m_{\max}^{3u}
>
c^{u-v}(c-1)^u.
\]

Define

\[
H_{u,v}(c)
=
R_{3u}\!\left(c^{u-v}(c-1)^u\right).
\]

Then

\[
m_{\max}>H_{u,v}(c).
\]

Let

\[
K_{\max}=\max\{K_{b,c},K_{c,a},K_{a,b}\}.
\]

For the target attaining `m_max`, P025-T19 gives

\[
m_{\max}\le K_i\mu\le K_{\max}\mu.
\]

Hence

\[
K_{\max}\mu>H_{u,v}(c),
\]

and since all quantities are integers,

\[
\boxed{
\mu
\ge
\left\lfloor\frac{H_{u,v}(c)}{K_{\max}}\right\rfloor+1.
}
\]

This is the first direct transport from an abc-type high-quality event to an explicit witness-precision floor. It does not prove that the floor tends to infinity; the triple-dependent complementary capacity may also grow.

That limitation is important. The theorem is an exact routing identity, not an asymptotic abc breakthrough.

## 9. Bounded pressure test

An independent exact prototype enumerated ordered primitive triples with

- `c<80`;
- prime-support dimension at most four;
- exact witness precision found within `mu<=12`.

It retained 1,154 states.

Within that bounded set:

- every state satisfied `lambda_abc<=mu<=U_2`;
- `mu-lambda_abc` reached `8`, confirming that the arithmetic lower bound alone is not complete;
- `lambda_abc=U_2` in 592 states, so the bracket certified the exact `mu` without a shortest-vector search in a little over half of this deliberately small sample;
- `U_2=mu` in 724 states.

These counts are executable exploration only. They are not distributional or asymptotic claims.

## 10. Architecture consequence

P025 now has three different precision notions that should not be merged:

1. **arithmetic-demand precision** `lambda_abc` — what the multiplicity load forces;
2. **exact relation-conditioned witness precision** `mu` — the first non-degenerate certificate radius;
3. **sparse generator ceiling** `U_2` — what the compact row/Pluecker generator can explicitly construct without global lattice search.

Thus the proof architecture has a certificate sandwich:

\[
\boxed{
\text{demand certificate}
\le
\text{true witness precision}
\le
\text{sparse construction certificate}.
}
\]

This is more informative than declaring the witness search simply `safe/unsafe` or `found/not found`.

At the same time, all three quantities are task-relative. They belong to the declared relation/certificate language and should not be promoted into physical or ontological state automatically.
