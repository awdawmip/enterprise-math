# P025 Supplement 130 — Rooted closure circuits and one-round relation-law precision

Status: `PROVED_WIP + EXECUTABLE_CHECKED / NOVELTY_UNVERIFIED`  
Owner: `program/p025-closure-basis-stage130`  
Scope: finite conjunction closure induced by an exact-state family; pressure test for A2/A4 relation-law precision.

## 1. Setup

Let `P` be a finite label set and let `Omega` be a nonempty family of exact Boolean states `X subseteq P`. Define the exact conjunction closure

\[
\operatorname{cl}_\Omega(S)
=
\bigcap\{X\in\Omega:S\subseteq X\},
\]

with the empty intersection interpreted as `P`.

By Stage 127, two conjunction queries are semantically equivalent exactly when they have the same closure.

For `b notin A`, call `(A,b)` a **rooted closure circuit** when

\[
b\in\operatorname{cl}_\Omega(A)
\]

and no proper subset `A' subsetneq A` already forces `b`.

The empty premise is allowed; it records mandatory labels in `cl(empty)`.

## 2. One-round circuit theorem

For every seed `S subseteq P`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{cl}_\Omega(S)
=
S\cup
\{b:\exists\text{ rooted circuit }(A,b),\ A\subseteq S\}.
}
\]

### Proof

Soundness is immediate from monotonicity of closure: if `A subseteq S` and `b in cl(A)`, then `b in cl(S)`.

Conversely, take `b in cl(S)\S`. Among subsets `A subseteq S` forcing `b`, choose one inclusion-minimal. Then `(A,b)` is a rooted closure circuit and its premise already lies in `S`. Hence every missing member of `cl(S)` is supplied in one parallel circuit round. QED.

Thus the **full rooted-circuit table** is a direct one-round presentation of the complete conjunction semantics.

## 3. Higher-order relation-law spectrum

Define

\[
N_k(\Omega)
=
\#\{(A,b): (A,b)\text{ rooted circuit},\ |A|=k\}.
\]

and

\[
h_{\rm circ}(\Omega)=\max\{|A|:(A,b)\text{ rooted circuit}\},
\]

with value zero if there are no nontrivial consequences.

Then Stage 129's unary-generated boundary has the exact circuit form

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{cl}_\Omega
\text{ is generated by mandatory core + singleton consequences}
\iff
h_{\rm circ}(\Omega)\le1.
}
\]

Indeed, a rooted circuit of premise size at least two is exactly an irreducible higher-order implication witness; conversely every higher-order closure defect contains an inclusion-minimal premise of size at least two.

## 4. Exact fixture

Take

\[
\Omega=\{\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b,c\}\}.
\]

The rooted circuits are

\[
\{c\}\Rightarrow a,
\qquad
\{c\}\Rightarrow b,
\qquad
\{a,b\}\Rightarrow c.
\]

Hence

\[
N_1=2,\qquad N_2=1,
\qquad h_{\rm circ}=2.
\]

The binary circuit is exactly the Stage-129 higher-order defect `a AND b -> c`.

## 5. Negative boundary: direct circuits need not be a globally irredundant Horn basis

The rooted-circuit table is minimal **premise-by-premise for each root**, but that does not imply global irredundancy under iterative forward chaining.

For the unary implication chain

\[
a\Rightarrow c\Rightarrow b,
\]

the exact closure system has rooted circuits

\[
a\Rightarrow c,
\qquad
c\Rightarrow b,
\qquad
a\Rightarrow b.
\]

The last implication is a rooted circuit because `{a}` is an inclusion-minimal premise forcing `b`, but it is globally redundant if iterative chaining through `c` is allowed.

Therefore two resources must be separated:

1. **direct one-round relation-law table**, naturally represented by all rooted circuits;
2. **iterative implication basis**, which may store fewer rules at the cost of derivation depth.

Stage 131 should study this storage/depth tradeoff rather than call the full circuit table a minimum basis.
