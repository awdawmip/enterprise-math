## 1. The cocycle changes what should be stored

Stage 84 proves

\[
\Lambda_{m\to r}
=
\Lambda_{m\to n}\Lambda_{n\to r}
\]

along every admissible same-sign divisor chain.

Therefore a long-range transition `m->n` is not independent information. The next compression question is:

> Which exponent transitions are sufficient to reconstruct every other transition?

The natural answer is the Hasse diagram: retain only prime-ratio cover edges.

## 2. Research domain

This route studies equal prime-power complements with exponent

\[

\]

Exponent one is deliberately outside this transport graph. This makes the primitive nodes mathematically meaningful: they are exponents whose pressure cannot be inherited from another equal-exponent shell already inside the research domain.

## 3. P025-D30 — difference transport graph

For the difference sign, define

\[
m\preceq_- n
\iff
m\mid n.
\]

The Hasse covers are exactly

\[
\boxed{m\prec_- mp}
\]

with `p` prime.

Every composite exponent has an incoming cover: divide by any prime factor. Every prime exponent has no proper divisor at least two.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{primitive roots of the difference transport graph}
=
\text{prime exponents}.
}
\]

This gives a structural explanation for why Stage 79's prime-exponent theorem is the primitive difference case.

## 4. P025-D31 — sum transport graph

For the sum sign, same-sign divisibility requires an odd quotient. Define

\[
\boxed{
m\preceq_+ n
\iff
m\mid n
\text{ and }n/m\text{ is odd}.
}
\]

Thus a Hasse cover is

\[
\boxed{m\prec_+ mp}
\]

with `p` an **odd** prime.

Multiplication by two is not a same-sign sum edge.

## 5. P025-T177 — sum components are indexed by `v_2(n)`

The condition that `n/m` is odd is equivalent to

\[
v_2(m)=v_2(n).
\]

Therefore the sum transport graph decomposes into disjoint components indexed by the exact two-adic valuation of the exponent.

For a component with

\[
v_2(n)=a\ge1,
\]

every exponent has the form

\[
2^a u,
\qquad u\text{ odd}.
\]

The unique minimal exponent in that component is

\[
\boxed{2^a.}
\]

For the odd component `a=0`, exponent one is outside the research domain. The minimal remaining nodes are therefore the odd primes.

Hence:

\[
\boxed{
\text{primitive roots of the sum transport graph}
=
\{\text{odd primes}\}
\cup
\{2^a:a\ge1\}.
}
\]

This is the exact sign-specific primitive classification.

## 6. Stage 79 and Stage 82 are now one picture

### Odd prime exponent

For either sign, an odd prime exponent has no incoming same-sign edge in the `n>=2` research graph. Its pressure is primitive relative to this transport system.

This is the setting of Stage 79.

### Fourth-power difference

Exponent four has the difference cover

\[
2\prec_-4.
\]

Stage 84 proves that the `(23,41)` hard state is a resonant lift along this edge.

So fourth-power difference is not primitive.

### Fourth-power sum

For the sum sign, the quotient `4/2=2` is even and therefore not an admissible same-sign edge. Exponent four is the root `2^2` of its sum component.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
4\text{ is nonprimitive for difference but primitive for sum}.
}
\]

This explains the Stage-82 sign contrast without appealing to parity as a vague heuristic.

### Ninth powers

Exponent nine has the cover

\[
3\prec_\pm9
\]

for both signs. The Stage-83 ninth-power counterexamples are therefore nonprimitive lifts from cube states.

## 7. P025-T178 — Hasse-cover sufficiency

Let

\[
m\mid n
\]

be an admissible same-sign transition in the `n>=2` graph.

Factor

\[
\frac nm
=p_1p_2\cdots p_s
\]

into primes, with all `p_i` odd on a sum route.

Define the cover chain

\[
e_0=m,
\qquad
e_i=e_{i-1}p_i,
\qquad
e_s=n.
\]

Repeated application of Stage 84 gives

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_{m\to n}
=
\prod_{i=1}^s
\Lambda_{e_{i-1}\to e_i}.
}
\]

Thus all long-range inheritance multipliers are generated by prime-ratio cover edges.

No non-cover transition needs to be stored as independent transport data.

This is **Hasse-cover sufficiency**. It is a sufficiency statement, not a claim that no alternative coordinate system can compress further.

## 8. P025-T179 — local diamond flatness

Suppose `r,s` are distinct admissible cover primes. Then there are two paths

\[
m\to mr\to mrs
\]

and

\[
m\to ms\to mrs.
\]

Hasse-cover sufficiency and the exact cocycle imply

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_{m\to mr}\Lambda_{mr\to mrs}
=
\Lambda_{m\to ms}\Lambda_{ms\to mrs}.
}
\]

Therefore every commuting-prime diamond is flat.

This local relation is the finite consistency law behind global path independence.

On logarithmic pressure increments

\[
\lambda_{a\to b}:=\log\Lambda_{a\to b},
\]

the same law becomes

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{m\to mr}+\lambda_{mr\to mrs}
=
\lambda_{m\to ms}+\lambda_{ms\to mrs}.
}
\]

## 9. What is genuinely primitive now

Stage 84 originally suggested a "primitive node pressure." The Hasse analysis sharpens that idea.

Node pressure itself is a potential. The irreducible local transport information lives on cover edges.

Thus the research split should be:

1. **primitive root states** — nodes with no incoming same-sign cover;
2. **cover-edge innovation** — attenuation/resonance/amplification introduced by one new prime factor in the exponent;
3. **derived long-range transport** — products of cover-edge multipliers.

The third class is not new information and should not be counted or stored independently.
