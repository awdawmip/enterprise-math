# P012 Supplement — What replaces Pythagoras in shortest-step geometry?

Status: `PROVED BOUNDARY RESULT`  
Parent: `P012`

## 1. The question

After defining intrinsic distance by shortest primitive-step length, it is tempting to ask whether the classical Pythagorean relation

\[
c^2=a^2+b^2
\]

should also be treated as foundational.

The answer is **no** for a generic graph metric and already no for the standard-axis integer lattice with its intrinsic \(L^1\) distance.

Pythagoras is a theorem about an inner-product / Euclidean norm structure. Shortest-step geometry supplies a metric without automatically supplying that additional structure.

## 2. P012-P01 — Exact coordinate-geodesic additivity

Status: `PROVED`

On \(\mathbb Z^2\) with standard-axis primitive steps, let

\[
O=(0,0),
\qquad
A=(a,0),
\qquad
B=(a,b).
\]

Then

\[
d(O,A)=|a|,
\qquad
d(A,B)=|b|,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{d(O,B)=d(O,A)+d(A,B)=|a|+|b|.}
\]

So the intrinsic right-angle rectangle law is additive in path length, not quadratic in squared length.

This is not a replacement identity obtained by deforming Pythagoras. It follows directly from the primitive step set: every required horizontal and vertical unit change must occur somewhere in any path.

## 3. P012-P02 — One discrete sphere contains multiple Euclidean radii

Status: `PROVED COUNTEREXAMPLE TO HIDDEN-EUCLIDEAN INTERPRETATION`

In the standard-axis lattice,

\[
d((0,0),(2,0))=2
\]

and

\[
d((0,0),(1,1))=2.
\]

Thus both states lie on the same intrinsic radius-2 sphere.

But their squared Euclidean coordinate norms differ:

\[
2^2+0^2=4,
\qquad
1^2+1^2=2.
\]

Therefore the intrinsic graph sphere cannot be interpreted as “a Euclidean circle whose true radius has merely been rounded to an integer.”

The same discrete distance value genuinely groups states with different Euclidean radii.

## 4. P012-P03 — The intrinsic \(L^1\) norm is not generated by an inner product

Status: `PROVED`

Any norm generated by an inner product satisfies the parallelogram identity

\[
\|x+y\|^2+\|x-y\|^2
=
2\|x\|^2+2\|y\|^2.
\]

Take

\[
x=(1,0),
\qquad y=(0,1)
\]

with the intrinsic lattice norm

\[
\|(u,v)\|_1=|u|+|v|.
\]

Then

\[
\|x+y\|_1=2,
\qquad
\|x-y\|_1=2,
\qquad
\|x\|_1=\|y\|_1=1.
\]

Hence the two sides of the parallelogram identity are

\[
2^2+2^2=8
\]

and

\[
2\cdot1^2+2\cdot1^2=4.
\]

They differ. Therefore no inner product induces this norm.

Consequently, a Pythagorean theorem in the ordinary inner-product sense is not intrinsic to the standard shortest-step geometry.

## 5. P012-P04 — Metric structure does not determine a unique Pythagorean structure

Status: `PROVED DESIGN CONSEQUENCE`

The graph metric determines:

- shortest-path lengths;
- geodesics;
- spheres and balls;
- graph isometries.

It does not by itself determine:

- an inner product;
- orthogonality;
- squared-length additivity;
- angle measure.

Those require extra structure.

Therefore Enterprise Math should not define “right angle” by importing a hidden Euclidean embedding after the metric has been chosen.

If a model needs orthogonality, it must add it explicitly, for example through:

- a combinatorial relation between primitive directions;
- a bilinear integer form;
- an incidence/cell structure;
- another declared discrete algebraic object.

The resulting Pythagorean-like theorem would then belong to that enriched geometry, not to graph distance alone.

## 6. Geodesics are plentiful rather than uniquely straight

For \(a,b\ge0\), every path from \((0,0)\) to \((a,b)\) containing exactly \(a\) right steps and \(b\) up steps has shortest possible length

\[
a+b.
\]

The number of such monotone geodesics is

\[
\boxed{\binom{a+b}{a}.}
\]

Thus even between two states there can be many exact shortest paths. A unique hidden straight segment is not part of the intrinsic structure.

This multiplicity can itself become a discrete geometric observable if later work needs a notion of directional degeneracy or path entropy, but no logarithm is required to count it.

## 7. Exact quarter-turn symmetry remains valid

The failure of Euclidean Pythagoras does not mean the lattice has no rotational structure.

The quarter-turn

\[
(x,y)\mapsto(-y,x)
\]

is still a graph automorphism and exact isometry. It maps the intrinsic sphere \(S_r\) to itself.

So discrete rotational symmetry and inner-product geometry are separate questions.

## 8. Boundary for future Enterprise geometry

P012's current foundation should therefore be layered:

1. **primitive relation**: allowed one-step transitions;
2. **intrinsic metric**: shortest-step / shortest-cost distance;
3. **optional directional/incidence structure**: if the application needs angles or orthogonality;
4. **optional Pythagorean theorem**: only after a structure strong enough to define perpendicularity has been explicitly supplied.

This prevents a hidden Euclidean continuum from re-entering through the words “angle”, “straight line”, or “Pythagorean theorem.”

## 9. Prior-art discipline

Manhattan/\(L^1\) geometry, the parallelogram law, inner-product norm characterization, and shortest-path geodesic counting are established mathematics.

Enterprise Math does not claim these facts as new. The project-specific boundary is methodological: metric structure is accepted at the exact discrete level, while inner-product/Pythagorean structure must earn its way back through explicit additional axioms rather than being silently inherited from a continuum embedding.
