## 1. Why the 151×151 certificate is not the real object

For one observed checkpoint segment of length `n`, the first two nontrivial equal-observation moments contribute the multiplicative pair

\[
\boxed{
g_n=(A_n,F_n),}
\]

where

\[
A_n=\binom{2n}{n},
\qquad
F_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom nk^3.
\]

A completely hidden tail of length one contributes

\[
\boxed{h=(4,8).}
\]

The existing low-order identifiability certificate proves multiplicative independence of

\[
g_1,\ldots,g_{150},h
\]

by a `151 x 151` matrix of selected `p`-adic valuations.

That proof is exact, but it hides a large deterministic block.  The central-binomial coordinate can be eliminated completely.  The remaining arithmetic uncertainty lives in a much smaller family of **pure Franel defects**.

## 2. Central-binomial recurrence

The elementary ratio is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{A_n}{A_{n-1}}
=\frac{2(2n-1)}n.
}
\]

This is established mathematics.  Its role here is to describe exactly which multiplicative directions the `A_n` coordinate can add.

Let

\[
G_N=\langle A_1,\ldots,A_N\rangle
\subset\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\times}.
\]

---

## 3. P022-DR01 — the `A`-group is exactly the prime group through `2N-1`

Let

\[
P_N=\langle p:\ p\text{ prime},\ p\le2N-1\rangle.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{G_N=P_N.}
\]

### Inclusion `G_N subseteq P_N`

Every prime divisor of

\[
A_n=\binom{2n}{n}
\]

is at most `2n`, and the only even prime is two.  Since no odd prime equals `2n`, every odd prime divisor is at most `2n-1<=2N-1`.

Hence every `A_n` lies in `P_N`.

### Inclusion `P_N subseteq G_N`

First,

\[
A_1=2.
\]

Now let `p` be an odd prime with

\[
p\le2N-1.
\]

Set

\[
j=\frac{p+1}{2}\le N.
\]

The recurrence gives

\[
\frac{A_j}{A_{j-1}}
=\frac{2p}{j},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
p=\frac j2\frac{A_j}{A_{j-1}}.}
\]

Every prime factor of `j` is strictly smaller than `p`.  Induction on the prime `p` therefore expresses `j` inside `G_j`, and hence expresses `p` inside `G_j subseteq G_N`.

Thus `G_N=P_N`. ∎

Because distinct primes are multiplicatively independent in `Q_+^x`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}G_N=\pi(2N-1).
}
\]

## 4. P022-DR02 — prime and composite odd boundaries are completely different

At index `n`, the only possible new prime direction beyond `G_(n-1)` is

\[
2n-1.
\]

### If `2n-1` is prime

Since

\[
2n-1>2(n-1),
\]

this prime divides none of

\[
A_1,\ldots,A_{n-1}.
\]

But

\[
v_{2n-1}(A_n)=1,
\]

because `(2n)!` contains exactly one copy of `2n-1` and `n!` contains none.

Therefore `A_n` creates one new multiplicative pivot.

### If `2n-1` is composite

Every prime factor `p` of `2n-1` satisfies

\[
p\le\frac{2n-1}{3}<n.
\]

Hence every such prime already lies in `G_(n-1)`.  Also `n` itself is generated by primes below `2n-1`.

The recurrence therefore implies

\[
\boxed{A_n\in G_{n-1}.}
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{
A_n\text{ increases multiplicative rank}
\iff
2n-1\text{ is prime}.}
\]

This explains the deterministic `A`-pivot pattern in the computational certificates without any determinant calculation.

## 5. Canonical triangular composite relation

The preceding proof can be made constructive.

For each positive integer `m`, recursively express `m` as a product of earlier central-binomial generators using

\[
p=\frac{(p+1)/2}{2}
\frac{A_{(p+1)/2}}{A_{(p-1)/2}}
\]

for odd primes `p`, with `2=A_1`.

If

\[
2n-1
\]

is composite, substituting these expansions into

\[
A_n=A_{n-1}\frac{2(2n-1)}n
\]

gives a canonical exponent vector

\[
\alpha_n=(\alpha_{n,1},\ldots,\alpha_{n,n-1})
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
A_n=
\prod_{j<n}A_j^{\alpha_{n,j}}.
}
\]

The relation is triangular: its newest column is exactly `A_n` with coefficient one, and only earlier `A_j` occur on the other side.

## 6. P022-DR03 — pure Franel defect

Apply the same exponent vector to the Franel coordinate and define

\[
\boxed{
D_n
=
\frac{F_n}{\prod_{j<n}F_j^{\alpha_{n,j}}}
}
\]

for every composite odd-boundary index.

Then the corresponding relation among joint generators is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{g_n}{\prod_{j<n}g_j^{\alpha_{n,j}}}
=(1,D_n).
}
\]

Thus all `A`-information cancels exactly and the unresolved direction is purely Franel.

The hidden-tail generator has the analogous relation

\[
\boxed{
\frac{h}{g_1^2}
=
\frac{(4,8)}{(2,2)^2}
=(1,2).
}
\]

Call `2` the **tail defect**.

## 7. P022-DR04 — the composite relations form a basis of the `A`-kernel

For segments `1,...,N` plus the tail there are

\[
N+1
\]

joint generators.

Their projection to the `A` coordinate has rank

\[
\pi(2N-1).
\]

Hence the relation space killed by `A`-projection has rank

\[
N+1-\pi(2N-1).
\]

Now count the explicit triangular relations:

- one tail relation;
- one relation for every `n in {2,...,N}` with composite `2n-1`.

The number of prime-boundary indices in `{2,...,N}` is the number of odd primes at most `2N-1`, namely

\[
\pi(2N-1)-1.
\]

Therefore the number of composite indices is

\[
(N-1)-(\pi(2N-1)-1)
=N-\pi(2N-1).
\]

Adding the tail relation gives exactly

\[
\boxed{N+1-\pi(2N-1)}
\]

relations.

They are independent because each composite relation has a different newest segment column and the tail relation has the unique tail column.

Thus they form a basis of the entire multiplicative relation lattice killed by the `A` projection.

Any multiplicative relation among

The Franel coordinate of that relation is therefore the corresponding product of

\[

\[
D_n.

Consequently:

\[
\boxed{
\{g_1,\ldots,g_N,h\}
\text{ multiplicatively independent}

if and only if

\[
\boxed{
\{2\}
\cup

This is the structural form of the low-order collision-identifiability problem.

The prime-boundary columns are no longer mysterious and require no Franel input.  **Every genuinely hard new direction occurs at a composite `2n-1` and is exactly a Franel-defect question.**

## 12. New global frontier

DR05 changes the open problem.

The previous formulation was:

> are the pairs `(A_n,F_n)` jointly multiplicatively independent forever?

The sharper formulation is:

> are the tail defect `2` and all composite Franel defects `D_n` multiplicatively independent?

Equivalently, for each new composite index, does `D_n` escape the multiplicative subgroup generated by the earlier defects?

A sufficient but stronger condition would be a fresh prime valuation for every new defect.  The `n=67` example proves that such a primitive-prime mechanism is **not necessary for the stored certificate**: a previously occurring Franel prime can detect the new dependency defect globally.

No infinite independence theorem is claimed.
