# Legendre Pressure Test — Supplement 04

Status: `ACTIVE RESEARCH NOTE`  
Scope: exact Alexander-dual descent from the `2k` divisor threshold to a threshold at most `floor((k+1)/2)`, plus a two-sided integer-root filtration.  
Discipline: **this note does not prove Legendre's conjecture.**

## 1. Why another duality matters

Supplements 02–03 replaced the large Möbius tail by the reduced Euler characteristic of a finite multiplicative threshold complex

\[
K(G,T)=\left\{F:\prod_{p\in F}p\le T\right\}
\]

and identified its uncancelled terms with the established quota-complex shell.

That still leaves the leading negative 1-dimensional shell. The square-basin condition gives more structure than an arbitrary quota complex: every divisor above the cutoff actually divides an integer state

\[
k^2<n<(k+1)^2.
\]

This note exploits that fact through combinatorial Alexander duality.

Combinatorial Alexander duality itself is established mathematics. Björner and Tancer give the standard finite-complex statement

\[
\widetilde H_i(K)
\cong
\widetilde H^{|V|-i-3}(K^*)
\]

for the Alexander dual complex. [SRC-BJORNER-TANCER-2009-ALEXANDER]

The project-specific question is what the dual complex becomes for the exact multiplicative cutoff generated by a square basin.

## 2. L016 — Large-divisor cofactor descent

Status: `PROVED`

Let

\[
k^2<n<(k+1)^2
\]

and let \(b\mid n\) satisfy

\[
b>2k.
\]

Write

\[
n=bh.
\]

Since \(n\le k^2+2k\) and \(b\ge2k+1\),

\[
h
\le
\left\lfloor
\frac{k^2+2k}{2k+1}
\right\rfloor.
\]

The quotient on the right has the exact parity split

\[
\left\lfloor
\frac{k^2+2k}{2k+1}
\right\rfloor
=
\left\lfloor\frac{k+1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
h\le\left\lfloor\frac{k+1}{2}\right\rfloor.}
\]

So every divisor term in the `b>2k` region carries an actual integer cofactor that has descended to at most half the root scale.

### Cutoff-edge window

If the divisor is an L010 shell edge

\[
b=pc,
\qquad
c\le2k<pc,
\]

then also

\[
b=pc\le2kp.
\]

Since \(n=bh>k^2\),

\[
2kph>k^2,
\]

hence, in integers,

\[
\boxed{2ph\ge k+1.}
\]

Thus every cutoff edge comes with a cofactor window

\[
\boxed{
2ph\ge k+1,
\qquad
h\le\left\lfloor\frac{k+1}{2}\right\rfloor.
}
\]

This relation is absent from a generic threshold complex: it uses both the multiplicative shell edge and the fact that the containing state lies immediately above \(k^2\).

## 3. L017 — Exact multiplicative Alexander-dual threshold

Status: `PROVED SPECIALIZATION OF ESTABLISHED ALEXANDER DUALITY`

Let

\[
G=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p>T\ge1
\]

be square-free, and let

\[
K(G,T)
=
\left\{F\subseteq\mathcal P:
\prod_{p\in F}p\le T
\right\}.
\]

Its Alexander dual is

\[
K(G,T)^*
=
\{F:\mathcal P\setminus F\notin K(G,T)\}.
\]

For a face product

\[
d=\prod_{p\in F}p,
\]

the complement product is \(G/d\). Therefore

\[
F\in K(G,T)^*
\iff
\frac{G}{d}>T
\iff
G>Td.
\]

Because all quantities are integers, this is equivalent to

\[
d\le\left\lfloor\frac{G-1}{T}\right\rfloor.
\]

Define

\[
T^*(G,T)
=
\left\lfloor\frac{G-1}{T}\right\rfloor.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
K(G,T)^*=K(G,T^*).
}
\]

So the Alexander dual stays inside the same class of finite multiplicative threshold complexes.

### Möbius-tail recursion

Let

\[
r=|\mathcal P|.
\]

Combinatorial Alexander duality gives

\[
\widetilde\chi(K(G,T))
=
(-1)^{r-3}
\widetilde\chi(K(G,T^*)).
\]

Using L013, each reduced Euler characteristic is a Möbius tail, hence

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{d\mid G\\d>T}}\mu(d)
=
(-1)^{r-3}
\sum_{\substack{d\mid G\\d>T^*}}\mu(d).
}
\]

The same identity can also be checked directly by sending \(d\) to its complementary divisor \(G/d\); the topological statement explains the associated dimension reversal.

## 4. L018 — Square-basin half-scale Alexander descent

Status: `PROVED`

Now let \(G\) be the square-free transverse small-prime support of an actual state

\[
n\in(k^2,(k+1)^2),
\]

and suppose \(G>2k\) so the large tail is nontrivial. Set

\[
T=2k.
\]

Since \(G\mid n\) up to possible multiplicities and omitted large prime factors, in particular

\[
G\le n\le k^2+2k.
\]

Therefore

\[
T^*
=
\left\lfloor\frac{G-1}{2k}\right\rfloor
\le
\left\lfloor\frac{k^2+2k-1}{2k}\right\rfloor.
\]

The right-hand side is exactly

\[
\left\lfloor\frac{k+1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
T^*\le\left\lfloor\frac{k+1}{2}\right\rfloor.
}
\]

A parity problem posed at the exact large-divisor threshold \(2k\) therefore Alexander-dualizes to a threshold no larger than half the root scale.

This is not a heuristic scale reduction: it is an exact finite integer inequality.

## 5. L019 — Two-sided integer-root squeeze on homology

Status: `PROVED FROM ESTABLISHED DUALITY + L015`

Let \(p\) be the least prime in the support, let

\[
r=|\mathcal P|,
\]

and suppose

\[
\widetilde\beta_s(K(G,T))>0.
\]

L015 already gives the original-shell bound

\[
p\le R_{s+1}(T).
\]

Combinatorial Alexander duality sends this homology to degree

\[
s^*=r-s-3
\]

in \(K(G,T^*)\).

If \(s^*\ge0\), the dual complex has nonzero shell homology in that degree, so applying L015 again gives

\[
p\le R_{s^*+1}(T^*)
=R_{r-s-2}(T^*).
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
p\le
\min\left(
R_{s+1}(T),
R_{r-s-2}(T^*)
\right)
}
\]

whenever \(r-s-3\ge0\).

For the square basin, L018 further yields

\[
\boxed{
p\le
R_{r-s-2}\!\left(
\left\lfloor\frac{k+1}{2}\right\rfloor
\right).
}
\]

This is a second, independent root filtration coming from the complementary support rather than from the original shell face.

## 6. The leading negative 1-dimensional layer

The current leading negative topology has

\[
s=1.
\]

If the transverse support has \(r\ge4\) distinct primes and \(\beta_1>0\), then

\[
\boxed{
p\le R_2(2k)}
\]

from the original shell, and simultaneously

\[
\boxed{
p\le R_{r-3}(T^*)}
\]

with

\[
T^*\le\left\lfloor\frac{k+1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]

So support size itself strengthens the least-prime restriction:

- \(r=4\): the dual bound is first-root/linear;
- \(r=5\): \(p\le R_2(T^*)\), already a second square-root squeeze at half scale;
- \(r=6\): \(p\le R_3(T^*)\);
- \(r=7\): \(p\le R_4(T^*)\);
- and so on.

In other words:

\[
\boxed{
\text{negative 1D shell}
+
\text{large support}
\Longrightarrow
\text{very small least-prime root shell}.
}
\]

This is stronger than the one-sided L015 filtration.

## 7. Interpretation

The parity problem now has three linked finite representations:

\[
\text{large Möbius tail at }2k
\longleftrightarrow
\widetilde H_*(K(G,2k))
\longleftrightarrow
\widetilde H_*(K(G,T^*)),
\]

with

\[
T^*\le\left\lfloor\frac{k+1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]

The second complex is not an arbitrary auxiliary object. It is the exact Alexander dual of the first, and L016 shows that the same half-scale bound is visible arithmetically as the actual cofactor of every divisor above \(2k\).

Thus the topology and the square-basin factorization are describing the same descent from two directions.

## 8. What this still does not prove

Alexander duality preserves information; by itself it cannot make a nonzero negative Euler contribution disappear. The new leverage is the **scale asymmetry** created by the square basin:

\[
2k
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
T^*\le(k+1)/2.
\]

The remaining task is to exploit that smaller dual threshold together with `root = cutoff = k` to prove a sign, an injection, or a recursion that is not valid for arbitrary threshold complexes.

Priority next attacks:

1. classify the dual complexes corresponding to original 1-dimensional negative shells;
2. exploit the two-sided root squeeze to show that large-support negative shells are confined to a small set of least-prime layers;
3. sum the remaining small-prime layers recursively through the half-scale dual threshold;
4. connect the dual threshold/cofactor state to the `b<=k` small transverse discrepancy region.

The general combinatorial Alexander-duality theorem is prior art; the square-basin half-scale specialization and the displayed integer inequalities are proved directly here.
