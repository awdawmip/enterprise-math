## Semantic review of the authorized delta

For `s=0,...,63`, the four labelled values are `s^2+1`, `s^2+s+1`, `abs(s^6-1)`, and `s^6+1`. The first, second and fourth are strictly positive. Since s is a nonnegative integer, the third is zero exactly when s=1. Thus there are 256 labelled values, 255 nonzero values and exactly one zero. The count is over labelled probe instances, not distinct numerical values.

Let C* be the product of the 255 nonzero values. This is a positive integer. If distinct hidden primes p and q avoid its prime divisors, neither divides any nonzero probe, so gcd(pq,a)=1 for each nonzero a. At the unique zero, every divisor of N divides zero and the positive greatest common divisor is gcd(N,0)=N. Consequently every output belongs to {1,N}; none satisfies 1<d<N. Replacing a signed probe by its absolute value does not change the gcd.

The finite-support argument leaves infinitely many primes after deletion of finitely many excluded primes. This elementary inference is sufficient for infinitely many support-avoiding choices. It does **not by itself** establish a balanced-prime abundance theorem. The original return's sections 6 and 13 explicitly import prime abundance in dyadic intervals; this unchanged premise still supplies the balanced-semiprime strengthening.
