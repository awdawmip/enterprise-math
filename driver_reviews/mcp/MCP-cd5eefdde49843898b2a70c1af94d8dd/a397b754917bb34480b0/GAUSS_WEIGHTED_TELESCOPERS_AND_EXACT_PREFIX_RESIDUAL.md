## 5. A narrowly scoped rational-carrier obstruction

This section is over Q(k) in characteristic zero. It concerns universal
rational certificates for the formal first-order systems, not finite
vectors in F_p. It neither asserts nor implies a global impossibility
for the scalar target.

First, the prefix S cannot be replaced by a rational multiple R(k)a_k.
That substitution would force

    r_a(k)R(k+1)-R(k)=r_a(k).                             (20)

Since r_a(k)=1-1/k+O(k^-2), a rational R of infinity degree d>=2 gives
a left leading term proportional to (d-1)k^(d-1), which cannot match a
right side tending to 1. If d<=0 the left side tends to 0. For d=1 its
constant term cancels and it also tends to 0. Hence no rational R solves
(20). This says nothing about a finite-field interpolation of S.

Next, there is no rational p(k) solving either bare-moment equation

    r_X(k)p(k+1)-p(k)=1, X=H or O.                       (21)

To prove this, clear b_X. The equation is
A(k)p(k+1)-b_X(k)p(k)=b_X(k). Consider the finite poles of p in any
integer-shift orbit. At its smallest pole alpha, the shifted first term
has a pole at alpha-1 with no other pole to cancel it, so A(alpha-1)=0.
This forces alpha=0 or 1/2. At its largest pole beta, the second term
requires b_X(beta)=0. This forces beta=-4/3,-5/3 for H, or -7/6,-11/6
for O. These are negative, incompatible with beta-alpha being a
nonnegative integer. The roots also force the relevant orbit to be real;
no complex orbit avoids the argument. Thus p has no finite pole and is
polynomial.

For a degree-d polynomial, the left leading coefficient is d-3/2
times its top coefficient, at degree d+1. It cannot vanish for integer d.
The right degree is 2, so d=1. At the two distinct roots of b_X, the
nonzero coefficient A forces p to vanish at both corresponding shifted
roots. A nonzero degree-one polynomial cannot do so. This proves (21).
Equation (15) exhibits its concrete affine remainder delta_X.

The same pole/degree argument proves no unit-weight rational Gauss
antidifference q for

    r_a(k)r_X(k)q(k+1)-q(k)=1.                           (22)

Now the smallest possible pole would be 5/6 or 2/3, while the largest
is again one of the negative b_X roots. There are no poles. The leading
degree coefficient is d-5/2, again forcing d=1, and the two b_X roots
again force two distinct zeros. The affine-weight certificates (6)
show exactly what this *unit-weight* obstruction leaves available.

In particular, consider the prescribed two-carrier rational flux ansatz

    Phi_X,k=S_k p(k)X_X,k+a_k q(k)X_X,k,

possibly with added terms involving the fixed forcing but no X_X,k.
Require an all-coefficient formal identity whose X-containing term is
S_k X_X,k, with no remaining homogeneous moment term. The coefficient
of S_k X_X,k necessarily imposes (21), regardless of q. Consequently
this specified ansatz is impossible over Q(k). The obstruction does not
exclude coupled U/V relations specific to the actual moments, carriers
involving the reciprocal D triangle or C, higher-order or nonrational
carriers, or finite-field certificates whose degree depends on p.

No finite-field pole-chain proof is asserted: integer shifts form cycles
in F_p and infinity degree may meet characteristic-p resonances. Only
the explicitly displayed affine defects in (15) are carried into the
