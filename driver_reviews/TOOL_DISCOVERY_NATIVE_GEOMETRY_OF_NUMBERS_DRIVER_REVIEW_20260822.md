## Accepted reusable interface

For finite or locally finite data with a declared semantic quotient/observation `pi:X->Q` and admissible finite bodies `A`, accept the following organized interface:

- raw capacity `nu(A)=|A|`;
- quotient capacity `kappa(A)=|pi(A)|`;
- fiber-free/injective predicate;
- collision defect `delta(A)=|A|-|pi(A)|`;
- witness extraction from same-fiber collisions;
- representative-set packing/covering compression for predicates factoring through `pi`;
- filtered successive collision minima `lambda_k=inf{t: delta(B_t)>=k}`;
- exact product/inclusion-exclusion laws where the declared quotient structure justifies them.

This is useful as an operational toolkit because it packages existence certificates, compression, packing/covering language, and filtered minima in one callable layer and demonstrates reuse on distinct problem families.

## Major novelty downgrade / dedup boundary

The underlying mechanisms are substantially pre-existing in Enterprise Math:

- predictive/operation quotients already compile observation-preserving equivalence classes;
- finite function/relation collision spectra already count and classify fibers/common-target collisions;
- irreversibility modules already expose explicit finite preimage multiplicity.

Consequently the implication

`|A|>|pi(A)| -> same-fiber collision`

is a finite pigeonhole/Blichfeldt-style theorem, not a new native Foundation theorem, and `kappa`, `delta`, and representative compression are not independent replacements for the existing quotient/fiber machinery.

What is accepted is the **tool composition layer** that reorganizes those ingredients into geometry-of-numbers-style questions: capacity threshold, packing/covering representative count, and successive collision minima.

## Mandatory semantic boundary

No quotient `pi` may be invented merely to obtain a desired capacity theorem. The observation/equivalence relation must already be declared or derived at the correct semantic level. Different `pi` give different capacities and minima.

No Euclidean volume, determinant, convexity, symmetric body, or lattice theorem becomes native by analogy.
