## C304-I. Exact lemma for any integer polynomial
Let `P in Z[t]` have degree at most `d`, let `T in Z`, and assume `P(T),...,P(T+d)` are exactly available. Define

`B_k = (Delta^k P)(T)/k! = (1/k!) sum_{j=0}^k (-1)^(k-j) binom(k,j) P(T+j)`, `0<=k<=d`.

**Theorem:** each `B_k in Z` and
`gcd(s,content(P)) = gcd(s,B_0,...,B_d)` for every positive integer `s`.
For any prime `p`, the common scalar's exact valuation is
`v_p(g)=min(v_p(s),min_k v_p(B_k))`.

**Proof:** The falling-factorial polynomials `(t-T)_k=(t-T)(t-T-1)...(t-T-k+1)` form a monic triangular **integral** basis of `Z[t]`. Its transition matrix to monomials is unimodular (determinant 1) with integral inverse. Newton's identity `P(t)=sum_k B_k (t-T)_k` therefore shows that the ideals generated by monomial coefficients and by these `B_k` are identical. The gcd and valuation identities follow. This is a general exact theorem, not a finite numerical pattern.

**Pitfall/counterexample:** `P(t)=t(t-1)` is even at every integer but has coefficient content 1. Taking the gcd of ordinary integer-point values gives a false extra factor 2; division by `k!` in Newton differences is essential. No integer factorization of `s` is needed to evaluate the gcd.
