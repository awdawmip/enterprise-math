## Theorem I — Universal modular recovery of integer content

Let P belong to Z[t], deg(P)<=d, let s>=1 and T be integers. Set M=s*d! (with 0!=1). Obtain only the d+1 residues r_j=P(T+j) mod M in {0,...,M-1}, j=0,...,d. Define delta_k as the k-th forward difference Delta^k(r)_0 taken modulo M, and beta_k=(delta_k/k!) mod s, for 0<=k<=d.

**Claim.** All delta_k are divisible by k! as ordinary integers and

  gcd(s, content(P)) = gcd(s,beta_0,...,beta_d).

For each prime p its exact capped valuation equals min(v_p(s),min_k v_p(beta_k)), with valuation of zero taken as infinity inside this cap. The algorithm does not require factoring s.

**Proof.** The shifted falling factorials (t-T)_k, k=0,...,d, form a Z-basis of Z[t] in degree <=d, because their monomial transition matrix is triangular with diagonal entries 1 and integral inverse. Newton interpolation gives the integral coefficients B_k=Delta^k P(T)/k!, and the ideal generated by the B_k equals the ideal of monomial coefficients; hence gcd_k B_k=content(P) up to sign. Delta is Z-linear, so delta_k is congruent to k! B_k mod M. As M is divisible by k!, delta_k is divisible by k!; after integer division, delta_k/k! is congruent to B_k modulo M/k!=s*d!/k!, hence modulo s. Taking the gcd with s proves the theorem. Prime valuations follow from gcd valuations. QED.

**Why factorial division cannot be skipped.** P(t)=t(t-1) has integer values all divisible by 2 yet polynomial coefficient content 1. For s=2, T=0, d=2, the ordinary values' gcd incorrectly gives 2 whereas beta_2=1 yields 1.

## Theorem II — Modular exact cancellation on the C195/H168 paths

Use the inherited C298/C300 integer identity f_i=A_i^shared/D_i^shared with

  D_i^shared=s_i * product_q q(t)^e_i(q),

and the inherited exact Z[t] cancellation A_i^shared=C_i*A_i^red where C_i is the product of two known primitive linear factors. Here deg A_i^red <= d_i with d_i=352 for 124 paths and d_i=265 for the special index 28. Define M_i=s_i*d_i!.

At any integer t with C_i(t)!=0, compute only the residue

  R = A_i^shared(t) mod (M_i*abs(C_i(t))).

Because A_i^shared(t)=C_i(t)*A_i^red(t), its residue R is divisible by abs(C_i(t)) and

  A_i^red(t) mod M_i
    = sign(C_i(t))*(R/abs(C_i(t))) mod M_i.

This uses exact integer division, not a modular inverse; it works even when the cancellation factor is not coprime to M_i.

The shared numerator at t is evaluated in factorized form: sum 79 integer-scaled products of affine numerator factors and exact integer quotients of the shared primitive denominator product by each term's denominator product. Select d_i+1 consecutive integers avoiding zero denominator-factor evaluations and C_i zeros; such a window exists since only finitely many roots are excluded. Compute each contribution modulo M_i*abs(C_i(t)), avoiding expansion of the full numerator coefficient array and its full exact evaluation values. Apply Theorem I to the resulting residues to obtain g_i=gcd(s_i,content(A_i^red)).

**Bit-resource guarantee.** With m=ceil(log2(M_i+1)) and h bounding the bit width of abs(C_i(T+j)), the algorithm stores O(d_i*m) bits of difference residues; it performs d_i+1 factor-circuit evaluations at modulus width <=m+h, d_i(d_i+1)/2 m-bit modular differences, and d_i+1 factorial divisions and gcds. This proves bounded-modulus reconstruction but not an unconditional asymptotic speedup without an explicit factor-circuit cost model.
